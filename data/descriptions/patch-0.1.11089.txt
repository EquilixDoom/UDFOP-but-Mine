Auras ||| Auras are cosmetic effects worn from the aura picker on the account card, each granted with a title. They include the Oblivion Ward, Golden Radiance, the Holo Shadow Cloak (with the Shadow Fang title), the developers' Seraph Wings and Crystal Resonance (with the Crystal Fist title). An aura now shows to everyone nearby within a second or two, up to 32 are drawn at once, and the cloak and wings fold away while riding or swimming. They sit correctly on both Morrowind bodies and Eye of the Beholder sprites. Lilac Warren is SylviaBun's aura: six lilac bunnies with cotton tails and little maid's headdresses hop round the player's feet on a ring of lilac light, leaving fading paw-print tracks, with an occasional happy binky and sparkles of lilac, pink and white. The Elite Breach adds Beaconlight (Redoubt 10), The Burning Stair (Redoubt 20) and Firstlight. The first time a player's aura comes into view it no longer freezes the game for several seconds, most noticeable when coming down from the Overworld near a crowd such as at Dagon's Breach, and auras take a fraction of the time to prepare after an update.
Billboard Look ||| Billboard Look is a setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data, off by default. On, Morrowind bodies and horses stand as flat upright pictures at their true height, like the classic sprites, instead of shrinking when seen from above or up close; off, they keep their true height from every angle.
Cover ||| With Enhanced AI, trees, crates, barrels, statues and solid decor are cover that blocks sight and stops missiles both ways, while area spells still burst where they land. Grass, flowers, small clutter and see-through props are not cover, and felled trees stop being cover. In winter the trees of a wood are cover the way they are the rest of the year: enemies hide behind the trunk and under the crown. Trees, rocks, crates and other cover also stop spells outdoors and inside buildings, as they already did in dungeons.
Crew ||| The crew are the named hands on the player's ship. Each near crew member is a real animated character with a green health bar, a name and a job such as First Mate, Bard, Bosun, Gunner or Carpenter, remembers fights and boardings, and chats in pairs. The crew strike up a sea shanty led by a bard, hurry about when the guns are out, and line the rail when grapples are thrown. The crew lasts twice as long as before, since cannon fire, fires on deck, rams and the sea serpent's blows take half as many men, though swivel-gun boats still go for the crew first at half the old pace and enemy crews are unchanged. Hands lost in a boarding cost the ship that much crew, the player's own blows and spells pass their crew by, and the player cannot pickpocket their own crew or a town's defenders. A crewed ship lying at a port's quay lists Crew first on the shipwright's Exchange tab, where men are signed on or off for gold (Buy 1, Buy 10, Sell 1, Sell 10) and the row shows the crew aboard against her full crew. Each port has sailors to spare (cheap), is short of them (dear) or neither, with prices drifting by the day, and every isle of the Veil is short of sailors; a man never sells for as much as the shipwright charges to hire one. Selling never takes her last man or a hand in the shore party and always leaves one named hand aboard, the least valuable going first (plain deckhands before anyone holding a post, a crew-hall recruit after the ship's own hands, the First Mate last). A ship that came into the realm through customs can't sell her crew, a booked passage's crew belong to her master, and crew can't be sold during a fight. Crew is never an item: it can't go into packs, shops, stalls, the market or trades.
Crowds ||| Crowds of players are drawn much more smoothly with the Morrowind bodies, and a nearer player taking over a body slot waits a moment instead of swapping constantly. Distant players in a big crowd keep walking smoothly even when their computer runs at a low frame rate. The crowd setting on the Other players card is Players shown at once and works across the whole game: Everyone (the default), Nearest 12 or Nearest 24, past which only the nearest other players are drawn. Always shown are the party, a duel opponent, anyone being traded with, anyone riding in a wagon or sailing on a boat with the player and both fighters of any nearby duel, and the number does not apply in a PvP zone (the open sea included), a siege, a Royal Tourney, the Arena or the Overworld travel view. A hidden player's wagon, horses, boat and torch are hidden too.
Death Screen ||| The death screen was fixed and audited, including resolving the online screen's rise and ensuring quick-loading (F11) after dying in town no longer leaves the death screen up. It shows the amount of gold lost and says Rise instead of "End the journey". It names any letters of credit lost and what they were worth, and when the character's trading is frozen it says that what was dropped was lost outright. After dying to a foe in a PvP zone it says: "Nothing dropped. Only another player's kill drops what you carry.", translated into every language.
Decks ||| Decks are the ship's walking surfaces. Forecastles, poops and stairs are part of the deck so nobody can hide out of a boarder's reach, bodies ride the ship and go down with her, and standing on a quay beside a ship does not count as being aboard, while riding another player's boat does. Players standing on a boat's deck stay put while she sails instead of jittering and sliding, and a player walking a deck is drawn walking smoothly rather than in lurches.
Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, the app has its own icon, and it finds and installs updates again. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. Downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app (to be dragged to the Bin once open), and on Windows pinned taskbar or Start menu entries may need repinning under the new name. It keeps the account and settings files in memory instead of reading them from disk every frame, as the browser does, giving smoother frames in a town. The app updates the game one file at a time instead of downloading about 130 MB and replacing the whole game folder, the full installer running only when the app itself changes or a file-by-file update fails, on Windows, macOS, Linux and the portable build. The app keeps far more of the graphics work it has already done between launches, so the few-second freezes the first time something is done in a session (opening the inventory or map, entering a dungeon, a city or a Dagon Breach) should stop from the second launch onward; the first launch after that update still does the work once.
Discord ||| The project's Discord is linked from the game. Its channel announcing the Oblivion Gates now also announces the sea serpent with the harbour bells, and when Sethrakul is slain it names the port and the top fighters. A kill is only posted for the site most players' games agree on, so faked fights do not reach it. Every world event post is a card in its event's colour (Dagon's Breach, the Sea Serpent, the Abyss Dungeon, the Battlespire) with its facts laid out as fields, and each kind of event can post to its own thread.
Dwarven Pistolet ||| The Dwarven Pistolet is a one-handed Dwemer flintlock that fires the Thunderlock's Dwemer Pellets for 4-15 damage, using Archery, weighing 2.5 kg and worth 260 gold. With one in each hand the pistols fire in turn, each reloading on its own, the off-hand shot hitting softer and leaning on Agility. It has its own look in first person, on the paper doll and in inventory lists, a modelled 3D gun on Morrowind models, and its own stance on Morrowind bodies: low with the muzzle down at rest, the arm out on the eye line when aiming. It is never sold in shops and no longer drops from any enemy, chest or boss, though ones already owned work as before; Magic and Rare guns no longer repeat their metal in the name. It runs the code of the prototype its feel was tuned in, with a few differences kept on purpose (the room's camera shake and light on the pistol, Weapon Widget switches, the holster and left-handed play). A pair fires the way it should: the second pistol fires the moment of the pull even while the first is mid-shot, each shot belongs to the pistol that fired it, a reloading pistol holds still and the one fired last is drawn in front. A one-handed weapon can be held in one hand with a pistol in the other, the two taking turns, the pistol taking twice as long to reload and the blade fighting on when no Pellets are left.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are sold wherever weapons are, in Weapon Smiths, Armorers, General Stores and Pawn Shops, and restocked each day. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang. On the Morrowind body it is held like a rifle, the right hand on its pistol grip and the left supporting the forend, carried at rest at a low ready with the muzzle down and across toward the left foot and coming up to a level ready stance while shooting, for a few seconds after and while foes are hurting the player, in first and third person, the Hourlock held the same way. In the Morrowind first-person view the aimed gun sits below the middle of the screen instead of riding over the crosshair, with the left hand still on its forend. It no longer drops from any enemy, chest or boss, though ones already owned work as before, and the Last Lock, the guns' Legendary, can no longer be found (its codex row says "No longer found"). Switching to a Dwarven Pistolet for a moment no longer clears its reload.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor. A felled Elite Breach lord falls the way the Oblivion Gate's Warden does: it drops to the floor, sinks into the stone and leaves its corpse where it fell, with a death cry, a burst from its chest, a white flash, a camera shake (none under reduced motion), a thud and embers in its element's colour. Flying lords fall out of the air, everyone in the hall sees the corpse in the same place, and players riding a moving piece of an Elite Breach are drawn on it.
Equipment ||| Equipment swaps are instant, and the character's old armour pictures are released when gear changes.
Event Board ||| The World Events board sits at the left of the screen, under the chat and above the buffs, hotbar and quick slots, following the HUD scale, and shows Dagon's Breach, the sea serpent, the Abyss Dungeon, fallen stars and town raids instead of the chat. Each event shows its time left or time until it starts, how far away it is, which way to go and where it is, and a raid under way shows how many raiders are down. It lists only events the player can still join, up to four on a tall screen and three on a smaller one, the rest counted with a pointer to the Timers window. When an event is about to start, and again when it begins, its news opens at the top of the board for a few seconds, with the event's own messages added; every gate, serpent, Abyss Dungeon and fallen star is announced to everyone, a raid within its region or 25 km of it, and every event message is saved in the journal. With no room the news shows on the message line instead, and under Enhanced Plus the board can be moved or hidden in the HUD editor. It is turned off under Settings, Interface, Messages & prompts, Event board, which sends event messages back to the chat and raid messages back to the message line.
Family Tab ||| The Family tab is part of Project Legacy, in the pause window between Holdings and System. It has the Tree (zoom, pan, play as a sibling, pass the mantle, and where each member lives), the House (model, seat, generations, the living and the fallen, and the family's homes) and the Hall of Ancestors (every house founded), and keypad / opens it. The character sheet on the Stats tab also shows the house, its model and the player's generation and, in an Enduring house, the age against the lifespan, the years Arkay has taken and when the player becomes an elder. It is not shown when playing without Project Legacy.
Fast Travel ||| Fast travel was fixed so that arriving in a raided town no longer crashes. It shows the journey's days again, which pass on the player's clock so the player arrives in the world's present, and cautious travel heals again. Fast travel no longer stutters every couple of map pixels, and crossing the map no longer briefly drops nearby players or makes the player stand still for them when joining the next area is slow.
Fishing ||| Fishing is a gathering profession done with a Fishing-Net, standing in water or at sea by daylight (07:00 to 17:59). Holding E winds the net and letting go throws it 3 to 12 metres, and the player hauls in by holding E to raise a band and keeping the net's weight inside. A haul gives 1 to 2 Raw Fish to the Stores, a missed tug still gives a plain catch, and two schools rise in each area every day for an extra fish. At sea there is a 1 in 50 chance of a Pearl and 1 in 100 of a Slaughterfish, about 1 haul in 200 brings a trophy fish, and the limit is 40 hauls a day per account. Each haul gives XP as if at the highest tier the rank unlocked. The net will not cast in a dungeon or settlement or below a quarter of fatigue. Fishing online no longer piles up memory while travelling.
Fleet ||| The Fleet page is the ledger of the player's ships. Ship deeds no longer sit in the pack, because a ship's title goes into the ledger, whether the deed was bought at a shop (she waits at that port) or the ship was taken as a prize. Each ship has a card with her name, class, value, position, hull, canvas, crew and stores. Summon brings her to a free berth at any port or onto the water the player clicks, Send away lays her up at the nearest port with her cargo, crew mend her where she lies and the shipwright's window opens for a ship laid up or lying at the port. Picking a ship up puts her parts and title in the pack, and selling her parts sells her. On the open sea, while it is a PvP zone, a ship can't be summoned or sent away; she must be sailed there.
Friendly Spells ||| Friendly spells integrated Mac's two drops onto ALLY-CAST, alongside CRICKET-DUNGEON and AUDIT PARTY-REST. Spells given to a party member or companion no longer pass through hills, and area blasts no longer reach over a hilltop.
Gilded Weapons ||| The Dragonfire Oath is a Daedric Dai-Katana that can drop from the Warden of the Burning Gate about one fight in a hundred, giving +40% damage, +15 Strength, +30 Long Blade and +15 Endurance; its Dragonfire makes a blow that lands on a Daedra burn it again for another third of the hit and burn every other Daedra within 3 m for the same. The Sunken Sovereign is an Ebony Saber that can drop from the Old Coil about one kill in a hundred for a ship that did damage, giving +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Long Blade and 10% life leech; its Undertow adds +10% damage to each blow on the same foe up to +50%, fading after 6 seconds without a hit, and heals the player for 10% when it kills. Both are shown in the Collections codex with their own hints. Gilded items are marked in crimson rather than gold so they no longer look like Rares, with a small gold crown with red jewels on the inventory tile, the item card and the loot banner, and the Old Coil's Gilded weapon is the Ebony Saber once more.
Graphics Presets ||| Graphics presets set a group of settings at once. Low turns Steady shadows off, and Medium, High and Ultra turn it on. High and Ultra can be chosen even when the enhanced outdoors is off, and a setting moved off a preset's value shows its Changed mark. A player who picked Low before this update sees the preset read Custom until pressing Low again.
Guild Hall ||| A guild hall is a guild-owned house bought by the guildmaster with treasury funds, featuring member chests, crafting, resting, customizable painting and yard furnishings, and a hall's roll as the first book on its shelf detailing its chapter's standing, Master, and officers. Windows inside buildings are now see-through, town boards fill in once three players have visited guild halls, and in rooms with many lamps, stationary people cast shadows from every lamp rather than just the nearest twelve. Online, if the player does not yet qualify for the next rank, the guild popup lists what is missing: the rank's name ("Next rank: Swordsman"), reputation against what is needed ("Reputation 14 of 20"), the guild-skill level the player is short of with their best guild skill named, a second guild skill at the lower bar and the guild's own skills by name. It does not appear when nothing is missing or at the top rank, and it shows on the enhanced UI skin and in all 13 supported languages. At rank 7 the hall says the next rank is a chapter chair (a council chair or the Master's) won by Merit at each Sunday's Turning, asking reputation 80, 14 real days in the guild and an account 7 real days old, and a service only a chair opens (the Mages Guild's Teleport, Zenithar's summoning) says so at any rank. The new Guild Chapters tutorial page opens when the rank is held at 7. The hall and the Standing page show what the realm still owes the player (already earned, arriving at most 15 a real day) and warn before a review puts the player on probation or lowers their rank, with when and why, and on probation they are told the stakes: below -10 expelled at the review, below 0 it runs 28 more days, at 0 or more it ends, and online earning the next rank ends it sooner. The rank text scrolls on its own so Talk, Service and Reforge stay on screen. Expelled or turned away for standing, the player sees at the hall their standing, the standing it takes members at, how many quests bring them back there and how fast it recovers on its own, and the hall names skills the guild also wants and says that its halls in other regions still have work when one is shut for the Season; the classic look's refusal says the same. A guildmaster can sell the guild's hall at its door, the way a home is sold: choose "Sell it" on the plaque, or use the menu when the door is in Info mode, or sell from the Guild tab. The gold goes to the guild's treasury and the guild's vault stays with the guild.
Guild Tab ||| The Guild tab is a full window of its own rather than a page inside the Social panel, opened from the Social panel's Guild tab or from the guild hall's chest. On wide screens it sits beside the chat and on phones it fills the screen, and a ← Social button returns to Friends, Party or Letters, showing a count when friend requests, party invitations or letters wait. Its pages (Overview, Members, Treasury, Vault, Arms and Settings, and Stores) are listed down the side, each with its name, a short line about what it holds and a count for Members and Vault, and the arrow keys move between them. A row of tiles at the top shows rank, the guild's gold and silver, its members (and how many are online), the vault and the hall; text is larger, each page's sections sit in their own cards, and the result of the last action shows in a bar at the bottom of the window. Long pages keep their scroll position when they update. The Members page shows whether each member is online, with a green dot while playing and "Online" or "Last online 3 h ago" under their rank, a member counting as online within ten minutes of their last check-in, and has a folded list, "What each rank may do", showing every rank's powers in the guild's own rank names. Settings lists everything standing in the way of disbanding before pressing, Overview says where the rest of the guild is run, and with no guild yet the tab says in two lines what a guild is for.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy. A home's crafting stations (forge, workbench, loom, mason's and jeweller's benches, alchemy and enchanting) can be used by the account's other characters as well as the buyer, while the storage still belongs to the character who bought it. An online home belongs to the account, not to one character: any character can go in at any hour, furnish and paint it, choose who may enter, let its rooms and collect rent, stock its trader and sell it, the gold going to the character who sells it or collects the rent. Each character keeps its own storage in a home, and the items a character placed there from its own pack come back to it the next time it goes online if another character sells the home or it goes when the buyer is deleted. Deleting an online character removes the homes it bought along with everything placed in them, homes left by characters deleted earlier are released, and a character can't be deleted while another character on the account still has goods for sale at a trader in one of its homes.
Hourlock ||| The Hourlock is the Brass Remnant's Gilded drop, a Gilded Dwarven Long Bow with fixed lines of +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Archery and +10 shock damage, and the power The Hour Tolls: every third arrow that lands deals double damage and a kill gives the arrow back. It was a Gilded Thunderlock before; a gun Hourlock already owned stays a gun and still refunds its pellet. It comes from the Brass Remnant's spoils one time in fifty, online.
Jungle of Bantha ||| The Jungle of Bantha is dense jungle on Hammerfell's south-eastern shore, from Lainlyn and Satakalaam east to Mournoth: giant trees close overhead with taller ones breaking through, young trees and palms fill the light gaps, and the floor is carpeted in ferns, broad leaves and grass. A humid haze hangs under the canopy, thickest at dawn and in rain, and it thins when climbing out of it. It is on by default, switched under "The world" in the Features menu, and always on online. Its big trees stand about 16 m apart and its young trees and palms at most one to a patch, so the player can see and walk between them while the crowns still meet overhead. Winding dirt trails cut through it, about one in every map square, and the floor carries as much grass as meadows elsewhere. Roads and tracks through it are lined with pairs of great trees, one on each side, about one pair every 150 metres, with vines strung between their crowns across the road well above a rider's head; the three pairings are a mahogany with a kapok leaning toward the road, with a fig, or with a kapok sunk deep into its mound, and every pair is set into the ground so no roots float on a slope.
Letters of Credit ||| Online, a letter of credit cannot be put into a chest, cupboard or body, since a shared container is everyone's, so it stays in the pack and is given to another player in a trade. The player's own storage (house cupboards, ship's chest), a pile they dropped themselves and the wagon still take letters, and nothing changes offline. Outside a trade a letter never reaches the player through another player's game, and letters already lying in a shared chest or on a body when the update arrived were removed. A revenant that kills the player never takes a letter, a letter a revenant already holds returns to the wallet on the next load, and a sworn revenant that deserts gives back any letter it carried. A player's kill takes a tenth of each letter of credit's worth into the remains instead of the whole letter, and the letter stays in the wallet.
Live Events ||| Live events for everyone online were added in 0.1.4169. Staff can run /event rocket, a show for everyone online in which mounds push up out of the ground and shoot poop into the sky and a great, very fat horse gallops across it neighing and farting, followed by a line of fat little horses; then the sky turns brown and every mound becomes a geyser while poop rains everywhere and a herd of fat horses farts across the sky in every direction. Everyone online sees the same moment of the show, it repeats until staff end it with /event off, and nothing hurts or slows anyone.
Logging ||| Logging is a gathering profession where wilderness trees are felled using a Wood-Axe and clean cuts with timber going to Stores, supporting all forest types and online visibility beyond the Bay. Roadside trees in the Jungle of Bantha can't be felled for Logging.
Main Menu ||| The main menu has Install beside Play, a profile mark that is a portrait of the last character, and Patreon and Ko-fi icons in the top-left corner that open their pages in the browser. It is no longer cut off on short windows, and the logo sits below the corner icons on narrow windows. The buttons are carved stone slabs in the Enhanced Plus style, all one width, and on phones they are a little more compact so the whole menu fits above the Screenshots and About buttons. Main menu clicks play the in-game click sound once the game data is loaded. The main menu first asks one question, Online or Offline, and Offline opens Continue, New Game, Load Game and the Test Room with a way back. Settings and Overhauls are in the bottom bar beside Screenshots and About, which no longer covers the buttons on phones, small or short screens and long languages. Double-clicking Offline no longer opens Load Game by accident, Escape puts the keyboard back on the button the player came from, and Tutorials and Screenshots are translated. The menus' animated background draws about four times faster, pixel for pixel the same.
Morrowind Body ||| The Morrowind body features climbing poses (hanging, shimmying, wall climbing, leaps, pull-overs, vault tucks), gripping animations, first-person hand reach, crowd optimization, port-made steel plate armor sets, synchronized attack speeds, spell-casting animations, and T-pose fixes. Placed home/yard decoration NPCs stand in Morrowind bodies, you can ride in third person, look down over the 3D horse's neck in first person, and 3D horses match sprite coats with unique flank markings. With the Morrowind model in first person the arms grip the wall while climbing even with the weapon sheathed. The cape hangs from the chest alone and moves only with it as one stiff sheet, so walking, swinging the arms and sitting no longer bend it, and leaning back swings the whole cape with the chest. Looting a fallen enemy in a dungeon no longer turns its Morrowind body back into the old sprite while the loot window is open, bodies no longer clip through floors and walls, and chain boots, gauntlets and pauldrons, including Legendary ones like The Wayrest Courier's Treads, show on the body (in iron) instead of bare skin. Morrowind bodies and horses no longer squash, stretch or shrink when the camera gets close to them; up close the camera cuts into them like any other 3D object.
Morrowind Models ||| Morrowind models bring the Morrowind rig into the game with range-based asset loading, parity audits, faster loads, physical dropped items with custom pictures/models, ground rarity outlines/lights, physics collisions, smooth shading across all body/armour/weapon parts, and fixed third-person horse riding view. Morrowind models (the player, NPCs, enemies and horses) no longer turn invisible until restart. If one body gets a bad position online, only that body stays at its last good position instead of every body drawn after it vanishing. With the Morrowind look a Battle Axe (one-handed) looks and swings like a one-handed axe and a War Axe (two-handed) like a two-handed one. Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data has Morrowind Model Detail: Pixelated, the default and the original look, or Sharp, which draws bodies, horses and the player's arms at the screen's full resolution and uses more video memory.
Online Death ||| Online death had fixes for a death that respawns and for the online death loop. The gear a fallen player leaves in the wilds holds only things that can be worn. A death taken back holds, and the operator has a Realm revive run. A party member's Resurrection saves a Bloodline character: while the death screen is up (up to two minutes) it brings the player back with no death on the family tree, no Succession and no tombstone. The death is held by the server, so closing the tab, reloading or logging in from another device does not escape it, and if the death is already settled the caster is told "<name> has fallen for good" and keeps their magicka. In the Wrothgarian Mountains and on the open Eltheric Ocean, dying to a monster, a ship, a fall or the sea no longer drops anything: the bag, cart, companions' packs and worn gear are kept. The gold a player's kill drops is a tenth of the purse instead of half, and if another player struck the victim in the last 10 seconds and something else finishes them, the kill is theirs. After dying to a foe in the Wrothgarian Mountains the player rises in the nearest town inside the zone, in the same ring they fell in. A player's kill is no longer charged twice (once into the remains and once on rising) when the game noticed the kill late, and a Resurrect after a player's kill no longer says the gold was spared.
Online Players ||| Other players are drawn as paperdolls with names or in the Morrowind body, one rig instance per peer built from their look, third-person at the feet. They no longer jump backwards near an area edge, teleport in sieges, Royal Tourneys, homes and ships, dash ahead after a lag spike or snap back when stepping into a new area, because movement updates carry their send time. When a player's movement stops reaching you they now stand still until they move again instead of running in place, including players who went quiet before you arrived. Other players far away are no longer drawn: a Draw distance setting on the Other players card chooses 150 m, 300 m (the default), 600 m or Everyone, the party and a dueled player always show, and the 150 m option can hide a distant archer. Positions near the edge between map areas arrive mostly once instead of two to four times, and other players stand on the ground without floating or jumping a metre at a time. A player who reconnects stands once, an old connection from before a restart or a second tab no longer leaving a frozen copy in town or in a dungeon, and logging in to the same account a second time in the same area closes the older connection. A player walking near the player no longer stands frozen for minutes then jumps, a player who dies is laid down once with no second body and cry, a body is shown instead of the player standing alive when coming into a place where someone has just died, an old message about a death no longer knocks down a player who has got up, and when the player crosses into the next area the area they left sees them change armour or clothes. Other players' armour pictures no longer pile up in memory as new outfits are met online, and no longer flicker away after a pause or a long load.
Party Quests ||| Party quests work online. Shared quests stay in step after a reload and neither member loses what they did when two act at the same moment. A received quest waits on the player who shared it only while they are in the party, a choice is answered once rather than by both, a shared quest's gold is split among the members who hold the quest rather than the whole party, a kill is counted once, a party member's own copy of a repeatable quest doesn't overwrite the player's, and share messages and the quest shelf's words are in the player's language.
Pause Menu ||| The enhanced pause menu displays guild reputation and features an online mailbox represented by an envelope beside the hourglass. When developers send a message, the envelope displays a count and a line in world chat announces post arrival; messages can be opened to read or accessed from anywhere by typing /mail in chat on either menu style, and messages with unclaimed items cannot be accidentally discarded. A Growth tab sits beside Stats. Enter on a menu tab no longer presses Ascend, and the Yes/No confirmation keeps keyboard focus inside the box and answers only once. A laurel beside the hourglass and the mailbox opens the Season window. Tapping outside the hourglass, mailbox or Season window to close it no longer presses what is underneath, a button that can't open its page no longer leaves the game paused with no menu, and on phones the tabs no longer sit under the icon row.
Performance ||| A GPU memory leak from opening the map in a dungeon or building was fixed, long sessions and long journeys no longer accumulate video or GPU memory because left areas are released over time, settings are read faster everywhere, and people, guards, and foes do not pop out of view at screen edges. Opening the Overworld no longer freezes the game as nearby dungeon models load one per frame after the view rises, deep snow processing is disabled while the Overworld is open, and saving in snowy areas is faster because snow-track data is recompressed only when changed. Many town and frame costs were cut: still flats share large buffers and one index buffer, yards and stables behind the player or far away are not drawn, collision no longer grows with the view distance, lamp shadows redraw only the sides someone is in front of and reuse last frame's answer, night towns and harbours make less garbage, shaders compile in the background, the sky skips stars by day and two-graphics-card laptops use the faster card. Entering the world freezes for less, since working out where every town's pilgrims go on their holy days and picking the day's elite halls in the Wrothgarian zone no longer stalls the game, and a town with decorated yards in view makes about 17-18% fewer draw calls a frame. Clicking in the world no longer stutters, and arriving in a large city or walking out of a building in one no longer freezes the game for several seconds, worst in Firefox. The game holds about 150 MB less memory once a town has loaded, and its peak on a walk is about 150 MB lower, with what is seen unchanged.
Quest Journal ||| The quest journal's Quests tab opens on the quest the card is showing. Under each active quest a line names the place its latest entry sends the player, in Daggerfall's words, and Show on map closes the journal and opens the travel map on the place, outdoors only, or says to ask around if it is not on the map yet. The chronicle shows "Time remains" on a quest with a deadline, counting down live. Finished quests have a Remove button and Clear archive removes them all, and active quests can be hidden without abandoning them, with Show hidden bringing them back. Typing in the quest journal's note field works again on the classic and Grimoire UIs, opening as the game's standard text-entry box. The journal (the L key) has an Abandon button on every quest except the main story's, clicked twice to set the quest aside, which keeps everything it had and stops it running until reclaimed from the pause menu's Quests tab under Abandoned, while bounties and revenant hunts keep their own Abandon. If a main-story step failed (a refusal, a missed deadline, a death) and the story stopped, the journal's Main Quests offers that step again from its start and the questor offers it once more; a step the story has already moved past is never offered. Restart no longer restarts a quest that has already paid out, and "Time remains" shows the deadline the quest names rather than its shortest timer.
Quest Tracker ||| The quest tracker is a small HUD card showing one quest with its title, newest journal entry, where it points and deadline left. By default it shows the quest the journal last changed, Track keeps one on the card and the choice is saved with the character, and the card steps aside for the Overworld's panel, a journey's junction and on a phone the chat. Features, Interface, Quest tracker turns it off. It does less work every frame.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. Quest people in dungeons stand clear of what was built over their spot. A save taken while a question box was up asks it again on load, a save just after a quest ended or just before the next one started no longer leaves a quest stuck or the next one lost, an older save learns which steps ended from its journal on first load, and a quickload inside the same dungeon no longer stands every quest person, item and foe twice. A quest's gold is paid once, people and items of an ended quest are put away, and a quest item dropped while the quest was set aside is no longer copied when picked up after taking the quest back. Raw tags like "%ra" in jokes and rumors, a talk answer that could end a quest with an error, a duel sent to the wrong town, a healer who never asked a second time and Lord K'avar's archers returning every time his hideout was left were fixed.
Raids ||| A town saved from raiders stays saved when the world's clock is corrected and pays the player once, and guards and townspeople are safe from the player's blade and horse during a raid. Raids are named 15 minutes before they start, including raids early in the game day, and show on the World Events board.
Readied Spellcasting Hands ||| With a spell readied the casting hands are raised and ready on screen until the cast, as in jefetienne's Readied Spellcasting Hands 1.3 ported 1:1. Self-only spells raise nothing since they cast at once. It is on by default and can be turned off under Features > Combat. With a one-handed weapon drawn and the other hand empty, a readied spell sits in the free hand instead of putting the weapon away: the swing button swings the weapon and the cast button (left-click, the touch cast or the hotbar) casts the spell, even while the swing button is held down. Sheathing keeps the spell readied and drawing again puts it back in the free hand. It works with the classic sprites and the Morrowind model, and other players see both hands.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy. The player's own Disintegrate no longer deletes a revenant: it still breaks through the last stand and will but brings the revenant to its knees like any killing blow, to Kill (its trophy weapon drops) or Spare (it joins as a companion). A revenant's lair is always a dungeon the player can enter: one lairing in a graveyard with no way in (28 had none, The Stuvam Graveyard among them) moves to the nearest enterable dungeon, and the map and hunt follow it.
Sailing ||| Players can sail together and set sail from their own ship, and sailing from the Overworld tells the player how many days of their time the voyage takes. Overworld travel by sea sails straight for a port that lies upwind instead of zig-zagging, steers well clear of islands at the new speeds and sails through fog instead of rowing. Other players' boats keep sailing smoothly between updates when the server is busy, a turning boat stays on its curve instead of drifting wide, and a boat that stops suddenly no longer jumps back, anyone on its deck staying aboard.
Seasons ||| The seat war runs in 8-week Seasons with crown/keeper honors, banner ribbons, Chronicle records, dynamic events (schisms, successions, crackdowns, rivalries, declines, ascendancies), Season Master honors, guild patronage bidding, townsfolk dialogue, and a chapter work guide above chapter cards on the Work tab. A laurel on the pause menu beside the hourglass and the mailbox opens the Season window, also opened by typing /season in chat. It shows the ranked season (time left, standing on the Arena, the Spire, Iliac Hand and the Gambit, and this season's deepest voyage and legends sunk at sea), the seats' Season (its week, when it ends and what the war for the seats is fought over: crowns, the ribbon, the weekly Tides and the reset) and, while one is on, the festival (its cakes, the Starfall and when it ends). Each row says what the board or event is in a line or two and has a button that takes the player straight to it.
Server ||| The server does much less work per player, and reconnecting and moving no longer rewrite the player's details. When nobody else is in the player's area their game stops sending its enemies and ship cabin. The account service makes fewer trips to its database, so busy evenings should load faster. An update that changes the online server disconnects everyone online once when it goes live.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away. Merchantmen, pirates, navy ships and packets carry players standing on their decks instead of sailing out from under them with their rails shoving them along, and other players see the player standing on that ship's deck. Frame drops at sea, especially with other ships nearby or sea creatures spawning, were cut by skipping collision checks on boats nowhere near what is being tested (the worst measured case went from about 16 ms of collision work a frame to about 4 ms).
Sky ||| The sun, moons, stars, seasons and calendar all run on the faster sky, which switched once at a moment when both schedules showed the same hour. The sky's square frame was removed. A hard, smooth arc that could cut across the sky at dusk is gone. The classic sky keeps only the two sky files it last drew instead of every one it ever read (about 8 MB each).
Snow ||| With Snowless Swamps and Jungles switched on, swamps and jungles get no deep snow or falling snow in winter, and rain/snow no longer become choppy after hours of open play. Snow and the rooms and castles the player has left now let go of what they held.
Spells ||| Ending the player's own spells was fixed, the aim hint always shows when a spell waits for a target, and a party member healing the player no longer repeats the cast message on every cast. Classic spellcasting runs at the same pace as the Morrowind animation, with the spell leaving the hands at half a second, and a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, so clicking or recasting during it does nothing and costs nothing. Casting speed scales with the Speed attribute, up to 25% faster at high Speed and 25% slower at low. Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Re-casting an effect already on the player or a target resets it to that cast's full duration instead of adding the new duration on top, and a shorter re-cast never cuts a longer one short. This covers buffs and debuffs alike, including Shield, Fortify, Regenerate, Levitate, Light, Detect, the resistances, Paralyze, Silence, Continuous Damage and Soul Trap, and re-casting Regenerate or Continuous Damage on its last round still gives the full ticks. Classic spellcasting runs at the pace of the Morrowind animation, a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, casting speed scales with the Speed attribute (up to 25% faster at high Speed, 25% slower at low), and Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Online, a Destruction mastered past 100 adds to Damage Health and Continuous Damage spells, 1% for each point of its effective value past 100 and +40% at 200, while gear that raises Destruction still makes spells cheaper. Spells cast at the ground outdoors now strike it: a Fireball aimed at the grass bursts there, a bolt fired into a hillside stops on the hill, enemies' spells do the same, and a Fireball bursting at the player's own feet outdoors catches them in the blast, as it always has in dungeons. A spell's sparks and shockwave land on the ground where it struck, slopes included, and Resurrect finds a fallen party member when aimed at the ground beside them outdoors. An enemy caster behind a ridge moves for a clear shot instead of spending its spell on the hill. A party member's spell that carries the same effect twice (for example Shield twice) gives one effect, as casting it on oneself always has, even when the spell also cures.
Staff Panel ||| The Staff Panel is a tool for staff running the online game, not something players see. Its server side has cases, desk, players, realm, economy, census and status pages and a chat staff menu, and its relay half provides invisible follow, hidden staff, freeze, Message on screen and staff feeds (Network bursts, Monster watch and Bot check); version 0.2.3 added Set bank, a Banned tab, a Hide toggle, Me/Red chat and an in-game staff view. Following a player takes the camera into dungeons with them, shows the players around them as bodies and, from the chat menu, opens that player's card in the Staff Panel; monsters ignore hidden staff, and crowded player tags merge into one "N players here" chip. The staff pages answer in fewer database round trips.
Starfall ||| Every real hour during the Witches' Festival is a night, and each night one of the Bay's twelve covens pulls three stars down into the wilds of its region, each near a different town. A star burns in the sky for a minute and a half before it falls, and chat, the map, the compass and the Timers window point to it. Once it lands the player holds it through four waves of the coven and then their master, who calls a circle twice and takes only a quarter of every blow while any of it stands; an unheld star is lost 15 minutes after it falls. On the 13th of Frostfall the last star is the Heart-Star, with a larger circle and larger rewards. A held star gives Renown, 30 silver, gold, 1 to 3 pieces of Starfall Glass, a magic item or better and Soul Cakes, and one star in four also gives a piece of the Starspun Weave, Mephala's nine-piece set (always from the Heart-Star). Each wave is larger and grows as the fight goes on: alone 4, 5, 6 then 7 foes, two more for every player beside, up to 24 in the largest. The coven's waves and the master's circle have twice their usual health, while the master is unchanged. The coven's breath between waves is 5 seconds, waves gather just outside the crater, and in a group a quickly cleared wave ends when cleared. The mound around the crater is solid and cannot trap a player. Every fallen star's town is named 10 minutes before it falls, everywhere in the Bay, and fallen stars no longer land inside World of Daggerfall's rock formations, the ground around each star being cleared before it falls.
Timers ||| The Timers window, opened by an hourglass by the profile icon online, shows shared events with live countdowns in the player's own clock: Dagon's Breach (when the next opens, and when a standing one seals and collapses), town raids in the region, the next raid elsewhere and how many come today, the Sunday Turning at 18:00 UTC and the Friday 18:00 UTC Reckoning when pledges lock, this week's sieges, tourneys, revolts and any Royal Tourney, the Season's end, new bounty hunts and raids rolling, and the daily reset at 00:00 UTC. It also counts down the sea serpent's rising, storm and dive and shows the next rising. Events running now are listed first, and tapping outside, Escape or B closes it. The window is grouped into World events, The Battlespire, Town raids, Seats and battles, and Resets, each heading showing how many of its events are under way. A bar of buttons at the top shows one group at a time (or All), each with its count and a green mark when something in it is live, and the choice is kept until the game is closed. An event under way says Now above its countdown. The window keeps one height and only its list scrolls, buttons are thumb-sized on a phone, and Elite Breach rows wear the Breach's gold and Dagon's Breach rows its red.
Travel Map ||| The travel map is a parchment held in the player's hands, drawn as a held sprite with a Features switch, a Morrowind map on every sheet, readable glyphs and a fourth painting in the hands. A default trip is walked, the map was audited several times, and players riding another's boat are marked on the map where their boat is. The Overworld travel view no longer leaves 1 to 1.7 GB of extra memory behind after a journey, and it draws faster while a route is on screen.
Travel Options ||| Travel options set how journeys run. Fare scaling counts the player's Mercantile, so a good haggler pays less, a journey stops for a hostile ship including a crossing by sea, and changing the settings takes effect straight away. A Player Controlled Inns Travel dial, on by default, makes any trip stopping at inns a journey, and online every trip over land on the travel map is travelled, not instant, with its options always on. Two speed dials returned in 0.1.6646 after being removed in 0.1.6444: one for the journey's speed and one for the speed while enemies are near, each going x5, x10, x20, x30, x40 and x60, with x100 on a road only. Leaving a road drops a dial at x100 to x60 at once and it does not climb back by itself, journeys start at x60, and the dials are in every interface (the Overworld's panel, the Enhanced journey bar, and a small speed box with the classic interface and GrimoireUI). Resting and Travel Options are no longer turned back by a quest's monsters still waiting to arrive in a town.
Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map. Wild Zone halls no longer vanish partway through their day after someone has been inside one. Dying in the Wrothgarian Mountains while the wagon is parked outside the zone, or sent to the stable, loses nothing from it, while a wagon driven, led or parked inside the zone still loses its cargo. A worn item looted from a player in the open PvP zone reaches the looter even if they share a guild or party, staying held until collected and going back to its owner if nobody does, and members of the same guild can no longer attack each other there, as with a party. Players can fight each other in the zone's dungeons again, as intended, and their blood shows there; every attack on another player underground had been refused. Since 0.1.11085 only another player's kill drops what the player carries, not any death: dying to a monster, a ship, a fall or the sea drops nothing, a player's kill drops a tenth of the purse instead of half, and after dying to a foe the player rises in the nearest town inside the zone, in the same ring. The zone's entry message, chip tip, map legend and tutorial page (now covering both zones) say so.
