Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, the app has its own icon, and it finds and installs updates again. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. Downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app (to be dragged to the Bin once open), and on Windows pinned taskbar or Start menu entries may need repinning under the new name. It keeps the account and settings files in memory instead of reading them from disk every frame, as the browser does, giving smoother frames in a town.
Horse ||| The weapon over the horse was fixed. Horses never die, you can gallop on their breath, feed them to grow your bond, must keep them fed, and can own more than one horse. A horse with a new coat met on the road no longer freezes the game, since the coat is painted a few pictures at a time instead of all forty at once.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster.
Performance ||| A GPU memory leak from opening the map in a dungeon or building was fixed, long sessions and long journeys no longer accumulate video or GPU memory because left areas are released over time, settings are read faster everywhere, and people, guards, and foes do not pop out of view at screen edges. Opening the Overworld no longer freezes the game as nearby dungeon models load one per frame after the view rises, deep snow processing is disabled while the Overworld is open, and saving in snowy areas is faster because snow-track data is recompressed only when changed. Many town and frame costs were cut: still flats share large buffers and one index buffer, yards and stables behind the player or far away are not drawn, collision no longer grows with the view distance, lamp shadows redraw only the sides someone is in front of and reuse last frame's answer, night towns and harbours make less garbage, shaders compile in the background, the sky skips stars by day and two-graphics-card laptops use the faster card. Entering the world freezes for less, since working out where every town's pilgrims go on their holy days and picking the day's elite halls in the Wrothgarian zone no longer stalls the game, and a town with decorated yards in view makes about 17-18% fewer draw calls a frame.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before.
Wagon ||| Wagons stay attached without stretching, are drawn on the Overworld, and feature multiple vehicle types with storage, bench seating, harnesses, turning wheels, third-person camera support, and performance culling. Worn-out wagons never become unusable, broken-down save wagons are marked "Failing" so they can be driven again, and smiths/Repairs Objects enchantments cannot repair wagons or horses. A parked team's harness stops recomputing its ropes while nothing moves.
World Map ||| Every port the player has discovered shows an anchor at every zoom, including the whole-bay view, drawn darker with a light outline so it stands out on a busy coast. The Enhanced world map opens on the player as if Find me were pressed, gliding to where they stand with the red cross blinking, while the classic map is unchanged. Online, pointing at open ground shows a card listing the ores its veins can be, what its dungeons' deep veins hold, its kingdom's own ore and whether the ground is witnessed yet. The Find me button is back on the world and town maps. Opening the map for the first time in a session takes 0.7 s on a desktop instead of 6.7 s.
Yard ||| A yard is the ground around the player's house. Standing on their own lot and pressing Decorate places catalogue pieces outside, priced as inside, wholly on the lot, up to 60 pieces a yard, seen by everyone walking the street. A piece can stand at most 4 m above the ground, so stacking towers is no longer possible, though pieces already higher stay put and can be moved down or removed. Outside, pieces hold nothing and give no light and doors are for doorways only, a piece on the road is refused, and a long piece turned at an angle can no longer be placed through the house's corner. Other players' yard decorations no longer draw across the whole map and tiny yard pieces stop drawing once only a few pixels tall, with a Settings > Other players > Yard decorations option of Off, 75 m, 150 m or 300 m (the default), the player's own yard always showing. Yard pieces standing above the 4 m height limit were removed with no refund, and property lines are shared with neighbours, the ground between two houses split down the middle, so decorating in front of a neighbour's door is no longer possible. A shop, temple or tavern next to a house no longer claims half its yard, while the ground between two houses is still split down the middle. A player's yard takes far fewer draw calls, its 3D pieces drawn together and its repeated sprites drawn as one, helping most in the browser version and on weaker CPUs and drivers.
