Class Skill Swap ||| On the Growth tab, one class skill that is not mastered can be changed for a skill outside the class, in the same group, for 100,000 gold. Level and skill values stay the same, the page warns if the new skill could never be mastered, and an heir keeps the changed class.
Crafting Stations ||| Crafting stations use the Enhanced Plus look with diamond strikes for mini-games like the anvil's heat, loom's beat, and workbench's plane, and every station now uses materials in your Materials Bag and pack as well as your Stores. Stations show each pattern's XP and the 500 XP bonus for making it the first time, while pieces made only of cloth bought from the Weavers (Linen and Wool, including the Fishing Net) earn no first-time bonus, which the stations and craft messages say. The profession stations' short notes ("Needs 2 Arcane Essence; your Stores hold 1.", "Needs 2 Steel Ingot.", the Stores-full note) span the row under the piece instead of being squeezed into a narrow column. A station says when the crafter's limit stops a craft's XP: only two crafts rise past Journeyman (rank 50), and the station names them. Every station spells out its quality odds as percentages, saying they improve the further the player's rank is above the recipe's, and says what its minigame asks for the bonus: the anvil's strikes, the plane along the grain, every stitch on the beat, the chisel on the marked line, the facet that catches the light. A Tailor's clothing, a Leatherworker's leather and the town Apothecary's bonus are each explained in a sentence of their own.
Dagon's Breach ||| Dagon's Breach is a nightly event. From the 17:00 omen the Sigil Broker is held in a cage by Dagon's Faithful, and killing every one, Summoner included, before the 20:00 breach frees her for everyone, even if someone else already killed the Summoner. She can be talked to from any side of the cage and trades until midnight once freed. If the faithful still stand when the breach opens they pass into it and she stays caged. Some nights it carries a portent, always announced in the chat, on the map's and marks cards and in the Discord omen post, on about two breaches in five, never two in a row and never on a Legion-Lord night. The Vanguard puts fighters on the Bridgehead, a platform out in the fire, where three waves and the Markynaz must be broken before the Warden wakes. The Sundering splits Valkynaz Ruhn into Ruhn's Blade and Ruhn's Flame, each with its own health, who kneel when downed and get back up unless the other half falls within 20 seconds. The Captive chains a Covenant knight in the Pen, freed by cutting down 2 to 4 gaolers, and the Covenant's ward then absorbs the next Dagon's Reckoning. The Dire Breach is the Vanguard and the Sundering together. Ruhn has six new attacks (Chains of the Warden, Dagon's Brand, Shared Torment, Scything Wheel, the Burning Gaze and the Unmaking), each portent adds a piece of Magic or better to the spoils, and the Weir Key chance rises from one in six to one in four on a night whose portent pays. About one breach in five a Tithe-Imp bolts from the fire and pays a tithe to everyone who lands a blow on it if caught within 22 seconds.
Dual Wielding ||| Equipping a one-handed weapon (sword, dagger, mace or war axe) in each hand draws and swings both, with H choosing which hand leads and no switch delay with both hands up. A shield, two-handed weapon, bow, or torch or lantern held in a hand still means one weapon. A pair is quicker, not twice as strong: the next blow straight after a strike comes from the other hand a little sooner, the off hand does 80% damage with -5 to hit at Agility 50 (shrinking to nothing at higher Agility and rising to -10 at lower), a heavy off-hand weapon swings slower, and the off hand's blows count as 0.8 of a blow for on-strike enchantments, per-hit powers and skill training. Measured, a matched pair of light weapons deals about 9% more damage per second than one of them and a heavier pair about 4%, for up to a quarter more fatigue per second and giving up the shield. Each weapon in a pair shows its own element with weapon glow on, the other weapon lowers straight down when one attacks, and the same rules apply in duels, sieges and the open PvP zone. Under Physical Combat And Armor Overhaul (on by default) a weapon in the left hand no longer rolls a shield block, since dual wielding gives up the shield's block, while shields block as before. In the Morrowind first-person view the arms spread wider with two weapons drawn, further for a pair of longswords than a pair of daggers, easing out on draw and back on sheathe, so long blades no longer cross in the middle of the screen; third person is unchanged. Running with two weapons drawn is no longer stiff: the arms stay swept behind but pump with the stride and the elbows bend.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor. In the Gauntlet, killing a Caster silences its pillar. A lord no longer gets weaker when members of the company fall, and is sized for the members who actually reached its hall, a late arrival adding their part at the health the lord has left. On Enhanced Plus the breach's windows (the card offer, the run recap, the boards and the call tracker) use that style and a start window opens the breach, with 1-6 to choose cards, Y for Ready, R for a respite and Enter to close the recap, while the classic skin keeps the list window.
Features ||| Features is the screen where every enhanceable feature is switched. It is a panel of tiles with on and off shown as green and red, an All off button and a search. Gear Wear ||| Gear wear is how weapons and armour degrade. Wear per hit is at Daggerfall's own rate, only damage that gets through armour wears the weapon and the piece hit, good armour wears slower, and claws and bites no longer wear armour. Every weapon type now has the same base condition, the Warhammer's 1,600, with the material ladder unchanged, so daggers, tantos and short bows no longer break far sooner than a warhammer of the same metal; an iron short bow takes about 800 hits of 20 damage. Enchanted weapons gain the most: a Cast-When-Strikes enchantment costs 10 condition a strike, so an iron Worm's Tooth lasts 160 strikes, and a Cast-When-Held enchantment pays its casting cost the first time it is equipped without breaking the weapon. Weapons update on load keeping the same percentage of condition, repairs are priced by the share of condition lost so light weapons and bows cost less to keep up, and broken enchanted gear stays in the pack for a smith to repair. Legendary items no longer lose condition when equipped, where the Aegis of Dawn lost about a third the first time it was put on, while other magic items still pay their usual cost. A worn book that is found keeps its wear when it stacks with, or splits from, another copy.
Glyphs ||| Pressing a glyph on the account card hides it, and again shows it. Hiding only hides the icon, and anything the glyph lets the player do still works. On the enhanced skin the 2 and the Z are drawn so they can no longer be mistaken for each other.
Guild Stores ||| Each guild has its own Guild Stores on the Guild tab, and guild writ deliveries go there. What a member gathered and put in stays their own harvest and anything else taken out counts as bought, taking from their own deposit first, then the guild's, then other members'. Stores hold up to 50,000 of each material, list the last 20 movements, and a leaving member's deposit stays theirs to take back if they rejoin. The put in / take out form sits above the list, leaving Units empty moves all of an item, and a Put in all button empties the player's Stores into the guild's.

Jungle of Bantha ||| The Jungle of Bantha is dense jungle on Hammerfell's south-eastern shore, from Lainlyn and Satakalaam east to Mournoth: giant trees close overhead with taller ones breaking through, young trees and palms fill the light gaps, and the floor is carpeted in ferns, broad leaves and grass. A humid haze hangs under the canopy, thickest at dawn and in rain, and it thins when climbing out of it. It is on by default, switched under "The world" in the Features menu, and always on online.
King's Gambit ||| The King's Gambit is a battle of armies and kings played at any tavern's card table, started with Play The King's Gambit on the Hold'em or Iliac Hand panel. The player chooses a regular to challenge (tiers from Amateur to Grandmaster, each with an army size, potions, blunder rate and minimum stake), puts down a stake of 1, 2 or 5 times that minimum stake and wins back twice it, picks one of the Iliac Bay's four kings and builds an army of up to 12 units from 300 points. On a board that stands on the table each turn has two actions (move a piece, strike or give it a potion) while fire, spikes and poison spread, and felling the other king wins. It plays with mouse, touch, keyboard or gamepad, forfeiting with gold down asks twice, and a guide explains every rule in thirteen languages. Online, two players sit at the board and press Ready, the table lays the board, rolls the dice and keeps a 60-second turn clock, others can watch, and a realm character can play for gold at the red table (10, 50, 200, 1,000 or 5,000 gold, the winner taking the pot). Counted online games feed a season board, whose first player after ten counted games against five different opponents wears the Grandmaster title, and the game can be turned off in Settings on the World tab. The board has stone walls, columns, rippling water, pieces as cards in stands that walk, lunge, shake when hit and topple into a pile, and its own sounds.

Layered Gearing ||| Layered Gearing draws a character on foot in the helmet, chest armor and leg armor it actually wears (leather, chain or plate in iron, steel, mithril, adamantium, ebony, orcish or daedric), each character using its own body. Leveling ||| Leveling is chosen at character creation on the "How will you grow?" screen, in the Enhanced Plus look. Online characters level the Daggerfall way, with Oblivion Remastered leveling shown locked as "Offline characters only", and online characters that were on Oblivion leveling switch to Daggerfall leveling the next time they are played, keeping their level, skills and any earned level-up. Offline the player can still pick either system. Online, maximum health is never below what an average roll at every level would have given with the current Endurance, and raising Endurance later counts for every level already gained, with nobody losing health. Every level gives exactly 5 attribute points instead of 4 to 6, and levelling up online no longer changes current health.
Loot Drops ||| Loot drops are what enemies leave. In the latest changes regular enemies keep 3 in 4 of their items offline, up from half, and online 5/8 to 7/8 by Renown. They drop blue, yellow and orange items more often, stepping up at Renown 10, 20, 30 and 40 and using the rolling player's Renown (the host's in a shared dungeon), and gear an enemy was wearing can drop as Magic, Rare or better, even from champions, elites and bosses. A full body keeps a potion or rest supply ahead of plain gear. Earlier, normal enemies were capped at 3 items, gold included (5 in an Elite Dungeon), champions stopped always dropping a Rare, and ordinary foes left each item only half the time with gold untouched. Luck multiplies drop chances, half at Luck 0 and half again at Luck 100, instead of adding a flat amount. Animated loot piles stop being animated behind the scenes after the player leaves a room, loads a save or empties a pile, and magic items from guild vendors, shops and loot no longer come on wands or holy items when their power only works while worn. Below level 20, an orc's or a daedra's metal drop is often a mid-tier metal (Elven at low levels, rising through Dwarven, Mithril and Adamantium to Ebony), so Orcish and Daedric gear drops less for brand-new characters, and in an online room that armour drop is the same for everyone.
Master Skills ||| Master Skills raises the skill hard cap from 100 to 200, with everything up to 100 working as before. Each point from 100 to 125 is about 4 times slower, 125 to 150 about 8 times slower and 150 to 200 about 16 times slower, and each point above 100 gives 25% of the benefit of a point below 100, with milestones at 125, 150, 175 and 200. A skill at 200 fights like a 140. Every skill can reach 100, but only mastered skills pass it: 2 of 3 primary, 2 of 3 major and 1 of 6 minor, chosen at 100 and permanent. It is always on online and a choice offline. Online, every mastered school past 100 strengthens its own spells and not just their price, by 1% per effective point up to +40% at 200, the same rule as Destruction's damage: Restoration makes heals heal more, Alteration makes the Shield hold more, Illusion makes invisibility, chameleon and shadow last longer, Thaumaturgy makes Spell Reflection and Spell Resistance hold more often, and Mysticism makes Silence, Dispel and Soul Trap take hold more often. Mastering two schools also allows choosing an Arcane Specialisation. Online, a mastered weapon skill past 100 pays in its own way: Long Blade and Archery make every blow land up to 40% harder at 200, Axe up to 80% harder against wounded foes (under half health), Blunt Weapon up to 80% harder against fresh foes (half health or more), each of those two landing up to 40% harder on every blow against the Oblivion Gate's court, the Elite Gates, the Remnant and the sea serpent, and Short Blade swings up to 40% faster, while Hand-to-Hand's damage already grew with the skill. Every short blade swings like a dagger, where the Tanto, Shortsword and Wakizashi had swung at long-blade speed. Mastering two weapons also allows a martial specialisation. A class with only one weapon skill (or school of magic) can reach a specialisation, and a class skill that is not mastered can be swapped on the Growth tab (see Class Skill Swap).
Moderation ||| Moderation gives moderators a shield, /mute and /unmute, and tabbyvish and Starempire42 are moderators with the blue shield. /siege void lets a Moderator void a seat's battle of the week won by an exploit. A void can no longer land after the Turning. The Shadow Fang title carries moderator powers (/mute, /unmute, /ban, /unban, /strikes and the reports' queue), and moderators cannot ban or mute a Shadow Fang holder. The relay counts the impossible monsters it drops itself, and an account that asks for far more spawns than play allows opens a moderation case on its own, one batch of findings being one line on the case.
Mods ||| The Mods pane was replaced by a unified system with every mod enabled, creator titles, one search across every tab, and a Mods & files section listing every mod with direct access to options. Mod settings say in a sentence or two what each setting does, settings that do nothing in this game say "Has no effect", and the About pane's credits say what each mod and library does while still naming every author, version and license.
Morrowind Body ||| The Morrowind body features climbing poses (hanging, shimmying, wall climbing, leaps, pull-overs, vault tucks), gripping animations, first-person hand reach, crowd optimization, port-made steel plate armor sets, synchronized attack speeds, spell-casting animations, and T-pose fixes. Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole. Travelling fast no longer spends time building ground already passed, and the land ahead loads up to three times faster, so holes in the view and drops to 1x speed are much rarer, including on straight roads. While ground is still loading the view may run at about 30 fps instead of 60 until the land is built. Quest destinations show as gold diamonds, the followed quest filled in and pinned to the screen edge with its distance and time left, and clicking a mark starts a journey there. Dungeons are slightly smaller and stay the same size when zooming. While travelling it forecasts the weather ahead and when it will be reached, and standing still it shows the next few hours' weather, with a forecast on hover for any place. Travel inside a town slows to x5 and follows the streets, and the journey stops with a reason if the city gates are shut for the night. A journey no longer drops to x1 while the land ahead is loading, or because of an enemy camp that has not noticed the player.
Passage Under Sail ||| Friendly merchant and navy ships moored at a port's quays can carry the player: look at her hull and press Activate, and her captain lists every port the player knows, nearest first, with the direction it lies and the fare. Quest Journal ||| The quest journal's Quests tab opens on the quest the card is showing. Questlines ||| Thirteen questlines run alongside the quests taken at guild halls and taverns, each opening with a letter or a courier when the player is ready: the Dagon Cycle, the Hour That Was Not, the Old Coil and eight introductions to the game's newer pursuits. Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. Every quest shows its objectives one at a time, with the journal and the quest tracker naming the step and a herald line telling when one is finished. Many quests ask the player to choose how they end, each ending paying and being remembered in its own way, and endings with guilds, temples and houses move the player's standing with them (online, a quest that ends unfinished still costs none). Quests that pay gear let the player choose one of three pieces made for the class, better the harder the quest.
Quick Slots ||| The hotbar is now on by default, ten slots above the health bars on keys 1 to 0. Features, Interface, Quick slots can be set to Diamond for the diamond, and if Off they stay off. With the diamond style, status-effect icons beside it no longer sit on a dark box, and an empty spell quick-slot no longer leaves a gap that pushed the quick block up. The styles are Off, Diamond (slots 1-4), Hotbar (the default, ten squares) and Diamonds (ten diamond-shaped slots), and with the pack or spellbook open, pointing at an item or spell and pressing a slot key puts it on that slot with every bar style.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy. The player's own Disintegrate no longer deletes a revenant: it still breaks through the last stand and will but brings the revenant to its knees like any killing blow, to Kill (its trophy weapon drops) or Spare (it joins as a companion). A revenant marked at its lair is found there, standing where it can be walked to from the entrance, and one the road had stood goes home to its lair when visited, the road body it left never coming back as the revenant.
Roaming Bands ||| The chance of a roaming band was raised from 0.45 to 0.75 by day and from 0.6 to 0.9 at night, so the nearest one is about a quarter closer. Other players being chased by bands can be seen from as far away as the bands themselves are drawn.
Running Hands ||| Running with nothing in either hand shows the arms pumping in step with the stride (on the head bob's own rhythm) using Daggerfall's own fist-and-forearm frames, the arm always running off the bottom of the screen. The arms keep running through a short hop, stay smooth when the player stops and starts, ease away when a spell is readied and work with every mod switched off, and a punch thrown mid-run shows at once. The running fists stay low, rising about a third of the way up the screen, and show their back and knuckles like fists at rest. Running with empty hands or fists keeps each hand on its own side instead of crossing in front of the chest, and in first person the running fists come up in front of each shoulder.
Salvage ||| A sunk ship leaves her wreckage afloat beside her casks, larger and paler than a cask. Sailing through wreckage hauls it aboard for Carpenter's Stores (2 to 6) and powder for fire barrels, letting the player repair to full and refill their fire barrels without making port. If swimming, the stores go into the pack one at a time and what is too heavy is lost, a fast travel or a door first has the crew stow wreckage from ships the player sank, and online everyone in the room sees the wreckage and whoever reaches it first hauls it in. A button on the Salvage page salvages every Magic item at once, asking first. Classic Daggerfall magic items (guild-shop and Item Maker pieces, like a "Blazing Holy Dagger of Fireballs") appear on the Salvage page marked "Classic magic" and explain why they give no shards, instead of silently missing. The enhanced inventory's Batch salvage button ticks pieces one by one or selects All Magic, All Rare or All junk, and a sweep never picks a Legendary piece. Pieces that cannot be salvaged (worn, locked, set with gems) are dimmed and say why, and one question lists the pieces and the shards to be gained, with Keep cancelling and leaving the selection in place.
Shared Foes ||| A monster handed over by another player's game is checked against what the game itself can build: one with a health or level no real spawn could have is removed from the world instead of fighting the player, and is never rescaled. The player is never blamed for a monster someone else's game handed over.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away. Winning a raid at sea ends it, removing the raid's crew and any late wave so travel, resting and the Overworld stop saying enemies are near, and an enemy ship that leaves the sea mid-fight takes its raid's crew with it. The pirate attack's victory gong rings again.
Shops ||| Shops operate on night shifts with attribute caps for online pricing, and player-transferred items from chests, bodies, or shops undergo server validity checks. Shops pay for a crafted Superior or Masterwork piece what they pay for the plain piece its recipe makes, since the magic or rare lines are power and not resale value, and patrons follow the same rule while found magic and rare items are priced as before. The keyed shelf prices a sale as the trade window does and no longer buys conjured pieces. General Stores and Pawn Shops no longer sell or buy ship deeds and parts, which are traded at shipyards, and shops do not buy the exchange's goods. Weapon Smiths always keep 50-100 arrows on the counter, Armorers and General Stores 14-29 and Pawn Shops 7-14. Every shelf item shows its price before it is clicked and a List / Pictures switch shows both sides as item tiles, the player's side is labelled "Your pack", the footer shows "To pay" (or "You get") and "Your gold" separately, a magic-only counter is titled "Sell magic items", Steal sits apart from the other buttons, and a button that cannot be used yet explains why when pressed. A shop never pays more for an item than was paid a shop for it, so carrying goods between regions makes no gold, while found loot sells as before and improving a bought item (setting a gem, honing, reforging) is paid for on selling. On a seat's Market Day and for a holder's members shops pay less for what is sold as well as asking less, essentials cannot be bought and sold back for profit, and a friend's caravan gives its friend's price both ways. Materials bought at a shop stay in the pack instead of the Stores, and selling a very large batch of gear no longer pays below zero but pays its real price as a letter of credit. Online, a looted weapon or piece of armour mended by its owner sells for the condition it was found in plus a third of what their own repair kits, Repairs Objects and the Varla Stone mended past that, while a smith's repair does not raise the price and wear after finding still lowers it. Orcish and Daedric pieces dropped by orcs and daedra, and bounty rewards, sell for the condition found even after a repair, an enchanted found piece sells at the Mages Guild for its found condition, and tempering a found piece no longer lowers its price.
Skill Training ||| Past 100, a skill only learns from enemies tough for that skill, and tougher enemies teach more. Movement skills past 100 count only real movement: running counts the ground actually covered, swimming and diving count and treading water does not, climbing counts only up or down the wall, a jump that carries the player somewhere counts, and being carried (such as on someone's boat) does not. Running past 100 now counts every step. A spell landing on a foe, or a foe's spell landing on the player, counts the foe being fought when training a mastered school past 100, so blasting a tough foe trains the school as fast as fighting it with a weapon, and a cast never trains slower than before. The Stats page shows how many uses each skill has toward its next point, says when it is ready (and, inside the 6 hours between skill checks, how long until a rest or journey raises it) and says why when it is held (at 100 unmastered, or the 95 lock). A "Skills can be improved" notice stays on screen while a rest or a journey would raise a skill.
Sky ||| The sun, moons, stars, seasons and calendar all run on the faster sky, which switched once at a moment when both schedules showed the same hour. The sky's square frame was removed. A hard, smooth arc that could cut across the sky at dusk is gone.
Snowfall ||| In winter, outside the deserts, real snow lies on the ground, deeper in the wild than in towns, building up while snow falls and melting back in clear weather. The player, monsters, guards and townsfolk leave tracks in it, which slowly fill back in (faster while it snows) and are kept in the save, and a fallen enemy leaves a hollow where it lay. With Basic Roads, paths and tracks keep only a thin layer of snow and main roads get a bank along their edges, and the winter ground under it has new textures. It has its own switch in Features, on by default in the enhanced outdoors. Foes' ground telegraphs and the player's technique marks always draw on top of deep snow, and deep snow can be turned off at Settings > Graphics > Sky, water & weather > Snowfall. Snow settles where it falls and melts as the air warms, so in a thaw white mountains stand over green valleys.
Translation Packs ||| Under the Language row, Translation pack installs a Daggerfall Unity translation (a folder or its .zip) from the player's own files, uploading nothing. A pack translates Daggerfall's own text too, dialogue, quests, books and the names of places, enemies, items and spells, and works for a language not yet in the list; the French pack's grammar works. Every language's translation has been updated to the game's new plain wording.
Travel Pathing ||| Travel pathing was reworked in 0.1.4216. Planned routes stay on the road from start to finish, including around corners, through towns on diagonal roads and up to a spot clicked near a road.
Wallet ||| Every character carries a Wallet, which new characters start with and existing ones received on next load. It holds letters of credit, Deadlands Embers and Welkynd Shards and shows gold and, online, silver. Its sheet is a ledger: Coin (gold, silver) and In the wallet (letters of credit, Deadlands Embers, Welkynd Shards), with each amount lined up on the right. In the Enhanced inventory the three move to the Wallet on Valuables, in the classic inventory nothing moves and the Wallet is on the Clothing and Misc page, and pressing it on the hotbar shows what it holds. It weighs nothing, never leaves the pack and cannot be dropped, sold, traded or stolen by revenants. The wallet and the Deadlands Embers and Welkynd Shards it holds always stay with the player, including when they die in the Wrothgarian zone. The wallet's row and card show what its contents weigh, and each holding in it shows its own weight; no weight itself changed.
Weather ||| Weather evolves within the day with cloud types by place, a weather field where the day's words are places walked into and out of, sandstorms, and no rain over snow. A world weather map (WEATHER3) was added then removed. Rainstorms now drizzle at their edges and pour only at their heart, snow falls lightly more often, light rain looks light with thinner fog and more daylight, and rain no longer vanishes in a puddle, hiding only when the eyes are underwater. Rain and snow fall in front of grass and banners, and rain keeps falling while travelling on the Overworld map. It rains about half as often, a woodland spring that used to be wet a third of the time now being wet about one hour in six, with the dry hours the region's usual sun, cloud, grey deck and fog. Rain comes in every strength, about a third of fronts being a light sprinkle from edge to middle, most a steady shower and only about a quarter building to a downpour at their heart, while thunderstorms are still downpours but come half as often. The Classic skin's weather and the classic weather with "Weather as places" off are unchanged. What falls depends on the air where the player stands, its climate, the season, the hour and the height, so cold peaks get snow, warm lowlands rain and sleet falls in between, one storm able to rain in a valley and snow on the ridge above. The jungle rains the year round and the Alik'r Desert almost never sees a flake. This applies to the enhanced look only; the classic look keeps Daggerfall's own weather.
