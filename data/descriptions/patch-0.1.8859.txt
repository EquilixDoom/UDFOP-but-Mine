Arrows ||| Arrows are crafted ammunition. A second recipe uses 1 Pine Plank, 1 Iron Ingot and 1 Harpy Feathers to make twenty arrows. A double-arrow fault was fixed. A shot that misses stays in the wall, floor, tree or ground it hit and can be taken back like any pile (up to 40 stay in one place, the oldest going first, gone once the player leaves the area), while conjured arrows still vanish and arena arrows are refunded by the healers. An arrow that hits an enemy survives half the time and can be looted from the body, and arrows no longer count toward a normal enemy's three-item drop limit. Twigs, a Pine Branch or a Wood Bundle used from the inventory fletch 2, 4 or 10 arrows, and Weapon Smiths always keep 50-100 arrows on the counter. Using a twig makes 2 arrows again, as intended, so the workbench's 20 arrows from 4 twigs are worth their plank and ingot.
Beautiful Villages ||| Beautiful Villages fixes floating scenery so trees stand on the ground drawn beneath them, houses and the stone gazebo no longer float as hills are drawn at their real size and shape, and a house in Akatosh's temple villages buried inside a hill now stands on open ground nearby where quests lead to it. Temples in Beautiful Villages towns, including the School of Julianos in Makehda and the temples of Kynareth, no longer sit behind a wall of stone, and the Akatosh Chantry's steps in Merdale are no longer buried under grass banks. Boulders in Beautiful Villages and Beautiful Cities towns show at their real size and market stalls stand where the town's author placed them.
Boarding ||| Boarding is how a ship is captured. After battering a ship until she strikes her colours, the player comes alongside, slows right down (heaving to beside her now stops the player within boarding reach, even at storm speeds) and presses Activate to throw grapples, which puts out her fires. Her crew and captain fight on her deck, and killing the captain and most of the crew takes her. The player's own crew comes along, and the player lands across from where they stood. Pirates can board the player too, with only one pirate raid at a time, and they fall back and cast off if beaten or timed out, then wait five minutes before grappling again. Beating off a pirate boarding now pays, the pirate being beaten then, so taking or sinking her afterwards is logged but not paid again. A ship's crew no longer rushes the rail shouting at nobody while the player stands at the helm, mustering only when a boarding can actually happen.
Boulders ||| Boulders can be quarried for stone. They glow and show on the compass, and the player presses E at "Quarry the stone". Each square holds a number of boulders a day: 1 to 3 in woodland, haunted woodland and subtropical squares, 2 to 4 in mountain woods, and 3 to 5 in mountains and deserts. A rock field's boulders now all appear. A boulder's glint sits on its stones, not in the air above them.
Calm HUD ||| Calm HUD, a Features option on by default, fades the health, fatigue and magicka bars out while all three are full and no enemy is targeted, and brings them back the moment a point is lost or spent.
Character Creation ||| Character creation is the screen where a new character is made. The creator tells the player what they rolled, a phone version exists, and a character made online no longer gets a skill check the moment it is created. The Project Legacy question (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage) and the leveling question are Enhanced Plus popups in which the option under the pointer is the one highlighted and clicking it selects it. Additionally, heads and hairs are now paired freely, from every pairing of the heads and hairs your Morrowind data offers a race at character creation, whereas there were previously ten. Classes are grouped as Magic, Stealth and Combat and each shows the two attributes it relies on. The class card shows the class's attributes, skills and health per level before its description, with health reading as the real range (for example "3-6 a level, more with Endurance"). On phones "Begin your life" and "Play as..." stay on screen, the province names on the map no longer overlap, the drums play only when a class is chosen, Start over sits beside Back, and buttons say "Play as an Acrobat". Small text is at least 12px.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster. The chat has a search bar above the player list where part of a name, or # and a tag, shows every tab's lines from or about that player, and /search <name> searches from the chat field while /search alone clears it. Chat tabs scroll sideways with the mouse wheel, the edge with more tabs fading and a clicked tab scrolling fully into view. Titles no longer appear in chat, while guild tags and glyphs still show after names and the title still shows over the head and on the Inspect card. The chat roster search finds every known player, not just the first page. /rp <text> says a line of roleplay on the current tab in green italics, and /help lists /trade, /lfg, /join, /leave and /rp. Searching for a player in the chat finds anyone online and not just the people listed on screen, since the search now asks the server too and players on other parts of the server show up a moment after typing.
Climates & Calories ||| Climates and Calories is a survival mod with Off, Boons, Casual and Hard settings, Boons being the default. Hunger and thirst have staged need tiles (such as 2/3 for Hungry), rations in the pack are eaten automatically when Hungry, and a tavern drink quenches thirst fully. Inns and taverns refill waterskins at the price of the cheapest soft drink and each region serves its own menu. Indoors the temperature is never worse than the street, in hot weather clothes count for half their warmth, and coming out of Scorching or Freezing tells the player. Corpse food works online. Felt temperature follows the original mod more closely: Resist Fire no longer makes a blizzard colder nor Resist Frost the desert hotter, swimming and rain and snow soak at the mod's rates (a cloak keeps snow off and a hood rain), robes and cloaks keep the sun off armour, underground the dungeon's air rules clothes, armour and food, and the open sea is cold in winter. Offline a long rest or fast-travel journey feels each hour as it passes, and in Hard a long journey can no longer collapse the player over and over or kill them on landing, collapsing counts as an hour's sleep on the ground, and saving mid-journey is refused. Leaving prison the player starts fed, watered and rested, a cured vampire is not starving, a werewolf in beast form neither hungers nor thirsts, and food that spoils shows its spoiled name. New characters start with the mod's Formal cloak and Shoes, both well worn, tavern menus follow its regional menus, prices and holiday free drinks, a meal ordered when nearly full is served whole, a soft drink gives +5 stamina and an alcoholic drink restores stamina and magicka, and online passing out drunk wakes the player at the next dawn. The player only gets wet when rain or snow is actually falling on them, and a light shower soaks slowly.
Courtship and Marriage ||| Courtship and marriage belong to Project Legacy. A townsperson who counts the player a friend can be courted by asking about Courtship in conversation, once a day, depending on Personality, Etiquette and tone, and when their heart is full the player can ask for their hand and is wed at the temple of their town. The spouse joins the house, keeping their own face and day, and every thirty days there is a one-in-four chance of a child, at most six with each spouse, who cannot be played until the mantle passes to them. If someone being courted dies the courtship ends. Online, two players can wed by standing in a temple together and pressing Propose marriage on the other, each keeping their own house, and word of a spouse's death or deletion reaches the player the next time they are online. Courtship counts a tone as it was taken: a tone the person likes helps the day's courtship and one they dislike costs a little affection (never below nothing), and they say so.
Crafting Stations ||| Crafting stations use the Enhanced Plus look with diamond strikes for mini-games like the anvil's heat, loom's beat, and workbench's plane, and every station now uses materials in your Materials Bag and pack as well as your Stores. Stations show each pattern's XP and the 500 XP bonus for making it the first time, while pieces made only of cloth bought from the Weavers (Linen and Wool, including the Fishing Net) earn no first-time bonus, which the stations and craft messages say.
DFO Grimoire ||| DFO Grimoire is a fourth look under Main menu > Overhauls > UI, dressing everything Enhanced Plus does in GrimoireUI's parchment, wood, red-and-gold presses, glass vials and lettering. The vitals are three vials, upright in the corner in Daggerfall's order or lying in a row under the hotbar, and the card offers Pages, Backdrop, Vitals, and Spellbook choices. The front door stands on a detailed desk, rarity/set/sigil names are inked deeper, parchment text is readable, stray ruled lines are removed, health bars scale with resolution, and settings open as scrolls. New features include a Text font setting under Overhauls > UI with five choices (Game, Sans, Monospace, Dyslexic, My font), distinct channel colours with colour squares and toggle options, player name colours, language channels in 15 languages with `/lang` and `/lc` commands, gold-highlighted `@mentions` with chimes, unread count seals on Grimoire tabs, and various UI/inventory/scry fixes. Its Tab dial uses Daggerfall's own icons, the character's face for Skills, the HUD star for Magic, the sack for Inventory and the scroll for Map, and the old paintings return under Settings > Overhauls > DFO Grimoire > Tab dial. Quick loot rarity marks show in it as well as in Enhanced Plus. Under the DFO Grimoire dress every mark on the card tables shows in the dress's own colours, a chosen, hovered or keyboard-selected tile is marked on every page that uses tiles, and buttons that cost something (Concede, Scuttle, Steal and the like) change when hovered or reached with the keyboard. In the main menu the two pages appear only where the screen is tall enough, otherwise one page is shown, dotted lines are no longer drawn, the pause window's tabs wrap in long languages, and on phones the side menu (including "Main menu") is readable.
Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, the app has its own icon, and it finds and installs updates again. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. Downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app (to be dragged to the Bin once open), and on Windows pinned taskbar or Start menu entries may need repinning under the new name. It keeps the account and settings files in memory instead of reading them from disk every frame, as the browser does, giving smoother frames in a town. The app updates the game one file at a time instead of downloading about 130 MB and replacing the whole game folder, the full installer running only when the app itself changes or a file-by-file update fails, on Windows, macOS, Linux and the portable build.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top.
Face Portraits ||| With Morrowind data attached, every face portrait shows the 3D head the character wears: save tiles, profile and account cards, character creation, the large HUD, the tab dial and the party panel. NPC faces in the talk window, the family and succession pages and the escort faces show the head their body wears, portraits show the helmet or hood being worn, the vampire head for a vampire and the werewolf head when transformed, and portraits face front. Catching vampirism changes the body's face right away and a cure changes it back, with no reload.
Firmament ||| The Firmament is a passive tree for everything done online, opened from the Firmament button on the Stats page. The player chooses one of seven birthsigns, then spends Stars on combat stars and Craft points on the life ring (gathering, crafting, trading, hunting and sailing), earning 2 Stars for every Renown level and 1 Craft point for every 8 profession ranks. Changes are free to plan until confirmed, taking stars back is free below Renown 10 and then costs 10 silver per star, and a full reset costs 10,000 gold. The stars never affect hits or spells against other players and switch off in duels and while mentoring. Every star adds something: small stars and roads give their bonus one for one as a percent, and an attribute bonus counts on a stat already at 100 (with the sky's full +15 a 100 Strength reads 115, offline the cap is 100). It works in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire and on phones, and its text is English for now.
Gilded Weapons ||| The Dragonfire Oath is a Daedric Dai-Katana that can drop from the Warden of the Burning Gate about one fight in a hundred, giving +40% damage, +15 Strength, +30 Long Blade and +15 Endurance; its Dragonfire makes a blow that lands on a Daedra burn it again for another third of the hit and burn every other Daedra within 3 m for the same. The Sunken Sovereign is an Ebony Saber that can drop from the Old Coil about one kill in a hundred for a ship that did damage, giving +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Long Blade and 10% life leech; its Undertow adds +10% damage to each blow on the same foe up to +50%, fading after 6 seconds without a hit, and heals the player for 10% when it kills. Both are shown in the Collections codex with their own hints.
Guild Hall ||| A guild hall is a guild-owned house bought by the guildmaster with treasury funds, featuring member chests, crafting, resting, customizable painting and yard furnishings, and a hall's roll as the first book on its shelf detailing its chapter's standing, Master, and officers. Windows inside buildings are now see-through, town boards fill in once three players have visited guild halls, and in rooms with many lamps, stationary people cast shadows from every lamp rather than just the nearest twelve. Online, if the player does not yet qualify for the next rank, the guild popup lists what is missing: the rank's name ("Next rank: Swordsman"), reputation against what is needed ("Reputation 14 of 20"), the guild-skill level the player is short of with their best guild skill named, a second guild skill at the lower bar and the guild's own skills by name. It does not appear when nothing is missing or at the top rank, and it shows on the enhanced UI skin and in all 13 supported languages.
Guilds ||| Guilds are player organisations founded on Renown 10 with restrictions on disbanding and standing reviews, functioning properly across regions, and online you can set entry permissions like an online home (Only me, My party, My guild, or Anyone). Townsfolk, shopkeepers, guild members, nobles and priests are dressed from a wardrobe of their own in Morrowind garments and colours, while a fighters' guild's people wear whatever armour came to hand. Online, a player is promoted the moment they qualify for the next rank by talking to a guild-service NPC at the hall, with no 28-day wait for a promotion, while demotion, probation and expulsion still wait 28 days and offline play is unchanged. Reputation earned before joining still counts, but a new member rises no faster than the guild records standing, about a rank and a half a day from the day they join, and the guild hall tells when the next promotion can come at the earliest.
HUD ||| The HUD's lost-part strip on the fatigue, health and magicka bars is now a dim strip and stays at most about a second before catching up. Notifications that have been moved now fade in where the player put them. The compass can be turned over in the HUD editor (Alt+U), whose length handle follows the pointer and which no longer pushes a resized piece off the screen. The riding panel and the ship's helm panel are pieces in the HUD editor (Alt+U) that can be moved, resized or hidden.
Horse ||| Horses never die, can be fed to grow the player's bond, must be kept fed, and the player can own more than one. A horse walks by default and holding Run (Shift unless rebound) gallops it, as on foot. The gallop is as fast as before and spends the horse's breath, and a winded or starving horse walks even with Run held until it can gallop again. Holding Run gallops it anywhere, in town or out, as long as it has the breath, online too, though riding into someone at a gallop tramples them, which is a crime and brings the guards. A walking horse has a slower clop, a slower head nod in first person and a trot on the 3D horse, and the saddle panel says Walking. With Horse care off in Settings > Features, or on the classic interface, horses ride as they always did. A horse with a new coat met on the road no longer freezes the game. The horses' coats on the road's wagons are painted in under half the time, so the first seconds after entering the world stutter less. With Billboard Look on, Morrowind horses stand upright like the classic sprites. The saddle panel is a small block in the bottom-right corner, shown or hidden with F2 (rebindable in Controls), and the Horse care setting chooses When needed, Always or Off; on touch screens it no longer covers the compass. Horse care and wagon upkeep each have Off, Boons (the default) and Hard tiers like Climates & Calories: on Boons nothing is taken (a hungry horse is no weaker, a worn wagon no slower, and a wagon kept Sound rolls 10% faster), Hard is the system as it was, and Off is the classic horse or wagon.
Iliac Hand ||| The Iliac Hand is a two-player collectible card game played on a tavern's card table: three holdings between the players, six turns, hold two of three to win. It has units, spells and Daedric Prince cards, painted in code with the Bay's creatures, artifacts and places. Every character carries a Card Binder from creation that holds their decks, and a Collections tab under Holdings shows the weapon codex and cards side by side. Its panel uses the Enhanced Plus stone-and-brass look and the game's pixel font, has a fold button ("Collapse the panel" / "Expand the panel") that tucks the table's words away, and fits any phone screen, scrolling inside it when the table is full. The turn is a track of six marks and the magicka a row of gems with staged spending dimmed, the opponent's hand is card backs beside their deck count, and each holding sits under its own painted location card with each side's power beside its name and the leading side lit. The player's hand sits in a tray with the picked card raised in brass, a game ends with a banner ("Victory", "Defeat", "A draw" or "No contest"), the log keeps one line for each event, online the board appears once the game is dealt, and playing by keyboard keeps the focus on the panel so Space does not make the character jump up from the table. The Grizzly Bear, the Sabretooth Tiger, the Spider, the Slaughterfish, the Mummy, the Frost Daedra, the Fire Daedra, the Archer and the Ranger joined the set with their own painted pictures, dropped by their creatures, so every creature in the game now has a card, and every card's rules and flavor text read in all thirteen fully translated languages. It is also the army art for The King's Gambit, each unit wearing its card's picture.
Invisibility ||| Invisibility works online. Party members can interact with each other while under Chameleon, Shadow or Invisibility. Concealed players outside the party stay hidden, as before. Monsters that cannot see through Invisibility no longer swing at the player just because they walked in front of them in dungeons, while the undead, vampires and Daedra still see through it, and wilderness enemies no longer notice an invisible traveller during fast travel.
King's Gambit ||| The King's Gambit is a battle of armies and kings played at any tavern's card table, started with Play The King's Gambit on the Hold'em or Iliac Hand panel. The player chooses a regular to challenge (tiers from Amateur to Grandmaster, each with an army size, potions, blunder rate and minimum stake), puts down a stake of 1, 2 or 5 times that least stake and wins back twice it, picks one of the Iliac Bay's four kings and builds an army of up to 12 units from 300 points. On a board that stands on the table each turn has two actions (move a piece, strike or give it a potion) while fire, spikes and poison spread, and felling the other king wins. It plays with mouse, touch, keyboard or gamepad, forfeiting with gold down asks twice, and a guide explains every rule in thirteen languages. Online, two players sit at the board and press Ready, the table lays the board, rolls the dice and keeps a 60-second turn clock, others can watch, and a realm character can play for gold at the red table (10, 50, 200, 1,000 or 5,000 gold, the winner taking the pot). Counted online games feed a season board, whose first player after ten counted games against five different opponents wears the Grandmaster title, and the game can be turned off in Settings on the World tab. The board has stone walls, columns, rippling water, pieces as cards in stands that walk, lunge, shake when hit and topple into a pile, and its own sounds.
LeFay Monument ||| A monument to Julian LeFay, one of the creators of Daggerfall, stands outside Privateer's Hold, beside the path every new character walks out on (it moved there from Gothway Garden),, with three granite steps, a marble pedestal, a gilt-tipped obelisk and a bronze plaque on each side. Looking at it offers Throw flowers or Read the inscription, and in Info mode a press reads the inscription. A thrown flower lands on the steps or at the foot on the player's side, each character keeps its 48 newest flowers through save and load, and the plaque counts how many were laid. Townspeople walk around it, and online the flowers are the player's alone while others see the monument. The monument's painted stone and bronze plaque are back, in Daggerfall's own colour palette.
Loot Rarity ||| Loot rarity is a Diablo-style ladder over Daggerfall's loot, built on Unleveled Loot's law and on by default, with the ladder shown on the items. Clothing can drop as Magic and Rare with lines of its own: standing with a social group, warmth and weatherproofing (for Climates & Calories), Personality, and street skills, with footwear leaning to movement skills. Superior and Masterwork clothing the player makes comes out Magic and Rare, as crafted weapons and armour already did. Six Legendary garments each have a power: the Alik'r Wayfarer's Robes (heat felt 20 less), the Wrothgar Pass Mantle (cold felt 20 less), the Vestments of Stendarr (Restoration spells cost 25% less), the Pilgrim's Sandals (tire 25% slower, falls hurt half as much), the Silks of Queen Barenziah (+5 standing with every group) and the Shade's Cowl (+25 Stealth while hooded). With the loot rarity setting off, clothing stays exactly as in Daggerfall. Resistance from rolled gear counts up to 45% per element, and only fills an Aetheric piece's resistance up to that same 45%. Hone raises one line within its own range, and some Rares and Legendaries carry an empty socket. One Rare or Legendary item in twelve from a body or a pile is cursed, with one extra bonus line and one drawback: a bad reputation, bad reactions, damage or wear in holy places, weaker blows against a kind of foe, or a health leech. Gilded is the new top of the rarity ladder, above Aetheric and Artifacts, with its name in gold leaf and a frame breathing light in every slot. A rarity affix of +N% casting speed rolls on jewellery and weapons and adds up across everything worn, up to 30%. Rare weapons can no longer roll Vampiric Effect (weapons that already have it keep it), life leech from a weapon's lines and gems together is capped at 10% of each hit, Hist-Sap regenerates 1% a round (2% under half health), and Open Hand deals half damage on the second bare-handed hit. Potency, a line on jewellery and weapons, gives +3% to +15% spell damage (at most +30% from everything worn), and Wellspring, a jewellery line, returns 2% to 10% of magicka when a foe is killed (at most 10% a kill). Three caster Legendaries are found most often among mages, covens and laboratories: The Eye of Magnus (Destruction spells meet a saving throw 15 lower), The Ring of Artaeum (1% of magicka back every magic round, 2% under half) and Shalidor's Brand (Destruction spells strike 25% harder while magicka is above three quarters). Spell damage from loot is capped at +50% all together and none of it reaches a fight against another player. The Gauntlets of the Rose's second hit lands on the Oblivion Gate's boss. The life leech line reads "N% life leech on this weapon's own blows", and an off-hand weapon's leech applies only when fighting with that hand (switch with H). The Gauntlets of the Rose (Open Hand) second blow from a bare-handed hit shows its own damage number tagged "Open Hand" next to the first, and Many Endings' echo shows its own number tagged "Many Endings".
Magic Repair ||| Magic repairs work online. Enchanted items still mend only with Allow Magic Repairs, which is on by default and always on online. Armour, clothing and jewellery still mend Daggerfall's 1 point at a time, one piece every four game minutes and only for the player. The Repairs Objects enchantment repairs the player's gear again when they wear a Cast When Held or Item Deteriorates piece, which used to take every repair and undo it at once and is now repaired last. The Repairs Objects enchantment mends worn gear first, then a worn Cast When Held or Item Deteriorates piece, and only then what is carried, so damaged loot in the pack no longer takes the repairs while the ring on the finger wears down, and while such a piece is damaged it holds the repairs.
Main Menu ||| The main menu has Install beside Play, a profile mark that is a portrait of the last character, and Patreon and Ko-fi icons in the top-left corner that open their pages in the browser. It is no longer cut off on short windows, and the logo sits below the corner icons on narrow windows. The buttons are carved stone slabs in the Enhanced Plus style, all one width, and on phones they are a little more compact so the whole menu fits above the Screenshots and About buttons. Main menu clicks play the in-game click sound once the game data is loaded. The main menu first asks one question, Online or Offline, and Offline opens Continue, New Game, Load Game and the Test Room with a way back. Settings and Overhauls are in the bottom bar beside Screenshots and About, which no longer covers the buttons on phones, small or short screens and long languages. Double-clicking Offline no longer opens Load Game by accident, Escape puts the keyboard back on the button the player came from, and Tutorials and Screenshots are translated.
Morrowind Body ||| The Morrowind body features climbing poses (hanging, shimmying, wall climbing, leaps, pull-overs, vault tucks), gripping animations, first-person hand reach, crowd optimization, port-made steel plate armor sets, synchronized attack speeds, spell-casting animations, and T-pose fixes. Placed home/yard decoration NPCs stand in Morrowind bodies, you can ride in third person, look down over the 3D horse's neck in first person, and 3D horses match sprite coats with unique flank markings. With the Morrowind model in first person the arms grip the wall while climbing even with the weapon sheathed. The cape hangs from the chest alone and moves only with it as one stiff sheet, so walking, swinging the arms and sitting no longer bend it, and leaning back swings the whole cape with the chest. Looting a fallen enemy in a dungeon no longer turns its Morrowind body back into the old sprite while the loot window is open, bodies no longer clip through floors and walls, and chain boots, gauntlets and pauldrons, including Legendary ones like The Wayrest Courier's Treads, show on the body (in iron) instead of bare skin.
Morrowind Models ||| Morrowind models bring the Morrowind rig into the game with range-based asset loading, parity audits, faster loads, physical dropped items with custom pictures/models, ground rarity outlines/lights, physics collisions, smooth shading across all body/armour/weapon parts, and fixed third-person horse riding view. Morrowind models (the player, NPCs, enemies and horses) no longer turn invisible until restart. With the Morrowind look a Battle Axe (one-handed) looks and swings like a one-handed axe and a War Axe (two-handed) like a two-handed one.
Morrowind People ||| With the Morrowind body on, the people and creatures around the player stand in Morrowind bodies too: dungeon and wilderness foes, the city watch, townsfolk, quest givers, stable and wagon yard keepers, ship crews, siege fighters, road parties and the Oblivion Gate's boss and host. Foes wear the armour and carry the weapons they rolled with a race and face of their own, townsfolk are dressed by their station, foes swing, flinch and fall with their tells on the body, and a body is drawn at its sprite's size. Creatures use Morrowind's creatures where Daggerfall's have a match and keep their classic sprite otherwise. Online everyone sees a foe as its owner does. Bodies are drawn for the nearest people only within one budget for the whole frame, and a Morrowind People setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data is Off, Near (the default, up to 24 bodies) or All (up to 48). With Morrowind models on, other characters and horses keep their size wherever the camera is, and NPCs whose helmet fails to load show their head. The horses that stand in towns, at World of Daggerfall's bandit camps and forts and in the Stables stand as the 3D horse (the nearest eight within 60 m), and people who used to stay as sprites, such as a town's figures that belong to no faction, World of Daggerfall's dock people, its captives, merchants and prisoners and the crews on Come Sail Away's boats, stand in Morrowind bodies. Past the body range the sprites still stand, as do other animals, children, vampires' people and Daedra Princes. With Morrowind attached, Breton men wear three new heads and three new hairs (nine faces between them), each of the ten Breton portraits at character creation shows the face most like it (portrait 6 wearing the eyepatch), and if the heads fail to load Morrowind's own Breton faces show. Eleven of Daggerfall's townspeople (all four Redguard men, all four Nord men, two Breton men and the hooded Redguard woman) wear their own 3D outfits in their picture's colours, with hoods over the hair, sleeves, hems and boots moving with the body, legs staying inside skirts and robes, and a woman wearing her modest shirt while Show Nudity is off. The new Breton heads are for player characters only (and no longer sit backwards), NPCs wearing Morrowind's own heads, and the Breton eyepatch face sits on the right head with its own hair. In towns with market stalls the food, goods and animals at the stalls show as themselves again with the Morrowind people on.
Online Players ||| Other players are drawn as paperdolls with names or in the Morrowind body, one rig instance per peer built from their look, third-person at the feet. They no longer jump backwards near an area edge, teleport in sieges, Royal Tourneys, homes and ships, dash ahead after a lag spike or snap back when stepping into a new area, because movement updates carry their send time. When a player's movement stops reaching you they now stand still until they move again instead of running in place, including players who went quiet before you arrived. Other players far away are no longer drawn: a Draw distance setting on the Other players card chooses 150 m, 300 m (the default), 600 m or Everyone, the party and a dueled player always show, and the 150 m option can hide a distant archer. Positions near the edge between map areas arrive mostly once instead of two to four times, and other players stand on the ground without floating or jumping a metre at a time. A player who reconnects stands once, an old connection from before a restart or a second tab no longer leaving a frozen copy in town or in a dungeon, and logging in to the same account a second time in the same area closes the older connection.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole.
Points of Interest ||| Points of interest built by the developers stand in the world, such as ruins, shrines and camps, streamed in as the player travels, solid to walk on and lit at night. Places that ask to be marked show on the world map and the travel map.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day. Sethrakul's waters stay open until it dives, so the 08:00 storm no longer locks ships out, and the serpent has a sixth less health, so two ships at average gunnery win every time in about 8.5 minutes though one ship alone still cannot. Inside its waters a gale fills the sails from every direction and ships turn 1.5 times faster, its blows throw a ship half as far and tear half as much canvas, it is 1.6 times thicker so slow cannonballs fired with lag still land, and players on someone else's ship can hurt it with their own bows and attack spells. Ships have a wide invisible buffer and bounce off each other, rewards no longer depend on being near when it dies, a real part of the damage or a quarter of the fight within range earns a hoard, and party members of any ship that dealt heavy damage also get one if they came to the fight. Killing the serpent has a rare chance to grant the title Coilbreaker (1 in 20 kills) and the aura The Maelstrom (1 in 50), a three-armed whirlpool of tide and foam around the player's feet, kept for good and announced in a popup the first time. A ship that did damage may also win The Sunken Sovereign, about one kill in a hundred.
Shadows ||| Shadows cover outdoor shadow/march cuts, static caster optimization, and eight casters. With Morrowind data attached, townsfolk, guards, shopkeepers, foes, travellers, and placed figures cast Morrowind body shadows instead of old sprite outlines, while off-screen figures throw shadows into view and player/household bodies remain shadowless. Moving lights, such as a boss's glow, no longer redraw all their shadows every frame.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night. The galleon, carrack and large boat wear their own painted liveries again, with the oxblood strakes, the gilt and the stern gallery, in Daggerfall's own colour palette.
Shipyards ||| Port towns have a shipyard on their seaward edge, its back to the water, where the shipmaster sells every ship Come Sail Away builds, from the Rowboat to the Carrack and the Large Galley, and buys the player's ships back. One window holds Ships for sale (each hull's size, crew, guns and price, with buying asking for confirmation), Your fleet (where each ship lies, her worth and what the shipmaster will pay, with Bring her here, lay up, rename and sell, a big sale paid as a letter of credit) and the Shipwright and Refits, which need Naval Combat on. The Rowboat goes into the pack as parts and every other ship's deed goes into the Fleet laid up at that port. The shipmaster will not buy a ship with cargo in her hold, anything in her cabin or her crew ashore with the player, or one that came into the realm through customs, and a sold ship's cabin furniture is sold too with half its value going to the bank. Townsfolk tell the player where the shipyard is and it is marked on the town map and as an anchor on the Overworld. Shipmaster cards show each ship's own 3D picture, and the Reforge, shipyard and naval-yard pages and a merchant's options each have their own icon. Towns away from the open sea, such as Ilessan Hills, no longer have a shipyard and a lighthouse beside a pond.
Spellbook ||| The spellbook gesture was recorded, and the Icon button lets the player pick any icon for a spell, or none so it shows its initials. The spellbook can no longer be dropped, put in a chest, pile or companion's pack, shift-dropped or sold at a shop, though it still goes into the player's own wagon, with the message "Your spellbook never leaves you." A spell the player made shows its real settings in the Spellbook, and only the parts its effects use.
Stable ||| The Stable page displays horses and wagons at a glance with Summon, Send away, and renaming. Bond progress bars, food hours for fed horses, winded state notices, origin stat hovers, and coloured wagon condition bars with Wagon Yard repair costs are included. The Stable's horse look is swatches and arrows instead of large buttons. Sending a wagon or horse away from the Stable keeps them there, no longer reappearing outside the next building or dungeon entered, and a wagon or horse never sent away is shown as With you.
Stores ||| The Stores hold what the player gathers, up to 5,000 of each material per character, and are kept in town, so the Stores page in any town moves materials. The page has filters, search and sorting, and Withdraw to pack takes up to 200 at a time, every material able to be withdrawn, with herbs coming out as Daggerfall's own northern or southern ingredient that works in the potion maker. Emptying the bag into the pack is possible from the Stores page anywhere, and a withdrawal whose answer was lost is sent again when opening the page, never minted twice. When the Stores have no room for what a craft would refine, the box says so. The Stores page opens with a row of station tiles marked Here or Away, a station the player is away from staying folded until its tile is tapped, with smelting, other workings and patterns shown as cards, and materials and items show the game's own item pictures. Ingredients looted, bought or given go into the Stores as "loose", usable for crafting at any station but not for writs, the guild's Stores or market sales, which only what was gathered can serve.

Temperature Display ||| A temperature plate at the right end of the compass (Enhanced HUD) shows the felt temperature, its word and a scale from deadly cold to scorching, staying on screen at every HUD scale and on phones, movable and hideable on its own and still shown when the compass is hidden; "Temperature on the HUD" in the Climates & Calories settings turns it off. Textures ||| Textures had incident fixes, one shape for every texture door, and performance work that deleted work instead of reducing quality (PERF-TEX/TEX2/UI and MENU1-WARM). Ships (the galleon, the carrack and the large boat), wagons (the Wagon Cart, Open Wagon, Caravan and its room), the Stable, Wagon Yard and Joinery, the shipyard and lighthouse, the LeFay monument's stone and the tapestries, banners and rugs in towns use Daggerfall's own textures, read from the player's own Daggerfall files, with the ships wearing the same textures as Come Sail Away's. Townspeople ||| Townspeople in the living world run from a monster that comes close, along the street rather than through walls, and keep their distance. Those who stop to talk turn to face each other, a group waits until all have walked over and then takes turns, and the player hears them only then. People keep the same company about two minutes before moving on, with much less repetition, and indoors people at a table start talking as soon as they sit together. A stranger who said nothing as the player passed now speaks when the player stops in front of them, and a townsperson who stops for the player stays exactly where they stopped. They no longer stand inside walls or out over the harbour water or walk on the spot, and since 0.1.6640 they keep room from one another: people waiting at one spot keep a little distance, two leaving a shop together walk out in single file, and a walker steps aside, keeping right. Each square has several gathering points and every neighbourhood has a corner where its people meet, stall-keepers set up at the square or their own market, and people head out for the evening and the morning market at their own time. Guild members, priests, adventurers, sellswords and couriers walk the streets in their class, and someone keeping their place on their own appears as one of Daggerfall's own pictures of their kind, becoming themselves again the moment they walk on or join a conversation. A cat that meowed many times a second while the player looked at certain townspeople was fixed. Townsfolk inside a building spread through the whole room and sit at tables set apart, never at a table split by a wall, nobody waits at a tavern for hours for lunch, and everyone in a room sees the same people in the same places. In the street townsfolk fall out with each other, more often in the evening between drinkers, buyers haggle at stalls, beggars ask for a coin, and when someone shouts the people nearby fall quiet and turn to look. Small voices fill the street (the night watch calling the hour, stalls crying their wares, beggars calling out, drinkers singing home), every player in a town sees the same incidents, and beggars and stall keepers turn to face whoever they are talking to. Wagon ||| Wagons stay attached without stretching, are drawn on the Overworld, and feature multiple vehicle types with storage, bench seating, harnesses, turning wheels, third-person camera support, and performance culling. Worn-out wagons never become unusable, broken-down save wagons are marked "Failing" so they can be driven again, and smiths/Repairs Objects enchantments cannot repair wagons or horses. A parked team's harness stops recomputing its ropes while nothing moves. Wagon paints are Daggerfall's colours, fabrics, woods, rugs, tiles and night sky, with every paint still applying, and carts, open wagons and caravans roll on the painted wheels they had before the Daggerfall-texture pass (seven wooden spokes, a dark hub and an iron tyre with its rivets). The wagons and the caravan have their own paint back, with the caravan's livery, shutters, curtains and sunburst, in Daggerfall's colours.
Wagon Yards ||| Cities and towns feature open-air Stables and Wagon Yards with regional keepers, signboards, purchases/buybacks, map markers, and townspeople directions. General Stores no longer handle horses/carts, stables sell Sacks of Oats, wagon yards sell Wagon Repair Kits online, and equipment can be mended there. The Stable, Wagon Yard, Joinery, shipyard and lighthouse signs have their emblems back: the horseshoe, the wheel, the saw, the anchor and the coil.
Weapon Techniques ||| Magic, Rare and Legendary weapons can drop with a technique, a special attack with its own key, by default the mouse's back side button and rebindable under Controls > Combat, with a Tech button for touch corners and a d-pad placement on controllers, and unarmed fighters find theirs on Gauntlets. There are twelve: Volley and Piercing Shot for bows (the Dwarven Thunderlock can carry Piercing Shot), Leap Strike and Whirlwind for long blades, Shadowstep and Lunge for short blades, Cleave and Headsman's Chop for axes, Ground Slam and Skull Crack for blunt weapons, and Flying Kick and Haymaker for bare hands. Holding the key aims, with a blue ground marker that turns red out of reach, letting go fires and a quick tap fires where the player looks. Every technique costs fatigue and has a cooldown shown on the Enhanced HUD, damage multiplies a normal hit by the item's +N% roll so a miss is still a miss, and techniques are disabled in duels and player-vs-player bouts, never hit another player and need the loot ladder. Each has its own feel, with view movement, shake, sparks and sounds, and a Technique camera motion setting under Settings > Accessibility > Motion scales the movement from Full to Off. Flying Kick can be used while jumping, falling or stepping off a ledge, while every other leap and dash still needs solid ground, and a kick near the ground no longer cancels the damage from a long fall.
Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map. Wild Zone halls no longer vanish partway through their day after someone has been inside one. Dying in the Wrothgarian Mountains while the wagon is parked outside the zone, or sent to the stable, loses nothing from it, while a wagon driven, led or parked inside the zone still loses its cargo. A worn item looted from a player in the open PvP zone reaches the looter even if they share a guild or party, staying held until collected and going back to its owner if nobody does, and members of the same guild can no longer attack each other there, as with a party.
