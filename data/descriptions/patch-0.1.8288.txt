Elite Dungeons ||| Elite Dungeons were added in 0.1.4216. A spawned or Elite dungeon's despawn timer (7 in-game days, about 14 real hours online) starts only when someone enters it, so walking past no longer starts it and a dungeon nobody enters stays where it is. A dungeon that has despawned comes back 24 real hours later in the same spot with fresh timers, so emptied regions repopulate, and a character's save no longer keeps despawned dungeons gone forever.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet.
Houses ||| Doors say Residence and list Go in and Buy it when pointed at, a quest's house reads "The <name> Residence" and is never for sale, a customs-brought house cannot be sold for nothing, pressing Sell twice does not error, shelves in ordinary houses do not swallow clicks, entering houses no longer drops players into the void or dark, and a Knightly Order's house is not offered for purchase at its owner's door. When a family member or online character dies permanently, their house and contents (door, bed, chests, furniture, and cupboards) pass to their heir; if the heir already owns a house in that region, the inherited house waits until they sell theirs. Windows no longer show the street where the building's own wall stands.
Keybindings ||| The keybinding standard (KB1) was introduced and audited, and shared keys were reworked in 0.1.4122. A key that is set but not confirmed says "Not saved yet - press Confirm." on its row, the top of the page counts the unsaved changes and highlights Confirm, and the Confirm bar no longer hides behind the Settings search bar or covers the row being bound on a phone. Polling a bound key no longer builds a list for every binding. Swing Weapon bound to Left Click, with mouse activate unbound, survives a restart.
Loot Rarity ||| Loot rarity is a Diablo-style ladder over Daggerfall's loot, built on Unleveled Loot's law and on by default, with the ladder shown on the items. Clothing can drop as Magic and Rare with lines of its own: standing with a social group, warmth and weatherproofing (for Climates & Calories), Personality, and street skills, with footwear leaning to movement skills. Superior and Masterwork clothing the player makes comes out Magic and Rare, as crafted weapons and armour already did. Six Legendary garments each have a power: the Alik'r Wayfarer's Robes (heat felt 20 less), the Wrothgar Pass Mantle (cold felt 20 less), the Vestments of Stendarr (Restoration spells cost 25% less), the Pilgrim's Sandals (tire 25% slower, falls hurt half as much), the Silks of Queen Barenziah (+5 standing with every group) and the Shade's Cowl (+25 Stealth while hooded). With the loot rarity setting off, clothing stays exactly as in Daggerfall. Resistance from rolled gear counts up to 45% per element, and only fills an Aetheric piece's resistance up to that same 45%. Hone raises one line within its own range, and some Rares and Legendaries carry an empty socket. One Rare or Legendary item in twelve from a body or a pile is cursed, with one extra bonus line and one drawback: a bad reputation, bad reactions, damage or wear in holy places, weaker blows against a kind of foe, or a health leech. Gilded is the new top of the rarity ladder, above Aetheric and Artifacts, with its name in gold leaf and a frame breathing light in every slot. A rarity affix of +N% casting speed rolls on jewellery and weapons and adds up across everything worn, up to 30%. Rare weapons can no longer roll Vampiric Effect (weapons that already have it keep it), life leech from a weapon's lines and gems together is capped at 10% of each hit, Hist-Sap regenerates 1% a round (2% under half health), and Open Hand deals half damage on the second bare-handed hit. Potency, a line on jewellery and weapons, gives +3% to +15% spell damage (at most +30% from everything worn), and Wellspring, a jewellery line, returns 2% to 10% of magicka when a foe is killed (at most 10% a kill). Three caster Legendaries are found most often among mages, covens and laboratories: The Eye of Magnus (Destruction spells meet a saving throw 15 lower), The Ring of Artaeum (1% of magicka back every magic round, 2% under half) and Shalidor's Brand (Destruction spells strike 25% harder while magicka is above three quarters). Spell damage from loot is capped at +50% all together and none of it reaches a fight against another player. The Gauntlets of the Rose's second hit lands on the Oblivion Gate's boss.
Morrowind Models ||| Morrowind models bring the Morrowind rig into the game with range-based asset loading, parity audits, faster loads, physical dropped items with custom pictures/models, ground rarity outlines/lights, physics collisions, smooth shading across all body/armour/weapon parts, and fixed third-person horse riding view. Morrowind models (the player, NPCs, enemies and horses) no longer turn invisible until restart.
Motherlodes ||| Motherlodes are daily rich ore heaps announced in chat and marked on the compass, requiring Mining 25 and Pick-Axe strikes for ore, XP, and silver without counting against harvest limits, and neither motherlodes nor veins, boulders, herbs, and trees appear inside rocks. Motherlodes appear on the compass from their warning onward, in their own colour, drawn over nearby veins.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass.
Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map. Wild Zone halls no longer vanish partway through their day after someone has been inside one.
