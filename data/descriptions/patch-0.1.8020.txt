Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player. A username can no longer be an email address: a player who signed up with one still signs in with it but other players see a generated name. After a server update players no longer all reconnect at once, each game waiting a moment (longer the more players are online), sign-in token requests and the account, realm, heartbeat and profession reads are answered cheaply from read copies of the database, the letterbox, Notice Board count and play time share one request, and when many players sign in at once they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error.
Adventuring Companies ||| Adventurers travel together in named companies such as "The Lantern Company" and dive dungeons more often as a group, and pilgrims going the same way on the same day travel as a band, each region keeping its own holy day for pilgrims setting out for its temple towns. A company introduces itself by name and the chronicle's People page lists the companies of the people the player knows. In a dungeon a company works through stop by stop, so the player meets them wherever their route has brought them, and may hear fighting ahead; talking to them lets the player have them join, lead on to their next stop or part ways. Badly hurt members fall back and a beaten company heads for the exit. Offline, for a day after a company passes through, the monsters at the places they reached lie dead with their packs emptied and the treasure picked over, though quest monsters and items and fixed monsters and treasure are never touched; a rival company that is not friendly will not like the player taking treasure it was heading for.
Alchemy Station ||| An alchemy station brews Daggerfall's twenty potions, at any Alchemist's for 50 gold a brew or at a home station. Ingredients come from the player's Stores. A brew makes one potion, two from Journeyman and three as a Master, and may come out Potent with +25% magnitude for healing, stamina, strength or power potions. Daggerfall's own potion maker is unchanged and earns nothing toward Alchemy. The stations name the craft they serve, "Provisioning" at the fire and the alchemy station. Online, pressing a home's alchemy station offers Make Potions or Brew on the Stores page, while offline it opens Daggerfall's maker as before.
Arena Bouts ||| An arena bout is a fight on the sand in which nobody dies. At 1 health the fighter goes down, and at 15% or less they can sheathe to yield. The healers make everyone whole afterwards, purses run from 50 gold for a first-tier win to 10,000 for the Grand Champion, and progress, titles and record are saved with the character. The player cannot rest, travel or leave through the gates during a bout, and set bonuses, sigil weapon bonuses and Legendary powers switch off against other players. An enchantment's own bite, such as a Damned blade's or any Health Leech weapon's, no longer kills the player in a bout but leaves them at 1 health like every other blow there.
Arrows ||| Arrows are crafted ammunition. A second recipe uses 1 Pine Plank, 1 Iron Ingot and 1 Harpy Feathers to make twenty arrows. A double-arrow fault was fixed. A shot that misses stays in the wall, floor, tree or ground it hit and can be taken back like any pile (up to 40 stay in one place, the oldest going first, gone once the player leaves the area), while conjured arrows still vanish and arena arrows are refunded by the healers. An arrow that hits an enemy survives half the time and can be looted from the body, and arrows no longer count toward a normal enemy's three-item drop limit. Twigs, a Pine Branch or a Wood Bundle used from the inventory fletch 2, 4 or 10 arrows, and Weapon Smiths always keep 50-100 arrows on the counter.
Boarding ||| Boarding is how a ship is captured. After battering a ship until she strikes her colours, the player comes alongside, slows right down (heaving to beside her now stops the player within boarding reach, even at storm speeds) and presses Activate to throw grapples, which puts out her fires. Her crew and captain fight on her deck, and killing the captain and most of the crew takes her. The player's own crew comes along, and the player lands across from where they stood. Pirates can board the player too, with only one pirate raid at a time, and they fall back and cast off if beaten or timed out, then wait five minutes before grappling again. Beating off a pirate boarding now pays, the pirate being beaten then, so taking or sinking her afterwards is logged but not paid again.
Boss Fight Integrity ||| In Oblivion Gate, Abyss Dungeon and Sea Serpent fights the server checks the boss's attacks itself, working out from where the player stood which blows reached them, and keeps its own count of each fighter's health and of each ship's hull against the serpent. Potions, healing spells and repairs count within a set amount over time, allies' healing spells do not use that amount, and a ship repaired and returned is counted fresh. The server gives the benefit of the doubt, counting a blow only if the player was well inside the attack's area both just before and just after it landed and never counting elemental damage. For now it only records this and fights play as before; once staff switch it on, a fighter the server counts as fallen will fall in their own game too, using up the Abyss Dungeon's one life or wrecking the ship at sea.
Bounty Boards ||| Bounty boards are notice boards in towns with two or more notice boards, where half become Bounty Boards. Every board in a town posts the same four hunts to every player, and notices change each in-game day. Pressing one opens the board window in the Enhanced Plus style. Clicking Take bounty repeatedly takes the board's notices in order from the top, after the last take the notice shows a short line instead of buttons, Give up asks for a second click and sits on the left, and the board keeps one size so the Take button stays put.
Captain Standing ||| A captain builds standing with each crown from Unsworn to Admiral, with perks in that crown's harbours: a shipwright discount, better-paid and more contracts, a letter of marque for sale (Privateer), the Admiralty's carrack for sale and seasoned hires (Commodore) and the navy always coming to aid against pirates (Admiral). A captain whose notoriety runs too high in a crown's waters gets none of them there. A letter of marque makes a rival crown's merchantmen and navy lawful prize in the player's crown's eyes, though the rival still calls it piracy, and contracts raise standing when finished and lower it when failed or given up.
Captain Tab ||| The Captain tab in Holdings keeps the player's log as a captain: leagues sailed, ships sunk, taken and beaten off, contracts done, the best prize and the plunder hauled. It also lists the harbourmaster contracts currently held.
Caravans ||| A merchant's caravan travels with a horse and wagon (two wagons from a great house's city) with its guards walking in front and behind, a noble's procession brings a baggage wagon, and pedlars and farmers lead a pack horse. The wagons' wheels turn, at camp the wagons are parked round one fire without standing in each other with each horse tied alongside its cart, and a wagon follows its horse round a bend. Caravans and travellers keep to the right of the road, and a caravan's mark on the travel map is a wagon. Talking to a caravan's merchant, a farmer or a pedlar allows buying and selling from a shop's stock and a limited purse, with friends getting 10% off. A merchant's caravan offers a guard contract from the day before it sets out until it arrives, paid by the day plus more for each foe beaten if the player stays within 300 m, and unpaid if they fall behind over an hour, let the merchant die or rob or kill any of them; offline the player can travel on with it to its next stop. Beating every guard of a caravan makes it surrender and its goods and purse are the player's to take, but any survivor who reaches a town reports the theft, pickpocketing or murder as a crime in the region where it happened. A road caravan's campfire is a fire again with Morrowind models on, and a roadside fight the player walks up to no longer ends by itself.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster. The chat has a search bar above the player list where part of a name, or # and a tag, shows every tab's lines from or about that player, and /search <name> searches from the chat field while /search alone clears it.
Come Sail Away ||| Come Sail Away is the naval mod. The splashes of its boats no longer show as white squares on the water. Owners of its boats no longer lose frames in towns for every boat placed wherever it is moored, other players' boats cost less, and the sea's helm and broadside panels do no work while the player is ashore.
Crew Halls ||| Inside every shipyard the crewmaster has six named sailors looking for a berth each day, each with a class, a grade from Green to Veteran, a trade (First Mate, Bosun, Gunner, Carpenter, Lookout, Cook, Deckhand or a Bard) and a signing fee, and the player chooses which ship they join and at what post. A ship comes with her whole crew, so on a full deck a recruit signs on in place of a chosen hand, a hire that adds men costs what the shipwright would charge if that is more than the fee, and hands can be paid off from the same page, naming who steps up and never the last hand. Hiring and paying off need Naval Combat on and cannot happen during a fight, and the crew page shows which hands are ashore with the player.
Crew Roles ||| Each named hand can be given a post from the ship's card: First Mate, Bosun, Gunner, Carpenter, Lookout, Cook or Deckhand. Each post has a place on deck, with the First Mate aft and Gunners at the rails, and hands keep to their posts most of the time. There is one First Mate per ship, and naming a new one makes the old one a Deckhand. Every hand earns rank from the fights and boardings they have been through, Green, Seasoned, Veteran and Old Salt. The six posts do more the longer their holder has served: the Gunner speeds the reload, the Carpenter the mending, the Lookout lets the player see further, the Cook keeps spirits up, the Bosun adds way under sail and the First Mate brings extra hands over the rail. A companion sailing with the player works his post while they are at the helm, and a new hand takes whichever post is emptiest.
DFO Grimoire ||| DFO Grimoire is a fourth look under Main menu > Overhauls > UI, dressing everything Enhanced Plus does in GrimoireUI's parchment, wood, red-and-gold presses, glass vials and lettering. The vitals are three vials, upright in the corner in Daggerfall's order or lying in a row under the hotbar, and the card offers Pages (cream page or tan scroll), Backdrop (the desk or the map), Vitals and Spellbook (the DFO book or GrimoireUI's own), each applied at once. The front door stands on a detailed desk and rarity, set and sigil names are inked deeper. Text on the parchment is readable, stray ruled lines are gone, health bars scale with the screen resolution, the in-game book opens a setting's description as a scroll beside it, and the decorations menu shows the selected tabs and filters.
Daggerfall Online ||| Daggerfall Online is the project's name. The rebrand was carried across every surface (BR4), including the Mac logo. The game is now DFO Online, with a new logo on the intro, the main menu, the menu's masthead, the website and the desktop launcher. Settings and menus say "the original game" or "classic" where they used to name it, and screenshots and saved loading shots are named dfo-... with exported saves as DFO-Online-Saves.zip.
Damned Weapons ||| One cursed Legendary weapon in four is Damned. Its curse line rolls at the very top of its range, but it costs 8 health on every hit and no temple can lift the curse. Unequipping it stops the bleeding, so it can be taken out for a boss and put away again.
Decorator ||| The decorator offers furnishings for homes. With the town mods on it adds 297 pieces such as beds and rugs, changing within a second when a town mod is turned on or off. It also offers what Daggerfall stands in its dungeons (thrones, cages, coffins, statues, chains, braziers) and a yard offers Daggerfall's street pieces and its own town's climate trees and plants, each with its own picture. People placed with the decorator (the Vendors list) can no longer be resized, with Smaller and Bigger gone for them. Those placed earlier at another size now stand at their normal size, moving one sets it to normal, an enlarged one gives back half the price difference and a shrunk one costs nothing to move.
Deep Waters ||| Far from every port pirates are more common, bolder and bigger, and every hold and floating cask is richer. The helm plate says when the player is in the deep.
Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, and the app has its own icon. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. The downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app, which can be dragged to the Bin once DFO Online opens, and on Windows a pinned taskbar or Start menu entry may need pinning again under the new name.
Disenchanting ||| At an enchanting station a crafted piece in the player's pack comes apart into Arcane Essence in the Stores. Pressing a home's enchanting station online asks for Daggerfall's item maker (Make Magic Items) or Disenchant, which opens the Stores page at the Enchanting Station section where a crafted piece comes apart into Arcane Essence, while offline the station opens Daggerfall's maker as before.
Dungeon Map ||| The dungeon map was reworked as an enhanced map with a 3D view, where holding the right mouse button turns and tilts the map at the same time and steep ramps in hallways fill in once walked. Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, and a secrets tier shows hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, and its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies. The compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked, optional exact quest guidance marks a quest's people and things on the map and compass, and secret walls are no longer labelled "Door". Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, with a secrets tier showing hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies, and the compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked. Optional exact quest guidance marks a quest's people and things on the map and compass, secret walls are no longer labelled "Door", and with All pressed the 3D map draws every ramp in full.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it.
Dying ||| Dying no longer leaves the player alive at 0% health with a window open over the death. After respawning online, paralysis, silence and fatigue or magicka drain are gone, though diseases still carry over. No single enemy blow can kill the player from full health but leaves them at 1 HP, and the next hit can still kill, with falls, spells, traps and world-boss attacks unchanged and a set's death saves (Unbroken, the Hour Turns) not used up on these blows.
Elite Gates ||| Elite Gates are the Elite Breach and the Battlespire's torn halls, added in 0.1.7947, whose release notes give only the pull request title.
Enhanced Lighting ||| Enhanced lighting is a rendering overhaul with a linear pipeline, shadows in three cascades, contact shadows, depth and air, a depth-aware ambient occlusion blur, rigs that cast shadows and water that receives them. It was overhauled in 0.1.4360 (shadows, lights and post chain), and it can be turned off online. The shadow cache is on by default, so lamp-lit rooms such as taverns, shops and guild halls keep their frame rate. The shadow flicker on Windows, a strobing on some NVIDIA cards and every shadow in a room blinking at once on others, came from how cached shadows were copied on Direct3D and is fixed; the "Shadow cache (faster, may flicker)" switch is gone. Taverns, shops, homes and guild halls are no longer lit evenly wall to wall: a building's own lamps burn at half their old strength so their light falls in warm pools with darker corners, candles and plain white lamps indoors burn with the street lanterns' warm flame colour, and the player's eyes brighten a dim room less. The player's torch, candle, light spells, other players' torches and the Classic lighting look are unchanged.
Etheric Sea ||| Sailing far out on the open Abecean, west of the Bay's mouth and well away from land, asks whether to sail on into the open sea, and three days later the ship comes out off Veilhaven, the first port past the Veil, with her crew and hold; sailing back into the Veil asks the way home. Past it lies an endless sea of isles, the same for every player: port isles have a town and a freeport quay, ruins a dungeon to delve, wild isles exotic goods and bare rocks are just rocks. The farther west, through the Shallows, the Reaches, the Deep Aether and the Abyss, the deadlier the corsairs and the richer every hold, with ruins in the Deep Aether and the Abyss being Elite dungeons. No crown's law reaches past the Veil, so there is no navy to call on and nothing sunk there is a crime. Ether-amber, starglass and voidsilk are made on the isles and wanted in every port of the Bay, six legendary ships haunt the depths with a purse on their heads, and past the Veil the travel map key opens a chart of the isles sighted. The Veil holds the Bay's magic back, so Recall, guild teleports, portal stones and a party's travel to its leader do not cross it and the Fleet cannot send a ship across; falling past it wakes the player at the nearest port isle. The light is cold and violet, and the crossing is part of the enhanced game and online play, though a captain already past the Veil can always sail home.
Exchange ||| An Exchange tab at a port buys and sells eight goods of the Bay: wine, grain, wool, iron, spices, silk, silver and ivory. Each crown's ports make some goods cheaply and pay well for others, every port has a twist of its own, prices drift day to day and each port deals in only so much of a good each day. Goods load into and out of the hold of the ship at the quay, a full hold slows her, and the exchange says how much more she can carry and will not sell her so much she cannot move. Shops do not buy the exchange's goods.
Eye of the Beholder ||| Eye of the Beholder is a mod, version 2.1, ported partially. Fixes made the body stand properly instead of as a statue or on its head, repaired the hovering body, the chop and the turn, added the cart, and stopped the camera clipping through walls and ceilings. The third-person sprite no longer flickers between two directions when starting to run or strafe at an angle, an attack plays one swing instead of a swing and a follow-through, and the "PingPong Offset" setting no longer does anything. In first person, looking up no longer shows the player's own body, which follows the mod's visibility setting that by default casts the shadow but is never seen.
Fealty ||| Fealty lets a guild holding a palace seat in a crown's kingdom or March swear to the crown's guild. The vassal pays its liege 5% of its Tithe each week after its own upkeep, and the liege adds half its reach to the vassal's defence. They cannot pledge against each other's seats and Conscription never takes from a vassal, and either side can break it, the breaker losing 10 Standing at every seat it holds. An offer made to a guild can be declined from the Seat tab. A vassal's 5% to its liege is called its "fealty share", so "Tribute" means only what a guild pays at the seat it pledged.
Gathering ||| Gathering covers herb patches, ore veins, boulders, trees, bodies and fishing water. E acts on one only when it is looked at, in reach and unobstructed, and a node that cannot yet be worked says why on its row (tool, rank or daily limit). Actions use the loot list, picked by clicking, activating or tapping, with a small list window on the Classic skin and touch screens. The minigames are drawn on and around the crosshair with one short hint that fades, progress as diamonds and a sound for every strike, find, slip, splash and tug. Without a Materials Bag everything gathered goes in the pack even over its weight, and the message says so. A herb patch, vein, boulder or tree goes the moment the player finishes working it instead of waiting for the server to answer, coming back if the server refuses the harvest, and goods, XP and rank-ups arrive faster after each harvest.
Gear Wear ||| Gear wear is how weapons and armour degrade. Wear per hit is at Daggerfall's own rate, only damage that gets through armour wears the weapon and the piece hit, good armour wears slower, and claws and bites no longer wear armour. Every weapon type now has the same base condition, the Warhammer's 1,600, with the material ladder unchanged, so daggers, tantos and short bows no longer break far sooner than a warhammer of the same metal; an iron short bow takes about 800 hits of 20 damage. Enchanted weapons gain the most: a Cast-When-Strikes enchantment costs 10 condition a strike, so an iron Worm's Tooth lasts 160 strikes, and a Cast-When-Held enchantment pays its casting cost the first time it is equipped without breaking the weapon. Weapons update on load keeping the same percentage of condition, repairs are priced by the share of condition lost so light weapons and bows cost less to keep up, and broken enchanted gear stays in the pack for a smith to repair. Legendary items no longer lose condition when equipped, where the Aegis of Dawn lost about a third the first time it was put on, while other magic items still pay their usual cost.
Gems ||| Each of the eight classic gems comes in five grades, Chipped, Flawed, regular, Flawless and Perfect, with better grades giving stronger bonuses. Socketed in a weapon a gem adds fire or frost damage, life leech, extra damage or an attribute, and in armour or jewellery a resistance or an attribute. Gems drop from bodies, dungeon treasure and tavern or guild caches, with deeper and deadlier places dropping better grades, and world bosses drop them too: one from the Oblivion Gate's Warden, one to every player who dealt their share of Sethrakul's damage, and two from the Brass Remnant, all Flawless or Perfect. A weapon's gems of the same kind together give at most one Rare line's worth, so mixing kinds pays more than doubling up, and the item card shows when the limit is hit. Graded gems later became about a third as rare from bodies, dungeon piles and tavern or guild markers, a mid-level foe dropping one about once every 33 kills and the deepest dungeons and boss-level foes once every 20 sources, though a gem's grade still depends on where it is found and boss, shop, Daggerfall and mined gems are unchanged.
Guild Tab ||| The Guild tab is split into pages: Overview, Members, Treasury, Vault, Arms and Settings, and Stores. The Members page shows whether each member is online, with a green dot while playing and "Online" or "Last online 3 h ago" under their rank, a member counting as online within ten minutes of their last check-in. It has a folded list, "What each rank may do", showing every rank's powers in the guild's own rank names. Settings lists everything standing in the way of disbanding before pressing, Overview says where the rest of the guild is run, and with no guild yet the tab says in two lines what a guild is for.
Guild Treasury ||| Any guild member can put Marks into the treasury but only the guildmaster can take them out. A guild cannot be disbanded while its treasury holds gold, and when a guild disbands or its last member leaves its Marks go to the guildmaster. Taking more gold than can be carried pays it as a letter of credit, and Deposit and Withdraw say "at most 1,000,000 at a time" if more is typed. Treasury says which of the gold can buy a hall or a new name or tag, only gold the realm characters put in, and the silver ledger says which way each line went, with a Tribute, upkeep or claim fee reading "paid ..." and a line the guild made itself reading as the guild's.
HUD ||| The HUD's lost-part strip on the fatigue, health and magicka bars is now a dim strip and stays at most about a second before catching up. Notifications that have been moved now fade in where the player put them. The compass can be turned over in the HUD editor (Alt+U), whose length handle follows the pointer and which no longer pushes a resized piece off the screen.
Harbourmaster ||| Every port's shipwright window has a Harbourmaster tab with the day's contracts while the ship lies at that port's quay: bounties on a named pirate, hunts in the crown's waters, cargo to carry to another port, a wreck's casks to raise and Admiralty surveys of ships through the spyglass. Contracts pay by level and rank and run to a deadline, finishing one raises standing with the crown while failing or giving one up lowers it, and three can be held at a time. A bounty's pirate waits in the waters named on the contract and the bounty is void with no loss of standing if someone else sinks her first.
Harbours ||| Port towns now have ships moored along their shore, sails stowed, the same for every player. A moored ship stays a while and sets sail, and merchantmen sail in and tie up at a free berth. Near a port merchantmen come and go, navy ships patrol the approaches and pirates lurk offshore, and a berthed navy ship sails out to meet a pirate while a merchant flees. A coastal seat can raise a Harbour, making the town a port for the holder's members. Where the sea floor shelves gently, berths and quays lie further from shore, and a port whose harbour lies far from any street no longer searches for its dock four times a second. A lake behind a port town is no longer taken for the sea, and ports, lighthouses and quays run lighter, with the port's textures loading over a few frames and the quays' hidden timbers left out of collision.
Heraldry ||| Every guild can raise a banner of field colour, border colour and device, with 16 colours and 24 devices at first, later 40 devices, 7 two-colour field splits and a device colour. Ash can only be the border. The guildmaster chooses on the Guild tab, the first choice is free and each change costs 500 silver from the silver treasury, and once a guild has heraldry two banners hang either side of its hall's door. A guild's heraldry is called its "arms" everywhere, as on the Arms page.
Home Votes ||| The door of another player's home offers Vote up and Vote down, becoming "Take back your vote" once voted, and on touch screens, the Classic interface or with World Tooltips off the door is clicked in Info mode. Each account has one vote per home, none on its own home, its own guild's hall or a Knightly Order house, voting needs a username and password held for a week and up to 30 votes an hour can be cast. If a home's votes add up to less than zero other players see it as Daggerfall built it, with no placed decorations and the town's own paint, while a hired trader stays open and the owner and a hall's guild members still see everything; nothing is deleted and the decorations return once the total is zero or above. A home with votes shows the total on its door and the owner is told when the decorations are hidden.
Hotbar ||| On a controller, LT raises the hotbar, A picks up and moves or swaps slots and Y clears a slot. With a controller, Add to hotbar picks the entry up and raises the bar so the player chooses the slot, and picking up the mouse while something is in hand drops it and lowers the bar. Spamming the hotbar no longer makes the game stutter.
Item Card ||| Hovering a piece shows what it would replace, with now to after per body part in green or red. An unidentified piece keeps its enchantments to itself until identified, and the card's fit was fixed. A weapon technique has its own section on the Enhanced item card, the way set pieces and sigils do, with a header of its glyph (three blue strike marks), its name and the roll (such as +23% [15-30]), what it does, and a footer with the key, hold-to-aim or press, the fatigue cost and the cooldown, and the shop's hover card shows the same. The Info box shows it in full with how the roll raises the technique's blow, the section greys out in a duel, and a letter of credit shows its worth as Worth.
Keybindings ||| The keybinding standard (KB1) was introduced and audited, and shared keys were reworked in 0.1.4122. A key that is set but not confirmed says "Not saved yet - press Confirm." on its row, the top of the page counts the unsaved changes and highlights Confirm, and the Confirm bar no longer hides behind the Settings search bar or covers the row being bound on a phone. Polling a bound key no longer builds a list for every binding.
Lighthouses ||| Port towns have a lighthouse, a whitewashed stone tower with red bands, a gallery and a lantern room with the keeper's cottage beside it. While the town's lanterns are lit (5 pm to 8 am) its lamp lights the harbour and two beams sweep a full circle over the sea and town, in the same place for everyone online. Talking to the lightkeeper (or pressing the door) climbs up to the gallery on foot, not at a ship's helm and not with enemies about, with no way to fall or climb off the gallery and the hatch taking the player back down. The lamp lights with Enhanced Lighting, and there is a Lighthouses page in the tutorial.
Live Chat ||| Live chat has one World tab and was audited before the merge. Chat is spread over more servers: world chat is split sixteen ways by account and each region's chat four ways, so more players fit at once, the World tab's Online count stays accurate past 512 players and it lists everyone while there are no more than 512. Typing /find <name> (or /who <name>) finds anyone online by name, and the Guild tab lists guildmates online wherever they are.
Loading ||| Pressing a key while the world loads no longer shows a red crash message. The game no longer fails to start with "boot failed: cannot access ... before initialization", which happened when starting near a port town or with the travel map's dungeon lists in play.
Login ||| A crash some online players hit right after logging in was fixed, and logging in no longer sends the player back to the title screen after the account service fell over under load. In 0.1.6566 logins stopped failing for everyone after the account server was overwhelmed by every game checking every town's yard decorations each minute: yards are now fetched only for towns near the player, the server keeps a short copy, and a failed fetch waits longer each time. When many players sign in at once, such as right after an update, they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error, are told they are in line and keep their place on retry.
Loot Window ||| A loot-side click takes immediately, and the loot buttons were fixed. The loot list on a body or pile scrolls, so the mouse wheel (or the pad) reaches every item and the list shows how many items are above and below.
Main Menu ||| The main menu has Install beside Play, a profile mark that is a portrait of the last character, and Patreon and Ko-fi icons in the top-left corner that open their pages in the browser. Market ||| The Market is a tab on every Notice Board, beside Notices and Work, where goods are traded in Marks (and later also in gold). Its views are grouped as Buy (Materials, Crafted, Auctions, Goods), Sell (Orders, My listings) and History, with the player's silver at the top, column names on listings and the Vendors list, and the Weavers' and Apothecaries' counters under one "Suppliers" line. My listings is split into the List form, the player's listings, auctions, bids and buy orders. A player lists 1 to 5,000 units at 1 to 1,000,000 Marks each, up to 30 listings of at most 10,000,000 Marks, each standing 72 hours on every board of its region, for a fee of 1% of its worth, and unsold goods come back. A sale pays at once less a 5% sales tax. A listing in the player's own region is theirs at once, and one in another region comes by courier after 15 minutes plus a minute per 10 map pixels for a fee. A signature material is cheap at home and dear abroad, History shows 7-day median prices, and Your trades lists the last 20 sales and purchases. A listing can be priced in gold or Drakes: gold has no listing fee, takes the buyer's purse, then letters of credit, then bank, and goods bought with one currency can only be resold for the same one. Worn, locked, bound, summoned and quest items, gold, boat deeds, parts, arrows and unfinished pieces cannot be listed, and dishes and jewellery have their own categories. A reported listing can be removed by a moderator. The Market starts loading as soon as the board opens. The market loads noticeably faster, especially when switching views and after buying, and a view opened in the last minute shows straight away without a "Reading the market..." flash.
Mining ||| Mining is a gathering profession utilizing wilderness ore veins, dungeon walls, and boulder quarrying with Pick-Axes and glint strikes, featuring region-specific ores and progression tied to confirmed land and player gathering activity[cite: 3]. Copper, silver, platinum, moonstone, dwarven scrap, mithril, adamantium, ebony and orichalcum veins each have their own colour, while lead and lodestone keep their classic look. Gold, Platinum, Mithril, Ebony and a kingdom's own ore only appear on ground that three different players, each on an account at least a week old, have gathered on, since the servers cannot check which land a player claims to stand on, and the world map card shows which ores are waiting on that for each place.
Morrowind People ||| With the Morrowind body on, the people and creatures around the player stand in Morrowind bodies too: dungeon and wilderness foes, the city watch, townsfolk, quest givers, stable and wagon yard keepers, ship crews, siege fighters, road parties and the Oblivion Gate's boss and host. Foes wear the armour and carry the weapons they rolled with a race and face of their own, townsfolk are dressed by their station, foes swing, flinch and fall with their tells on the body, and a body is drawn at its sprite's size. Creatures use Morrowind's creatures where Daggerfall's have a match and keep their classic sprite otherwise. Online everyone sees a foe as its owner does. Bodies are drawn for the nearest people only within one budget for the whole frame, and a Morrowind People setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data is Off, Near (the default, up to 24 bodies) or All (up to 48).
Notice Board ||| The Notice Board is the corkboard that a town's rumour boards open, in Enhanced Plus style. Each town has one, the board nearest its middle in towns with three or more bulletin boards; the other boards are Daggerfall's rumour boards again, and Bounty Boards are unchanged. Notes stand straight on a dark panel with larger text, tabs show counts (new notes on Notices, open writs on Work), and on a phone the tab row scrolls sideways. Notices shows town and server news first, with a pointer to the Bounty Board and server notices under a red seal, then players' notes, and every board in a town shows the same notes. "N new" floats over a board with unread notes as the player approaches. A registered player can pin a note of up to 800 characters for 1, 3 or 7 days, up to 3 across every board, which can carry a button to ask to join the party or the guild, or a Challenge to a duel. Only a Guildmaster or an Officer who can invite can pin a guild recruitment note. A note can be taken down at any time by its author, any registered player can report one, and after three different reports it is hidden until a moderator looks, with moderators able to remove any note and a muted player unable to pin. Messages across the tabs are short and plain, and the board's window downloads in the background after the game starts so it is usually ready when first opened. Many fixes keep half-written forms and notes from being lost. The Notice Board's trader directory now looks only at the traders of its own region, so sign-ins, saves and everything else on the account service stop failing under load.
Online Mode ||| Online mode is the enhanced lane whole: every enhancement and every mod is on while online. Night gates and guild halls were added online (OL5). An out-of-date game can no longer go online and stays offline until updated, and some updates disconnect everyone online once when they go live. The macOS app and the portable Windows app do not update themselves and need the newest build downloaded. The multiplayer server restarts when updates arrive, disconnecting everyone for a moment so they reconnect on their own. Games in a crowded town send less and the server bundles other players' movements, and an area next to a duel, a Wrothgarian zone fight or a sea serpent fight says so.
Online Players ||| Other players are drawn as paperdolls with names or in the Morrowind body, one rig instance per peer built from their look, third-person at the feet. They no longer jump backwards near an area edge, teleport in sieges, Royal Tourneys, homes and ships, dash ahead after a lag spike or snap back when stepping into a new area, because movement updates carry their send time. When a player's movement stops reaching you they now stand still until they move again instead of running in place, including players who went quiet before you arrived. Other players far away are no longer drawn: a Draw distance setting on the Other players card chooses 150 m, 300 m (the default), 600 m or Everyone, the party and a dueled player always show, and the 150 m option can hide a distant archer. Positions near the edge between map areas arrive mostly once instead of two to four times, and other players stand on the ground without floating or jumping a metre at a time.
Outlaws ||| Each region has up to four named bands of outlaws, each with a hideout off a road between two of its towns, and a band holds up the parties too weak to fight it off and keeps what it took. A found hideout shows tents, a fire, the band and a chest of stolen goods, and killing every outlaw routs the band, the dead staying down on return, the robbed people grateful, the region's towns talking about it and a new band moving in later. Travellers warn about camps near the road they are on, and a band the player has heard of is marked on the travel map as a rumoured hideout, near it but never exactly on it.
Performance ||| A GPU memory leak from opening the map in a dungeon or building was fixed, long sessions and long journeys no longer accumulate video or GPU memory because left areas are released over time, settings are read faster everywhere, and people, guards, and foes do not pop out of view at screen edges. Opening the Overworld no longer freezes the game as nearby dungeon models load one per frame after the view rises, deep snow processing is disabled while the Overworld is open, and saving in snowy areas is faster because snow-track data is recompressed only when changed. Many town and frame costs were cut: still flats share large buffers and one index buffer, yards and stables behind the player or far away are not drawn, collision no longer grows with the view distance, lamp shadows redraw only the sides someone is in front of and reuse last frame's answer, night towns and harbours make less garbage, shaders compile in the background, the sky skips stars by day and two-graphics-card laptops use the faster card.
Player Trade ||| Player trade was added in 0.1.3720. In a trade each letter of credit shows its worth on its own row ("Worth 12,500 gold") across the pack, the offer and theirs, working on touch. A realm trade of an item looted in the last couple of minutes no longer fails, a character whose trade is on hold can no longer move its crafted pieces through another character, and when the realm refuses a trade because a character is under review it says whose character it is.
Prices ||| Prices are said in real time, and NaN prices were fixed. Books and potion recipes no longer jump to a much higher price after passing between players online, which let them sell back for more than they cost.
Quays ||| Every harbour berth now has a timber quay on piles, with a jetty, bollards, crates and lantern posts. A port's quays stand from about 1.6 km out so they are seen before the berth is reached, the port's own ships lie alongside them, and online every player sees the same quays except with differing Iliac Puddle Deep Waters settings. Every quay is overhauled with cross-braced piles, timbers under the decks, fender piles and iron ladders down into the water, cleats and coils of rope by the bollards, a cargo crane on each harbour's main quay and lamp posts along the long jetties.
Quest Journal ||| The quest journal's Quests tab opens on the quest the card is showing. Under each active quest a line names the place its latest entry sends the player, in Daggerfall's words, and Show on map closes the journal and opens the travel map on the place, outdoors only, or says to ask around if it is not on the map yet. The chronicle shows "Time remains" on a quest with a deadline, counting down live. Finished quests have a Remove button and Clear archive removes them all, and active quests can be hidden without abandoning them, with Show hidden bringing them back. Typing in the quest journal's note field works again on the classic and Grimoire UIs, opening as the game's standard text-entry box.
Quests ||| Quests received many fixes. A quest monster killed by a party member counts for the player's quest, a quest's monsters are the player's own and players outside the party cannot see or hit them, and a quest's hours, date and season follow the sky with a daily window recurring every 30 real minutes. Quest-givers are back, every party member's copy of a shared quest picks the same random monsters, and quest items in the world show their name when looked at. Quests that would not start in towns redesigned by Beautiful Villages and Beautiful Cities now start by using the nearest town that has a missing building, quest characters whose building is gone live in a house of their own town, and shared party quests no longer move to another building on every sync. Individual quests fixed include the Bodyguard quest, a knightly order's artifact hunt, the Totem of Tiber Septim, The Qualifying Examination, The Possessed Child, Pothago's Dark Brotherhood contract and, online, Protect an Honored Mage, whose three-hour guard now lasts its full three hours of play with the attack always coming inside it. Raiding Parties ||| Raiding parties, online, were added in 0.1.4605. Kills made after a raid's fight drifts past the edge of town count as long as the player is still on the town's map square, dying, fast travelling, Recall or /unstuck in the middle of a raid no longer stops raiders from coming on return, and the raid card on the map shows the town's real count against its real target of 15 to 25.
Readied Spellcasting Hands ||| With a spell readied the casting hands are raised and ready on screen until the cast, as in jefetienne's Readied Spellcasting Hands 1.3 ported 1:1. Renown ||| Renown is the character's standing and drives level and the Renown bar. It was briefly account-wide, and each character now has its own Renown again, with the level beside a name being that character's. Nobody lost a level, since each character started from its own Renown plus what the account earned while shared. Renown still comes at three quarters of the old rate with the hourly limit shared by all the player's characters, a town defended pays the character that fought for it at that level, and it stays separate from silver while still raising health and magicka, opening sigil stages and founding guilds. The Renown earned reaches the server every few minutes instead of every minute, with the bar still showing what was earned at once and a level-up and the hour's limit still reported within the minute, and once all the Renown an hour allows has been earned nothing more is collected until the next hour.
Reputation ||| Guild and faction reputation recovers over time away even when the clock catches up before faction data loads. The player's reputation travels between towns, so strangers who have heard of their deeds, good or bad, may greet them by them, though hearsay alone never makes anyone a friend or an enemy.
Room Copies ||| A full town, building or dungeon no longer turns the player away but joins another copy of the same place (up to eight), and a party in the open world joins its leader's copy. A sea serpent, a raid, a circle-rite and the card tables stay with the main copy of a place.
Saves ||| Loading after an online rest no longer resets hunger and thirst for free, and a save with a damaged clock loads safely instead of freezing the game. On load, older items get their fixes wherever the save keeps them, including things set out in the house, quest items, a revenant's takings and a sworn revenant's pack, enemies' and guards' gear, and Project Legacy's bequests and remains. The fixes move weapons to the new condition pool, give items made with no condition one, restore the Brigandine, Fur or Mail word on Roleplay & Realism armour, name old Sigil Stones Deadlands Embers, and turn a rolled jewel on an unwearable Wand into an Amulet. A rolled Wand set out in the house becomes an Amulet but still looks like a wand where it stands until picked up. With a window open, F11 (Quick Load) works only from the death screen, since loading from under the pack, a loot window or a sell counter could duplicate what the window held, and any load closes every open window before the save is read.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day. Sethrakul's waters stay open until it dives, so the 08:00 storm no longer locks ships out, and the serpent has a sixth less health, so two ships at average gunnery win every time in about 8.5 minutes though one ship alone still cannot. Inside its waters a gale fills the sails from every direction and ships turn 1.5 times faster, its blows throw a ship half as far and tear half as much canvas, it is 1.6 times thicker so slow cannonballs fired with lag still land, and players on someone else's ship can hurt it with their own bows and attack spells. Ships have a wide invisible buffer and bounce off each other, rewards no longer depend on being near when it dies, a real part of the damage or a quarter of the fight within range earns a hoard, and party members of any ship that dealt heavy damage also get one if they came to the fight.

Other players' horses and wagons more than 400 m away and off-screen wagons, carts, harnesses and tents are not updated or drawn. The Small Cart's boards and stakes run square to its box and the Caravan's side windows are centred, and the Cart slot in the inventory shows the wagon actually driven.
Wagon Parking ||| A wagon left parked on a road in a city, hamlet or village for 10 minutes is come for by a guard, and a town holds four parked wagons, the one parked longest thrown out first when a fifth arrives, each player counting once per town. The guard lifts the wagon with its horses over their head and throws it out of town to land about 40 m past the town's edge, tumbling through the air, seen by everyone nearby. Nothing is lost: the wagon keeps its cargo, paint and horse and can be fetched or summoned, and summoning while the guard still carries it stops the throw. The player is told when the watch throws their wagon, a player riding in the back is set down first, a horse left on its own is never thrown, and offline the 10-minute rule still applies with the clock kept in the save.
Wagon Yards ||| Every city and town has a Stable and a Wagon Yard, open-air yards on the town's open ground clear of homes, guild halls and roads, each with its own keeper named for the town and a signboard. The Stable sells horses and buys them back, and the Wagon Yard sells and buys back the Small Cart, the Open Wagon (9000 gold) and the Caravan (25000), at the same price in every region, with keepers that cannot be stolen from. General Stores no longer sell or buy horses, carts or wagons. Yards keep clear of the boulders, stalls, walls and crop fields the town mods add, can be seen through the windows of nearby houses, are shown on both town maps, are marked on the Overworld with a horseshoe and a wheel, and townsfolk give directions to them.
Water Surface ||| The enhanced water surface covers oceans, rivers and ponds, with a basin, swell, foam and puddles that draw and swim. It is darker and less see-through, the shore is the art's own outline exactly and the water is the art's own, with shallows at 0.80 opacity and ?water=mask for debugging. At night water takes a quarter of a light diffusely and shows each light as a highlight, and it was audited. From high ground the far sea no longer fades into a pale band of fog ending in a hard square edge, but runs on through the haze to the horizon in one piece, with the same colour, light and sun glints as the water near the player and the sky reflected as the near water does, at no extra cost.
Weapon Techniques ||| Magic, Rare and Legendary weapons can drop with a technique, a special attack with its own key, by default the mouse's back side button and rebindable under Controls > Combat, with a Tech button for touch corners and a d-pad placement on controllers, and unarmed fighters find theirs on Gauntlets. There are twelve: Volley and Piercing Shot for bows (the Dwarven Thunderlock can carry Piercing Shot), Leap Strike and Whirlwind for long blades, Shadowstep and Lunge for short blades, Cleave and Headsman's Chop for axes, Ground Slam and Skull Crack for blunt weapons, and Flying Kick and Haymaker for bare hands. Holding the key aims, with a blue ground marker that turns red out of reach, letting go fires and a quick tap fires where the player looks. Every technique costs fatigue and has a cooldown shown on the Enhanced HUD, damage multiplies a normal hit by the item's +N% roll so a miss is still a miss, and techniques are disabled in duels and player-vs-player bouts, never hit another player and need the loot ladder. Each has its own feel, with view movement, shake, sparks and sounds, and a Technique camera motion setting under Settings > Accessibility > Motion scales the movement from Full to Off.
Wordmark ||| The wordmark was reworked to be the logo and then fixed to be type again (BR3). The Daggerfall Online logo is repaired, with the torn rows through "DAGGERFALL" and "The Elder Scrolls" mended and the clipped tops of its letters closed.
World Bosses ||| World bosses were added in 0.1.4255. A world boss's court no longer turns players away when full: a newcomer joins a copy of the court and can fight the same boss for the same rewards beside the players in that copy.
World Map ||| Every port the player has discovered shows an anchor at every zoom, including the whole-bay view, drawn darker with a light outline so it stands out on a busy coast. The Enhanced world map opens on the player as if Find me were pressed, gliding to where they stand with the red cross blinking, while the classic map is unchanged. Online, pointing at open ground shows a card listing the ores its veins can be, what its dungeons' deep veins hold, its kingdom's own ore and whether the ground is witnessed yet. The Find me button is back on the world and town maps.
Yard ||| A yard is the ground around the player's house. Standing on their own lot and pressing Decorate places catalogue pieces outside, priced as inside, wholly on the lot, up to 60 pieces a yard, seen by everyone walking the street. A piece can stand at most 4 m above the ground, so stacking towers is no longer possible, though pieces already higher stay put and can be moved down or removed. Outside, pieces hold nothing and give no light and doors are for doorways only, a piece on the road is refused, and a long piece turned at an angle can no longer be placed through the house's corner. Other players' yard decorations no longer draw across the whole map and tiny yard pieces stop drawing once only a few pixels tall, with a Settings > Other players > Yard decorations option of Off, 75 m, 150 m or 300 m (the default), the player's own yard always showing. Yard pieces standing above the 4 m height limit were removed with no refund, and property lines are shared with neighbours, the ground between two houses split down the middle, so decorating in front of a neighbour's door is no longer possible.
