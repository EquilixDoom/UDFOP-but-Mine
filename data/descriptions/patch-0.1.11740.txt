Alchemy Station ||| An alchemy station brews Daggerfall's twenty potions, at any Alchemist's for 50 gold a brew or at a home station. Ingredients come from the player's Stores. A brew makes one potion, two from Journeyman and three as a Master, and may come out Potent with +25% magnitude for healing, stamina, strength or power potions. The potion maker at an Alchemist's or a Mages Guild hall brews from the player's Stores and pack and earns Provisioning experience like the alchemy station. The stations name the craft they serve, "Provisioning" at the fire and the alchemy station. Online, pressing a home's alchemy station offers Make Potions or Brew on the Stores page, while offline it opens Daggerfall's maker as before.
Animal Volume ||| Animal Volume is an audio setting for how loud dogs, cats, pigs and cows are.
Beautiful Cities ||| Beautiful Cities is a mod pair, both on by default, each with a switch under Mods that applies on the next start. City walls no longer have 3-metre gaps beside their corner towers, and the crate of belongings that comes with a moved house now shows up in the new house when the city uses the Beautiful Cities layout. Bushes no longer grow out of house walls in towns rebuilt by Beautiful Cities and Beautiful Villages, including the one by Penwall Derry's east gate. Seven houses that had a ground-floor corner with no floor were fixed, and the House2 interior doors that opened onto nothing past a thin lip of floor now stay shut and say "This door will not open."
Booked Passage ||| A ship booked passage on is sailed by her master while under way, so the player cannot steer her; if the voyage stops, from an enemy nearby or running aground, the player can handle her until the master takes her up again. Her helm panel shows where she is bound, with the time dial and Leave the helm. Booked ships no longer sail onto land near docks, including the stretches of sea chart near Daggerfall that are really dry land, and finish the last stretch into port. Booked ships and ferries steer around other ships, including other players' boats and the player's own, hold clear of ships moored off a shore when landing, and row off a shore they are stuck beside.
Boss Traits ||| Boss traits give tougher foes special rules: elite foes have one trait and raid leaders have two, and the world bosses (the Oblivion Gate's lord, the Remnant, the Spire's lords and Valkynaz) have one elemental resistance plus Blade-turning, Stoneskinned or Thorned. The traits are Warded (a ward absorbs hits first and grows back if left alone), Flameproof, Frostproof, Venomproof, Stormproof and Spellproof (a quarter of that element's spell damage), Blade-turning and Stoneskinned (half damage from blades and arrows, or from blunt weapons and fists), Curbing (no hit takes more than 5% of its health), Wrathful (hits much harder after 75 seconds) and Thorned (sends part of every weapon hit back). A foe's traits show under its name on the target frame with its ward's strength, hovering over one explains it, and a resisted hit's number is marked Resisted.
Chat Channels ||| Chat channels were added to chat in 0.1.3961. Trade (buy, sell and swap with players anywhere, /trade or /tr) and LFG (find a group or one more for yours, /lfg) are opt-in channels, so the tab bar stays as it was until wanted. A + beside the chat tabs picks channels, one click joining or leaving with joined channels showing a checkmark, and /join trade, /join lfg, /leave trade, /leave lfg and the switches in Settings > Other players > Chat work too, saying /trade or /lfg before joining joins the channel. Region chat is one channel per region again, as it was before region chat was split.
Collision ||| Collision decides what the player walks through. Trees, rocks, stumps, fallen logs, barrels, crates, statues and other solid props are solid on foot and on horseback and the player slides around them, while grass, flowers, ferns and bushes can be walked through. Furniture in a home or yard, and a mountain, camp or town that loads in around the player, appear first so the player can walk out, and turn solid once clear. It also received a performance fix (PERF-COL1), from a field bug reported on Discord. Wagons, caravans and horses no longer catch on shop signs, since only a sign's post stops them while arrows and clicks still meet the board. Walking into a split-rail fence no longer leaves the player stuck on its rail, and monsters do not get stuck on fences either.
Companions ||| Companions are allies who follow the player: a boat's hands taken ashore and sworn revenants. Up to three slots are shared between sworn revenants and any ship's crew ashore. They attack anything hostile, never take damage from the player's own blows, follow through doors by the path the player walked, and arrive and leave through a swirling violet portal. A companion who falls is carried off to recover for 8 hours, and loyalty matters, since a neglected one deserts and a disloyal Unhinged, Craven or Brutal one may turn on the player. A pause-menu Stats page shows who is At your side or Away, with Send away, Call and Release, and each companion's pack has a weight limit by strength. Companions do not follow the player into the Burning Court, and a companion through the carrack's middle house leads down to her gun deck. A companion standing between the player and a body, loot pile, ship's helm, door or container within reach no longer takes the click, which uses that object, while with nothing usable behind them clicking still opens their pack, and the on-screen label shows which one a click will use. With Morrowind data attached, crew companions and sworn revenants keep their own race, face and clothes through every door, dungeon and reload, and a revenant is the same person every time it returns. A Companion Idle Volume setting in the Audio tab sets how loud companions moan and cry out while idle, and 0% silences them.
Copy to Offline ||| Copying a character to offline moves the world's timers onto the character's calendar, and the copy gets raids of its own. Quest time limits, journal dates, campfires and shop stock keep their distance. A character made with Copy to offline shows "Copied from online: offline only" on its tile instead of a Bring online button that did nothing, since a copy cannot come back online.
Crafting ||| Crafting received fixes to how crafts settle. A craft whose answer was lost settles on its own, a slow answer still pays the smith or furnisher when it lands, a station's fee the purse cannot cover is shown, and a recipe cannot be changed while the heat or plane is under way. A maker's mark is part of the piece's signed record, and a Masterwork piece, or any furniture a Master Joiner makes, carries its maker's name. Refining for a craft costs no extra station fee. When short of something, the box names the raw good needed (for example "short of Iron x2"), not the ingot, and a refused refining step is named in the message. Crafted Superior and Masterwork pieces can roll the special lines loot can, such as focus, cast speed, potency and wellspring on jewelry and the weapon and armour equivalents, while pieces crafted before stay as they were. Every crafting profession now climbs at Smithing's pace, with a first-time bonus set for each profession (500 experience in Smithing, 511 in Outfitting, 1,823 in Building and 3,155 in Provisioning) that every station shows. A piece sewn from cloth the player made earns that bonus, while cloth bought from the Weavers does not.
Crashes ||| A crash when the game was minimised or put in the background while a world was loading was fixed. On the desktop app, crash.log also records the last ten minutes of graphics work before the game stops, and when the graphics process itself crashes it records the moment the graphics were lost in that crash's own entry and marks when they came back, so a minute with no stutter reads hitch=0.
Crime ||| A crime is now charged once. Running from one group of guards and meeting another no longer charges it twice, and a crime committed while fleeing the watch can no longer reach the court uncharged and raise the player's standing with its sentence. Pleading guilty no longer loops: the plea waits until the Crime tutorial page on top of it has been read, so each plea is answered once and the fine is taken and the sentence halved only once. Anything stolen is marked stolen, including a robbed caravan's goods and the hold of a lawful ship boarded or sunk, so a merchant still buys it but never puts it back on the shelf. When the player is found guilty (jail, a fine or banishment) the guards take every stolen item they carry and say how many, while winning the case or leaving through the Thieves Guild keeps them, the player's own items put into someone else's cupboard and taken back are not marked stolen, and paying a fine or doing a penance restores reputation with the people of the kingdom too. A hostile traveller killed by someone else is no longer counted as the player's murder.
Death Screen ||| The death screen was fixed and audited, including resolving the online screen's rise and ensuring quick-loading (F11) after dying in town no longer leaves the death screen up. It shows the amount of gold lost and says Rise instead of "End the journey". It names any letters of credit lost and what they were worth, and when the character's trading is frozen it says that what was dropped was lost outright. Closing the game on the death screen no longer skips the two-minute wait: coming back while still down lies the player down again for the time that is left.
Decorate ||| Decorate is the house furnishing mode. Doors are in its catalogue, and Daggerfall's interior doors can be hung in any open doorway, with every free doorway marked green and the one looked at gold. A door hangs only where it can close, stays open, shut or locked as left, can be moved, and blocks the way while shut, and it parts the rooms either side, making two rooms for the room tabs and renting. In the In this room and In this yard views, Turn left and Turn right turn a placed piece 15 degrees where it stands, at once and for free, and a flat picture flips to face the other way. A bed placed in a home, caravan or ship's cabin can be slept in, and a placed fire (bowl of fire, campfire, brazier or one of Daggerfall's fireplaces) is a real fire that warms, dries and lets the player rest and cook by it. A Cooking station makes any piece, such as a stove or cauldron, the home's fire, and online using it opens The Fire on the Stores page.
Decorator ||| The decorator offers furnishings for homes. With the town mods on it adds 297 pieces such as beds and rugs, changing within a second when a town mod is turned on or off. It also offers what Daggerfall stands in its dungeons (thrones, cages, coffins, statues, chains, braziers) and a yard offers Daggerfall's street pieces and its own town's climate trees and plants, each with its own picture. People placed with the decorator (the Vendors list) can no longer be resized, with Smaller and Bigger gone for them. Those placed earlier at another size now stand at their normal size, moving one sets it to normal, an enlarged one gives back half the price difference and a shrunk one costs nothing to move. Decorations set against a wall sit in front of it instead of sinking into it, at any size and with the grid on, and the grid snaps along the wall. The furniture catalogue, the pieces in the room, the built-in pieces and the player's own things are laid out as picture tiles showing each piece's 3D model, name, price and badges for what it does, with a Pictures / List switch bringing the old lines back. Every tab, type, size and filter has its own icon, filters sit under named rows and fold behind one Filters button on phones, a placed piece's buttons are grouped by what they do with the costly Remove standing alone, and the placing bar is icon buttons with a Keys button. Tab opens and closes the catalogue, whose types are icons, with starred favourites and a Hotbar button. Placing, moving, copying, removing and renaming wait until Confirm is pressed, leaving with changes open asks first, and a placed piece can be given a name. Each chest and station has a Who may use setting, and discarding changes no longer puts items taken from a chest back into it.
Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, the app has its own icon, and it finds and installs updates again. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. Downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app (to be dragged to the Bin once open), and on Windows pinned taskbar or Start menu entries may need repinning under the new name. It keeps the account and settings files in memory instead of reading them from disk every frame, as the browser does, giving smoother frames in a town. The app updates the game one file at a time instead of downloading about 130 MB and replacing the whole game folder, the full installer running only when the app itself changes or a file-by-file update fails, on Windows, macOS, Linux and the portable build. A ready update now runs on the next start instead of being switched in under the running game by a cancelled page change.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top. A headstone the party has searched counts as searched for the whole party and the game says who searched it, coffins a stranger opened in tombs are the player's to search (including standing right next to one), and party members no longer vanish for a few seconds when the player goes through a tomb door. Bodies and their loot no longer return on re-entering a dungeon while another player there has the game in the background, since the room follows whoever is actually playing, and a room left stuck by a background player's game closing without saying goodbye now moves on to whoever is playing. Containers and bodies that wrongly said "Something in here is from a newer version of the game" can be looted again, though after a game update a container looted a few minutes before may be full once more.
Dwarven Pistolet ||| The Dwarven Pistolet is a one-handed Dwemer flintlock that fires the Thunderlock's Dwemer Pellets for 4-15 damage, using Archery, weighing 2.5 kg and worth 260 gold. With one in each hand the pistols fire in turn, each reloading on its own, the off-hand shot hitting softer and leaning on Agility. It has its own look in first person, on the paper doll and in inventory lists, a modelled 3D gun on Morrowind models, and its own stance on Morrowind bodies: low with the muzzle down at rest, the arm out on the eye line when aiming. It is never sold in shops and no longer drops from any enemy, chest or boss, though ones already owned work as before; Magic and Rare guns no longer repeat their metal in the name. It runs the code of the prototype its feel was tuned in, with a few differences kept on purpose (the room's camera shake and light on the pistol, Weapon Widget switches, the holster and left-handed play). A pair fires the way it should: the second pistol fires the moment of the pull even while the first is mid-shot, each shot belongs to the pistol that fired it, a reloading pistol holds still and the one fired last is drawn in front. A one-handed weapon can be held in one hand with a pistol in the other, the two taking turns, the pistol taking twice as long to reload and the blade fighting on when no Pellets are left. Since 0.1.11168 the Pistolet and the Thunderlock drop only from the Sky-Ketch's hold, with a Gilded version of each. The Pistolets are drawn in their own colours, sit lower in the hands, take the room's light, and the forearm no longer cuts off at the edge of its picture.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are no longer sold in shops. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang. On the Morrowind body it is held like a rifle, the right hand on its pistol grip and the left supporting the forend, carried at rest at a low ready with the muzzle down and across toward the left foot and coming up to a level ready stance while shooting, for a few seconds after and while foes are hurting the player, in first and third person, the Hourlock held the same way. In the Morrowind first-person view the aimed gun sits below the middle of the screen instead of riding over the crosshair, with the left hand still on its forend. Since 0.1.11168 the Thunderlock drops only from the Sky-Ketch's hold, along with its Gilded version, Volendrung's Echo. It has a new two-handed first-person sprite and inventory picture, sits smaller and lower in first person, and fires about 3% slower, one shot every 2.2 seconds.
Dwemer Pellets ||| Dwemer Pellets are the ammunition of the Thunderlock and the Pistolet. They are no longer sold in shops: they are a rare find in loot even with Loot rarity turned off, every share of the Sky-Ketch's hold pays 20 to 40 of them, and every construct brought down on the Sky-Ketch drops a pile for each player aboard, Spheres, Sentries, Wardens, the Watch-Captain and the Tonal Warden always and vent spiders sometimes.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor. At a Redoubt's rest the company's choices (the next circle's decrees, Dagon's Bargain, the respite and the Ready vote) stay open for 30 seconds.
Enchanting ||| Enchanting is a crafting profession. The item maker costs 10% less from Journeyman and 20% less as a Master, and disenchanting a piece gives XP by its tier, a quarter for pieces far below the player's rank. A Masterwork piece can be enchanted further and the item maker takes any crafted piece with its enchantment, though a piece's points cannot exceed what its recipe makes and Aetheric pieces such as Ruhn's Regalia and the raid sets are refused. An enchanted jewel that was a Wand and became an Amulet on loading from a guild vault or market listing no longer gets the character flagged by the anti-cheat check. Carrying capacity no longer turns up on most Rare pieces, since every kind of line now comes up only as often as its share. Regens Health: In Darkness heals 1 health every 4 magic rounds at night, indoors and in dungeons.
Enhanced Sounds ||| Enhanced sounds are controlled by one Enhanced sounds switch. A limiter sits on the final output so heavy blows, roars and the gate music cannot push the mix past full volume, and everyday sound is unchanged. In the open country it adds surf on the shore, crows, owls, distant wolves and frogs depending on the region and time of day, murmuring towns by day, smiths hammering at their anvils and noisy taverns in the evening.
Event Board ||| The World Events board sits at the left of the screen, under the chat and above the buffs, hotbar and quick slots, following the HUD scale, and shows Dagon's Breach, the sea serpent, the Abyss Dungeon, fallen stars and town raids instead of the chat. Each event shows its time left or time until it starts, how far away it is, which way to go and where it is, and a raid under way shows how many raiders are down. It lists only events the player can still join, up to four on a tall screen and three on a smaller one, the rest counted with a pointer to the Timers window. When an event is about to start, and again when it begins, its news opens at the top of the board for a few seconds, with the event's own messages added; every gate, serpent, Abyss Dungeon and fallen star is announced to everyone, a raid within its region or 25 km of it, and every event message is saved in the journal. With no room the news shows on the message line instead, and under Enhanced Plus the board can be moved or hidden in the HUD editor. It is turned off under Settings, Interface, Messages & prompts, Event board, which sends event messages back to the chat and raid messages back to the message line. Settings > Interface (or the Features page) has a toggle for each kind of event: Oblivion Gates, the Sea Serpent, the Abyss Dungeon, the Starfall, raiding parties and festivals. A kind turned off disappears from the board and its news cards and its messages go quietly to the journal, while the Timers window still lists everything. A live raid shows its kind in its title and how its objective stands.
Firmament ||| The Firmament is a passive tree for everything done online, opened from the Firmament button on the Stats page. The player chooses one of seven birthsigns, then spends Stars on combat stars and Craft points on the life ring (gathering, crafting, trading, hunting and sailing), earning 2 Stars for every Renown level and 1 Craft point for every 8 profession ranks. Changes are free to plan until confirmed, taking stars back is free below Renown 10 and then costs 10 silver per star, and a full reset costs 10,000 gold. The stars never affect hits or spells against other players and switch off in duels and while mentoring. Every star adds something: small stars and roads give their bonus one for one as a percent, and an attribute bonus counts on a stat already at 100 (with the sky's full +15 a 100 Strength reads 115, offline the cap is 100). It works in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire and on phones, and its text is English for now. Every weapon family has two Firmament notables that raise its skill, and Axe and Blunt get a third; Blade Dancer, Iron Knuckles, Headsman and Bonebreaker keep every stat and gain the skill on top, and the Ritual's Battlemage's Focus is now Templar's Mace, with no star moved so shared build codes still load. Fist stars now apply to bare-handed blows. It is drawn inside the pause menu's Growth tab and can go full screen with one press, over the whole screen on a phone. The summary panel says whether the stars are active and why not (still loading, no birthsign accepted, mentoring a lower-level party, in a duel or Arena fight, or another tab or device playing online), and names a birthsign accepted in another window, tab or device. Stars that failed to load are retried until they do.
Gathering ||| Gathering covers herb patches, ore veins, boulders, trees, bodies and fishing water. E acts on one only when it is looked at, in reach and unobstructed, and a node that cannot yet be worked says why on its row (tool, rank or daily limit). Actions use the loot list, picked by clicking, activating or tapping, with a small list window on the Classic skin and touch screens. The minigames are drawn on and around the crosshair with one short hint that fades, progress as diamonds and a sound for every strike, find, slip, splash and tug. Without a Materials Bag everything gathered goes in the pack even over its weight, and the message says so. A herb patch, vein, boulder or tree goes the moment the player finishes working it instead of waiting for the server to answer, coming back if the server refuses the harvest, and goods, XP and rank-ups arrive faster after each harvest. Gathering past the Veil works on the islands' herbs, trees, ore veins and boulders as in the Bay, each spot giving its full finds only once three players with accounts at least a week old have gathered there, and fishing in the Eltheric Ocean works. Every gathering act has its own sounds (felling, mining, herbs and the Basket, skinning, net fishing, goods going into the bag and a chime for a clean finish or a new rank), sounding the moment the tool hits and from where it happens, and on the Enhanced skin each axe or pick blow dips the view and hands slightly, following Technique camera motion. Other players see and hear the gathering, covered by their Attack sounds switch. The sounds are part of Enhanced sounds, and with it off or on the Classic skin Daggerfall's own sounds play, timed to the blows. The loot panel and its tooltip no longer cover skinning, or any other gathering minigame, while it plays. Sparkles on gathering trees cover the whole tree and grow with it.
Gilded Weapons ||| The Dragonfire Oath is a Daedric Dai-Katana that can drop from the Warden of the Burning Gate about one fight in 250, giving +40% damage, +15 Strength, +30 Long Blade and +15 Endurance; its Dragonfire makes a blow that lands on a Daedra burn it again for another third of the hit and burn every other Daedra within 3 m for the same. The Sunken Sovereign is an Ebony Saber that can drop from the Old Coil about one kill in 250 for a ship that did damage, giving +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Long Blade and 10% life leech; its Undertow adds +10% damage to each blow on the same foe up to +50%, fading after 6 seconds without a hit, and heals the player for 10% when it kills. Both are shown in the Collections codex with their own hints. Gilded drops are rarer everywhere: the Hourlock is 1 in 100, and the Dragonfire Oath and the Sunken Sovereign are 1 in 250 each.
Guild Hall ||| A guild hall is a guild-owned house bought by the guildmaster with treasury funds, featuring member chests, crafting, resting, customizable painting and yard furnishings, and a hall's roll as the first book on its shelf detailing its chapter's standing, Master, and officers. Windows inside buildings are now see-through, town boards fill in once three players have visited guild halls, and in rooms with many lamps, stationary people cast shadows from every lamp rather than just the nearest twelve. Online, if the player does not yet qualify for the next rank, the guild popup lists what is missing: the rank's name ("Next rank: Swordsman"), reputation against what is needed ("Reputation 14 of 20"), the guild-skill level the player is short of with their best guild skill named, a second guild skill at the lower bar and the guild's own skills by name. It does not appear when nothing is missing or at the top rank, and it shows on the enhanced UI skin and in all 13 supported languages. At rank 7 the hall says the next rank is a chapter chair (a council chair or the Master's) won by Merit at each Sunday's Turning, asking reputation 80, 14 real days in the guild and an account 7 real days old, and a service only a chair opens (the Mages Guild's Teleport, Zenithar's summoning) says so at any rank. The new Guild Chapters tutorial page opens when the rank is held at 7. The hall and the Standing page show what the realm still owes the player (already earned, arriving at most 15 a real day) and warn before a review puts the player on probation or lowers their rank, with when and why, and on probation they are told the stakes: below -10 expelled at the review, below 0 it runs 28 more days, at 0 or more it ends, and online earning the next rank ends it sooner. The rank text scrolls on its own so Talk, Service and Reforge stay on screen. Expelled or turned away for standing, the player sees at the hall their standing, the standing it takes members at, how many quests bring them back there and how fast it recovers on its own, and the hall names skills the guild also wants and says that its halls in other regions still have work when one is shut for the Season; the classic look's refusal says the same. A hall holds 300 pieces: the keeper pays for what is placed, and what comes back goes to the guild treasury.
Hired Traders ||| The Hired trader station (25,000 gold), made from a Vendors figure, sells the player's pieces for 30 days. Visitors buy from the trader in the player's house, the Vendors tab lists the region's traders, and Set waypoint marks a trader's town with a gold coin on both maps and points the compass. Hired-trader patrons pay less for worn pieces, the same way shops do. Buying from another player's trader no longer sends the buyer to the title screen when the game is slow to answer, and the piece from an interrupted purchase arrives in the pack the next time a trader's stall or a town Notice Board's Market tab is opened.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A horse walks by default and holding Run (Shift unless rebound) gallops it, as on foot. The gallop is as fast as before and spends the horse's breath, and a winded or starving horse walks even with Run held until it can gallop again. Holding Run gallops it anywhere, in town or out, as long as it has the breath, online too, though riding into someone at a gallop tramples them, which is a crime and brings the guards. A walking horse has a slower clop, a slower head nod in first person and a trot on the 3D horse, and the saddle panel says Walking. Horse Volume (Options > Audio > Volume) sets how loud horses are, and 0% silences them. Horses snort, blow and breathe, whinny when mounted, breathe hard on a long gallop and spook when enemies are near, and their hooves sound different on grass, in snow and in shallow water while roads keep the classic clop. After a login inside a dungeon the horse, and a wagon hitched to it, come with the player to the nearest temple, town or graveyard.

The jeweller's bench takes Chipped, Flawed, Flawless and Perfect gems, each grade adding its own share of enchantment points.

Mining ||| Mining is a gathering profession utilizing wilderness ore veins, dungeon walls, and boulder quarrying with Pick-Axes and glint strikes, featuring region-specific ores and progression tied to confirmed land and player gathering activity. Naval Combat ||| Naval combat lets boats with guns fight, and needs Come Sail Away. The Large Boat carries swivel guns, the Small Ship and the Carrack carry long guns with bow chain-shot chasers and stern fire barrels, and the Large Galley carries long guns along her sides and great guns over her bow. At the helm the player looks to a side and holds Attack, then lets go to fire the broadside, looking higher to fire further, and holds Crouch to brace behind the rail for less damage but guns that hold fire. Each side reloads on its own, with a thin crew slower and a boat with no crew slowest, and the player's gunners lose accuracy as crew are killed. Cannon balls hole the hull, cut sails and kill crew, with hits below the waterline doing most damage, and a ship can catch fire. Features, Naval Combat has settings for ship count, pirates boarding, raiders' plunder and the broadside camera, and a series of changes made busy bays run much smoother. Gun smoke drifts with the wind and each cannonball lands with a tall spout of water scaled up with distance, a ball passing close whistles and one landing beside the ship throws water over the rail and rocks the deck, the side a shot came from flashes on the helm plate, a ship that sinks while on fire may blow its powder magazine seen by every player, and a badly damaged ship has smoke rising along the deck. The target card shows the enemy's crew and the broadside's tally says how many crew went down, each battery shows its reload seconds, and within 700 m of a hostile ship a journey runs at normal speed with the Overworld view closing near one; online, sea combat near another player always runs in real time. Other players see the same fight, chain shot and grapeshot kill crew on ships another player controls, and the aiming arcs stay visible in a crowded fight. Aiming the bow cannons moves the camera out ahead of the bow, and aiming the stern fire barrels moves it over the stern with the barrel's drop and landing spot drawn.

Oblivion Gates ||| Oblivion Gates are nightly raid events featuring a handcrafted model of cracked basalt and fire with multi-court Warden boss fights, damage charts, and rewards, alongside tavern card games like Iliac Hand played against regulars or online players for stakes and collection cards. Smithing ||| Smithing is a crafting profession at an Anvil section beside the Forge on the Stores page, done in a Weaponsmith's or Armorer's (50 gold a craft) or a home with a Forge station. Weapons, plate and shields are made in every metal from Iron to Warforged Steel, chain armour is made from Steel for three quarters of plate's ingots, and tools (Wood-Axe, Pick-Axe, Sickle, Spade) are made from Iron. Craft puts the ingot in the fire and the player strikes three times while its glow is in the band, which widens with Strength and Agility, three in the band being a clean craft one quality step better and Quick craft skipping the heat. Pieces are Crude, Standard, Fine, Superior or Masterwork, with a clean heat, specialisation and Warforged Steel ingot each adding a step. Crude wears out sooner, Fine lasts longer and is lighter, Superior and Masterwork also come enchanted (Magic and Rare), and a Masterwork carries its maker's name. A tool's quality is its life, 37 uses Crude up to 65. A craft gives 20 XP a tier of its metal plus 500 the first time that recipe is made, a quarter for recipes well below rank, and every piece shows its quality and maker with a provenance record kept by the server. At an anvil (Smithing) or a loom (Outfitting), a piece can be tempered one quality step better, Crude to Standard, Standard to Fine, Fine to Superior. To temper or reforge a worn piece, take it off first. Daedric Ingots can be smelted at any forge from an Ebony Ingot, a Daedra's Heart and a Deadlands Ember, which opens every Daedric weapon, armour piece and repair kit to smiths.
Sound Volume ||| Moving the Sound Volume setting changes sounds that are already playing (rain, wind, torches, a horse) straight away, not at the next new sound.
Spawned Dungeons ||| Spawned dungeons are ported and expire, 2 in-game days when cleared and 7 in general. They are announced on their own pixel in metres, appear only where the ground is dry land above the beach line and no longer on beaches or at sea, and pixels that once held a shoreline spawn are now empty land or sea for everyone. Random dungeons in the online wilderness are a quarter fewer; one that remains is the same place as before. Everyone stands on the same ground around one, whether the dungeon is still around or has expired, and an expired one leaves its level clearing and ground tiles until it comes back.
Specialisation Reset ||| An arcane or martial specialisation can be given up for 200,000 gold each: in the Growth tab's Specialisations guide the player opens the chosen path and presses Reset, and a confirmation shows the price, what is lost and which paths can be picked afterwards. Mastered skills stay mastered, so any path the masteries unlock, including the same one, can be chosen, and a bought signature spell stays in the spellbook as an ordinary spell. Online the price is paid from coins, then letters of credit, then the bank account, and offline from coins or letters of credit. If no other path of that kind could ever open, the Reset button explains why instead of charging, and the specialisation choice boxes state what a later reset costs. A newly chosen specialisation can be tried for a day, during which changing it is free and immediate, and a paid change (1,000 silver) takes effect at once and locks the next paid change for 7 days.
Spells ||| Ending the player's own spells was fixed, the aim hint always shows when a spell waits for a target, and a party member healing the player no longer repeats the cast message on every cast. Classic spellcasting runs at the same pace as the Morrowind animation, with the spell leaving the hands at half a second, and a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, so clicking or recasting during it does nothing and costs nothing. Casting speed scales with the Speed attribute, up to 25% faster at high Speed and 25% slower at low. Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Re-casting an effect already on the player or a target resets it to that cast's full duration instead of adding the new duration on top, and a shorter re-cast never cuts a longer one short. This covers buffs and debuffs alike, including Shield, Fortify, Regenerate, Levitate, Light, Detect, the resistances, Paralyze, Silence, Continuous Damage and Soul Trap, and re-casting Regenerate or Continuous Damage on its last round still gives the full ticks. Classic spellcasting runs at the pace of the Morrowind animation, a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, casting speed scales with the Speed attribute (up to 25% faster at high Speed, 25% slower at low), and Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Online, a Destruction mastered past 100 adds to Damage Health and Continuous Damage spells, 1% for each point of its effective value past 100 and +40% at 200, while gear that raises Destruction still makes spells cheaper. Identify's window asks for what the cast actually spent, so a spell-cost discount no longer leaves a caster with a small magicka pool unable to identify.
Staff Events ||| Staff events are a staff tool for running a monster event on a city or area, with a hidden kill meter and a yellow mini boss. The mini boss always spawns and a boss appears each time the meter fills, every player sees the mini boss's real health, and it stands at the biggest group. Packs keep coming while the boss waits, more and faster with more players, and the reward is earned by kills alone.
Staff Panel ||| The Staff Panel is a tool for staff running the online game, not something players see. Its server side has cases, desk, players, realm, economy, census and status pages and a chat staff menu, and its relay half provides invisible follow, hidden staff, freeze, Message on screen and staff feeds (Network bursts, Monster watch and Bot check); version 0.2.3 added Set bank, a Banned tab, a Hide toggle, Me/Red chat and an in-game staff view. Following a player takes the camera into dungeons with them, shows the players around them as bodies and, from the chat menu, opens that player's card in the Staff Panel; monsters ignore hidden staff, and crowded player tags merge into one "N players here" chip. The staff pages answer in fewer database round trips. It can destroy every flagged copy of an item on a character in one act, remove extra realm-locked copies and unlock the ones a player keeps, leaves banned and trade-held gold out of the economy page, shows the total game worth, and counts a hidden staff member in no online total.
The Shattered Hour ||| The islands hang in the void on jagged rock spires with half-buried gears and chains swaying on the clock's tick, the way back looks into the hall you came from in dark coursed stone lit gold from the front, the arena tells you the fight through its floor, the Stomp's ring rises as a low wall of brass light you can see to jump, the Remnant shows what it's about to do, and when it falls its heart tears out and rises before going dark while the dial breaks free and rolls away. A party member standing at a fallen player's body has 60 seconds to raise them before the death counts for good.
Travel Map ||| The travel map is a parchment held in the player's hands, drawn as a held sprite with a Features switch, a Morrowind map on every sheet, readable glyphs and a fourth painting in the hands. A default trip is walked, the map was audited several times, and players riding another's boat are marked on the map where their boat is. The Overworld travel view no longer leaves 1 to 1.7 GB of extra memory behind after a journey, and it draws faster while a route is on screen. Clicking near a town walks around it and clicking its middle goes in, and with "travel only to places" on, clicking anywhere on a town travels to it.
Tutorials ||| New characters get a short welcome tour covering moving and climbing, using things, the HUD, and the menu and saving, replacing the old tutorial, and any key a tutorial mentions is the key currently bound. The first time the player reaches something new a short note explains it, with about 56 systems having one from the inventory and maps to shops, banks, guilds, card tables, horses and wagons, boats, the arena, the Abyss Dungeon, family, revenants, gathering, chat, parties, duels and the open zone. Wagon Parking ||| A wagon left parked on a road in a city, hamlet or village for 10 minutes is come for by a guard, and a town holds four parked wagons, the one parked longest thrown out first when a fifth arrives, each player counting once per town. The guard lifts the wagon with its horses over their head and throws it out of town to land about 40 m past the town's edge, tumbling through the air, seen by everyone nearby. Nothing is lost: the wagon keeps its cargo, paint and horse and can be fetched or summoned, and summoning while the guard still carries it stops the throw. Decorations in Daggerfall City's eastern and northern blocks stay after a restart, a town's yards hold up to 6,500 pieces between them, and the decorator asks the player to remove one of their own when a town is full. Other players' yards appear a few at a time, so arriving in a decorated city no longer stalls the game.
