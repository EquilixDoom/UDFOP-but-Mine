Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player. A username can no longer be an email address: a player who signed up with one still signs in with it but other players see a generated name. After a server update players no longer all reconnect at once, each game waiting a moment (longer the more players are online), sign-in token requests and the account, realm, heartbeat and profession reads are answered cheaply from read copies of the database, the letterbox, Notice Board count and play time share one request, and when many players sign in at once they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error. Most menus that talk to the server (the Guild tab, the market, the Seat tab, letters, the account card and signing in) load much faster, since the account server now runs next to its database instead of across the Pacific, and joining the world, the chat's player list and the friends list come up sooner after signing in. When the servers are overloaded the game holds off on loading homes, yards, seats, traders, guild invitations, Marks and the save list and asks again later, waiting longer the longer the overload lasts, while actions such as buying, crafting, saving and claiming still go through, and the servers also turn those requests away for a moment to recover. Town homes, rooms and decorations, gathering nodes, traders, Marks, guild invites, lineages, unions, cloud saves, the Arena board and the town seats list now load from read copies of the database instead of the one main database.
Arcane Specialisations ||| Mastering two schools of magic allows choosing a specialisation, one of fifteen, one for every pair of schools: Battlemage, Trickster, Riftwalker, Warden, Wardwright, Shadowcaster, Spellbreaker, Bloodmage, Elementalist, Seer, Lightbringer, Enchanter, Necromancer, Soulbinder and Hierophant. The choice is permanent like a mastery. Each has its own passive and a signature spell that the Mages Guild sells only to its own specialists and that does something extra when they cast it, priced and trained by the specialisation's two schools. The player is asked once when two schools are first mastered and can choose any time afterwards from the Specialisation card on the Master Skills page (enhanced) or the S row on the skill pages (classic). Chain Lightning and Rift Lance never arc onto allies, calmed foes or through walls, signature spell names show in the player's language, and specialisations work wherever Master Skills is on but do nothing in duels and other refereed fights or against other players.
Court Writs ||| Court writs are tasks posted by a region's Court, shown on the Notice Board's Work tab and paying Marks and Renown. Writs ask for herbs, metal, stone, logs and Pine Logs in some regions. A region's Court posts them once a player registered for a week has gathered there, and Take fills a writ whole from the player's Stores, up to 3 a day. A writ's Renown goes to the character who delivers it, and the day's first writ always asks for the highest tier on offer. The Work tab shows "Court writs today" at the top instead of under the last card. A writ's card tells how many of the material the player holds that it cannot take and why, since only gathered or silver-bought materials fill writs.
Crafting ||| Crafting received fixes to how crafts settle. A craft whose answer was lost settles on its own, a slow answer still pays the smith or furnisher when it lands, a station's fee the purse cannot cover is shown, and a recipe cannot be changed while the heat or plane is under way. A maker's mark is part of the piece's signed record, and a Masterwork piece, or any furniture a Master Joiner makes, carries its maker's name. Refining for a craft costs no extra station fee. When short of something, the box names the raw good needed (for example "short of Iron x2"), not the ingot, and a refused refining step is named in the message. Crafted Superior and Masterwork pieces can roll the special lines loot can, such as focus, cast speed, potency and wellspring on jewelry and the weapon and armour equivalents, while pieces crafted before stay as they were.
DFO Grimoire ||| DFO Grimoire is a fourth look under Main menu > Overhauls > UI, dressing everything Enhanced Plus does in GrimoireUI's parchment, wood, red-and-gold presses, glass vials and lettering. The vitals are three vials, upright in the corner in Daggerfall's order or lying in a row under the hotbar, and the card offers Pages, Backdrop, Vitals, and Spellbook choices. The front door stands on a detailed desk, rarity/set/sigil names are inked deeper, parchment text is readable, stray ruled lines are removed, health bars scale with resolution, and settings open as scrolls. New features include a Text font setting under Overhauls > UI with five choices (Game, Sans, Monospace, Dyslexic, My font), distinct channel colours with colour squares and toggle options, player name colours, language channels in 15 languages with `/lang` and `/lc` commands, gold-highlighted `@mentions` with chimes, unread count seals on Grimoire tabs, and various UI/inventory/scry fixes. Its Tab dial uses Daggerfall's own icons, the character's face for Skills, the HUD star for Magic, the sack for Inventory and the scroll for Map, and the old paintings return under Settings > Overhauls > DFO Grimoire > Tab dial. Quick loot rarity marks show in it as well as in Enhanced Plus. Under the DFO Grimoire dress every mark on the card tables shows in the dress's own colours, a chosen, hovered or keyboard-selected tile is marked on every page that uses tiles, and buttons that cost something (Concede, Scuttle, Steal and the like) change when hovered or reached with the keyboard.
Decorator ||| The decorator offers furnishings for homes. With the town mods on it adds 297 pieces such as beds and rugs, changing within a second when a town mod is turned on or off. It also offers what Daggerfall stands in its dungeons (thrones, cages, coffins, statues, chains, braziers) and a yard offers Daggerfall's street pieces and its own town's climate trees and plants, each with its own picture. People placed with the decorator (the Vendors list) can no longer be resized, with Smaller and Bigger gone for them. Those placed earlier at another size now stand at their normal size, moving one sets it to normal, an enlarged one gives back half the price difference and a shrunk one costs nothing to move. Decorations set against a wall sit in front of it instead of sinking into it, at any size and with the grid on, and the grid snaps along the wall. The furniture catalogue, the pieces in the room, the built-in pieces and the player's own things are laid out as picture tiles showing each piece's 3D model, name, price and badges for what it does, with a Pictures / List switch bringing the old lines back. Every tab, type, size and filter has its own icon, filters sit under named rows and fold behind one Filters button on phones, a placed piece's buttons are grouped by what they do with the costly Remove standing alone, and the placing bar is icon buttons with a Keys button.
Door Griefing ||| Door griefing was stopped. Foes no longer stand in doorways, so a crowd cannot seal a building or dungeon door, clicking a door works with a peaceful guard in front of it, and the indoor watch spawns inside the threshold, spread out. A door another player locked with a Lock spell is no longer locked for the player, and shop and inn doors in Daggerfall that trapped players inside (Lock Level 40+) open again, including copies a save had already kept.
Etheric Sea ||| Sailing west of the Bay's mouth on the Abecean asks whether to sail into the open sea, leading to Veilhaven three days later, while sailing back asks the way home with warnings if enemies/players are close. Past the Veil lies an endless sea of isles (port isles with towns/quays, ruins with dungeons, wild isles with goods, and bare rocks), growing deadlier and richer further west through Shallows, Reaches, Deep Aether, and Abyss. No crown's law reaches past the Veil (no navy, crimes aren't counted, corsairs sail under sea names), ether-amber, starglass, and voidsilk are traded, wild isles float casks and refill in seven days, six legendary ships haunt the depths with purses, and the travel map key opens a chart of sighted isles. The Veil blocks magic (Recall, teleports, portal stones, party travel, Fleet ships) and falling wakes players at the nearest port isle. The light is cold and violet, the sea west of the Bay is water again, and while classic keeps Daggerfall's sea, enhanced/online play features this crossing. After crossing the Veil a ship too heavily loaded to move says so ("You're going to need a bigger boat"), and everything carried, gold and wagon included, counts against a ship's hold. The sea past the Veil is now called the Eltheric Ocean, its proper name, across the chart, harbourmasters, the crossing, the captain's log and the legends. The player can rise into the Overworld while sailing it, as in the Iliac Bay, by pressing Overworld (O) on the chart, then click the sea to sail there or a charted port or ruin to travel to it; enemy bands roam its isles and, with Naval Combat on, the Eltheric's own corsairs sail its open sea, chasing the player and slowing a journey as they close in, visible to other players online. The water has a real seabed down to about two hundred metres, so the player can swim, dive and surface there with seafloor plants, fish, creatures and sunken loot, though the ocean holes still appear only in the Iliac Bay. Your Ship can no longer be boarded from past the Veil.
Fatigue ||| Standing still on the ground no longer tires the player. Running out of fatigue with enemies nearby no longer kills the player outright at full health: each time they hit empty they lose a tenth of their health and see "You are too exhausted to fight - get clear and rest!".
Gathering ||| Gathering covers herb patches, ore veins, boulders, trees, bodies and fishing water. E acts on one only when it is looked at, in reach and unobstructed, and a node that cannot yet be worked says why on its row (tool, rank or daily limit). Actions use the loot list, picked by clicking, activating or tapping, with a small list window on the Classic skin and touch screens. The minigames are drawn on and around the crosshair with one short hint that fades, progress as diamonds and a sound for every strike, find, slip, splash and tug. Without a Materials Bag everything gathered goes in the pack even over its weight, and the message says so. A herb patch, vein, boulder or tree goes the moment the player finishes working it instead of waiting for the server to answer, coming back if the server refuses the harvest, and goods, XP and rank-ups arrive faster after each harvest. Gathering past the Veil works on the islands' herbs, trees, ore veins and boulders as in the Bay, each spot giving its full finds only once three players with accounts at least a week old have gathered there, and fishing in the Eltheric Ocean works. Every gathering act has its own sounds (felling, mining, herbs and the Basket, skinning, net fishing, goods going into the bag and a chime for a clean finish or a new rank), sounding the moment the tool hits and from where it happens, and on the Enhanced skin each axe or pick blow dips the view and hands slightly, following Technique camera motion. Other players see and hear the gathering, covered by their Attack sounds switch. The sounds are part of Enhanced sounds, and with it off or on the Classic skin Daggerfall's own sounds play, timed to the blows.
Harbourmaster ||| Every port's shipwright window has a Harbourmaster tab with the day's contracts while the ship lies at that port's quay: bounties on a named pirate, hunts in the crown's waters, cargo to carry to another port, a wreck's casks to raise and Admiralty surveys of ships through the spyglass. Contracts pay by level and rank and run to a deadline, finishing one raises standing with the crown while failing or giving one up lowers it, and three can be held at a time. A bounty's pirate waits in the waters named on the contract and the bounty is void with no loss of standing if someone else sinks her first. Cargo contracts send the player only to port towns on the sea, a harbourmaster's board stands only in a port town, and a cargo already held for an unreachable place is cancelled with a notice ("Contract void - ... No ship can make port there.") with standing with the crown unchanged.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests.
Houses ||| Doors say Residence and list Go in and Buy it when pointed at, a quest's house reads "The <name> Residence" and is never for sale, a customs-brought house cannot be sold for nothing, pressing Sell twice does not error, shelves in ordinary houses do not swallow clicks, entering houses no longer drops players into the void or dark, and a Knightly Order's house is not offered for purchase at its owner's door. When a family member or online character dies permanently, their house and contents (door, bed, chests, furniture, and cupboards) pass to their heir; if the heir already owns a house in that region, the inherited house waits until they sell theirs. Windows no longer show the street where the building's own wall stands. A door's label names the building that door opens, so the Issaqumbaa tavern is no longer labelled "Residence" or offered for sale, and houses built on grassy hills no longer float over the slope, as the hill reaches under the whole house.
Iliac Hand ||| The Iliac Hand is a two-player collectible card game played on a tavern's card table: three holdings between the players, six turns, hold two of three to win. It has units, spells and Daedric Prince cards, painted in code with the Bay's creatures, artifacts and places. Every character carries a Card Binder from creation that holds their decks, and a Collections tab under Holdings shows the weapon codex and cards side by side. Its panel uses the Enhanced Plus stone-and-brass look and the game's pixel font, has a fold button ("Collapse the panel" / "Expand the panel") that tucks the table's words away, and fits any phone screen, scrolling inside it when the table is full. The turn is a track of six marks and the magicka a row of gems with staged spending dimmed, the opponent's hand is card backs beside their deck count, and each holding sits under its own painted location card with each side's power beside its name and the leading side lit. The player's hand sits in a tray with the picked card raised in brass, a game ends with a banner ("Victory", "Defeat", "A draw" or "No contest"), the log keeps one line for each event, online the board appears once the game is dealt, and playing by keyboard keeps the focus on the panel so Space does not make the character jump up from the table.
Language ||| Settings > Interface > Language offers 13 languages besides English: Bulgarian, Czech, Danish, French, German, Indonesian, Italian, Korean, Norwegian, Portuguese (Brazil), Romanian, Russian and Spanish. The choice applies at once and is remembered, and if the browser reads one of them first the game offers it once in that language. The translations cover the game's own menus and screens (the front door and Settings, the pause window, the death screens, account and online messages, the Professions and Stores pages and every crafting station, the Features screen and every mod's settings) and are machine translated, which the game says. The classic windows draw any script, including Cyrillic, Greek, Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and the About pane credits each translation. The new card table's words and Hold'em's fold are translated into all 13 complete languages, and the magicka label uses Daggerfall Unity's own text so it follows an installed Daggerfall Unity language pack.
Mace of Molag Bal ||| The Mace of Molag Bal works as described: draining a foe's strength raises the player's past 100, spell points it drains count once even on two quick hits, and the bonus ends when the Mace is taken off.
Master Skills ||| Master Skills raises the skill hard cap from 100 to 200, with everything up to 100 working as before. Each point from 100 to 125 is about 4 times slower, 125 to 150 about 8 times slower and 150 to 200 about 16 times slower, and each point above 100 gives 25% of the benefit of a point below 100, with milestones at 125, 150, 175 and 200. A skill at 200 fights like a 140. Every skill can reach 100, but only mastered skills pass it: 2 of 3 primary, 2 of 3 major and 1 of 6 minor, chosen at 100 and permanent. It is always on online and a choice offline. Online, every mastered school past 100 strengthens its own spells and not just their price, by 1% per effective point up to +40% at 200, the same rule as Destruction's damage: Restoration makes heals heal more, Alteration makes the Shield hold more, Illusion makes invisibility, chameleon and shadow last longer, Thaumaturgy makes Spell Reflection and Spell Resistance hold more often, and Mysticism makes Silence, Dispel and Soul Trap take hold more often. Mastering two schools also allows choosing an Arcane Specialisation.
Mining ||| Mining is a gathering profession utilizing wilderness ore veins, dungeon walls, and boulder quarrying with Pick-Axes and glint strikes, featuring region-specific ores and progression tied to confirmed land and player gathering activity. Copper, silver, platinum, moonstone, dwarven scrap, mithril, adamantium, ebony and orichalcum veins each have their own colour, while lead and lodestone keep their classic look. Gold, Platinum, Mithril, Ebony and a kingdom's own ore only appear on ground that three different players, each on an account at least a week old, have gathered on, since the servers cannot check which land a player claims to stand on, and the world map card shows which ores are waiting on that for each place. Ore veins are clearly different colours, Moonstone a pale blue, Lodestone rust red and Copper orange among them, and a boulder no longer looks like Lodestone. Prospector (Mining 50) shows surface veins on the compass from 500 m (dungeon veins stay at 200 m), marks each nearby vein on the Overworld on its own by its ore, and still gives gems a tenth more often. Deep Delver (Mining 50) makes dungeon veins yield double and shows every vein in the dungeon on the compass. Stonebreaker is now Surveyor (Mining 100): the player's own word vouches for unwitnessed ground so surface veins there can be any of that land's ores, a region's own ore still needing witnesses, while quarrying still yields Cut Stone directly. Dungeon veins sit on the floor at the wall's foot, and a vein or Motherlode cracks as it is worked, each blow knocking chunks of ore loose toward the player that land on the ground and sink away, with the cracks closing up again if the player stops before it breaks.
Motherlodes ||| Motherlodes are daily rich ore heaps announced in chat and marked on the compass, requiring Mining 25 and Pick-Axe strikes for ore, XP, and silver without counting against harvest limits, and neither motherlodes nor veins, boulders, herbs, and trees appear inside rocks. Motherlodes appear on the compass from their warning onward, in their own colour, drawn over nearby veins. With Motherlode Sense (Mining 100) the day's Motherlodes are marked on the map and the Overworld from the start of the day, each showing its ore and, until it rises, when it rises (such as "Tue 13:00", on the player's own clock), with warnings still coming 30 minutes ahead. Motherlodes past the Veil still rise only in the Iliac Bay.
Oblivion Gates ||| Oblivion Gates are nightly raid events featuring a handcrafted model of cracked basalt and fire with multi-court Warden boss fights, damage charts, and rewards, alongside tavern card games like Iliac Hand played against regulars or online players for stakes and collection cards. The Oblivion Gate boss's own mark and his host's paths no longer shade large areas of empty ground, so the ground markings in a boss AoE frame cost about a quarter less to draw.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy is built-in and enabled by default, where characters found houses with surnames and seats, choosing death mechanics (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage). Features include inherited stats/reputation, bequests, realm synchronization, permadeath protections, vampire/werewolf/wereboar Enduring exemptions, a fourth Family tab page ("How it works") explaining rules/inheritance/heirlooms/marriage/symbols, tree links ("New to the family?"), and wrapping long names. Passing the mantle online can no longer overwrite a sibling with the new heir's name and class, heirs resumed from the Online page start at the family seat instead of Privateer's Hold, and a home owned on a farm or manor can be made the family seat, with family members met there beginning their story there. The remains of the house's fallen appear where they fell, as a body with a line of light over it in dungeons and in the open, pressed like any body to take back their bones, heirloom and share of the purse, and only in the dungeon the relative actually fell in. Whether the player is "in a fight" is judged where they are, so remains no longer stay shut because of an enemy back in the street, and a duel, the player's own arena bout or a hostile ship within reach counts as a fight, also for Play as, Pass the mantle and weddings. Putting the bones back no longer leaves the death quest stuck.
Quest Journal ||| The quest journal's Quests tab opens on the quest the card is showing. Under each active quest a line names the place its latest entry sends the player, in Daggerfall's words, and Show on map closes the journal and opens the travel map on the place, outdoors only, or says to ask around if it is not on the map yet. The chronicle shows "Time remains" on a quest with a deadline, counting down live. Finished quests have a Remove button and Clear archive removes them all, and active quests can be hidden without abandoning them, with Show hidden bringing them back. Typing in the quest journal's note field works again on the classic and Grimoire UIs, opening as the game's standard text-entry box. The journal (the L key) has an Abandon button on every quest except the main story's, clicked twice to set the quest aside, which keeps everything it had and stops it running until reclaimed from the pause menu's Quests tab under Abandoned, while bounties and revenant hunts keep their own Abandon.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest.
Sea Chart ||| The chart past the Veil (opened with the map key) uses the game's own enhanced font, with names drawn after every island marker with a dark outline, each on whichever side of its island is clear. A depth ruler along the top names each stretch of water in view (the Shallows, the Reaches, the Deep Aether, the Abyss, the Veil and the world's edge), each depth band is its own shade of water, a league grid replaces the old stars and the world's edge is marked. Each island type has its own shape as well as colour (a port is round with a harbour dot, a ruin a diamond, a wild isle a peak, a bare rock a cross), larger islands show their real size when zoomed in, Veilhaven is ringed in gold, and a key under the chart explains every icon. A legend's haunt is a red-washed square while she sails and hatched grey while she lies sunk, the player's ship is a white arrow in a brass ring with an edge arrow giving the distance when off-screen, and pointing at or tapping an island rings it, draws a bearing line and shows a card with its name, type and distance. The chart fits the screen at any size and its words follow the chosen language, though the sea's own names are still in English for now.
Seats ||| Seat towns feature palace charters, map markers, and kingdom banners, with chapters featuring officer and Master ranks determined by accumulated merit, reputation, and tenure to provide unique services and focus settings. The weekly Turning hands out Charters again, with only one request at a time settling the week so the servers are not swamped, and acting at a seat in the moment the week is turning says "The week is turning at the seats. Try again in a moment." and is never applied to a half-turned week. Town seats are worked out once a minute for the whole server instead of by every server process on every request, so the seats list, Watch claims, Levies, Tithes and sieges load faster and put far less strain on the database.
Shipyards ||| Port towns have a shipyard on their seaward edge, its back to the water, where the shipmaster sells every ship Come Sail Away builds, from the Rowboat to the Carrack and the Large Galley, and buys the player's ships back. One window holds Ships for sale (each hull's size, crew, guns and price, with buying asking for confirmation), Your fleet (where each ship lies, her worth and what the shipmaster will pay, with Bring her here, lay up, rename and sell, a big sale paid as a letter of credit) and the Shipwright and Refits, which need Naval Combat on. The Rowboat goes into the pack as parts and every other ship's deed goes into the Fleet laid up at that port. The shipmaster will not buy a ship with cargo in her hold, anything in her cabin or her crew ashore with the player, or one that came into the realm through customs, and a sold ship's cabin furniture is sold too with half its value going to the bank. Townsfolk tell the player where the shipyard is and it is marked on the town map and as an anchor on the Overworld. Shipmaster cards show each ship's own 3D picture, and the Reforge, shipyard and naval-yard pages and a merchant's options each have their own icon.
Shops ||| Shops operate on night shifts with attribute caps for online pricing, and player-transferred items from chests, bodies, or shops undergo server validity checks. Shops pay for a crafted Superior or Masterwork piece what they pay for the plain piece its recipe makes, since the magic or rare lines are power and not resale value, and patrons follow the same rule while found magic and rare items are priced as before. The keyed shelf prices a sale as the trade window does and no longer buys conjured pieces. General Stores and Pawn Shops no longer sell or buy ship deeds and parts, which are traded at shipyards, and shops do not buy the exchange's goods. Weapon Smiths always keep 50-100 arrows on the counter, Armorers and General Stores 14-29 and Pawn Shops 7-14. Every shelf item shows its price before it is clicked and a List / Pictures switch shows both sides as item tiles, the player's side is labelled "Your pack", the footer shows "To pay" (or "You get") and "Your gold" separately, a magic-only counter is titled "Sell magic items", Steal sits apart from the other buttons, and a button that cannot be used yet explains why when pressed.
Stores ||| The Stores hold what the player gathers, up to 5,000 of each material per character, and are kept in town, so the Stores page in any town moves materials. The page has filters, search and sorting, and Withdraw to pack takes up to 200 at a time, every material able to be withdrawn, with herbs coming out as Daggerfall's own northern or southern ingredient that works in the potion maker. Emptying the bag into the pack is possible from the Stores page anywhere, and a withdrawal whose answer was lost is sent again when opening the page, never minted twice. When the Stores have no room for what a craft would refine, the box says so. The Stores page opens with a row of station tiles marked Here or Away, a station the player is away from staying folded until its tile is tapped, with smelting, other workings and patterns shown as cards, and materials and items show the game's own item pictures.
Tavern Cards ||| Every tavern and inn has its own card table, a green-felt poker table with six stools standing near the entrance, and pressing activate there sits the player down to play Texas Hold'em with the tavern's regulars or with other players online. The character is seated for everyone to see, and the cards are physical on the cloth: dealt, fanned, peeked at, chips dragged to bet and the deck riffled. Online the relay deals, owning the shuffle, hole cards and seat clock so no client can see another's hand, and gold tables take a buy-in of real gold held in escrow by the account service and paid out on the hand's receipt. The tavern's ordinary dining tables are no longer card tables. Gold tables accept buy-ins again after refusing every one, the table panel shows a refused stake as a warning, a stake being asked as a wait and the player's turn highlighted, quick buttons (Min, Max, half pot, Pot) set the stake, the slider shows its unit, a minus button hides the seats and log, and the log shows one line per event. Card tables no longer stand in corridors, doorways or openings, so about one tavern in ten has no card table and some keep only the green chips table, and going all in and losing at an online table no longer crashes the game. The Hold'em and Iliac Hand panels at tavern tables use the Enhanced Plus stone-and-brass look and the game's pixel font, and the classic skin gets the same look for these panels.
Weather ||| Weather evolves within the day with cloud types by place, a weather field where the day's words are places walked into and out of, sandstorms, and no rain over snow. A world weather map (WEATHER3) was added then removed. Rainstorms now drizzle at their edges and pour only at their heart, snow falls lightly more often, light rain looks light with thinner fog and more daylight, and rain no longer vanishes in a puddle, hiding only when the eyes are underwater. Rain and snow fall in front of grass and banners, and rain keeps falling while travelling on the Overworld map. It rains about half as often, a woodland spring that used to be wet a third of the time now being wet about one hour in six, with the dry hours the region's usual sun, cloud, grey deck and fog. Rain comes in every strength, about a third of fronts being a light sprinkle from edge to middle, most a steady shower and only about a quarter building to a downpour at their heart, while thunderstorms are still downpours but come half as often. The Classic skin's weather and the classic weather with "Weather as places" off are unchanged.
