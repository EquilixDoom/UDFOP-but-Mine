Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player. A username can no longer be an email address: a player who signed up with one still signs in with it but other players see a generated name. After a server update players no longer all reconnect at once, each game waiting a moment (longer the more players are online), sign-in token requests and the account, realm, heartbeat and profession reads are answered cheaply from read copies of the database, the letterbox, Notice Board count and play time share one request, and when many players sign in at once they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error. Most menus that talk to the server (the Guild tab, the market, the Seat tab, letters, the account card and signing in) load much faster, since the account server now runs next to its database instead of across the Pacific, and joining the world, the chat's player list and the friends list come up sooner after signing in.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster. The chat has a search bar above the player list where part of a name, or # and a tag, shows every tab's lines from or about that player, and /search <name> searches from the chat field while /search alone clears it. Chat tabs scroll sideways with the mouse wheel, the edge with more tabs fading and a clicked tab scrolling fully into view. Titles no longer appear in chat, while guild tags and glyphs still show after names and the title still shows over the head and on the Inspect card.
Elites ||| Elites are tougher special foes. In the open world 5% of foes stand as elites, with no limit of one at a time, after a brief spell at 2% that was undone. They appear only from foe level 3 and never as city watch or allies, ordinary dungeons hold one 20% of the time, and Elite Dungeons keep their 3 to 4. Elites are named everywhere (on hover, death notices, corpses), appear offline while Loot rarity is on, and level-1 or level-2 characters meet none. Additionally, the Greater Giant in the open zone drops its proper elite-table loot again (magic/common pieces, a chance at rare/legendary, and gold by level). Online, elite enemies do not appear in the wilds or rise from searched graves until the character reaches Renown 5, while shared dungeons keep their elites as before.
Letters of Credit ||| Online, a letter of credit cannot be put into a chest, cupboard or body, since a shared container is everyone's, so it stays in the pack and is given to another player in a trade. The player's own storage (house cupboards, ship's chest), a pile they dropped themselves and the wagon still take letters, and nothing changes offline. Outside a trade a letter never reaches the player through another player's game, and letters already lying in a shared chest or on a body when the update arrived were removed.
Loot Rarity ||| Loot rarity is a Diablo-style ladder over Daggerfall's loot, built on Unleveled Loot's law and on by default, with the ladder shown on the items. Clothing can drop as Magic and Rare with lines of its own: standing with a social group, warmth and weatherproofing (for Climates & Calories), Personality, and street skills, with footwear leaning to movement skills. Superior and Masterwork clothing the player makes comes out Magic and Rare, as crafted weapons and armour already did. Six Legendary garments each have a power: the Alik'r Wayfarer's Robes (heat felt 20 less), the Wrothgar Pass Mantle (cold felt 20 less), the Vestments of Stendarr (Restoration spells cost 25% less), the Pilgrim's Sandals (tire 25% slower, falls hurt half as much), the Silks of Queen Barenziah (+5 standing with every group) and the Shade's Cowl (+25 Stealth while hooded). With the loot rarity setting off, clothing stays exactly as in Daggerfall. Resistance from rolled gear counts up to 45% per element, and only fills an Aetheric piece's resistance up to that same 45%. Hone raises one line within its own range, and some Rares and Legendaries carry an empty socket. One Rare or Legendary item in twelve from a body or a pile is cursed, with one extra bonus line and one drawback: a bad reputation, bad reactions, damage or wear in holy places, weaker blows against a kind of foe, or a health leech. Gilded is the new top of the rarity ladder, above Aetheric and Artifacts, with its name in gold leaf and a frame breathing light in every slot. A rarity affix of +N% casting speed rolls on jewellery and weapons and adds up across everything worn, up to 30%. Rare weapons can no longer roll Vampiric Effect (weapons that already have it keep it), life leech from a weapon's lines and gems together is capped at 10% of each hit, Hist-Sap regenerates 1% a round (2% under half health), and Open Hand deals half damage on the second bare-handed hit. Potency, a line on jewellery and weapons, gives +3% to +15% spell damage (at most +30% from everything worn), and Wellspring, a jewellery line, returns 2% to 10% of magicka when a foe is killed (at most 10% a kill). Three caster Legendaries are found most often among mages, covens and laboratories: The Eye of Magnus (Destruction spells meet a saving throw 15 lower), The Ring of Artaeum (1% of magicka back every magic round, 2% under half) and Shalidor's Brand (Destruction spells strike 25% harder while magicka is above three quarters). Spell damage from loot is capped at +50% all together and none of it reaches a fight against another player.
Name in Game ||| A Name in game choice in the Account section of the profile card picks Account name or Character name. With Character name, other players see the playing character's name over their head, in chat and in the online list, shown within moments in every room, and letters from the friends list and answers to notice-board duels still reach the player. If the character's name cannot be shown online, other players see the account name instead and the chat says why once: when the name is longer than 24 characters, uses accented letters or symbols, is caught by the name filter, looks like an email address or is another player's username.
Online Death ||| Online death had fixes for a death that respawns and for the online death loop. The gear a fallen player leaves in the wilds holds only things that can be worn.
Player Trade ||| Player trade was added in 0.1.3720. In a trade each letter of credit shows its worth on its own row ("Worth 12,500 gold") across the pack, the offer and theirs, working on touch. A realm trade of an item looted in the last couple of minutes no longer fails, a character whose trade is on hold can no longer move its crafted pieces through another character, and when the realm refuses a trade because a character is under review it says whose character it is. A purse, letter of credit or pile of gold holding more than 2,147,483,647 gold, more than Daggerfall itself can count, is flagged as tampered, and that character cannot trade, sell or send gold to other players until the problem is gone or staff clear it.
Prices ||| Prices are said in real time, and NaN prices were fixed. Books and potion recipes no longer jump to a much higher price after passing between players online, which let them sell back for more than they cost. An item that reaches the player through another player's game (a chest, a body, a shelf) cannot carry a higher price than an honest item of its kind could.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2.
Skill Training ||| Past 100, a skill only learns from enemies tough for that skill, and tougher enemies teach more. Movement skills past 100 count only real movement: running counts the ground actually covered, swimming and diving count and treading water does not, climbing counts only up or down the wall, a jump that carries the player somewhere counts, and being carried (such as on someone's boat) does not. Running past 100 now counts every step. A spell landing on a foe, or a foe's spell landing on the player, counts the foe being fought when training a mastered school past 100, so blasting a tough foe trains the school as fast as fighting it with a weapon, and a cast never trains slower than before.
Spells ||| Ending the player's own spells was fixed, the aim hint always shows when a spell waits for a target, and a party member healing the player no longer repeats the cast message on every cast. Classic spellcasting runs at the same pace as the Morrowind animation, with the spell leaving the hands at half a second, and a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, so clicking or recasting during it does nothing and costs nothing. Casting speed scales with the Speed attribute, up to 25% faster at high Speed and 25% slower at low. Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Re-casting an effect already on the player or a target resets it to that cast's full duration instead of adding the new duration on top, and a shorter re-cast never cuts a longer one short. This covers buffs and debuffs alike, including Shield, Fortify, Regenerate, Levitate, Light, Detect, the resistances, Paralyze, Silence, Continuous Damage and Soul Trap, and re-casting Regenerate or Continuous Damage on its last round still gives the full ticks. Classic spellcasting runs at the pace of the Morrowind animation, a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, casting speed scales with the Speed attribute (up to 25% faster at high Speed, 25% slower at low), and Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Online, a Destruction mastered past 100 adds to Damage Health and Continuous Damage spells, 1% for each point of its effective value past 100 and +40% at 200, while gear that raises Destruction still makes spells cheaper.
Town Watch ||| The town watch keeps the town day and night in three eight-hour shifts plus a day off. Patrols walk in pairs around their own district, in larger towns a watchman stands at each gate, off duty he wears his own clothes as a townsperson, and larger towns keep a larger watch. In the 14 cities whose palace stands in its own walled grounds the watch now lives where its door opens onto the streets, so it walks its patrols instead of staying at the palace. A watchman on duty greets the player by their standing with the region's law, a first minor offence in a region draws a Hold! warning and not an arrest (the next brings one), and the watch shouts "Halt!" from one watchman at most once every 15 seconds. A monster that gets into town can attack townspeople, one blow killing one, and the watch defends them as it does the player. Paying a fine no longer counts as killing the watchman who walked away. All of this is in the living world, and Classic play keeps Daggerfall's own watch and townspeople. The watch posted at a city's gates stops strangers coming in and asks their business, and strangers answer civilly, curtly or with open hostility, sellswords and sailors rougher; patrols stop strangers on their rounds, every stranger stopped at a gate is questioned, and a gate stop no longer keeps a visitor from leaving. The watch breaks up a quarrel even at a change of shift, and only one of a patrol pair calls the hour. When a monster attacks in town the guards still come to help but walk off after the fight instead of vanishing, leaving the world only once far away or behind the player, and if another monster turns up while they walk off the same guards turn back to the fight, a guard attacked on his way out defending himself before carrying on.
Werewolf ||| The werewolf (WEREWOLF1) shipped at last. A beast cannot surrender, so when the watch stops the player they choose F frighten or N fight on. Frighten roars at the watch with a chance of 50% at even level, 5% per level of difference, from 10% to 90%, and if it works the guards run and the crime is forgotten, otherwise they stand their ground and the guard's blow lands. A frightened guard runs a few seconds then leaves, no body left behind, guards already on their way are called off while guards defending the town from monsters stay, and the watch never stops a beast on sight. Online, the full moon forces the change for its night only, dusk to dawn, 15 real minutes, with full-moon nights two real hours apart then none for fourteen. The curse's +40 Strength, Agility, Endurance and Speed and +30 to its seven skills apply only in beast form, so in human form the player is their own character again and the beast itself is unchanged. Changing form fully heals once a day, with changing back or changing again with Hircine's Ring no longer healing, and the beast's claws count as silver against creatures that need a better metal, so a Lich or an Ancient Lich takes reduced damage from them. The beast's claws no longer count as silver, so a Lich or an Ancient Lich takes full damage from them. In beast form plate gauntlets add their metal's damage bonus to every claw hit in every fight, duels and sieges included (Elven +1, Dwarven +2, Mithril and Adamantium +3, Ebony +4, Orcish +5, Daedric +6), while leather, chain, brigandine and broken gauntlets add nothing and no gauntlets make claws weaker, so they must be put on before changing. Loading a save in beast form no longer leaves the character treated as a beast until restart.
