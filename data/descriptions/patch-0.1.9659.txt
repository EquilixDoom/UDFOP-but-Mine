Arcane Essence ||| Arcane Essence is a crafting material. It stays in the player's Stores, never the pack, and sells on the market like any Stores material. From Enchanting 50, an enchanting station spends 2 Arcane Essence to roll one line of a known Magic piece again, or 5 for a Rare's, which is the Mages Guild's Reforge paid in Essence. At an enchanting station each line of a piece has its own Reforge button in a list under the piece's name and price, and the name is readable instead of wrapping one word per line.
Auras ||| Auras are cosmetic effects worn from the aura picker on the account card, each granted with a title. They include the Oblivion Ward, Golden Radiance, the Holo Shadow Cloak (with the Shadow Fang title), the developers' Seraph Wings and Crystal Resonance (with the Crystal Fist title). Lilac Warren is SylviaBun's aura: six lilac bunnies with cotton tails and little maid's headdresses hop round the player's feet on a ring of lilac light, leaving fading paw-print tracks, with an occasional happy binky and sparkles of lilac, pink and white. Battlemage's Panoply ||| The Battlemage's Panoply is an Aetheric Sigil Set of nine Mithril pieces: the Warhelm, both Pauldrons, the Cuirass, Gauntlets, Greaves, Boots, the Spire-Shield and the Spire-Saber. Two pieces give Battlemage's Bearing (+5 to +15 to every resistance and +10 to +30 magicka), four give Battlemage's Discipline (a spell cast arms the next weapon blow with its element for +20% to +50%, and weapon blows return 5% to 15% of the last spell's cost), and six give Battlemage's Reprieve (a blow that takes the player under 20%, never a killing blow, raises a shield of 15% to 40% of their health for 6 seconds, half of it to party members within 8 m, recovering in 240 down to 120 seconds). Pieces drop from Elite Breach Redoubts at 1 in 12 at Redoubt 1, improving to 1 in 4 from Redoubt 16 and 1 in 3 at a Valkynaz's, guaranteed after 10 rolls without one, and the Broker sells one piece of the player's choice once a week for 20 Deadlands Embers. In an Elite Breach the Reprieve and Shed Skin answer the Spire's blows, and death-saving powers recover at the next Redoubt, not on a timer.
Bug Reports ||| Typing /bug in chat, or opening the mailbox beside the hourglass in the pause menu on the enhanced skin and pressing Report a bug or exploit, reports an exploit, a bug or a security problem privately, seen only by the developers who read reports and not by other players or moderators. The form asks what it is, a short title and what happens and how to make it happen, shows what is sent with it (the username and the game's build) before sending, and lists the player's reports with whether a developer has read each and whether it was fixed. Exploits should be kept out of chat, Discord and the Notice Board until fixed, and reporting another player still goes to the moderators with /report <name>.
Captain's Stars ||| A captain level (up to 30) is earned from sinking and capturing ships, beating off boarders, claiming prizes, finishing contracts, distance sailed, treasure charts, charting isles and selling goods at the exchange. Each level after the first gives one point to spend in the Captain's Stars, a skill tree in the Firmament's style opened from the Captain page: the Master (left) improves seamanship (speed under sail and oars, turning, cargo, hull, repairs and better exchange prices) and the Commander (right) improves combat (reload speed, gun damage, accuracy, grapeshot, chain shot and heated shot, boarding, ram damage and how long a rammed ship stays locked). Keystones give a big bonus with a cost (for example Master of the Wind: +12% speed under sail, -12% under oars), stars apply to every ship the player owns including laid-up and packed ones, removing or changing stars is free, and keystone costs apply in combat.
Chat Safety ||| /block <name> hides everything a player says to the player (chat, invites, notes, letters, and trade, duel, wedding, card or journal requests), /unblock undoes it and /blocked lists who is blocked; the block is tied to the account, so a new name or character does not get around it, and it follows the player to every device. Clicking a name on any chat line offers Report or Block, and /report <name> [reason]: <note> sends the moderators everything that player said in the player's chat, the game server signing every relayed line so a report can only carry words really said. /rules shows the Code of Conduct, agreed once before the first chat line, covering harassment, hate, sexual remarks, sharing personal details, spam and scams while leaving trash talk about the game fine. Breaking the rules leads to a mute, then longer mutes (30 minutes, a day, a week), then a ban; three established players reporting someone within an hour mutes them for 30 minutes until a moderator looks; and guest accounts and accounts under 14 days old can post one public channel line every 4 seconds. Moderators have /cases, /case <name>, /dismiss <name>, /modhelp and /ban <name> [days|perm]: <reason>, and see every past mute and ban.
Crafting Stations ||| Crafting stations use the Enhanced Plus look with diamond strikes for mini-games like the anvil's heat, loom's beat, and workbench's plane, and every station now uses materials in your Materials Bag and pack as well as your Stores. Deck Fittings ||| On the Fleet page Deck opens a top-down plan of the ship's deck where crates and chests are picked from a list and placed by clicking, selected, dragged, turned with Q and E, removed with Delete and kept with Save layout, up to 24 fittings, each sitting fully on the open deck and clear of the others. Dual Wielding ||| Equipping a one-handed weapon (sword, dagger, mace or war axe) in each hand draws and swings both, with H choosing which hand leads and no switch delay with both hands up. A shield, two-handed weapon, bow, or torch or lantern held in a hand still means one weapon. A pair is quicker, not twice as strong: the next blow straight after a strike comes from the other hand a little sooner, the off hand does 80% damage with -5 to hit at Agility 50 (shrinking to nothing at higher Agility and rising to -10 at lower), a heavy off-hand weapon swings slower, and the off hand's blows count as 0.8 of a blow for on-strike enchantments, per-hit powers and skill training. Measured, a matched pair of light weapons deals about 9% more damage per second than one of them and a heavier pair about 4%, for up to a quarter more fatigue per second and giving up the shield. Each weapon in a pair shows its own element with weapon glow on, the other weapon lowers straight down when one attacks, and the same rules apply in duels, sieges and the open PvP zone. Under Physical Combat And Armor Overhaul (on by default) a weapon in the left hand no longer rolls a shield block, since dual wielding gives up the shield's block, while shields block as before.
Dungeon Scaling ||| Dungeons answer a strong character. It starts as the player's three best combat or magic skills climb past 75 and needs Master Skills, always on online. At best combat skills of 80 enemies get +2 hit and dodge, health x1.2 and damage x1.05, rising to +36 hit and dodge, x3.4 health and x1.6 damage at 200. How much applies depends on the place: 11% in mines and natural caves, 22% in strongholds, cemeteries and nests, 44% in prisons and harpy and scorpion nests, 88% in crypts and orc and giant strongholds, and 100% in laboratories, covens, vampire haunts and dragon's dens. The wilderness counts too, 22% by day and 44% at night, while weak creatures stay weak and towns never change. Monsters are also tougher by the place they are met, using the grading that decides its loot: in a Crypt the strongest foes get up to a third more health, in a Vampire Haunt up to 70% more, and in a Dragon's Den or the Volcanic Caves up to double health and up to 25% harder blows. Rats, bats and imps gain nothing, and offline, turning off Loot rarity turns the scaling off too. From character level 10 and Renown 15, foes in dungeons and the wilds keep pace with the player: each level past 10 a foe hits 7% harder and stands 4% tougher and each Renown past 15 adds 1% to its hits and 0.5% to its health, both compounding up to level 30 and Renown 50 (level 15, Renown 22 gives x1.5 hits and x1.26 health; level 30, Renown 50 gives x5.5 and x2.6). Plain foes, champions, elites, revenants and the open zone's foes all answer, offline only the level counts, and the scaling leaves alone towns and buildings, the city watch, allies, the Burning Court, the Shattered Hour, an Elite Breach and the arena's floor, with every foe Daggerfall's own when the loot ladder is off. In a party a foe hits each player at that player's own level.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are sold wherever weapons are, in Weapon Smiths, Armorers, General Stores and Pawn Shops, and restocked each day. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang. On the Morrowind body it is held like a rifle, the right hand on its pistol grip and the left supporting the forend, carried at rest at a low ready with the muzzle down and across toward the left foot and coming up to a level ready stance while shooting, for a few seconds after and while foes are hurting the player, in first and third person, the Hourlock held the same way.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now.
Etheric Sea ||| Sailing west of the Bay's mouth on the Abecean asks whether to sail into the open sea, leading to Veilhaven three days later, while sailing back asks the way home with warnings if enemies/players are close. Past the Veil lies an endless sea of isles (port isles with towns/quays, ruins with dungeons, wild isles with goods, and bare rocks), growing deadlier and richer further west through Shallows, Reaches, Deep Aether, and Abyss. No crown's law reaches past the Veil (no navy, crimes aren't counted, corsairs sail under sea names), ether-amber, starglass, and voidsilk are traded, wild isles float casks and refill in seven days, six legendary ships haunt the depths with purses, and the travel map key opens a chart of sighted isles. The Veil blocks magic (Recall, teleports, portal stones, party travel, Fleet ships) and falling wakes players at the nearest port isle. The light is cold and violet, the sea west of the Bay is water again, and while classic keeps Daggerfall's sea, enhanced/online play features this crossing. After crossing the Veil a ship too heavily loaded to move says so ("You're going to need a bigger boat"), and everything carried, gold and wagon included, counts against a ship's hold. The sea past the Veil is now called the Eltheric Ocean, its proper name, across the chart, harbourmasters, the crossing, the captain's log and the legends. The player can rise into the Overworld while sailing it, as in the Iliac Bay, by pressing Overworld (O) on the chart, then click the sea to sail there or a charted port or ruin to travel to it; enemy bands roam its isles and, with Naval Combat on, the Eltheric's own corsairs sail its open sea, chasing the player and slowing a journey as they close in, visible to other players online. The water has a real seabed down to about two hundred metres, so the player can swim, dive and surface there with seafloor plants, fish, creatures and sunken loot, though the ocean holes still appear only in the Iliac Bay. Your Ship can no longer be boarded from past the Veil. Every port isle sells at least one ordinary trade good alongside its ether goods, Veilhaven selling spice, shown on the sea chart's isle card.
Exchange ||| An Exchange tab at a port buys and sells eight goods of the Bay: wine, grain, wool, iron, spices, silk, silver and ivory. Each crown's ports make some goods cheaply and pay well for others, every port has a twist of its own, prices drift day to day and each port deals in only so much of a good each day. Goods load into and out of the hold of the ship at the quay, a full hold slows her, and the exchange says how much more she can carry and will not sell her so much she cannot move. Shops do not buy the exchange's goods. The exchange no longer lets the player buy a cheap good back at the price they sold it for when their trade stars are maxed.
Firmament ||| The Firmament is a passive tree for everything done online, opened from the Firmament button on the Stats page. The player chooses one of seven birthsigns, then spends Stars on combat stars and Craft points on the life ring (gathering, crafting, trading, hunting and sailing), earning 2 Stars for every Renown level and 1 Craft point for every 8 profession ranks. Changes are free to plan until confirmed, taking stars back is free below Renown 10 and then costs 10 silver per star, and a full reset costs 10,000 gold. The stars never affect hits or spells against other players and switch off in duels and while mentoring. Every star adds something: small stars and roads give their bonus one for one as a percent, and an attribute bonus counts on a stat already at 100 (with the sky's full +15 a 100 Strength reads 115, offline the cap is 100). It works in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire and on phones, and its text is English for now. Every weapon family has two Firmament notables that raise its skill, and Axe and Blunt get a third; Blade Dancer, Iron Knuckles, Headsman and Bonebreaker keep every stat and gain the skill on top, and the Ritual's Battlemage's Focus is now Templar's Mace, with no star moved so shared build codes still load. Fist stars now apply to bare-handed blows.
First-Person Arms ||| First-person arms follow the camera by default, with Morrowind's lag one toggle away, and the weapon and arms tilt with the look. Flags ||| A ship's flag follows her state: her colours, down when struck, and the player's orange on a prize. The pirate flagship and merchant carracks now fly colours at all. Guild Hall ||| A guild hall is a guild-owned house bought by the guildmaster with treasury funds, featuring member chests, crafting, resting, customizable painting and yard furnishings, and a hall's roll as the first book on its shelf detailing its chapter's standing, Master, and officers. Windows inside buildings are now see-through, town boards fill in once three players have visited guild halls, and in rooms with many lamps, stationary people cast shadows from every lamp rather than just the nearest twelve. Online, if the player does not yet qualify for the next rank, the guild popup lists what is missing: the rank's name ("Next rank: Swordsman"), reputation against what is needed ("Reputation 14 of 20"), the guild-skill level the player is short of with their best guild skill named, a second guild skill at the lower bar and the guild's own skills by name. The new Guild Chapters tutorial page opens when the rank is held at 7. The hall and the Standing page show what the realm still owes the player (already earned, arriving at most 15 a real day) and warn before a review puts the player on probation or lowers their rank, with when and why, and on probation they are told the stakes: below -10 expelled at the review, below 0 it runs 28 more days, at 0 or more it ends, and online earning the next rank ends it sooner. The rank text scrolls on its own so Talk, Service and Reforge stay on screen. Expelled or turned away for standing, the player sees at the hall their standing, the standing it takes members at, how many quests bring them back there and how fast it recovers on its own, and the hall names skills the guild also wants and says that its halls in other regions still have work when one is shut for the Season; the classic look's refusal says the same.
Guilds ||| Guilds are player organisations founded on Renown 10 with restrictions on disbanding and standing reviews, functioning properly across regions, and online you can set entry permissions like an online home (Only me, My party, My guild, or Anyone). Townsfolk, shopkeepers, guild members, nobles and priests are dressed from a wardrobe of their own in Morrowind garments and colours, while a fighters' guild's people wear whatever armour came to hand. Online, a player is promoted the moment they qualify for the next rank by talking to a guild-service NPC at the hall, with no 28-day wait for a promotion, while demotion, probation and expulsion still wait 28 days and offline play is unchanged. Reputation earned before joining still counts, but a new member rises no faster than the guild records standing, about a rank and a half a day from the day they join, and the guild hall tells when the next promotion can come at the earliest. A member on probation is no longer expelled over reputation the guild still owes them, the probation being renewed instead. Online, Mages Guild quests "Guard the Guild" and "Protect an Honored Mage" need standing 10 and 20, and new apprentices are no longer handed them. Becoming a vampire no longer shuts the player out of the Dark Brotherhood or the Thieves Guild: membership and rank carry over when they turn and stay if they are cured, and a vampire who lost either gets it back, rank and all, the first time the game reads their guilds. A guild's quest-giver can always be asked for work as a stranger, even after an expulsion, each quest done raising standing with that guild by 5.
Item Card ||| Hovering a piece shows what it would replace, with now to after per body part in green or red. An unidentified piece keeps its enchantments to itself until identified, and the card's fit was fixed. A weapon technique has its own section on the Enhanced item card, the way set pieces and sigils do, with a header of its glyph (three blue strike marks), its name and the roll (such as +23% [15-30]), what it does, and a footer with the key, hold-to-aim or press, the fatigue cost and the cooldown, and the shop's hover card shows the same. The Info box shows it in full with how the roll raises the technique's blow, the section greys out in a duel, and a letter of credit shows its worth as Worth. Armour and weapons of Rare quality and above say their metal (Iron, Ebony and the rest) on the line under the name even before they are identified, a Legendary keeping its own name and so does one handed down as an heirloom.
Live Chat ||| Live chat has one World tab and was audited before the merge. Chat is spread over more servers: world chat is split sixteen ways by account and each region's chat four ways, so more players fit at once, the World tab's Online count stays accurate past 512 players and it lists everyone while there are no more than 512. Typing /find <name> (or /who <name>) finds anyone online by name, and the Guild tab lists guildmates online wherever they are. World and region chat, friends, parties and guild lines are steadier at busy hours, since chat-server traffic that the relay had been refusing now flows a way that cannot be refused, and when a room's server restarts just as the player connects they get in on the first try. When another tab or window takes over the session, the World tab no longer keeps showing the old "Online" count.
Loot Window ||| A loot-side click takes immediately, and the loot buttons were fixed. The loot list on a body or pile scrolls, so the mouse wheel (or the pad) reaches every item and the list shows how many items are above and below. Searching the wagon or storage keeps the player's place in the list, and on phones the window's two columns stay the same width and no longer run off the screen. Chests, corpses and the wagon can be filtered by name, item type, rarity and material, each menu listing only what the pile holds with a count. Take all moves items from the list on screen into the pack from the top until the next does not fit (emptying someone else's cupboard this way is still stealing), Stash all does the same from the current pack page into the wagon, storage or Materials Bag, and looking at the player's own cupboard or chest, or a stranger's cupboard already stocked today, lists what it holds with nothing taken until it is opened.
Martial Specialisations ||| Mastering any two weapon skills (Long Blade, Short Blade, Axe, Blunt Weapon, Archery, Hand-to-Hand) allows choosing one of fifteen martial paths, such as the Blademaster, Ranger, Juggernaut, Assassin or Brawler, a permanent choice like a mastery. Each path has a passive of its own (for example the Vanguard's foes hit 10% lighter and the Woodsman gets fatigue back on every kill) and a signature technique that uses the Weapon Technique key whenever the player wields one of the path's two weapons and that weapon has no technique of its own, a path with Archery having a bow form and a melee form that share one cooldown, working with the loot ladder off. Timed-effect techniques (Rallying Cleave, Cyclone, Phantom Strike, Earthsplitter, Savage Whirl) start their effect as they are used so every foe the sweep reaches gets it, an arcane path and a martial path can be held at once, and the choice is made on the skill screen (W on the classic sheet or the Martial Specialisation card under Master Skills), with the game also asking once the first time a path opens.
Master Skills ||| Master Skills raises the skill hard cap from 100 to 200, with everything up to 100 working as before. Each point from 100 to 125 is about 4 times slower, 125 to 150 about 8 times slower and 150 to 200 about 16 times slower, and each point above 100 gives 25% of the benefit of a point below 100, with milestones at 125, 150, 175 and 200. A skill at 200 fights like a 140. Every skill can reach 100, but only mastered skills pass it: 2 of 3 primary, 2 of 3 major and 1 of 6 minor, chosen at 100 and permanent. It is always on online and a choice offline. Online, every mastered school past 100 strengthens its own spells and not just their price, by 1% per effective point up to +40% at 200, the same rule as Destruction's damage: Restoration makes heals heal more, Alteration makes the Shield hold more, Illusion makes invisibility, chameleon and shadow last longer, Thaumaturgy makes Spell Reflection and Spell Resistance hold more often, and Mysticism makes Silence, Dispel and Soul Trap take hold more often. Mastering two schools also allows choosing an Arcane Specialisation. Online, a mastered weapon skill past 100 pays in its own way: Long Blade and Archery make every blow land up to 40% harder at 200, Axe up to 80% harder against wounded foes (under half health), Blunt Weapon up to 80% harder against fresh foes (half health or more), each of those two landing up to 40% harder on every blow against the Oblivion Gate's court, the Elite Gates, the Remnant and the sea serpent, and Short Blade swings up to 40% faster, while Hand-to-Hand's damage already grew with the skill. Every short blade swings like a dagger, where the Tanto, Shortsword and Wakizashi had swung at long-blade speed. Mastering two weapons also allows a martial specialisation.
Naval Combat ||| Naval combat lets boats with guns fight, and needs Come Sail Away. The Large Boat carries swivel guns, the Small Ship and the Carrack carry long guns with bow chain-shot chasers and stern fire barrels, and the Large Galley carries long guns along her sides and great guns over her bow. At the helm the player looks to a side and holds Attack, then lets go to fire the broadside, looking higher to fire further, and holds Crouch to brace behind the rail for less damage but guns that hold fire. Each side reloads on its own, with a thin crew slower and a boat with no crew slowest, and the player's gunners lose accuracy as crew are killed. Cannon balls hole the hull, cut sails and kill crew, with hits below the waterline doing most damage, and a ship can catch fire. Features, Naval Combat has settings for ship count, pirates boarding, raiders' plunder and the broadside camera, and a series of changes made busy bays run much smoother. Gun smoke drifts with the wind and each cannonball lands with a tall spout of water scaled up with distance, a ball passing close whistles and one landing beside the ship throws water over the rail and rocks the deck, the side a shot came from flashes on the helm plate, a ship that sinks while on fire may blow its powder magazine seen by every player, and a badly damaged ship has smoke rising along the deck. The target card shows the enemy's crew and the broadside's tally says how many crew went down, each battery shows its reload seconds, and within 700 m of a hostile ship a journey runs at normal speed with the Overworld view closing near one; online, sea combat near another player always runs in real time. Other players see the same fight, chain shot and grapeshot kill crew on ships another player controls, and the aiming arcs stay visible in a crowded fight.
Online Foes ||| Online enemies receive damage properly and feature improved movement synchronization so players are less likely to swing at empty air, alongside enhanced rendering for summoned and called monsters. Another player's monsters can no longer hit twice as hard by claiming a revenant's signature strike, can only cast spells their own kind knows (a paralysed one casting nothing), and one player can no longer make every monster near the player a champion, at most 17 of theirs counting as champions on the player's screen.
Online Mode ||| Online mode is the enhanced lane whole: every enhancement and every mod is on while online. Night gates and guild halls were added online (OL5). An out-of-date game can no longer go online and stays offline until updated, and some updates disconnect everyone online once when they go live. The macOS app and the portable Windows app do not update themselves and need the newest build downloaded. The multiplayer server restarts when updates arrive, disconnecting everyone for a moment so they reconnect on their own. Games in a crowded town send less and the server bundles other players' movements, and an area next to a duel, a Wrothgarian zone fight or a sea serpent fight says so. Loading an online character into the world takes about 3 seconds less, since the game asks the server for the family, character list, towns' homes and homes of arrival towns all at once while it reads the world. The game refuses more enemies from any one player than an honest game can send and believes only as many elites, revenants and Greater Giants as honest play produces. Renown, raid rewards, bounty and orc camps, the Watch, silver finds and profession XP count only for the realm character being played on this device, and setting the computer's clock back no longer makes the player vanish, stick on "reconnecting" or be refused with "token expired"; entering a siege no longer risks a permanent refusal, crossing into the next area during a server update no longer disconnects the player, and an outdated copy of the game is asked to update before it joins the world online.
Online Players ||| Other players are drawn as paperdolls with names or in the Morrowind body, one rig instance per peer built from their look, third-person at the feet. They no longer jump backwards near an area edge, teleport in sieges, Royal Tourneys, homes and ships, dash ahead after a lag spike or snap back when stepping into a new area, because movement updates carry their send time. When a player's movement stops reaching you they now stand still until they move again instead of running in place, including players who went quiet before you arrived. Other players far away are no longer drawn: a Draw distance setting on the Other players card chooses 150 m, 300 m (the default), 600 m or Everyone, the party and a dueled player always show, and the 150 m option can hide a distant archer. Positions near the edge between map areas arrive mostly once instead of two to four times, and other players stand on the ground without floating or jumping a metre at a time. A player who reconnects stands once, an old connection from before a restart or a second tab no longer leaving a frozen copy in town or in a dungeon, and logging in to the same account a second time in the same area closes the older connection. A player walking near the player no longer stands frozen for minutes then jumps, a player who dies is laid down once with no second body and cry, a body is shown instead of the player standing alive when coming into a place where someone has just died, an old message about a death no longer knocks down a player who has got up, and when the player crosses into the next area the area they left sees them change armour or clothes.
Pirates ||| A beaten pirate tries to run about as long as before she must strike, not 1.6 times as long. A cautious brigantine no longer attacks merchant carracks, and from level 5 raids are on galleons only. The Carrack (the pirate flagship and the merchant carrack) and the Large Boat (the pirate sloop and the coasting trader) are new models, the carrack fully textured and the boat clinker-built. The pirate sloop now closes to 50 m, where it was 55, because the new boat is a taller target. If pirates raid the player while they are ashore with their cabin ship and they beat them, the raid ends, the crews that kept spawning passively after the fight are gone and leftover raiders are removed from the street and the room, and the player cannot fast-travel away from a raid still going.
Player Trade ||| Player trade was added in 0.1.3720. In a trade each letter of credit shows its worth on its own row ("Worth 12,500 gold") across the pack, the offer and theirs, working on touch. A realm trade of an item looted in the last couple of minutes no longer fails, a character whose trade is on hold can no longer move its crafted pieces through another character, and when the realm refuses a trade because a character is under review it says whose character it is. A purse, letter of credit or pile of gold holding more than 2,147,483,647 gold, more than Daggerfall itself can count, is flagged as tampered, and that character cannot trade, sell or send gold to other players until the problem is gone or staff clear it. The third time the realm's rules hold a character's trading it is frozen for a week and then reopens on its own, each later time for a month, strikes clearing a month after the last one, and the notice names the item and says when trading reopens. Pieces of Ruhn's Regalia (and the raiding parties' sets) enchanted before enchanting them was closed no longer freeze trading. Carrying a Weir Key no longer flags the character for review or freezes their trading, and characters whose trading was frozen for a week because of the Weir Key are unfrozen, a forged piece still carried holding its trade until it is gone. A crafted piece listed, auctioned or handed to a commission leaves the character's own pack (or home) on the realm at that moment and comes back as the very piece that left if cancelled, expired or unsold, and a crafted piece bought back from another player can be sold again even while its maker's trading is frozen.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy is built-in and enabled by default, where characters found houses with surnames and seats, choosing death mechanics (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage). Features include inherited stats/reputation, bequests, realm synchronization, permadeath protections, vampire/werewolf/wereboar Enduring exemptions, a fourth Family tab page ("How it works") explaining rules/inheritance/heirlooms/marriage/symbols, tree links ("New to the family?"), and wrapping long names. Passing the mantle online can no longer overwrite a sibling with the new heir's name and class, heirs resumed from the Online page start at the family seat instead of Privateer's Hold, and a home owned on a farm or manor can be made the family seat, with family members met there beginning their story there. The remains of the house's fallen appear where they fell, as a body with a line of light over it in dungeons and in the open, pressed like any body to take back their bones, heirloom and share of the purse, and only in the dungeon the relative actually fell in. Whether the player is "in a fight" is judged where they are, so remains no longer stay shut because of an enemy back in the street, and a duel, the player's own arena bout or a hostile ship within reach counts as a fight, also for Play as, Pass the mantle and weddings. Putting the bones back no longer leaves the death quest stuck. The family's record is checked against the family's own rules when it is saved to the realm.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying.
Ramming ||| Ramming works: hitting a ship with the bow at speed damages her and the player takes some back, bracing reduces the damage taken, and a galley's ram hits three times as hard. Ramming a ship hard enough locks the two ships together for 20 seconds, the enemy lowering its sails and slowing to a crawl while its guns keep firing, and the player can board straight away without battering the enemy into surrender first, the whole enemy crew fighting them, with the target card showing "Fouled - E: board" while the ship is in reach.
Revenant Arts ||| One revenant in three is born with an art: Mending, Ward, War Cry, Siphon, Barrage or Nova. Against the player from Rank II (with Enhanced AI), Mending, Ward and War Cry are channelled, shown by a coloured disc around its feet and broken by hitting or hurting it, while Siphon, Barrage and Nova are thrown at the player and marked on the ground like other telegraphed attacks; a paralysed revenant cannot use its art and a silenced one cannot either except War Cry. For the player once sworn, at any rank, Mending heals them when they drop low, Ward shields them and War Cry raises their Strength as it goes into a fight, and Siphon, Barrage and Nova strike hostile foes it can see within 12 m and hit harder at a higher rank. However many Menders are at the player's side they together heal at most once a minute (every 45 seconds with a fully hasted Grave), a Siphon gives the player a quarter of the life it draws, and the Revenants and Companions pages say what each art does.
Revenant Gear ||| Sworn revenants have ten gear slots (a weapon, a shield, a helm, two pauldrons, a cuirass, gauntlets, greaves, boots and a charm), opened by Gear on a companion's card on the Companions page, where pieces from the pack are given and Take back returns them; people, orcs and centaurs wear weapons and armour and every kind can wear a charm. A revenant's rank limits what it can bear (Rank I up to Silver and Magic, Rank II Dwarven and Rare, Rank III Adamantium, Rank IV Orcish and Legendary, Rank V Daedric), the wearable pieces being listed first and a piece it cannot wear saying which rank it needs. A given piece replaces what the revenant would have rolled and its rarity bonuses apply, a shield needs a whole one-handed weapon given with it, given gear wears down in battle with its condition shown and a broken piece not worn until repaired, and gear counts as the player's inventory, returned on release with a deserter keeping the more valuable half.
Revenant Missions ||| Mission on a sworn revenant sends it to any of Tamriel's nine provinces to Plunder for loot, Forage for crafting materials or Sell-sword for gold, taking one to four days of the character's time depending on the province (online those days pass only while playing, a day abroad being about two hours of play), the haul going straight into the purse, pack and Materials Bag on return. Each province has its own danger and goods (Skyrim's orichalcum and pelts, Morrowind's ebony and Dwemer metal, Black Marsh's venom), the odds depend on the revenant's rank and gear against the province's danger and are shown before sending, a good trip brings the whole haul and more loyalty, a hard trip half and a hurt revenant needing rest, and a revenant never deserts while abroad. Up to two can be abroad at once, and Recall brings one back at once empty-handed after the page asks.
Revenant Stars ||| Every sworn revenant has its own star tree, built like the Firmament and the Captain's Stars, opened by Stars on a companion's card on the Companions page. A revenant earns XP from fights won at the player's side and from missions (until level 10), up to level 20, getting one point per level after the first and one per rank past the first, so a Rank V revenant starts with 4 points and can reach 23. The Blade covers health and damage (keystones Deathless and Bloodrage), the Grave its art (stars make it up to 40% stronger and its cooldowns up to 25% shorter, Awakened Art teaches an artless revenant one of its kind's arts at 75% strength, keystone Grave Pact) and the Road missions and loyalty (better odds, up to +40% gold and materials, missions up to 15% shorter, smaller loyalty losses and shorter rests, keystones Fortune's Road and Undying Vigil). A click takes a star and those on the way to it, a right-click removes it, changes are pending until Accept, changing stars is free except while the revenant is abroad, and a revenant that deserts or betrays loses its stars.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy.
Ship Handling ||| Ships sail on every heading. Pointed straight into the wind a ship makes a little over half her best speed, where before she stalled or was blown backwards, and she is fastest with the wind behind her off to one side, with no more tacking. Ships are faster under sail (a galleon with the wind on her side nearly two thirds faster), a storm drives her harder up to twice a fair breeze, and in fog she keeps nearly a third of her speed. They pick up speed in about 7 seconds instead of 25, slow to boarding speed in about 8, and turn on a wider circle at the faster speed. The rudder works at a standstill and bites hardest at half sail, rowing after striking sail no longer holds speed, and AI ships turn and gather speed at the player's own ship's pace. Classic handling is one switch away in Features, Naval Combat, Ship handling. Ships no longer stick on rocks or in place at sea, and one run aground can float off. With no enemies near, no ship chasing and no boarding or aiming going on, a ship's sails and oars give 15% more speed.
Ship Hold ||| F4 (rebindable, "Cargo" under Come Sail Away) opens the hold of the ship the player is on, at the helm, on deck or in the cabin, and away from it opens the last ship they helmed or the first ship in their fleet with a hold, wherever it is moored or laid up. The helm panel and each ship on the Fleet page have a Hold button, and the hold works as real storage in the Enhanced inventory, where cargo can be put in as well as taken out, a laid-up ship's packed weight updating when the hold closes, taking cargo out of a packed ship's hold counting its weight once, and the hold bindable to a controller button.
Ship Names ||| Any ship can be given a name of up to 24 characters that shows on her plaque for every player, through the same filter as player names. On the Fleet page Show name puts the ship's name on both sides of the bow on a dark wooden board with the name in gold in Daggerfall's own lettering, fitted to the hull's curve just below the rail and reading the right way from either side on every hull from the Rowboat to the Carrack, seen by every player; Hide name removes it, only a named ship can show it, and it is free and works wherever the ship is.
Ship Paint ||| On the Fleet page Paint lets the player choose a colour for each part of the ship (hull strakes, trim and gilding and sails on the Large Boat, Small Ship and Carrack, wood stain and sails on the Large Galley, wood stain on the Rowboat) from fourteen paints (Oxblood, Crimson, Tar black, Navy, Bay blue, Teal, Sea green, Forest green, Ochre, Gold leaf, White lead, Slate grey, Tyrian purple and Tan) or the original look, keeping the grain, seams and shading and the stern windows' night glow. Ships with a cabin (Small Ship, Large Galley and Carrack) can also take a cabin wood stain applying the next time the player enters it. Painting is free, works wherever the ship is and is seen by every player, and Remove paint restores the original look.
Ship Refits ||| Refits cost gold and materials (timber, iron and pitch) from the pack or her hold, with four tiers: Hold carries 20% more cargo per tier before she rides low, Rigging is 4% faster under sail, Hull gives 10% more hull and canvas, and Guns make cannon balls do 8% more damage. Bigger ships cost more, from half the price for a Rowboat to two and a half times for a Carrack, and a ship bought on the bank's credit cannot be refitted while that region's bank is owed anything. After the shipwright's three refit tiers each upgrade line has nine metal tiers (Steel, Silver, Elven, Dwarven, Mithril, Adamantium, Ebony, Orcish and Daedric), each needing a captain level from 3 for Steel up to 28 for Daedric and ingots of its metal or plain weapons and plate armour of that metal for the shipwright to melt down (never worn, locked or bound, player-made, enchanted, conjured, Magic-or-better, Legendary or socketed gear), ingots used first, with the refit panel showing what each tier adds now and next.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night. The galleon, carrack and large boat wear their own painted liveries again, with the oxblood strakes, the gilt and the stern gallery, in Daggerfall's own colour palette. Towns with ships in port run smoother online, the ships costing about a quarter of their old share of each frame and leaving far less garbage. Ship text no longer calls a ship "her" or "she" across the sea, the Fleet page, the shipyard, the crew hall, the Captain's Stars, the sea chart, the tutorials and the crew's chatter, saying "the ship", "this ship" or "it", and the 13 translated languages match. There is no stutter the first time a named or painted ship comes into view and painted sea ships no longer slow rendering.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away.
Shot Types ||| Each battery can be loaded with round, grape, chain or heated shot, the broadsides sharing one load and the bow chasers and stern guns each having their own. Grape kills crew and does little to the hull and loses force at long range, chain shreds sails and rigging, and heated shot often sets the hull on fire and a hit to the sails can set them burning. The load is changed with the mouse wheel while aiming, the helm's Shot button or the Shot key (unbound by default, under Combat), switching unloads a ready battery and reloads it, each load has its own ball, muzzle flash, splash and hit, and pirates coming alongside to board load grape.
Standing ||| Standing is how regions and the law see the player. A negative standing now recovers faster, a point every 7 days instead of every 112, with the law or a guild. A screen shows every region that knows the player's name, how it sees them and what it will cost, every change from a crime, sentence, penance, contract or raid defence is announced in a line, and the Enhanced Plus Standing page has a Dress line showing what the outfit is doing. For a seat, a low Tithe (half the cap or less) gives +2 each week, a high one (over three quarters) gives minus 3, and a seat held without a challenge gives +5, with the seat's board saying which way the Standing moved since the last Turning. Every change to a guild's standing online is said in chat ("Standing: Mages Guild +25 (now 40, 10 more to come)"), and the guild hall and Standing page show what the realm still owes the player at the daily pace of 15 a real day, days turning at midnight UTC. When a quest's reward passes the day's pace the player is told how much more is owed, or at 100 that the rest is lost, and joining a guild in the same minute as a standing loss is no longer dropped by the server. A standing below 0 also recovers a point every 7 days of the character's own time, resting and travelling counting and online time logged off too, quests and that recovery raising a standing at most 15 a day online with a chapter writ on a notice board adding 2 more, and the Standing page shows the quest count under each guild, temple or knightly order that would turn the player away.
Stats Card ||| The enhanced paperdoll card has a Stats button that flips it to a stats page. The back shows damage, hit chance, critical chance and what a critical does, backstab chance and expected damage, with a table per swing direction (sideways, thrust, down-right, down-left, overhead) and a bow's release, and a breakdown of weapon roll, material, Strength, proficiency, race, attack term, foe armour and dodging. Defence shows overall armour, the seven part numbers, dodging, biography and health with attributes and skills, and hit and damage are scored against a foe of the player's level in four armour classes using real armour numbers. It is Enhanced skin only. The stats screen shows resistances. Opening the Stats page no longer changes the player's next blows, where it used to spend a set's Riposte or Battlemage's Discipline and nudge the rounding of damage bonuses.
Titles ||| Titles are worn from the account card beside an aura or glyph, some given to particular players: SirMcMobdon, Tabby, Flylighter (Crystal Fist, with three purple slashes, alongside Disciple), Penitent (Diggleborf's, gold fading into sky blue), Aegis of Oblivion (Sureme's, violet, with a glyph of three pillars through a ring) and Primarch (made for GA00250, in light gold with a three-barred cross glyph). Players who first played on the evening of September 24 in the Americas hold the Founder title. Changing the aura, buying an insignia or unlinking Patreon no longer hides a Charter's title. A title or aura earned, including the Abyss Dungeon's Hourbreaker and The Turning Hour, gets a loot popup in Enhanced Plus the first time each is earned. The $50 Hierophant Patreon tier grants its title and glyph in game, the rose "Hierophant" and the crown beside the name, appearing on the next sign-in for an already linked Patreon. WikiMaid is SylviaBun's title, drawn amethyst into lilac into a bunny's blush, with a glyph of a lilac bunny in a maid's lace headdress beside her name (and on the account card, the chat roster and the travel view). The Elite Breach adds Spirewalker, Beacon-Lighter, Kynbane, Battlemage of the Spire, Wellhead-Walker, the Unfallen, the Lone Beacon, Spire Champion and First of the Spire.
Weir Keys ||| A Weir Key opens an Elite Breach and is spent on opening. Closing a Dagon's Breach can drop one: the first close always gives one, after that the chance is 1 in 6 (1 in 4 with the rite broken), and after five misses in a row the next close is certain; clearing an Abyss Dungeon's Hour drops one 1 in 3, a Valkynaz's Redoubt 1 in 4 (at most once a week), and elite foes in dungeons can drop one too (1 in 60 in an Abyss Dungeon, 1 in 150 in an Elite Dungeon, 1 in 400 in a Regular Dungeon). Everyone who had already closed a Dagon's Breach got one key as a launch gift, and the player must have closed a Dagon's Breach before they can open a keyed one. A key that fails to open comes back on its own, and carrying one no longer freezes trading.
