Accounts ||| Accounts hold a player's login and their cloud saves, served by an account Worker that issues tokens and checks passwords. The service was built in 0.1.3607 and 0.1.3690, then repaired after an outage caused by an overly costly password-hashing setting on Cloudflare, and later given a clock fix. The account server has since been protected from overload: after it fell over again in 0.1.6625 games now wait longer after each failed ask instead of retrying at once, and in 0.1.6640 signing in, joining a room and loading a town's house layouts were made far cheaper for it. The letterbox, the count over a town's Notice Boards and the play time now share one request to the account service instead of three, cutting its database work by about a third per player. A username can no longer be an email address: a player who signed up with one still signs in with it but other players see a generated name. After a server update players no longer all reconnect at once, each game waiting a moment (longer the more players are online), sign-in token requests and the account, realm, heartbeat and profession reads are answered cheaply from read copies of the database, the letterbox, Notice Board count and play time share one request, and when many players sign in at once they wait in a short line instead of getting a "server" error. Most menus that talk to the server (the Guild tab, the market, the Seat tab, letters, the account card and signing in) load much faster, since the account server now runs next to its database instead of across the Pacific, and joining the world, the chat's player list and the friends list come up sooner after signing in. When the servers are overloaded the game holds off on loading homes, yards, seats, traders, guild invitations, Marks and the save list and asks again later, waiting longer the longer the overload lasts, while actions such as buying, crafting, saving and claiming still go through, and the servers also turn those requests away for a moment to recover. Town homes, rooms and decorations, gathering nodes, traders, Marks, guild invites, lineages, unions, cloud saves, the Arena board and the town seats list now load from read copies of the database instead of the one main database. Logging in, saving and the town, seat and notice-board panels stay responsive at peak hours, as several of the busiest requests ask the account database far fewer times, and when the service is overloaded the game backs off its background checks (mail, the notice board, the day's motherlodes) instead of asking again at once.
Arena Bouts ||| An arena bout is a fight on the sand in which nobody dies. At 1 health the fighter goes down, and at 15% or less they can sheathe to yield. The healers make everyone whole afterwards, purses run from 50 gold for a first-tier win to 10,000 for the Grand Champion, and progress, titles and record are saved with the character. The player cannot rest, travel or leave through the gates during a bout, and set bonuses, sigil weapon bonuses and Legendary powers switch off against other players. An enchantment's own bite, such as a Damned blade's or any Health Leech weapon's, no longer kills the player in a bout but leaves them at 1 health like every other blow there. Hits from one side of the opponent no longer always count as backstabs, and backstabs no longer happen against players. The arena waits for the server instead of briefly showing the player's offline rank and season, their title and wins never having been lost.
Copied Items ||| When the realm finds the same item in two places it locks that item only, so the character keeps trading and nobody has to review it. A locked item stays with the player: it cannot be traded, sold, dropped or left where others can take it, while the wagon and the player's own storage still take it. The player is told which item was locked, once on each device, and an item handed over by someone whose game closed before it saved the hand-off is locked but no longer counts against them. Shop shelves no longer hand back an item already taken, and another player can no longer plant a copy of the player's item through a body or a container.
Crime ||| A crime is now charged once. Running from one group of guards and meeting another no longer charges it twice, and a crime committed while fleeing the watch can no longer reach the court uncharged and raise the player's standing with its sentence. Pleading guilty no longer loops: the plea waits until the Crime tutorial page on top of it has been read, so each plea is answered once and the fine is taken and the sentence halved only once. Anything stolen is marked stolen, including a robbed caravan's goods and the hold of a lawful ship boarded or sunk, so a merchant still buys it but never puts it back on the shelf. When the player is found guilty (jail, a fine or banishment) the guards take every stolen item they carry and say how many, while winning the case or leaving through the Thieves Guild keeps them, the player's own items put into someone else's cupboard and taken back are not marked stolen, and paying a fine or doing a penance restores reputation with the people of the kingdom too.
Dual Wielding ||| Equipping a one-handed weapon (sword, dagger, mace or war axe) in each hand draws and swings both, with H choosing which hand leads and no switch delay with both hands up. A shield, two-handed weapon, bow, or torch or lantern held in a hand still means one weapon. A pair is quicker, not twice as strong: the next blow straight after a strike comes from the other hand a little sooner, the off hand does 80% damage with -5 to hit at Agility 50 (shrinking to nothing at higher Agility and rising to -10 at lower), a heavy off-hand weapon swings slower, and the off hand's blows count as 0.8 of a blow for on-strike enchantments, per-hit powers and skill training. Measured, a matched pair of light weapons deals about 9% more damage per second than one of them and a heavier pair about 4%, for up to a quarter more fatigue per second and giving up the shield. Each weapon in a pair shows its own element with weapon glow on, the other weapon lowers straight down when one attacks, and the same rules apply in duels, sieges and the open PvP zone.
Dungeon Map ||| The dungeon map was reworked as an enhanced map with a 3D view, where holding the right mouse button turns and tilts the map at the same time and steep ramps in hallways fill in once walked. Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, and a secrets tier shows hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, and its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies. The compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked, optional exact quest guidance marks a quest's people and things on the map and compass, and secret walls are no longer labelled "Door". Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, with a secrets tier showing hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies, and the compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked. Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top. A headstone the party has searched counts as searched for the whole party and the game says who searched it, coffins a stranger opened in tombs are the player's to search (including standing right next to one), and party members no longer vanish for a few seconds when the player goes through a tomb door.
First-Person Arms ||| First-person arms follow the camera by default, with Morrowind's lag one toggle away, and the weapon and arms tilt with the look. An earlier version fixed to the screen like classic Daggerfall was reworked, and the poisoned arms toggle was abandoned. Bare-handed, both fists are on screen in the Morrowind arms too, punching in turn each with its own hand, and a pair of weapons shows the left weapon in the left hand swinging with its own blows, now on the game's own skeletons as well, with the arm not attacking dipping slightly at the shoulder. The arms pump while the player runs empty-handed, and in third person with two weapons the arms swing against each other as the player walks or runs.
Fists ||| Bare-handed, both fists are on screen, punches alternating between the hands: the hand that leads opens, the other answers if the player strikes again quickly, and the idle fist tucks in toward the middle. Each punch shows the arm that throws it on its own side, using Daggerfall's own fist art, which has punches for both arms.
Gathering ||| Gathering covers herb patches, ore veins, boulders, trees, bodies and fishing water. E acts on one only when it is looked at, in reach and unobstructed, and a node that cannot yet be worked says why on its row (tool, rank or daily limit). Actions use the loot list, picked by clicking, activating or tapping, with a small list window on the Classic skin and touch screens. The minigames are drawn on and around the crosshair with one short hint that fades, progress as diamonds and a sound for every strike, find, slip, splash and tug. Without a Materials Bag everything gathered goes in the pack even over its weight, and the message says so. A herb patch, vein, boulder or tree goes the moment the player finishes working it instead of waiting for the server to answer, coming back if the server refuses the harvest, and goods, XP and rank-ups arrive faster after each harvest. Gathering past the Veil works on the islands' herbs, trees, ore veins and boulders as in the Bay, each spot giving its full finds only once three players with accounts at least a week old have gathered there, and fishing in the Eltheric Ocean works. Every gathering act has its own sounds (felling, mining, herbs and the Basket, skinning, net fishing, goods going into the bag and a chime for a clean finish or a new rank), sounding the moment the tool hits and from where it happens, and on the Enhanced skin each axe or pick blow dips the view and hands slightly, following Technique camera motion. Other players see and hear the gathering, covered by their Attack sounds switch. The sounds are part of Enhanced sounds, and with it off or on the Classic skin Daggerfall's own sounds play, timed to the blows. The loot panel and its tooltip no longer cover skinning, or any other gathering minigame, while it plays.
Guild Hall ||| A guild hall is a guild-owned house bought by the guildmaster with treasury funds, featuring member chests, crafting, resting, customizable painting and yard furnishings, and a hall's roll as the first book on its shelf detailing its chapter's standing, Master, and officers. Windows inside buildings are now see-through, town boards fill in once three players have visited guild halls, and in rooms with many lamps, stationary people cast shadows from every lamp rather than just the nearest twelve. Online, if the player does not yet qualify for the next rank, the guild popup lists what is missing: the rank's name ("Next rank: Swordsman"), reputation against what is needed ("Reputation 14 of 20"), the guild-skill level the player is short of with their best guild skill named, a second guild skill at the lower bar and the guild's own skills by name. It does not appear when nothing is missing or at the top rank, and it shows on the enhanced UI skin and in all 13 supported languages. At rank 7 the hall says the next rank is a chapter chair (a council chair or the Master's) won by Merit at each Sunday's Turning, asking reputation 80, 14 real days in the guild and an account 7 real days old, and a service only a chair opens (the Mages Guild's Teleport, Zenithar's summoning) says so at any rank. The new Guild Chapters tutorial page opens when the rank is held at 7. The hall and the Standing page show what the realm still owes the player (already earned, arriving at most 15 a real day) and warn before a review puts the player on probation or lowers their rank, with when and why, and on probation they are told the stakes: below -10 expelled at the review, below 0 it runs 28 more days, at 0 or more it ends, and online earning the next rank ends it sooner. The rank text scrolls on its own so Talk, Service and Reforge stay on screen.
Guilds ||| Guilds are player organisations founded on Renown 10 with restrictions on disbanding and standing reviews, functioning properly across regions, and online you can set entry permissions like an online home (Only me, My party, My guild, or Anyone). Townsfolk, shopkeepers, guild members, nobles and priests are dressed from a wardrobe of their own in Morrowind garments and colours, while a fighters' guild's people wear whatever armour came to hand. Online, a player is promoted the moment they qualify for the next rank by talking to a guild-service NPC at the hall, with no 28-day wait for a promotion, while demotion, probation and expulsion still wait 28 days and offline play is unchanged. Reputation earned before joining still counts, but a new member rises no faster than the guild records standing, about a rank and a half a day from the day they join, and the guild hall tells when the next promotion can come at the earliest. A member on probation is no longer expelled over reputation the guild still owes them, the probation being renewed instead. Online, Mages Guild quests "Guard the Guild" and "Protect an Honored Mage" need standing 10 and 20, and new apprentices are no longer handed them.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy.
Horse ||| Horses never die, can be fed to grow the player's bond, must be kept fed, and the player can own more than one. A horse walks by default and holding Run (Shift unless rebound) gallops it, as on foot. The gallop is as fast as before and spends the horse's breath, and a winded or starving horse walks even with Run held until it can gallop again. Holding Run gallops it anywhere, in town or out, as long as it has the breath, online too, though riding into someone at a gallop tramples them, which is a crime and brings the guards. A walking horse has a slower clop, a slower head nod in first person and a trot on the 3D horse, and the saddle panel says Walking. With Horse care off in Settings > Features, or on the classic interface, horses ride as they always did. A horse with a new coat met on the road no longer freezes the game. The horses' coats on the road's wagons are painted in under half the time, so the first seconds after entering the world stutter less. With Billboard Look on, Morrowind horses stand upright like the classic sprites. The saddle panel is a small block in the bottom-right corner, shown or hidden with F2 (rebindable in Controls), and the Horse care setting chooses When needed, Always or Off; on touch screens it no longer covers the compass. Horse care and wagon upkeep each have Off, Boons (the default) and Hard tiers like Climates & Calories: on Boons nothing is taken (a hungry horse is no weaker, a worn wagon no slower, and a wagon kept Sound rolls 10% faster), Hard is the system as it was, and Off is the classic horse or wagon. The first-person horse (and the cart's horse) darkens with the world around it, at night, in a cave mouth or under a storm, following the Features > First-person lighting switch like the weapon and hands.
Instances ||| At a dungeon or building where one of the player's quests is running, at Privateer's Hold, or where a party member is already in an instance, the door asks whether to enter the public world or the player's own instance, which only the player and their party can enter. An instance keeps its own looted chests and slain monsters (returning after half an hour) and closes for good when the quest that opened it ends, a party member's instance shows at the door as "Name's instance", and the door only asks when there is a real choice (homes and the Wrothgarian open zone never have instances). Esc at the door leaves without entering, and quest teleports, Recall, loading a save and staff teleports return the player to the instance they were in, or to the public room if it has closed. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, and at a quest's dungeon whose shared version is laid smaller the prompt says the quest's business is in the player's instance only.
Inventory ||| The inventory drag carries the icon, and the hold that sometimes did not take was fixed. Stack splitting, double-click equip and item lock were added (the last two Enhanced Plus only). It works on phones, with the character picture hidden until Body is tapped and tabs scrolling in one row, and the wagon, storage and Materials Bag have a search box and category menu, with Shift-click moving a whole stack from the pack into them. Item cards compare what the item would change against what it would replace, showing up or down by how much, "new", and the lines that would be lost. Gear can be marked as junk from its card and "Sell junk" at a shop puts every junk piece on the counter in one press, and quick loot has a "Take gear" setting (All, Magic+, Rare+) for the take-all key. Containers, the wagon, storage and pack pages holding hundreds of items open in a fraction of a second, where a 700-item container used to freeze the game for many seconds, clicking, taking and picking in a long list is near-instant, and item pictures fill in as they load without redrawing the whole window.
Keybindings ||| The keybinding standard (KB1) was introduced and audited, and shared keys were reworked in 0.1.4122. A key that is set but not confirmed says "Not saved yet - press Confirm." on its row, the top of the page counts the unsaved changes and highlights Confirm, and the Confirm bar no longer hides behind the Settings search bar or covers the row being bound on a phone. Polling a bound key no longer builds a list for every binding. Swing Weapon bound to Left Click, with mouse activate unbound, survives a restart. When a key is assigned that is already in use, the confirmation appears beside the binding being changed instead of at the top of the controls list.
Language ||| Settings > Interface > Language offers 13 languages besides English: Bulgarian, Czech, Danish, French, German, Indonesian, Italian, Korean, Norwegian, Portuguese (Brazil), Romanian, Russian and Spanish. The choice applies at once and is remembered, and if the browser reads one of them first the game offers it once in that language. The translations cover the game's own menus and screens (the front door and Settings, the pause window, the death screens, account and online messages, the Professions and Stores pages and every crafting station, the Features screen and every mod's settings) and are machine translated, which the game says. The classic windows draw any script, including Cyrillic, Greek, Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese and Korean, and the About pane credits each translation. The new card table's words and Hold'em's fold are translated into all 13 complete languages, and the magicka label uses Daggerfall Unity's own text so it follows an installed Daggerfall Unity language pack. Translations are updated in all 13 languages, including the guild names and messages.
Live Chat ||| Live chat has one World tab and was audited before the merge. Chat is spread over more servers: world chat is split sixteen ways by account and each region's chat four ways, so more players fit at once, the World tab's Online count stays accurate past 512 players and it lists everyone while there are no more than 512. Typing /find <name> (or /who <name>) finds anyone online by name, and the Guild tab lists guildmates online wherever they are. World and region chat, friends, parties and guild lines are steadier at busy hours, since chat-server traffic that the relay had been refusing now flows a way that cannot be refused, and when a room's server restarts just as the player connects they get in on the first try.
Loot Window ||| A loot-side click takes immediately, and the loot buttons were fixed. The loot list on a body or pile scrolls, so the mouse wheel (or the pad) reaches every item and the list shows how many items are above and below. Searching the wagon or storage keeps the player's place in the list, and on phones the window's two columns stay the same width and no longer run off the screen.
Magic Repair ||| Magic repairs work online. Enchanted items still mend only with Allow Magic Repairs, which is on by default and always on online. Armour, clothing and jewellery still mend Daggerfall's 1 point at a time, one piece every four game minutes and only for the player. The Repairs Objects enchantment repairs the player's gear again when they wear a Cast When Held or Item Deteriorates piece, which used to take every repair and undo it at once and is now repaired last. The Repairs Objects enchantment mends worn gear first, then a worn Cast When Held or Item Deteriorates piece, and only then what is carried, so damaged loot in the pack no longer takes the repairs while the ring on the finger wears down, and while such a piece is damaged it holds the repairs. With a Repairs Objects enchantment worn, every worn piece of armour and clothing repairs 2% of itself per tick, the same as weapons, so a battered steel set is whole in about an hour.
Market ||| The Market is a Notice Board tab for trading in Marks or gold, featuring grouped views (Buy, Sell, History), silver display, column names, supplier counters, and detailed split lists. Listings stand 72 hours for a 1% fee with immediate sales (minus 5% tax) and courier delivery, with strict gold/Drakes currency separation and item exclusions. Seat boards, market sales, and courier deliveries no longer slow down account services in regions where a guild holds a Charter. Market stalls look the way the pack draws them, with the keeper behind the counter and goods on the counter.
Online Death ||| Online death had fixes for a death that respawns and for the online death loop. The gear a fallen player leaves in the wilds holds only things that can be worn. A death taken back holds, and the operator has a Realm revive run. A party member's Resurrection saves a Bloodline character: while the death screen is up (up to two minutes) it brings the player back with no death on the family tree, no Succession and no tombstone. The death is held by the server, so closing the tab, reloading or logging in from another device does not escape it, and if the death is already settled the caster is told "<name> has fallen for good" and keeps their magicka.
Player Trade ||| Player trade was added in 0.1.3720. In a trade each letter of credit shows its worth on its own row ("Worth 12,500 gold") across the pack, the offer and theirs, working on touch. A realm trade of an item looted in the last couple of minutes no longer fails, a character whose trade is on hold can no longer move its crafted pieces through another character, and when the realm refuses a trade because a character is under review it says whose character it is. A purse, letter of credit or pile of gold holding more than 2,147,483,647 gold, more than Daggerfall itself can count, is flagged as tampered, and that character cannot trade, sell or send gold to other players until the problem is gone or staff clear it. The third time the realm's rules hold a character's trading it is frozen for a week and then reopens on its own, each later time for a month, strikes clearing a month after the last one, and the notice names the item and says when trading reopens. Pieces of Ruhn's Regalia (and the raiding parties' sets) enchanted before enchanting them was closed no longer freeze trading.
Privateer's Hold ||| Online, every character starts Privateer's Hold on their own, with its enemies alive, its doors shut and its loot untouched. Local chat in the Hold tells the player that nobody can hear them there. Online, Privateer's Hold is a shared dungeon like every other, with the same enemies, doors, levers and loot for everyone inside, and other players can be seen, chatted with and partied with there. Once the player leaves it they cannot go back in: its door, a Recall to a spot marked inside it and a quest that would send them there all say "You cannot go back into Privateer's Hold." and a Recall or quest arrival lands outside instead. A new game still starts inside the Hold and a character saved inside still loads back in, and offline nothing changes. The Hold can be visited again: its public hall is still for new characters only, but the player can return to their own instance of it at any time.
Professions ||| The Professions page shows all thirteen professions. At ranks 50 and 100 the player chooses a specialisation, the first choice free and later changes costing 1,000 Marks (silver today) and taking a week, with the old one working until then. A toast says what each harvest brought, a banner marks ranks 25, 50, 75 and 100, the first harvest each session says where goods went (the Stores) and which key opens them, and the Down arrow opens Professions and Stores on either skin. Professions listed as practised grew from Herbalism, Mining and Logging to include Smithing, Carpentry, Fishing, Masonry, Cooking and Jewelcrafting, with the page explaining how to gather each. Mining, Logging, Herbalism (the Basket included), Fishing and Hunting no longer stop for the day, and the chip under the compass and the Professions tab show the day's count without a limit. Some crafts were merged so there are five instead of eight: a merged craft keeps the higher of its two XP totals, offers four choices at a rank each named for the craft it acts on, and a player may still take two crafts past Journeyman, now two of five. Each rank's choice is the higher craft's where it chose, else the other's. A pending perk change no longer blocks choosing perks when the player reaches a new rank.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate.
Running Hands ||| Running with nothing in either hand shows the arms pumping in step with the stride (on the head bob's own rhythm) using Daggerfall's own fist-and-forearm frames, the arm always running off the bottom of the screen. The arms keep running through a short hop, stay smooth when the player stops and starts, ease away when a spell is readied and work with every mod switched off, and a punch thrown mid-run shows at once. The running fists stay low, rising about a third of the way up the screen, and show their back and knuckles like fists at rest.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night. The galleon, carrack and large boat wear their own painted liveries again, with the oxblood strakes, the gilt and the stern gallery, in Daggerfall's own colour palette. Towns with ships in port run smoother online, the ships costing about a quarter of their old share of each frame and leaving far less garbage.
Shipyards ||| Port towns have a shipyard on their seaward edge, its back to the water, where the shipmaster sells every ship Come Sail Away builds, from the Rowboat to the Carrack and the Large Galley, and buys the player's ships back. One window holds Ships for sale (each hull's size, crew, guns and price, with buying asking for confirmation), Your fleet (where each ship lies, her worth and what the shipmaster will pay, with Bring her here, lay up, rename and sell, a big sale paid as a letter of credit) and the Shipwright and Refits, which need Naval Combat on. The Rowboat goes into the pack as parts and every other ship's deed goes into the Fleet laid up at that port. The shipmaster will not buy a ship with cargo in her hold, anything in her cabin or her crew ashore with the player, or one that came into the realm through customs, and a sold ship's cabin furniture is sold too with half its value going to the bank. Townsfolk tell the player where the shipyard is and it is marked on the town map and as an anchor on the Overworld. Shipmaster cards show each ship's own 3D picture, and the Reforge, shipyard and naval-yard pages and a merchant's options each have their own icon. Towns away from the open sea, such as Ilessan Hills, no longer have a shipyard and a lighthouse beside a pond. The shipyard's fleet page reads properly again.
Stable ||| The Stable page displays horses and wagons at a glance with Summon, Send away, and renaming. Bond progress bars, food hours for fed horses, winded state notices, origin stat hovers, and coloured wagon condition bars with Wagon Yard repair costs are included. The Stable's horse look is swatches and arrows instead of large buttons. Sending a wagon or horse away from the Stable keeps them there, no longer reappearing outside the next building or dungeon entered, and a wagon or horse never sent away is shown as With you. With horse care Off, the Stable page shows every horse the player owns and lets them ride any of them.
Standing ||| Standing is how regions and the law see the player. A negative standing now recovers faster, a point every 7 days instead of every 112, with the law or a guild. A screen shows every region that knows the player's name, how it sees them and what it will cost, every change from a crime, sentence, penance, contract or raid defence is announced in a line, and the Enhanced Plus Standing page has a Dress line showing what the outfit is doing. For a seat, a low Tithe (half the cap or less) gives +2 each week, a high one (over three quarters) gives minus 3, and a seat held without a challenge gives +5, with the seat's board saying which way the Standing moved since the last Turning. Every change to a guild's standing online is said in chat ("Standing: Mages Guild +25 (now 40, 10 more to come)"), and the guild hall and Standing page show what the realm still owes the player at the daily pace of 15 a real day, days turning at midnight UTC. When a quest's reward passes the day's pace the player is told how much more is owed, or at 100 that the rest is lost, and joining a guild in the same minute as a standing loss is no longer dropped by the server.
Town Watch ||| The town watch keeps the town day and night in three eight-hour shifts plus a day off. Patrols walk in pairs around their own district, in larger towns a watchman stands at each gate, off duty he wears his own clothes as a townsperson, and larger towns keep a larger watch. In the 14 cities whose palace stands in its own walled grounds the watch now lives where its door opens onto the streets, so it walks its patrols instead of staying at the palace. A watchman on duty greets the player by their standing with the region's law, a first minor offence in a region draws a Hold! warning and not an arrest (the next brings one), and the watch shouts "Halt!" from one watchman at most once every 15 seconds. A monster that gets into town can attack townspeople, one blow killing one, and the watch defends them as it does the player. Paying a fine no longer counts as killing the watchman who walked away. All of this is in the living world, and Classic play keeps Daggerfall's own watch and townspeople. The watch posted at a city's gates stops strangers coming in and asks their business, and strangers answer civilly, curtly or with open hostility, sellswords and sailors rougher; patrols stop strangers on their rounds, every stranger stopped at a gate is questioned, and a gate stop no longer keeps a visitor from leaving. The watch breaks up a quarrel even at a change of shift, and only one of a patrol pair calls the hour. When a monster attacks in town the guards still come to help but walk off after the fight instead of vanishing, leaving the world only once far away or behind the player, and if another monster turns up while they walk off the same guards turn back to the fight, a guard attacked on his way out defending himself before carrying on. Paying the watch's fine on the spot raises standing in that region by 5, up to the point where the watch stops recognising the player, where a paid fine only bought a day without stops before. Guards called into a building walk round through the doorway to reach the player, even between deep shelves or inside a closet, and attack when they get there, a locked door or a ladder keeping them out for only two seconds, and guards and dungeon enemies standing next to the player turn to face them and attack instead of standing still.
Weapon Glow ||| Weapons with a fire, frost or shock damage bonus, whether the weapon's own or from a Ruby or Sapphire set in it, wear an animated outline of that element: fire gives off rising flames and drifting embers, frost forms a breathing rime with glinting ice spikes and shock crackles along the edge and throws off arcs. The bigger the bonus the bigger and brighter the outline. On the Morrowind first-person and third-person models the outline wraps the 3D weapon and the blade's surface is charged with its element, an enchanted Morrowind weapon with no element glows with an arcane light, and once the servers are updated other players' weapons glow on their Morrowind bodies. A weapon not yet identified keeps its element hidden, and the glow can be turned off under Settings > Combat > Weapons in hand > Weapon glow, which decides only what the player sees.
Yard ||| A yard is the ground around the player's house. Standing on their own lot and pressing Decorate places catalogue pieces outside, priced as inside, wholly on the lot, up to 60 pieces a yard, seen by everyone walking the street. A piece can stand at most 4 m above the ground, so stacking towers is no longer possible, though pieces already higher stay put and can be moved down or removed. Outside, pieces hold nothing and give no light and doors are for doorways only, a piece on the road is refused, and a long piece turned at an angle can no longer be placed through the house's corner. Other players' yard decorations no longer draw across the whole map and tiny yard pieces stop drawing once only a few pixels tall, with a Settings > Other players > Yard decorations option of Off, 75 m, 150 m or 300 m (the default), the player's own yard always showing. Yard pieces standing above the 4 m height limit were removed with no refund, and property lines are shared with neighbours, the ground between two houses split down the middle, so decorating in front of a neighbour's door is no longer possible. A shop, temple or tavern next to a house no longer claims half its yard, while the ground between two houses is still split down the middle. A player's yard takes far fewer draw calls, its 3D pieces drawn together and its repeated sprites drawn as one, helping most in the browser version and on weaker CPUs and drivers. A yard piece just placed, moved or removed no longer snaps back on the player's own screen for up to a minute afterwards, and stalls and boulders placed in the player's own yard are back where they were put, at the size they were placed.
