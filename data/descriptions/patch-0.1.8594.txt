Account Recovery ||| Account recovery lets an operator issue a recovery code (RECOVER-OP) so a player can regain access to an account. New accounts get a set of 8 recovery codes, each working once if the password is forgotten. The Account section of the profile shows how many are left, and Make new codes gives a fresh set on typing the password and one current code, after which the old codes stop working. Codes can be copied or saved as a file (dfo-recovery-codes-<username>.txt), using a code spends only that code and using the last one gives a fresh 8, someone who learns the password alone cannot replace the codes, and the codes screen cannot be closed by accident until the player confirms they have written them down.
Billboard Look ||| Billboard Look is a setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data, off by default. On, Morrowind bodies and horses stand upright like the classic sprites, so they look shorter when the camera is above them; off, they keep their true height from every angle.
Chat Channels ||| Chat channels were added to chat in 0.1.3961. Trade (buy, sell and swap with players anywhere, /trade or /tr) and LFG (find a group or one more for yours, /lfg) are opt-in channels, so the tab bar stays as it was until wanted. A + beside the chat tabs picks channels, one click joining or leaving with joined channels showing a checkmark, and /join trade, /join lfg, /leave trade, /leave lfg and the switches in Settings > Other players > Chat work too, saying /trade or /lfg before joining joins the channel.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster. The chat has a search bar above the player list where part of a name, or # and a tag, shows every tab's lines from or about that player, and /search <name> searches from the chat field while /search alone clears it. Chat tabs scroll sideways with the mouse wheel, the edge with more tabs fading and a clicked tab scrolling fully into view. Titles no longer appear in chat, while guild tags and glyphs still show after names and the title still shows over the head and on the Inspect card. The chat roster search finds every known player, not just the first page. /rp <text> says a line of roleplay on the current tab in green italics, and /help lists /trade, /lfg, /join, /leave and /rp.
Compass ||| The compass marks things around the player including nearby ships, gathering spots within 150 m in profession colours, and placed boats as teal markers, working on Classic and Enhanced compasses and the Overworld view, with redone lighting where floors read as stone and brass instead of amber. Compass marks draw over the Grimoire frame.
Crew Spirits ||| Crew spirits rise with victories, prizes, plunder, port time and grog, and sink with losses and wrecks. High spirits mean faster reload, quicker mending and an extra boarding hand, and low spirits slow them. Once the crew has had the day's grog, the shipwright shows their real spirits (such as "low") and says "Tomorrow" instead of claiming they are as high as they go.
Dungeon Respawn ||| A dungeon's dead stay dead. Ordinary dungeons outside the Wrothgarian zone respawn after 20 minutes and elite dungeons after 40. Chests and foes in Abyss Dungeons and Wrothgarian Zone dungeon halls come back after 30 minutes instead of an hour.
Horse ||| Horses never die, can be fed to grow the player's bond, must be kept fed, and the player can own more than one. A horse walks by default and holding Run (Shift unless rebound) gallops it, as on foot. The gallop is as fast as before and spends the horse's breath, and a winded or starving horse walks even with Run held until it can gallop again. Holding Run gallops it anywhere, in town or out, as long as it has the breath, online too, though riding into someone at a gallop tramples them, which is a crime and brings the guards. A walking horse has a slower clop, a slower head nod in first person and a trot on the 3D horse, and the saddle panel says Walking. With Horse care off in Settings > Features, or on the classic interface, horses ride as they always did. A horse with a new coat met on the road no longer freezes the game. The horses' coats on the road's wagons are painted in under half the time, so the first seconds after entering the world stutter less. With Billboard Look on, Morrowind horses stand upright like the classic sprites.
Joinery ||| Every city and town has a Joinery beside its Stables and Wagon Yard, room permitting: a carpenter's open-air yard with a shingled workshop, a timber rack, a log pile, sawhorses and a chopping block, and a table, chairs and a bench on show by the gate. Its workbench is a Carpentry station, opened by talking to the carpenter or using the sign or the bench, which opens the Stores page at The Workbench, with a craft or a saw costing 50 gold as at a Furniture Store's bench and carpentry still done online. It can be found by asking townspeople under "Carpenters", on either town map, or by the saw mark on the Overworld map. Using a Joinery's workbench, or opening Esc > Stores afterwards, no longer crashes the game.
Lamp Keeper ||| Every so often a shopkeeper is a lamp. It has its own lines and trades as usual, and talking to it earns the title I Love Lamp online when signed in to a registered account.
LeFay Monument ||| A monument to Julian LeFay, one of the creators of Daggerfall, stands outside Privateer's Hold, beside the path every new character walks out on (it moved there from Gothway Garden),, with three granite steps, a marble pedestal, a gilt-tipped obelisk and a bronze plaque on each side. Looking at it offers Throw flowers or Read the inscription, and in Info mode a press reads the inscription. A thrown flower lands on the steps or at the foot on the player's side, each character keeps its 48 newest flowers through save and load, and the plaque counts how many were laid. Townspeople walk around it, and online the flowers are the player's alone while others see the monument.
Lighthouses ||| Port town lighthouses feature whitewashed stone towers with red bands, glass lantern rooms, copper domes, and keeper's cottages. Beacons are lit dusk to dawn, two beams sweep the sea/town, and doors/keepers allow climbing to galleries on foot without falling hazards. Lighthouse and shipyard office windows have small leaded panes, and a tutorial page exists. The lighthouse beacon burns with Daggerfall's own brazier fire.
Loot Rarity ||| Loot rarity is a Diablo-style ladder over Daggerfall's loot, built on Unleveled Loot's law and on by default, with the ladder shown on the items. Clothing can drop as Magic and Rare with lines of its own: standing with a social group, warmth and weatherproofing (for Climates & Calories), Personality, and street skills, with footwear leaning to movement skills. Superior and Masterwork clothing the player makes comes out Magic and Rare, as crafted weapons and armour already did. Six Legendary garments each have a power: the Alik'r Wayfarer's Robes (heat felt 20 less), the Wrothgar Pass Mantle (cold felt 20 less), the Vestments of Stendarr (Restoration spells cost 25% less), the Pilgrim's Sandals (tire 25% slower, falls hurt half as much), the Silks of Queen Barenziah (+5 standing with every group) and the Shade's Cowl (+25 Stealth while hooded). With the loot rarity setting off, clothing stays exactly as in Daggerfall. Resistance from rolled gear counts up to 45% per element, and only fills an Aetheric piece's resistance up to that same 45%. Hone raises one line within its own range, and some Rares and Legendaries carry an empty socket. One Rare or Legendary item in twelve from a body or a pile is cursed, with one extra bonus line and one drawback: a bad reputation, bad reactions, damage or wear in holy places, weaker blows against a kind of foe, or a health leech. Gilded is the new top of the rarity ladder, above Aetheric and Artifacts, with its name in gold leaf and a frame breathing light in every slot. A rarity affix of +N% casting speed rolls on jewellery and weapons and adds up across everything worn, up to 30%. Rare weapons can no longer roll Vampiric Effect (weapons that already have it keep it), life leech from a weapon's lines and gems together is capped at 10% of each hit, Hist-Sap regenerates 1% a round (2% under half health), and Open Hand deals half damage on the second bare-handed hit. Potency, a line on jewellery and weapons, gives +3% to +15% spell damage (at most +30% from everything worn), and Wellspring, a jewellery line, returns 2% to 10% of magicka when a foe is killed (at most 10% a kill). Three caster Legendaries are found most often among mages, covens and laboratories: The Eye of Magnus (Destruction spells meet a saving throw 15 lower), The Ring of Artaeum (1% of magicka back every magic round, 2% under half) and Shalidor's Brand (Destruction spells strike 25% harder while magicka is above three quarters). Spell damage from loot is capped at +50% all together and none of it reaches a fight against another player. The Gauntlets of the Rose's second hit lands on the Oblivion Gate's boss. The life leech line reads "N% life leech on this weapon's own blows", and an off-hand weapon's leech applies only when fighting with that hand (switch with H).
Magic Repair ||| Magic repairs work online. Enchanted items still mend only with Allow Magic Repairs, which is on by default and always on online. Armour, clothing and jewellery still mend Daggerfall's 1 point at a time, one piece every four game minutes and only for the player. The Repairs Objects enchantment repairs the player's gear again when they wear a Cast When Held or Item Deteriorates piece, which used to take every repair and undo it at once and is now repaired last.
Morrowind Body ||| The Morrowind body features climbing poses (hanging, shimmying, wall climbing, leaps, pull-overs, vault tucks), gripping animations, first-person hand reach, crowd optimization, port-made steel plate armor sets, synchronized attack speeds, spell-casting animations, and T-pose fixes. Placed home/yard decoration NPCs stand in Morrowind bodies, you can ride in third person, look down over the 3D horse's neck in first person, and 3D horses match sprite coats with unique flank markings. With the Morrowind model in first person the arms grip the wall while climbing even with the weapon sheathed. The cape hangs from the chest alone and moves only with it as one stiff sheet, so walking, swinging the arms and sitting no longer bend it, and leaning back swings the whole cape with the chest.
Morrowind People ||| With the Morrowind body on, the people and creatures around the player stand in Morrowind bodies too: dungeon and wilderness foes, the city watch, townsfolk, quest givers, stable and wagon yard keepers, ship crews, siege fighters, road parties and the Oblivion Gate's boss and host. Foes wear the armour and carry the weapons they rolled with a race and face of their own, townsfolk are dressed by their station, foes swing, flinch and fall with their tells on the body, and a body is drawn at its sprite's size. Creatures use Morrowind's creatures where Daggerfall's have a match and keep their classic sprite otherwise. Online everyone sees a foe as its owner does. Bodies are drawn for the nearest people only within one budget for the whole frame, and a Morrowind People setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data is Off, Near (the default, up to 24 bodies) or All (up to 48). With Morrowind models on, other characters and horses keep their size wherever the camera is, and NPCs whose helmet fails to load show their head. The horses that stand in towns, at World of Daggerfall's bandit camps and forts and in the Stables stand as the 3D horse (the nearest eight within 60 m), and people who used to stay as sprites, such as a town's figures that belong to no faction, World of Daggerfall's dock people, its captives, merchants and prisoners and the crews on Come Sail Away's boats, stand in Morrowind bodies. Past the body range the sprites still stand, as do other animals, children, vampires' people and Daedra Princes. With Morrowind attached, Breton men wear three new heads and three new hairs (nine faces between them), each of the ten Breton portraits at character creation shows the face most like it (portrait 6 wearing the eyepatch), and if the heads fail to load Morrowind's own Breton faces show. Eleven of Daggerfall's townspeople (all four Redguard men, all four Nord men, two Breton men and the hooded Redguard woman) wear their own 3D outfits in their picture's colours, with hoods over the hair, sleeves, hems and boots moving with the body, legs staying inside skirts and robes, and a woman wearing her modest shirt while Show Nudity is off. Townspeople without an outfit yet keep their varied wardrobe.
Online Death ||| Online death had fixes for a death that respawns and for the online death loop. The gear a fallen player leaves in the wilds holds only things that can be worn. A death taken back holds, and the operator has a Realm revive run.
Online Mode ||| Online mode is the enhanced lane whole: every enhancement and every mod is on while online. Night gates and guild halls were added online (OL5). An out-of-date game can no longer go online and stays offline until updated, and some updates disconnect everyone online once when they go live. The macOS app and the portable Windows app do not update themselves and need the newest build downloaded. The multiplayer server restarts when updates arrive, disconnecting everyone for a moment so they reconnect on their own. Games in a crowded town send less and the server bundles other players' movements, and an area next to a duel, a Wrothgarian zone fight or a sea serpent fight says so. Loading an online character into the world takes about 3 seconds less, since the game asks the server for the family, character list, towns' homes and homes of arrival towns all at once while it reads the world.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid.
Performance ||| A GPU memory leak from opening the map in a dungeon or building was fixed, long sessions and long journeys no longer accumulate video or GPU memory because left areas are released over time, settings are read faster everywhere, and people, guards, and foes do not pop out of view at screen edges. Opening the Overworld no longer freezes the game as nearby dungeon models load one per frame after the view rises, deep snow processing is disabled while the Overworld is open, and saving in snowy areas is faster because snow-track data is recompressed only when changed. Many town and frame costs were cut: still flats share large buffers and one index buffer, yards and stables behind the player or far away are not drawn, collision no longer grows with the view distance, lamp shadows redraw only the sides someone is in front of and reuse last frame's answer, night towns and harbours make less garbage, shaders compile in the background, the sky skips stars by day and two-graphics-card laptops use the faster card. Entering the world freezes for less, since working out where every town's pilgrims go on their holy days and picking the day's elite halls in the Wrothgarian zone no longer stalls the game, and a town with decorated yards in view makes about 17-18% fewer draw calls a frame. Clicking in the world no longer stutters, and arriving in a large city or walking out of a building in one no longer freezes the game for several seconds, worst in Firefox.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed.
Resting ||| Online, the player rests at a lit fire, a bed (room, house, ship's cabin or dungeon bed on their floor) or a guild hall that allows it. They hold still six seconds, the rest breaks when an enemy comes, they are hurt or the fire goes out, and when the bar fills they sleep a whole night, eight hours on their own clock, in one step, healing health, stamina and magicka as it fills. A night comes once every ten minutes of play, resting sooner being a short rest that heals and passes no time, and the enhanced HUD's status bar shows a Rested tile counting minutes to the next night. A night at a fire, tent or bed heals fully while a guild hall floor is rough, monsters can still find the player at night, and the loiter option is gone online since the sky runs on real time. Resting ages spells, diseases, hunger and skill checks as an hour of rest should. Resting while poisoned or diseased warns the player first. Online, the tile counting down until the player can sleep through a night again is called "Next night", not "Rested". Beds in dormitory-style inn rooms (and ship bunks) offer Rest and Loiter again.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night.
Sitting ||| Using a chair, bench or throne inside a building sits the player on it, in the same pose as at the card tables, with other players seeing them seated and third person showing them on the chair. Moving, jumping, attacking, pressing Escape or using it again stands them up.
Terrain ||| Terrain was moved off the main thread so the crossing hitch dies, the terrain block is built once a frame, and the terrain scale Daggerfall Unity streams at was adopted. Rolling hills run across the whole country, gentle in some regions and rolling hard in others and taller on the high ground, and rivers and streams cut their own valleys with the water at the bottom. Each climate has its own landform: mountains have long ridgelines that hold their line for kilometres with spurs and smooth valleys, the Alik'r has dune seas lying across the wind with a long rise to windward and a steep slip face to lee, swamps are near-flat with shallow hollows and low hummocks, and rainforests have karst towers standing over plains. Where climates meet, their lands blend across about a map pixel. The Dragontail and Wrothgarian Mountains are taller and sharper, with pointed peaks up to 400 m over the ridges where they were 200 m and rounded, on ridges 40% taller, while the mountain woods around them keep their rounded hills.
Text Font ||| The Text Font feature includes a "My font" option taking the name of a computer-installed font, applying changes at once. The installed-font picker is a scrolling, filterable list.
Textures ||| Textures had incident fixes, one shape for every texture door, and performance work that deleted work instead of reducing quality (PERF-TEX/TEX2/UI and MENU1-WARM). The steel plate cuirass has its skirt, painted with its own texture. The desert ground has new sand textures with ripples, from Sands of the Alik'r. Ships (the galleon, the carrack and the large boat), wagons (the Wagon Cart, Open Wagon, Caravan and its room), the Stable, Wagon Yard and Joinery, the shipyard and lighthouse, the LeFay monument's stone and the tapestries, banners and rugs in towns use Daggerfall's own textures, read from the player's own Daggerfall files, with the ships wearing the same textures as Come Sail Away's. The caravan's windows and the ships' stern windows are Daggerfall's leaded window that can be seen through, and the signboards STABLES, WAGONS, JOINERY, SHIPS and CREW are lettered on Daggerfall planks.
Titles ||| Titles are worn from the account card beside an aura or glyph, some given to particular players: SirMcMobdon, Tabby, Flylighter (Crystal Fist, with three purple slashes, alongside Disciple), Penitent (Diggleborf's, gold fading into sky blue), Aegis of Oblivion (Sureme's, violet, with a glyph of three pillars through a ring) and Primarch (made for GA00250, in light gold with a three-barred cross glyph). Players who first played on the evening of September 24 in the Americas hold the Founder title. Changing the aura, buying an insignia or unlinking Patreon no longer hides a Charter's title. A title or aura earned, including the Abyss Dungeon's Hourbreaker and The Turning Hour, gets a loot popup in Enhanced Plus the first time each is earned.
Town Watch ||| The town watch keeps the town day and night in three eight-hour shifts plus a day off. Patrols walk in pairs around their own district, in larger towns a watchman stands at each gate, off duty he wears his own clothes as a townsperson, and larger towns keep a larger watch. In the 14 cities whose palace stands in its own walled grounds the watch now lives where its door opens onto the streets, so it walks its patrols instead of staying at the palace. A watchman on duty greets the player by their standing with the region's law, a first minor offence in a region draws a Hold! warning and not an arrest (the next brings one), and the watch shouts "Halt!" from one watchman at most once every 15 seconds. A monster that gets into town can attack townspeople, one blow killing one, and the watch defends them as it does the player. Paying a fine no longer counts as killing the watchman who walked away. All of this is in the living world, and Classic play keeps Daggerfall's own watch and townspeople. The watch posted at a city's gates stops strangers coming in and asks their business, and strangers answer civilly, curtly or with open hostility, sellswords and sailors rougher; patrols stop strangers on their rounds, every stranger stopped at a gate is questioned, and a gate stop no longer keeps a visitor from leaving. The watch breaks up a quarrel even at a change of shift, and only one of a patrol pair calls the hour. When a monster attacks in town the guards still come to help but walk off after the fight instead of vanishing, leaving the world only once far away or behind the player, and if another monster turns up while they walk off the same guards turn back to the fight, a guard attacked on his way out defending himself before carrying on. Paying the watch's fine on the spot raises standing in that region by 5, up to the point where the watch stops recognising the player, where a paid fine only bought a day without stops before.
Wagon ||| Wagons stay attached without stretching, are drawn on the Overworld, and feature multiple vehicle types with storage, bench seating, harnesses, turning wheels, third-person camera support, and performance culling. Worn-out wagons never become unusable, broken-down save wagons are marked "Failing" so they can be driven again, and smiths/Repairs Objects enchantments cannot repair wagons or horses. A parked team's harness stops recomputing its ropes while nothing moves. Wagon paints are Daggerfall's colours, fabrics, woods, rugs, tiles and night sky, with every paint still applying, and carts, open wagons and caravans roll on the painted wheels they had before the Daggerfall-texture pass (seven wooden spokes, a dark hub and an iron tyre with its rivets).
