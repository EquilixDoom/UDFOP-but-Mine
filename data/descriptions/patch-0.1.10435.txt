Abyss Dungeons ||| An Abyss Dungeon is the hardest dungeon in the game, online only. One at a time rises beside a busy city in the region with the most players online, with a column of brass-gold light over it, and the first player to reach its door finds it and everyone online hears where, after which its ring, compass mark, Timers row and notice-board note stay until it is gone. It has three foes at every spot, each four times as tough and hitting two and a half times as hard, six elite foes, half again the loot and no campfires. At its deepest point stands the Rift into the Shattered Hour, where the Orrery of Endings, the Unmoored Steps and the Brass Remnant are met, and the Remnant's fight, spoils, collapse and one-life rule are shared by everyone in the Hour at once. Each Abyss Dungeon keeps one of six Endings and two of nine omens, and fighters earn Hours broken toward the Hourbreaker title and The Turning Hour aura. Crew and sworn companions wait outside the Rift, and leaving the Hour for any reason puts the player on the ground at the dungeon's door. A new one always rises by a town: when players are crowded at sea it goes to the busiest region on land or one of the Bay's great cities, and one that already rose at sea shows up by a great city.
Auras ||| Auras are cosmetic effects worn from the aura picker on the account card, each granted with a title. They include the Oblivion Ward, Golden Radiance, the Holo Shadow Cloak (with the Shadow Fang title), the developers' Seraph Wings and Crystal Resonance (with the Crystal Fist title). An aura now shows to everyone nearby within a second or two, up to 32 are drawn at once, and the cloak and wings fold away while riding or swimming. They sit correctly on both Morrowind bodies and Eye of the Beholder sprites. Lilac Warren is SylviaBun's aura: six lilac bunnies with cotton tails and little maid's headdresses hop round the player's feet on a ring of lilac light, leaving fading paw-print tracks, with an occasional happy binky and sparkles of lilac, pink and white. The Elite Breach adds Beaconlight (Redoubt 10), The Burning Stair (Redoubt 20) and Firstlight. Firstlight is a one-of-a-kind aura worn only by the first company to take Redoubt 10 of the Battlespire: a dial of the ten Redoubts lights round the wearer's feet one after another, ten spires of dawn-light rise as each is taken, and when the tenth falls a sunburst runs across the ground and a shaft of first light rises past the head. Witchfire, from the Witches' Festival, is an aura of green fire and tallow candles.
Blood ||| Blood is the port's own blood effect. Droplets fly from the wound under gravity and leave their mark on floor, wall or ceiling, with ceiling drops dripping back down. Spatter lands the way the blow threw it in one to three uneven clumps, pools are several blobs with fingers where the blood ran, and stain art is 128 pixels per stain, generated from noise and built in the background so the first hit does not stutter. Blood splashes no longer redo their image work on every hit.
Class Skill Swap ||| On the Growth tab, one class skill that is not mastered can be changed for a skill outside the class, in the same group, for 100,000 gold. Level and skill values stay the same, the page warns if the new skill could never be mastered, and an heir keeps the changed class. Repeating the swap could push a level far past what any class can reach, so a skill's training now leaves the class with it: level and progress never drop, but after giving up a trained skill for a less trained one, the class skills make up the difference before the next level comes any closer. The swap form tells how many points that is, beside the picks and again at confirmation.
Collision ||| Collision decides what the player walks through. Trees, rocks, stumps, fallen logs, barrels, crates, statues and other solid props are solid on foot and on horseback and the player slides around them, while grass, flowers, ferns and bushes can be walked through. Furniture in a home or yard, and a mountain, camp or town that loads in around the player, appear first so the player can walk out, and turn solid once clear. It also received a performance fix (PERF-COL1), from a field bug reported on Discord.
Compass ||| The compass marks things around the player including nearby ships, gathering spots within 150 m in profession colours, and placed boats as teal markers, working on Classic and Enhanced compasses and the Overworld view, with redone lighting where floors read as stone and brass instead of amber. Compass marks draw over the Grimoire frame. On the Enhanced HUD it shows the Sea Serpent and a found Abyss Dungeon.
Cooking ||| Cooking is a profession for making dishes at a lit fire. The player stands by it and opens The Fire on the Stores page, with no fee. Dishes are Hunter's Stew and Fisherman's Supper (rank 0), Orchard Tart (rank 10) and Feast of the Hearth (rank 70), each giving a bonus such as Endurance plus 5 for 2 hours or Strength, Endurance and Willpower plus 5 for a day for the player and nearby party. They are cooked in three pans, or five for a feast, and eating a dish again refreshes it instead of stacking. XP follows rank, plus 500 the first time each dish is cooked, and cooking a raw fish from the pack still works as a plain meal. Raw Fish in the Materials Bag can be cooked over a fire, the cooked fish going into the pack.
Daggerfall Enemy Expansion ||| Kamer & Kab's Daggerfall Enemy Expansion 1.3.4 is a mod in the Mods pane, on by default, and online the room's setting applies to everyone in it. It adds fifty new enemies with their art, changes sixteen classic monsters and two classic classes, and lets quests name the new enemies. The new enemies animate at the size the mod gives them, turn up through the mod's encounter tables in every dungeon type, the water and each climate's towns and wilderness, cast their own spells and carry attack effects such as the Blood Spider's blood leech and the Gloom Wraith's silence. The Troll gets back up ten seconds after it falls unless fire burns the body to ash, and with Morrowind attached the new enemies stand in Morrowind's bodies where it has the same creature. Their names are translated into every language the game offers.
Discord Reports ||| Discord reports collects fixes for bugs reported on Discord, including four reports, DISC6 and DISC7, five bugs fixed at their roots, DISC10, DISC19 (six reports) and DISC21. A tenth Redoubt taken alone is announced alone, naming the player, while a group is announced as a company of those who actually went in.
Duels ||| Duels are the player-versus-player foundation where fights are judged by the server against actual weapons and gear, using a separate duel vitality bar that leaves real health untouched, showing both bars from the start and recording wins and losses from server results. Melee and ranged attacks in duels, sieges and the Wrothgarian Mountains share the same damage ceiling as spells, at most 180 damage in any 5 seconds counting blows and casts together. Boons and other buffs count properly in duels, sieges and the Royal Tourney. In a duel the health bar shows the duel vitality and drops when the opponent hits, returning to real health when the duel ends.
Dungeon Map ||| The dungeon map was reworked as an enhanced map with a 3D view, where holding the right mouse button turns and tilts the map at the same time and steep ramps in hallways fill in once walked. Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, and a secrets tier shows hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, and its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies. The compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked, optional exact quest guidance marks a quest's people and things on the map and compass, and secret walls are no longer labelled "Door". Selecting Info mode underground lights up for six seconds what is near, in sight and nameable within 8 m, with a secrets tier showing hidden movers that only a lever chain reaches. A lever that moves something out of sight marks the spot on the map until found, its plaque in Info mode says which way its work lies, and the compass points back to the entrance along the trail walked. Optional exact quest guidance marks a quest's people and things on the map and compass, secret walls are no longer labelled "Door", and with All pressed the 3D map draws every ramp in full. A dungeon's automap keeps the player's exploration of both the shared dungeon and their instance, and switching between them no longer wipes the map, its notes or its portals. Find me also brings the player back to their own floor.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top. A headstone the party has searched counts as searched for the whole party and the game says who searched it, coffins a stranger opened in tombs are the player's to search (including standing right next to one), and party members no longer vanish for a few seconds when the player goes through a tomb door. Bodies and their loot no longer return on re-entering a dungeon while another player there has the game in the background, since the room follows whoever is actually playing, and a room left stuck by a background player's game closing without saying goodbye now moves on to whoever is playing. Dungeon lifts and moving floors no longer trap the player or push them out of the map: a lift that meets the head waits until the player steps clear, a lowering floor stops above them, and a player who falls out of a dungeon is returned to its entrance.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor. In the Gauntlet, killing a Caster silences its pillar. A lord no longer gets weaker when members of the company fall, and is sized for the members who actually reached its hall, a late arrival adding their part at the health the lord has left. On Enhanced Plus the breach's windows (the card offer, the run recap, the boards and the call tracker) use that style and a start window opens the breach, with 1-6 to choose cards, Y for Ready, R for a respite and Enter to close the recap, while the classic skin keeps the list window. In an Athenaeum the Orrery's six stones stand in the hall, each with a dial, a hand showing its hour and two levers, the right turning the stone forward and the left back. The door at the end of a span no longer stays shut when the player stops to time jumps, and if it will not let the player through it says why. Damage numbers on Spire lords and enemies show the damage each hit actually did, adding up to the fight's damage chart, the Gauntlet's caster plates glow gold and dim once their caster is silenced, and the company tracker counts only the people who came in.
Enchanting ||| Enchanting is a crafting profession. The item maker costs 10% less from Journeyman and 20% less as a Master, and disenchanting a piece gives XP by its tier, a quarter for pieces far below the player's rank. A Masterwork piece can be enchanted further and the item maker takes any crafted piece with its enchantment, though a piece's points cannot exceed what its recipe makes and Aetheric pieces such as Ruhn's Regalia and the raid sets are refused. An enchanted jewel that was a Wand and became an Amulet on loading from a guild vault or market listing no longer gets the character flagged by the anti-cheat check. The item maker takes each side effect's setting once, so a drawback cannot be stacked for extra points, and the Enchanting Station lists crafted pieces too small to give Arcane Essence, grayed out with the reason: a piece needs 100 enchantment points for one Essence.
Enhanced Sounds ||| Enhanced sounds are controlled by one Enhanced sounds switch. A limiter sits on the final output so heavy blows, roars and the gate music cannot push the mix past full volume, and everyday sound is unchanged.
Gathering Nodes ||| Every map square now holds twice the herb patches, trees and ore veins each day in every climate, with the daily limit per profession unchanged. Each node can still be worked once a day by each character, so the next harvest is wherever the next node stands. Herbs, veins, boulders, trees and fishing schools catch the light on their own picture instead of wearing an outline: small star-shaped glints flash on the node and fade, and from a distance a soft sheen passes over it now and then, in warm daylight with a hint of the profession's colour. The node being worked goes quiet, walls, rocks and fog hide or fade the glint, the glints follow the light where the node stands (bright in daylight, dim but findable at night, underground and in a storm) and hold still with reduced motion on, and nodes behind the player no longer cost a full-screen pass each frame.
Growth Tab ||| The Growth tab sits in the pause menu next to Stats and holds everything about how the character grows: a Level page for attributes and, with a level up waiting, where to place points and press Ascend; a Specialisations guide to all thirty paths, fifteen arcane and fifteen martial, showing availability, requirements skill by skill, passive bonus, signature spell or technique and how to use it, where the path is chosen; the online Firmament; and the Captain's Stars. The Firmament and the Captain's Stars can go full screen with one press, and the page list marks what is waiting. The Stats page's Ascend and Firmament buttons, the Master Skills specialisation cards and the Captain page's Open the stars lead to it, and on the classic interface the S and W rows open the guide. Unsaved star changes prompt before the player leaves the page, a newly chosen specialisation's stat bonuses apply at once, and the wording of specialisations, the level-up screen and the Firmament is in plain terms and translated into every language. The window keeps its size when switching pages, with the page list always on the left and a short description under each entry, the Level page opens on a card showing the level, and mastery bars are thin red bars the same height as the yellow ones.
Guild Refusal ||| A guild refusal now names the guild. At a guild's Buy Spells counter a refused purchase says why (no spellbook, or not enough gold), and a guild or coven whose quest has not been finished says so and names that quest.
HUD ||| The HUD's lost-part strip on the fatigue, health and magicka bars is now a dim strip and stays at most about a second before catching up. Notifications that have been moved now fade in where the player put them. The compass can be turned over in the HUD editor (Alt+U), whose length handle follows the pointer and which no longer pushes a resized piece off the screen. The riding panel and the ship's helm panel are pieces in the HUD editor (Alt+U) that can be moved, resized or hidden. Two tiles beside the Renown bar show Skills ready and Firmament stars, both movable with Alt+U, and hovering any buff, debuff, need or disease tile shows what it does.
Harbours ||| Port towns feature moored ships with stowed sails that arrive and depart, merchantmen, patrolling navy ships, and offshore pirates. Coastal seats can raise a Harbour, berths and quays lie further from shore where the sea floor shelves gently, harbours far from streets don't constantly search for docks, lake confusion is fixed, ports/lighthouses/quays run lighter, and docks no longer cross each other. Port towns along the coast that had no harbour now have one, including Daggerfall, Sentinel and Wayrest, and a harbour can stand a little way from its town where the shore lies far out.
Hearth Offerings ||| A Hearth Offering is set out at the door of a home during the Witches' Festival and filled with Soul Cakes. Other players can take one cake from each offering per day, and the owner can take it back in at any time, even after the festival, with the bowl and every cake still in it. When other players have taken 25 cakes from a player's offerings the player earns the Hearthkeeper title, and taking 10 cakes from others' offerings earns the Soul Cake icon. Interacting in a town with an offering no longer crashes the game, and the names under the crosshair show again.
Heirlooms ||| Heirlooms belong to Project Legacy. A fallen character may leave an heirloom, their most valuable worn weapon or armour, renamed for the house, and their remains lie where they fell with the heirloom and part of their purse. The heir gets the death quest The Bones of (name), shown in the journal and circled on the map, and the remains open like a container on arrival. Sold or dropped bones return to where the ancestor fell. Laying the remains to rest at a temple or the family seat gives the ancestor's blessing (+3 to their best skill, at most +9 on any one skill) and makes the heirloom stronger with each generation that carries it home. An heirloom cannot be taken twice by rewinding or switching family members. An heir can reach the remains of an ancestor who died inside another player's instance: where the shared dungeon is laid smaller, its door offers an instance of the heir's own and the remains lie where they fell.
Hoods ||| The hood of a worn cloak or robe can be raised and lowered from its card in the pack. A raised hood shields a vampire from the sun and protects a character whose class has the Damage from Sunlight disadvantage. Bare-headed, the sun works as before, and a hood carried in the pack, not worn, doesn't count. For vampires a raised hood still protects from the sun's penalty but no longer gives the night's +20 in daylight, while indoors, underground and at night the full +20 is kept. The female hooded armour no longer floats over the shoulders.
Inventory ||| The inventory drag carries the icon, and the hold that sometimes did not take was fixed. Stack splitting, double-click equip and item lock were added (the last two Enhanced Plus only). It works on phones, with the character picture hidden until Body is tapped and tabs scrolling in one row, and the wagon, storage and Materials Bag have a search box and category menu, with Shift-click moving a whole stack from the pack into them. Item cards compare what the item would change against what it would replace, showing up or down by how much, "new", and the lines that would be lost. Gear can be marked as junk from its card and "Sell junk" at a shop puts every junk piece on the counter in one press, and quick loot has a "Take gear" setting (All, Magic+, Rare+) for the take-all key. Containers, the wagon, storage and pack pages holding hundreds of items open in a fraction of a second, where a 700-item container used to freeze the game for many seconds, clicking, taking and picking in a long list is near-instant, and item pictures fill in as they load without redrawing the whole window. A Lantern slot after the Crystals holds a lantern that hangs at the belt, is lit while it hangs and goes out when taken off.
Invisibility ||| Invisibility works online. Party members can interact with each other while under Chameleon, Shadow or Invisibility. Concealed players outside the party stay hidden, as before. Monsters that cannot see through Invisibility no longer swing at the player just because they walked in front of them in dungeons, while the undead, vampires and Daedra still see through it, and wilderness enemies no longer notice an invisible traveller during fast travel. Worn items that make the player invisible, blending or a shade no longer stay switched off after a blow lands: the effect comes back within six game hours while the item is still worn.
Joinery ||| Every city and town has a Joinery beside its Stables and Wagon Yard, room permitting: a carpenter's open-air yard with a shingled workshop, a timber rack, a log pile, sawhorses and a chopping block, and a table, chairs and a bench on show by the gate. Its workbench is a Carpentry station, opened by talking to the carpenter or using the sign or the bench, which opens the Stores page at The Workbench, with a craft or a saw costing 50 gold as at a Furniture Store's bench and carpentry still done online. It can be found by asking townspeople under "Carpenters", on either town map, or by the saw mark on the Overworld map. Using a Joinery's workbench, or opening Esc > Stores afterwards, no longer crashes the game. The carpenter, the signboard and the workbench all open the workbench with its craft buttons.
Jungle of Bantha ||| The Jungle of Bantha is dense jungle on Hammerfell's south-eastern shore, from Lainlyn and Satakalaam east to Mournoth: giant trees close overhead with taller ones breaking through, young trees and palms fill the light gaps, and the floor is carpeted in ferns, broad leaves and grass. A humid haze hangs under the canopy, thickest at dawn and in rain, and it thins when climbing out of it. It is on by default, switched under "The world" in the Features menu, and always on online. Its big trees stand about 16 m apart and its young trees and palms at most one to a patch, so the player can see and walk between them while the crowns still meet overhead. Winding dirt trails cut through it, about one in every map square, and the floor carries as much grass as meadows elsewhere.
Letters of Credit ||| Online, a letter of credit cannot be put into a chest, cupboard or body, since a shared container is everyone's, so it stays in the pack and is given to another player in a trade. The player's own storage (house cupboards, ship's chest), a pile they dropped themselves and the wagon still take letters, and nothing changes offline. Outside a trade a letter never reaches the player through another player's game, and letters already lying in a shared chest or on a body when the update arrived were removed. A revenant that kills the player never takes a letter, a letter a revenant already holds returns to the wallet on the next load, and a sworn revenant that deserts gives back any letter it carried. Characters left with negative gold by the old large-sale bug can be repaired by staff, which resets the gold, returns what they were owed as a letter of credit and lifts the trading freeze.
Level-Up Notification ||| A level-up notification was added. Under DFO Grimoire the level-up notes are written in full, readable ink.
Loading ||| Pressing a key while the world loads no longer shows a red crash message. The game no longer fails to start with "boot failed: cannot access ... before initialization", which happened when starting near a port town or with the travel map's dungeon lists in play. Loading into the world does less work: the longest freeze while the world is built is about half as long, the map of Tamriel is fitted in a fraction of the time and the roads' travellers are planned faster.
Logging ||| Logging is a gathering profession where wilderness trees are felled using a Wood-Axe and clean cuts with timber going to Stores, supporting all forest types and online visibility beyond the Bay. The Jungle of Bantha stands 48 trees per map square a day for Logging, four times the richest woods elsewhere, and all of them can be felled; on ground not yet vouched for, trees that would be Teak or Mahogany stand as Pine.
Meaner Monsters ||| Meaner Monsters is a mod, version 1.5.2, ported one to one and audited (AUDIT MM1). With Daggerfall Enemy Expansion on (the default, and always online) it plays as MUDEX, the version its author Ralzar made for that mod, which sets the health, damage, level and armour of 35 monsters, classic and new, and gives every dungeon type themed encounter tables that do not scale with the player's level. Without the expansion it works as before.
Monster Volume ||| A Monster Volume setting (0-100%) under Audio controls monster calls, attack sounds and pain cries. Monsters call 60% less often, with the city watch and companions unchanged.
Name Filter ||| The name filter was reworked so that it keeps Scunthorpe, avoiding false matches inside innocent words. It refuses names containing "prostitute" or "strangler", even run together with another word, and in chat, letters and notes these words are starred out.
Oblivion Gates ||| Oblivion Gates are nightly raid events featuring a handcrafted model of cracked basalt and fire with multi-court Warden boss fights, damage charts, and rewards, alongside tavern card games like Iliac Hand played against regulars or online players for stakes and collection cards. The Oblivion Gate boss's own mark and his host's paths no longer shade large areas of empty ground, so the ground markings in a boss AoE frame cost about a quarter less to draw. Inside the gate, other players' sounds play 4 dB quieter and Hellfire, Meteor, Brand and Torment play at most two copies of their sound, and the fight's effects and the monsters a gate can field are prepared on arrival rather than mid-fight.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole. Travelling fast no longer spends time building ground already passed, and the land ahead loads up to three times faster, so holes in the view and drops to 1x speed are much rarer, including on straight roads. While ground is still loading the view may run at about 30 fps instead of 60 until the land is built. Quest destinations show as gold diamonds, the followed quest filled in and pinned to the screen edge with its distance and time left, and clicking a mark starts a journey there. Dungeons are slightly smaller and stay the same size when zooming. While travelling it forecasts the weather ahead and when it will be reached, and standing still it shows the next few hours' weather, with a forecast on hover for any place. Travel inside a town slows to x5 and follows the streets, and the journey stops with a reason if the city gates are shut for the night. A journey no longer drops to x1 while the land ahead is loading, or because of an enemy camp that has not noticed the player. It shows the land out to the horizon, the haze thinning to where land meets sky, with the camera able to tilt up to 15 degrees, while rain, snow, fog and sandstorms still close in. Cloud shadows carry out to the far hills, distant land can be clicked to travel there, and distant hills fill the gap while nearby land loads.
Performance ||| A GPU memory leak from opening the map in a dungeon or building was fixed, long sessions and long journeys no longer accumulate video or GPU memory because left areas are released over time, settings are read faster everywhere, and people, guards, and foes do not pop out of view at screen edges. Opening the Overworld no longer freezes the game as nearby dungeon models load one per frame after the view rises, deep snow processing is disabled while the Overworld is open, and saving in snowy areas is faster because snow-track data is recompressed only when changed. Many town and frame costs were cut: still flats share large buffers and one index buffer, yards and stables behind the player or far away are not drawn, collision no longer grows with the view distance, lamp shadows redraw only the sides someone is in front of and reuse last frame's answer, night towns and harbours make less garbage, shaders compile in the background, the sky skips stars by day and two-graphics-card laptops use the faster card. Entering the world freezes for less, since working out where every town's pilgrims go on their holy days and picking the day's elite halls in the Wrothgarian zone no longer stalls the game, and a town with decorated yards in view makes about 17-18% fewer draw calls a frame. Clicking in the world no longer stutters, and arriving in a large city or walking out of a building in one no longer freezes the game for several seconds, worst in Firefox. Shaders for Oblivion Gate and Elite Gate effects, gathering-node glints, the sea's effects, spell impacts and ground snow compile in the background instead of freezing the game, each effect appearing a moment later, and on most graphics cards the gate and node effects start compiling during loading.
Questlines ||| Thirteen questlines run alongside the quests taken at guild halls and taverns, each opening with a letter or a courier when the player is ready: the Dagon Cycle, the Hour That Was Not, the Old Coil and eight introductions to the game's newer pursuits. A failed step comes back the next day, and a questline's page says if a step needs a feature switched on or a registered account. A questline's ending pays a named piece of its own (22 in all), and online the Dagon Cycle, the Hour and the Old Coil also pay titles held by the account, with the Covens' Hoard able to pay a Weir Key once an account, only for what the service saw the player do. Questlines are always on online and a Features switch, on by default, offline. The Thirteenth of Frostfall, a festival questline from Morwen, a Wyrd Sister of Glenmoril, opens at level 6 online with a registered account while the Witches' Festival runs: the player holds a fallen star, faces the Coven on the Bluff and holds three more stars to earn the Starwarden title and 30 Soul Cakes, with an optional Heart-Star objective and a choice at the end of what becomes of the Spinner's spindle. Each new questline's opening letter waits until the last one has reached the player, and an hour of the character's time after it.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. Every quest shows its objectives one at a time, with the journal and the quest tracker naming the step and a herald line telling when one is finished. Many quests ask the player to choose how they end, each ending paying and being remembered in its own way, and endings with guilds, temples and houses move the player's standing with them (online, a quest that ends unfinished still costs none). Quests that pay gear let the player choose one of three pieces made for the class, better the harder the quest. A quest giver in a Mages Guild hall no longer stands inside the guild's own quest-giver, clicking an NPC a quest is waiting on shows the quest's message and reward right away, and the questlines' givers carry the names their letters give them.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy. The player's own Disintegrate no longer deletes a revenant: it still breaks through the last stand and will but brings the revenant to its knees like any killing blow, to Kill (its trophy weapon drops) or Spare (it joins as a companion). A revenant marked at its lair is found there, standing where it can be walked to from the entrance, and one the road had stood goes home to its lair when visited, the road body it left never coming back as the revenant. Revenants no longer take the player's horse, cart, deeds or spellbook, and anything one took before comes back on the next load.
Roleplay & Realism ||| Roleplay and Realism is a mod. Items' worn-condition loot now applies to every dungeon's treasure, the player can rest in the towns' coloured beds, and the Master Armorer quest finds the armorer's shop in the new towns. With its realistic enemy equipment on, Daggerfall Enemy Expansion's new classes are armed as the class they are drawn from, class enemies wear their second weapon and armour follows what each class may wear, a Monk wearing about half the armour it did, and an enemy no longer holds a two-handed weapon and a shield at once.
Server ||| The server does much less work per player, and reconnecting and moving no longer rewrite the player's details. When nobody else is in the player's area their game stops sending its enemies and ship cabin. The account service makes fewer trips to its database, so busy evenings should load faster. Gathering, the Notice Board's writs and traders, town yards, the guild chapters and the arena board ask far less of the account database, and when the account service is slow the game waits a moment before asking for display data again, with gathering, crafting, trading and saving never held back.
Shadows ||| Shadows cover outdoor shadow/march cuts, static caster optimization, and eight casters. With Morrowind data attached, townsfolk, guards, shopkeepers, foes, travellers, and placed figures cast Morrowind body shadows instead of old sprite outlines, while off-screen figures throw shadows into view and player/household bodies remain shadowless. Moving lights, such as a boss's glow, no longer redraw all their shadows every frame. The brief flashes of a gate boss's landings, fall and Wrath no longer cast shadows, so they no longer force shadow redraws.
Ship Handling ||| Ships sail on every heading. Pointed straight into the wind a ship makes a little over half her best speed, where before she stalled or was blown backwards, and she is fastest with the wind behind her off to one side, with no more tacking. Ships are faster under sail (a galleon with the wind on her side nearly two thirds faster), a storm drives her harder up to twice a fair breeze, and in fog she keeps nearly a third of her speed. They pick up speed in about 7 seconds instead of 25, slow to boarding speed in about 8, and turn on a wider circle at the faster speed. The rudder works at a standstill and bites hardest at half sail, rowing after striking sail no longer holds speed, and AI ships turn and gather speed at the player's own ship's pace. Classic handling is one switch away in Features, Naval Combat, Ship handling. Ships no longer stick on rocks or in place at sea, and one run aground can float off. With no enemies near, no ship chasing and no boarding or aiming going on, a ship's sails and oars give 15% more speed. After a journey across the sea the ship stops instead of rowing on by itself.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away. Winning a raid at sea ends it, removing the raid's crew and any late wave so travel, resting and the Overworld stop saying enemies are near, and an enemy ship that leaves the sea mid-fight takes its raid's crew with it. The pirate attack's victory gong rings again. Going below to the ship's cabin is not allowed while enemies are nearby.
Snowfall ||| In winter, outside the deserts, real snow lies on the ground, deeper in the wild than in towns, building up while snow falls and melting back in clear weather. The player, monsters, guards and townsfolk leave tracks in it, which slowly fill back in (faster while it snows) and are kept in the save, and a fallen enemy leaves a hollow where it lay. With Basic Roads, paths and tracks keep only a thin layer of snow and main roads get a bank along their edges, and the winter ground under it has new textures. It has its own switch in Features, on by default in the enhanced outdoors. Foes' ground telegraphs and the player's technique marks always draw on top of deep snow, and deep snow can be turned off at Settings > Graphics > Sky, water & weather > Snowfall. Snow settles where it falls and melts as the air warms, so in a thaw white mountains stand over green valleys. The weather's snow no longer costs about 18 ms a frame while the snow on the ground catches up with the weather: it keeps to its 3 ms and the snowline settles in over a few seconds.
Spells ||| Ending the player's own spells was fixed, the aim hint always shows when a spell waits for a target, and a party member healing the player no longer repeats the cast message on every cast. Classic spellcasting runs at the same pace as the Morrowind animation, with the spell leaving the hands at half a second, and a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, so clicking or recasting during it does nothing and costs nothing. Casting speed scales with the Speed attribute, up to 25% faster at high Speed and 25% slower at low. Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Re-casting an effect already on the player or a target resets it to that cast's full duration instead of adding the new duration on top, and a shorter re-cast never cuts a longer one short. This covers buffs and debuffs alike, including Shield, Fortify, Regenerate, Levitate, Light, Detect, the resistances, Paralyze, Silence, Continuous Damage and Soul Trap, and re-casting Regenerate or Continuous Damage on its last round still gives the full ticks. Classic spellcasting runs at the pace of the Morrowind animation, a new cast cannot start until the hands finish the motion, casting speed scales with the Speed attribute (up to 25% faster at high Speed, 25% slower at low), and Detect Treasure also finds what is hidden in searched objects. Online, a Destruction mastered past 100 adds to Damage Health and Continuous Damage spells, 1% for each point of its effective value past 100 and +40% at 200, while gear that raises Destruction still makes spells cheaper. Disintegrate works on online bosses, landing the biggest blow the fight allows, and damage-over-time spells keep burning on them and the sea serpent, with recasting refreshing the effect. Chain Lightning, Earthshaker and Dragonfire show their own damage numbers tagged with the power's name.
Starfall ||| Every real hour during the Witches' Festival is a night, and each night one of the Bay's twelve covens pulls three stars down into the wilds of its region, each near a different town. A star burns in the sky for a minute and a half before it falls, and chat, the map, the compass and the Timers window point to it. Once it lands the player holds it through four waves of the coven and then their master, who calls a circle twice and takes only a quarter of every blow while any of it stands; an unheld star is lost 15 minutes after it falls. On the 13th of Frostfall the last star is the Heart-Star, with a larger circle and larger rewards. A held star gives Renown, 30 silver, gold, 1 to 3 pieces of Starfall Glass, a magic item or better and Soul Cakes, and one star in four also gives a piece of the Starspun Weave, Mephala's nine-piece set (always from the Heart-Star).
Status Box ||| The status box crash and an empty status box were fixed. Catching a disease shows an "Unwell" tile on the Enhanced HUD and "You feel unwell." in the Status box while it incubates, the disease's name appearing once its symptoms begin.
Stores ||| The Stores hold what the player gathers, up to 5,000 of each material per character, and are kept in town, so the Stores page in any town moves materials. The page has filters, search and sorting, and Withdraw to pack takes up to 200 at a time, every material able to be withdrawn, with herbs coming out as Daggerfall's own northern or southern ingredient that works in the potion maker. Emptying the bag into the pack is possible from the Stores page anywhere, and a withdrawal whose answer was lost is sent again when opening the page, never minted twice. When the Stores have no room for what a craft would refine, the box says so. The Stores page opens with a row of station tiles marked Here or Away, a station the player is away from staying folded until its tile is tapped, with smelting, other workings and patterns shown as cards, and materials and items show the game's own item pictures. Ingredients looted, bought or given go into the Stores as "loose", usable for crafting at any station but not for writs, the guild's Stores or market sales, which only what was gathered can serve. Putting goods in no longer locks the buttons for a long time, since everything put in at once is saved once, and Temper at the Anvil and Temper at the Loom are back on the page.

Tutorials ||| New characters get a short welcome tour covering moving and climbing, using things, the HUD, and the menu and saving, replacing the old tutorial, and any key a tutorial mentions is the key currently bound. Weapons ||| Weapons move as before Diverse Weapons, and Daedric and other weapons show their own model so a longsword is no longer drawn as a katana. The weapon leaves the hand while a spell is readied. The mace head is no longer pale and black.
Weir Keys ||| A Weir Key opens an Elite Breach and is spent on opening. Closing a Dagon's Breach can drop one: the first close always gives one, after that the chance is 1 in 6 (1 in 4 with the rite broken), and after five misses in a row the next close is certain; clearing an Abyss Dungeon's Hour drops one 1 in 3, a Valkynaz's Redoubt 1 in 4 (at most once a week), and elite foes in dungeons can drop one too (1 in 60 in an Abyss Dungeon, 1 in 150 in an Elite Dungeon, 1 in 400 in a Regular Dungeon). Everyone who had already closed a Dagon's Breach got one key as a launch gift, and the player must have closed a Dagon's Breach before they can open a keyed one. A key that fails to open comes back on its own, and carrying one no longer freezes trading. Weir Keys stay in the pack and cannot be stowed in the wagon or in storage; keys stowed earlier are cleared on load, and the account's real key count is unchanged.
Witches' Festival ||| The Witches' Festival is the first live event, running from 20 October to 7 November. Killing foes, gathering, crafting, fishing, winning tavern games, finishing quests and bounties, sailing, the endgame and holding a fallen star can earn Soul Cakes, which go straight into the pack, more often from the dead and the summoned and doubled on All Hallows' Eve and on the in-game 13th of Frostfall and 3rd of Hearthfire; a linked account and being online are needed to keep them. A Glenmoril witch outside the taverns trades them for The Wyrd's Raiment, a nine-piece Aetheric set, the Witchwarden title, the Witchfire aura, profile icons and six Iliac Hand festival cards. World Map ||| Every port the player has discovered shows an anchor at every zoom, including the whole-bay view, drawn darker with a light outline so it stands out on a busy coast. The Enhanced world map opens on the player as if Find me were pressed, gliding to where they stand with the red cross blinking, while the classic map is unchanged. Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map. Wild Zone halls no longer vanish partway through their day after someone has been inside one. Dying in the Wrothgarian Mountains while the wagon is parked outside the zone, or sent to the stable, loses nothing from it, while a wagon driven, led or parked inside the zone still loses its cargo. A worn item looted from a player in the open PvP zone reaches the looter even if they share a guild or party, staying held until collected and going back to its owner if nobody does, and members of the same guild can no longer attack each other there, as with a party. Players can fight each other in the zone's dungeons again, as intended, and their blood shows there; every attack on another player underground had been refused. A companion's pack is at stake with the player's there: a death drops what the crew hand or sworn revenant carries, companions cannot be sent away from inside the zone, and one knocked out there leaves its pack with the player.
