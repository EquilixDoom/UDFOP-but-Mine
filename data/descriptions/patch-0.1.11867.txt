Armor Types ||| Each piece of armor shows its type, Leather, Chain or Plate, on the item card, in the loot and trader panels, in the two trade windows and on the questline reward card. Leather gives Agility and Speed bonuses more often, and more carry weight, Poison resistance and Frost resistance; Chain gives resistance bonuses twice as often, more Magic, Fire and Frost resistance and less Shock resistance; Plate gives an armor bonus more often, and more Strength, Endurance and carry weight. A Reforge uses the same frequencies, and armor the player already has does not change.
Auras ||| Auras are cosmetic effects worn from the aura picker on the account card, each granted with a title. They include the Oblivion Ward, Golden Radiance, the Holo Shadow Cloak (with the Shadow Fang title), the developers' Seraph Wings and Crystal Resonance (with the Crystal Fist title). An aura now shows to everyone nearby within a second or two, up to 32 are drawn at once, and the cloak and wings fold away while riding or swimming. They sit correctly on both Morrowind bodies and Eye of the Beholder sprites. Lilac Warren is SylviaBun's aura: six lilac bunnies with cotton tails and little maid's headdresses hop round the player's feet on a ring of lilac light, leaving fading paw-print tracks, with an occasional happy binky and sparkles of lilac, pink and white. The Elite Breach adds Beaconlight (Redoubt 10), The Burning Stair (Redoubt 20) and Firstlight. Sage's Smoke is a smoke aura with nirnroots circling the waist and small green runes round the feet, and the smoke never clouds the player's own first-person view.
Boarding ||| Boarding is how a ship is captured. After battering a ship until she strikes her colours, the player comes alongside, slows right down (heaving to beside her now stops the player within boarding reach, even at storm speeds) and presses Activate to throw grapples, which puts out her fires. Her crew and captain fight on her deck, and killing the captain and most of the crew takes her. The player's own crew comes along, and the player lands across from where they stood. Pirates can board the player too, with only one pirate raid at a time, and they fall back and cast off if beaten or timed out, then wait five minutes before grappling again. Beating off a pirate boarding now pays, the pirate being beaten then, so taking or sinking her afterwards is logged but not paid again. A ship's crew no longer rushes the rail shouting at nobody while the player stands at the helm, mustering only when a boarding can actually happen. When an enemy ship strikes its colours, E: board appears as soon as the player is within boarding range, at any speed. The grapples then pull the other ship alongside, the player's sails come in and the ship slows to a stop during the pull, and pirates who board are pulled alongside the same way.
Bow ||| Bow is an engine topic. The bow salvage was done and the bow scale was fixed. With Draw And Release Bows off, holding attack fires again each time the bow is ready, and each click is one draw and one arrow, with the arrow leaving on the release. The Morrowind bow draws properly, the nocked arrow travelling to the string and being drawn back while the bow's limbs and string bend. Bows hit a little harder and arrows fly faster.
Captain's Stars ||| A captain level (up to 30) is earned from sinking and capturing ships, beating off boarders, claiming prizes, finishing contracts, distance sailed, treasure charts, charting isles and selling goods at the exchange. Each level after the first gives one point to spend in the Captain's Stars, a skill tree in the Firmament's style opened from the Captain page: the Master (left) improves seamanship (speed under sail and oars, turning, cargo, hull, repairs and better exchange prices) and the Commander (right) improves combat (reload speed, gun damage, accuracy, grapeshot, chain shot and heated shot, boarding, ram damage and how long a rammed ship stays locked). Keystones give a big bonus with a cost (for example Master of the Wind: +12% speed under sail, -12% under oars), stars apply to every ship the player owns including laid-up and packed ones, removing or changing stars is free, and keystone costs apply in combat. The tree is drawn inside the pause menu's Growth tab, where it can go full screen with one press. The two ramming stars now extend grapple reach: Iron Beak is renamed Long Lines (+15% grapple reach) and Grappling Irons gives +25% grapple reach and one extra boarder.
Character Sheet ||| The character sheet page has one door and includes a setting under Settings → Interface → Character sheet for Every faction's standing (off by default) to list reputation with every guild, temple, knightly order, kingdom, witches' coven, vampire clan and Daedric Prince. The Standing page now lists your reputation with every guild, temple, knightly order, kingdom, coven, vampire clan and Daedric Prince by default, not only the ones you belong to. The Level box shows when the character is at the highest level.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster. The chat has a search bar above the player list where part of a name, or # and a tag, shows every tab's lines from or about that player, and /search <name> searches from the chat field while /search alone clears it. Chat tabs scroll sideways with the mouse wheel, the edge with more tabs fading and a clicked tab scrolling fully into view. Titles no longer appear in chat, while guild tags and glyphs still show after names and the title still shows over the head and on the Inspect card. The chat roster search finds every known player, not just the first page. /rp <text> says a line of roleplay on the current tab in green italics, and /help lists /trade, /lfg, /join, /leave and /rp. Searching for a player in the chat finds anyone online and not just the people listed on screen, since the search now asks the server too and players on other parts of the server show up a moment after typing. An item can be linked into a chat line as a coloured [Name] by typing [ or pressing Tab in the chat field, and the Worn label in the chat's item list is larger.
Courtship and Marriage ||| Courtship and marriage belong to Project Legacy. A townsperson who counts the player a friend can be courted by asking about Courtship in conversation, once a day, depending on Personality, Etiquette and tone, and when their heart is full the player can ask for their hand and is wed at the temple of their town. The spouse joins the house, keeping their own face and day, and every thirty days there is a one-in-four chance of a child, at most six with each spouse, who cannot be played until the mantle passes to them. If someone being courted dies the courtship ends. Online, two players can wed by standing in a temple together and pressing Propose marriage on the other, each keeping their own house, and word of a spouse's death or deletion reaches the player the next time they are online. Courtship counts a tone as it was taken: a tone the person likes helps the day's courtship and one they dislike costs a little affection (never below nothing), and they say so. With Project Legacy on, townspeople offer four new options under Tell me about: Pay a compliment, Give a gift (50 gold), Hug and Kiss, each usable once a day per person and each showing what it changed. A hug needs the person to count the player a friend or be courted, a kiss needs courtship of 34 or more or an engagement or marriage, and if it is too soon the person says what is needed without costing gold or regard.
Crafting Writs ||| The Work tab on every Notice Board posts three crafting writs a day for an account: a potion, a dish and a Repair Kit, or a second potion if none of its characters smiths. The Mages Guild, the Fighters Guild and the Temples of Stendarr and Mara ask for them. They are filled with potions, dishes and kits the player made, whatever they were made from, not found or bought ones, by any of the account's characters, and are drawn at the best crafter's rank. Each pays silver, crafting experience, Renown and standing with the guild that asked, and the card shows what is held toward each writ and why Deliver is waiting. A potion must still be brewed at an alchemy station, and bought or found bottles and pieces sold and bought back do not count. The card says when goods were left out and what to do.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are sold wherever weapons are, in Weapon Smiths, Armorers, General Stores and Pawn Shops, and restocked each day. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang. On the Morrowind body it is held like a rifle, the right hand on its pistol grip and the left supporting the forend, carried at rest at a low ready with the muzzle down and across toward the left foot and coming up to a level ready stance while shooting, for a few seconds after and while foes are hurting the player, in first and third person, the Hourlock held the same way. In the Morrowind first-person view the aimed gun sits below the middle of the screen instead of riding over the crosshair, with the left hand still on its forend. It reloads faster as Speed rises, down to 1.36 seconds, while the Pistolet is unchanged.
Dwemer Pellets ||| Dwemer Pellets are the ammunition of the Thunderlock and the Pistolet. They are no longer sold in shops: they are a rare find in loot even with Loot rarity turned off, every share of the Sky-Ketch's hold pays 20 to 40 of them, and every construct brought down on the Sky-Ketch drops a pile for each player aboard, Spheres, Sentries, Wardens, the Watch-Captain and the Tonal Warden always and vent spiders sometimes. The limit on pellets from constructs counts per voyage, the Hammer Wardens, the Watch-Captain and the Tonal Warden always drop theirs, constructs the ship removes by itself drop none, and piles lie where the construct fell.
Enchanting ||| Enchanting is a crafting profession. The item maker costs 10% less from Journeyman and 20% less as a Master, and disenchanting a piece gives XP by its tier, a quarter for pieces far below the player's rank. A Masterwork piece can be enchanted further and the item maker takes any crafted piece with its enchantment, though a piece's points cannot exceed what its recipe makes and Aetheric pieces such as Ruhn's Regalia and the raid sets are refused. An enchanted jewel that was a Wand and became an Amulet on loading from a guild vault or market listing no longer gets the character flagged by the anti-cheat check. Rare weapons, armor, jewellery and clothing, found (once identified) or crafted as Masterworks, can be enchanted at the item maker; the piece's existing enchantment, and any curse drawback, stays, shows at the top of the maker's lists and uses none of its enchantment points. Legendary items still cannot be enchanted.
Fishing ||| Fishing is a gathering profession done with a Fishing-Net, standing in water or at sea by daylight (07:00 to 17:59). Holding E winds the net and letting go throws it 3 to 12 metres, and the player hauls in by holding E to raise a band and keeping the net's weight inside. A haul gives 1 to 2 Raw Fish to the Stores, a missed tug still gives a plain catch, and two schools rise in each area every day for an extra fish. At sea there is a 1 in 50 chance of a Pearl and 1 in 100 of a Slaughterfish, about 1 haul in 200 brings a trophy fish, and the limit is 40 hauls a day per account. Each haul gives XP as if at the highest tier the rank unlocked. The net will not cast in a dungeon or settlement or below a quarter of fatigue. Fishing online no longer piles up memory while travelling. Intelligence and Agility set the net's tug window as well as the haul, and a school's extra fish counts on the first 40 schools of the day.
Foe Health Bar ||| A foe's health bar no longer shows a dark or empty box under it. The poise bar appears only when there is something to show, a resting foe shows only its wind-up track, and the bar has one shadow instead of two. The Sky-Ketch's constructs, the Oblivion gate's legion, the Elite Breach's enemies and the Shattered Hour's Echoes show a health bar when struck.
Gathering ||| Gathering covers herb patches, ore veins, boulders, trees, bodies and fishing water. E acts on one only when it is looked at, in reach and unobstructed, and a node that cannot yet be worked says why on its row (tool, rank or daily limit). Actions use the loot list, picked by clicking, activating or tapping, with a small list window on the Classic skin and touch screens. The minigames are drawn on and around the crosshair with one short hint that fades, progress as diamonds and a sound for every strike, find, slip, splash and tug. Without a Materials Bag everything gathered goes in the pack even over its weight, and the message says so. A herb patch, vein, boulder or tree goes the moment the player finishes working it instead of waiting for the server to answer, coming back if the server refuses the harvest, and goods, XP and rank-ups arrive faster after each harvest. Gathering past the Veil works on the islands' herbs, trees, ore veins and boulders as in the Bay, each spot giving its full finds only once three players with accounts at least a week old have gathered there, and fishing in the Eltheric Ocean works. Every gathering act has its own sounds (felling, mining, herbs and the Basket, skinning, net fishing, goods going into the bag and a chime for a clean finish or a new rank), sounding the moment the tool hits and from where it happens, and on the Enhanced skin each axe or pick blow dips the view and hands slightly, following Technique camera motion. Other players see and hear the gathering, covered by their Attack sounds switch. The sounds are part of Enhanced sounds, and with it off or on the Classic skin Daggerfall's own sounds play, timed to the blows. The loot panel and its tooltip no longer cover skinning, or any other gathering minigame, while it plays. Gathering that runs past midnight UTC stops cleanly and says why, materials headed for the bag are no longer lost when the server's reply goes missing, and the node choice list works after a death. Rain makes the Sickle's steady hand harder.
Instances ||| At a dungeon or building where one of the player's quests is running, at Privateer's Hold, or where a party member is already in an instance, the door asks whether to enter the public world or the player's own instance, which only the player and their party can enter. An instance keeps its own looted chests and slain monsters (returning after half an hour) and closes for good when the quest that opened it ends, a party member's instance shows at the door as "Name's instance", and the door only asks when there is a real choice (homes and the Wrothgarian open zone never have instances). Esc at the door leaves without entering, and quest teleports, Recall, loading a save and staff teleports return the player to the instance they were in, or to the public room if it has closed. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, and at a quest's dungeon whose shared version is laid smaller the prompt says the quest's business is in the player's instance only. When a quest leads to a dungeon the shared world builds smaller, the door lists the player's instance first and Public last.
Intro Cinematic ||| The intro cinematic was rebuilt around the score on Mac's remastered master, with the BEGIN button as its only gate, and reworked again in 0.1.4313. In 0.1.11110 the film and the main menu played the main theme through the game's own FM music system, and in 0.1.11284 they went back to the original theme recording. Since 0.1.11808 the intro is re-shot over the Imperial City, from dawn over Lake Rumare to a climb until Tamriel lies below with its provinces, borders, rivers and cities named, with a five-line narrator in every language the game speaks and the DFO Online logo landing over the map. Since 0.1.11867 its title screen is the main menu.
Jewelcrafting ||| Jewelcrafting is a profession that makes eight pieces (rings, marks, bracelets, bracers, amulets, torcs, cloth amulets and wands) at a Pawn Shop or Gem Store (50 gold) or a home jeweller's bench (50,000 gold). Silver pieces and Cloth Amulets are available from rank 0, Gold from 25, Platinum from 55 and Wands from 70, and the gems are the eight a vein gives plus the sea's Pearl. Gold gives +10% enchantment points, Platinum +20% and a set gem +10%. Pieces come Crude to Masterwork, with Superior and Masterwork rolling a magic property and a Masterwork carrying the maker's name. Each is cut in three facets (five with a gem), and catching every facet gives a clean piece, one quality step better. XP comes by tier plus 500 the first time each piece of each metal or wood is made. The jeweller's bench takes Chipped, Flawed, Flawless and Perfect gems, each grade adding its own share of enchantment points. Jewellery gets Hand-to-Hand and Critical Strike skill lines as often as the other skills it prefers, though only on pieces made since 0.1.11867.
Leveling ||| Leveling is chosen at character creation on the "How will you grow?" screen, in the Enhanced Plus look. Online characters level the Daggerfall way, with Oblivion Remastered leveling shown locked as "Offline characters only", and online characters that were on Oblivion leveling switch to Daggerfall leveling the next time they are played, keeping their level, skills and any earned level-up. Offline the player can still pick either system. The highest character level is 45 for every level system, the classic system gives the last levels when mastered skills go above 100, and a character already above 45 keeps its level.
Lockpicking ||| Picking a lock is played, not rolled. In Steal mode a locked front door, dungeon door, chest or crate (and in Grab mode chests and crates) opens a lock of pin tumblers, each lifted to the shear line and set in the lock's own order; harder locks have more pins and some have spool pins that false-set. Lockpicking skill sets how forgiving each pin is and Agility widens or narrows it, a slip drops a pin and a careless lift can snap the pick. Auto attempt rolls the chance the old way and a failed roll snaps a pick, Leave costs nothing, and with Gentle work on only Auto attempt is offered. A Lockpick is needed and a pick that snaps is gone; General Stores and Pawn Shops keep a stack, and classes that train Lockpicking start with five. Magically held dungeon doors still need magic.
Loom ||| The Loom is a Stores page section at a Clothing Store (50 gold a craft) or a home Loom station. Two hides cure to a leather, with rat, bat, bear and tiger giving Cured Leather and others Hardened Leather, and three Spider Silk weave a Silk Bolt, while Harpy Feathers go into arrows. Curing and weaving give no XP. Linen and Wool can also be made: two Root Tendrils weave into a Linen Bolt, and two furs (Rat, Wolf or Tiger Pelts, Boar or Bear Hides) spin into a Wool Bolt, which the Weavers still sell too. The Loom shows the number of silver-bought bolts that must be used before Craft.
Main Menu ||| The main menu has Install beside Play, a profile mark that is a portrait of the last character, and Patreon and Ko-fi icons in the top-left corner that open their pages in the browser. It is no longer cut off on short windows, and the logo sits below the corner icons on narrow windows. The buttons are carved stone slabs in the Enhanced Plus style, all one width, and on phones they are a little more compact so the whole menu fits above the Screenshots and About buttons. Main menu clicks play the in-game click sound once the game data is loaded. The main menu first asks one question, Online or Offline, and Offline opens Continue, New Game, Load Game and the Test Room with a way back. Settings and Overhauls are in the bottom bar beside Screenshots and About, which no longer covers the buttons on phones, small or short screens and long languages. Double-clicking Offline no longer opens Load Game by accident, Escape puts the keyboard back on the button the player came from, and Tutorials and Screenshots are translated. Since 0.1.11867 the intro's title screen is the main menu: Online and Offline sit below the logo over the map of Tamriel, Tap to continue is removed, Skip intro and Escape go to the title screen, and with Skip start video on the game opens on it. The music volume decreases when the first button is pushed.
Market ||| The Market is a Notice Board tab for trading in Marks or gold, featuring grouped views (Buy, Sell, History), silver display, column names, supplier counters, and detailed split lists. Listings stand 72 hours for a 1% fee with immediate sales (minus 5% tax) and courier delivery, with strict gold/Drakes currency separation and item exclusions. Seat boards, market sales, and courier deliveries no longer slow down account services in regions where a guild holds a Charter. Market stalls look the way the pack draws them, with the keeper behind the counter and goods on the counter. A piece is sold from the pack by picture: each shows its icon, rarity-colored name and wear, selecting one shows its card, pieces can be found by name or filtered by kind, rarity or material, and pieces that cannot be sold sit in a closed Not for the market list with the reason. From your pack, From the Stores, A crafted piece and An auction (Masterworks) are buttons, and the Goods view has a Sell from your pack button.
Master Skills ||| Master Skills raises the skill hard cap from 100 to 200, with everything up to 100 working as before. Each point from 100 to 125 is about 4 times slower, 125 to 150 about 8 times slower and 150 to 200 about 16 times slower, and each point above 100 gives 25% of the benefit of a point below 100, with milestones at 125, 150, 175 and 200. A skill at 200 fights like a 140. Every skill can reach 100, but only mastered skills pass it: all 3 primary, 2 of 3 major and 1 of 6 minor, chosen at 100 and permanent. It is always on online and a choice offline. Online, every mastered school past 100 strengthens its own spells and not just their price, by 1% per effective point up to +40% at 200, the same rule as Destruction's damage: Restoration makes heals heal more, Alteration makes the Shield hold more, Illusion makes invisibility, chameleon and shadow last longer, Thaumaturgy makes Spell Reflection and Spell Resistance hold more often, and Mysticism makes Silence, Dispel and Soul Trap take hold more often. Mastering two schools also allows choosing an Arcane Specialisation. Online, a mastered weapon skill past 100 pays in its own way: Long Blade and Archery make every blow land up to 40% harder at 200, Axe up to 80% harder against wounded foes (under half health), Blunt Weapon up to 80% harder against fresh foes (half health or more), each of those two landing up to 40% harder on every blow against the Oblivion Gate's court, the Elite Gates, the Remnant and the sea serpent, and Short Blade swings up to 40% faster, while Hand-to-Hand's damage already grew with the skill. Every short blade swings like a dagger, where the Tanto, Shortsword and Wakizashi had swung at long-blade speed. Mastering two weapons also allows a martial specialisation. Characters that had mastered two primary skills keep them and get the third slot.
Morrowind Body ||| The Morrowind body features climbing poses (hanging, shimmying, wall climbing, leaps, pull-overs, vault tucks), gripping animations, first-person hand reach, crowd optimization, port-made steel plate armor sets, synchronized attack speeds, spell-casting animations, and T-pose fixes. Placed home/yard decoration NPCs stand in Morrowind bodies, you can ride in third person, look down over the 3D horse's neck in first person, and 3D horses match sprite coats with unique flank markings. With the Morrowind model in first person the arms grip the wall while climbing even with the weapon sheathed. The cape hangs from the chest alone and moves only with it as one stiff sheet, so walking, swinging the arms and sitting no longer bend it, and leaning back swings the whole cape with the chest. Looting a fallen enemy in a dungeon no longer turns its Morrowind body back into the old sprite while the loot window is open, bodies no longer clip through floors and walls, and chain boots, gauntlets and pauldrons, including Legendary ones like The Wayrest Courier's Treads, show on the body (in iron) instead of bare skin. Two-handed thrusts and guards no longer pass the arms through the body, one-handed weapons are held low and forward while running, and bow, crossbow and thrown weapons turn the upper body with the aim.
Motherlodes ||| Motherlodes are daily rich ore heaps announced in chat and marked on the compass, requiring Mining 25 and Pick-Axe strikes for ore, XP, and silver without counting against harvest limits, and neither motherlodes nor veins, boulders, herbs, and trees appear inside rocks. Motherlodes appear on the compass from their warning onward, in their own colour, drawn over nearby veins. With Motherlode Sense (Mining 100) the day's Motherlodes are marked on the map and the Overworld from the start of the day, each showing its ore and, until it rises, when it rises (such as "Tue 13:00", on the player's own clock), with warnings still coming 30 minutes ahead. Motherlodes past the Veil still rise only in the Iliac Bay. A motherlode can only be struck once it has risen, and a strike stops cleanly when its lode sinks.
Naval Sound ||| Cannon fire can be heard across the bay, with splintering hulls, exploding fire barrels and grapples biting, and a ship's bell rings when she strikes her colours. With Enhanced sounds the sea under the deck swells with wind and weather, with the wind in the rigging, gulls near the coast and the ship's bell; the one-off deck sounds of waves slapping the hull, creaking timbers and ropes, flapping sails and spray over the bow were removed in 0.1.11763. Taking the helm brings the sea's own music (a calm, an open-sea shanty, a storm, a night watch, a jig for landfall and battle music that keeps playing when boarding), and the Trireme's oar strokes follow Sound Volume.
Outfitting ||| Outfitting is crafting clothing and leather. It makes leather armour (cuirass, greaves, helm, pauldrons, gauntlets and boots) from Cured Leather at 10 or Hardened at 55, all of Daggerfall's 76 garments in Linen, Wool or Silk in one of its ten clothing colours, and home pieces such as rugs (3 Wool Bolts), tapestries (4) and Large and Small Skins. It also makes the Fishing-Net (2 Linen Bolts). A craft gives 20 XP a tier of its cloth or leather plus 500 the first time, with Linen and Wool giving none, and stitching in time with the needle eight times gives a clean craft. Cloth woven or spun at the Loom gives the first-time bonus whatever the source of its Root Tendrils and furs, and so does cloth from a guild member. Cloth bought with silver from the Weavers or the Market does not, and the next bolts used, up to the number bought, give none.
Prices ||| Prices are said in real time, and NaN prices were fixed. Books and potion recipes no longer jump to a much higher price after passing between players online, which let them sell back for more than they cost. An item that reaches the player through another player's game (a chest, a body, a shelf) cannot carry a higher price than an honest item of its kind could. Merchants pay 50% less for Daedric weapons, armor and shields, while the buying price, and the value of a gem in one, do not change.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. Some new online characters who missed the letter from Princess Morgiah and the other royal letters have the main quest repair itself the next time they play, and her letter comes at level 3 or higher.
Ramming ||| Ramming returned in 0.1.11842 after being retired in 0.1.11785: sailing into the side of a ship at speed damages its hull and the player's ship takes some damage back, but a ram cannot sink a ship, it lowers the hull until the ship surrenders and can then be boarded, and it does not lock the two ships together. A side hit does more damage than a hit on the bow or stern, more speed does more up to a limit, a small boat does less damage to a large ship, a low hull weakens the ram, and a wrecked ship cannot ram. A lawful ship at peace with the player cannot be rammed, unless the player fired on it first, and that ram is piracy. The Large Galley has a bow ram again that does two times the damage, an enemy galley can ram the ship it fights, and Brace halves the damage from a galley's ram.
Reforge ||| The Reforge is a service of the Mages Guild. It always swaps a line for a different kind of line, on any line, as often as the player can afford, each reforge on the same piece costing twice the last, and Technique lines cannot be reforged though honing still raises them. Imbue raises a plain piece to Magic, or a found Magic piece to Rare, for Welkynd Shards and gold, with its lines rolled fresh. A reforge keeps a name the player gave the piece at the Item Maker. A Reforge on armor uses the same type frequencies as armor loot.
Resource Highlights ||| Resource highlights is a setting under Settings > Gameplay > Climbing & gathering that chooses how herbs, veins, trees and other gathering spots stand out: Glint (the sparkle), Outline (a colored outline round each spot) or Both. Hunting bodies keep their glow.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night. The galleon, carrack and large boat wear their own painted liveries again, with the oxblood strakes, the gilt and the stern gallery, in Daggerfall's own colour palette. Towns with ships in port run smoother online, the ships costing about a quarter of their old share of each frame and leaving far less garbage. Ship text no longer calls a ship "her" or "she" across the sea, the Fleet page, the shipyard, the crew hall, the Captain's Stars, the sea chart, the tutorials and the crew's chatter, saying "the ship", "this ship" or "it", and the 13 translated languages match. There is no stutter the first time a named or painted ship comes into view and painted sea ships no longer slow rendering. Ship parts already too heavy to take out of a chest or other storage are fixed when the save loads, with nothing in their hold lost, and a character too weak to carry a ship's parts is told "The ship's parts are too heavy for you to carry." Other players' ships no longer block or push the player's ship outside PvP waters, the Sethrakul fight's bumpers were removed, and in PvP waters (the Eltheric Ocean past Veilhaven) ships collide as before.
Sky-Ketch ||| The Mzunthar is a Rourken sky-ketch that crosses the Iliac Bay every ten hours, online only, sighted an hour before she arrives and keeping station over the fields east of Daggerfall for seven hours. Each party of up to eight boards its own ship for a raid through the Boarding Watch (five waves), the Gallery of Hammers, the Starved Engine, the Sailwalk and the Tonal Warden, all tougher since 0.1.11269. A questline from Imrah Bel-Tavir of Sentinel and two books go with her, and her hold is the only source of the Dwarven Thunderlock and Pistolet. A player who dies with a companion still standing lies where they fell for 60 seconds, awaiting a party member's Resurrect. Its messages, tracker, map card, questline text, books and Discord announcements say "it" and use plainer words. Boarding was repaired and a fall from it wakes the player in the fields at a quarter of their health; crews aboard when it weighs anchor have 45 minutes to finish, and a Tonal Warden fight is lost only after the bridge has been empty for two minutes.
Third-Person View ||| Third-person view has a body, a camera and a wheel, also working without Morrowind. The third-person body stands on the capsule's feet, not the camera's, and while climbing the character turns to face the wall and turns back after. Over the Shoulder (Settings > Camera: Off, Right or Left) puts the Morrowind body's camera behind a shoulder, moving above the head when a weapon or spell is held so shots go to the crosshair; it is off by default.
Titles ||| Titles are worn from the account card beside an aura or glyph, some given to particular players: SirMcMobdon, Tabby, Flylighter (Crystal Fist, with three purple slashes, alongside Disciple), Penitent (Diggleborf's, gold fading into sky blue), Aegis of Oblivion (Sureme's, violet, with a glyph of three pillars through a ring) and Primarch (made for GA00250, in light gold with a three-barred cross glyph). Players who first played on the evening of September 24 in the Americas hold the Founder title. Changing the aura, buying an insignia or unlinking Patreon no longer hides a Charter's title. A title or aura earned, including the Abyss Dungeon's Hourbreaker and The Turning Hour, gets a loot popup in Enhanced Plus the first time each is earned. The $50 Hierophant Patreon tier grants its title and glyph in game, the rose "Hierophant" and the crown beside the name, appearing on the next sign-in for an already linked Patreon. WikiMaid is SylviaBun's title, drawn amethyst into lilac into a bunny's blush, with a glyph of a lilac bunny in a maid's lace headdress beside her name (and on the account card, the chat roster and the travel view). The Elite Breach adds Spirewalker, Beacon-Lighter, Kynbane, Battlemage of the Spire, Wellhead-Walker, the Unfallen, the Lone Beacon, Spire Champion and First of the Spire. Nirnroot Sage is Blob-Marley's title, in green fading to the pale of smoke, with a glowing nirnroot glyph beside his name.
Wordmark ||| The wordmark was reworked to be the logo and then fixed to be type again (BR3). The Daggerfall Online logo is repaired, with the torn rows through "DAGGERFALL" and "The Elder Scrolls" mended and the clipped tops of its letters closed. In 0.1.11757 a gold dragon coiled through a red D, F and O replaced the placeholder logo, and it was centred on the intro, main menu and website in 0.1.11808.
