Abyss Dungeons ||| An Abyss Dungeon is the hardest dungeon in the game, online only. One at a time rises beside a busy city in the region with the most players online, with a column of brass-gold light over it, and the first player to reach its door finds it and everyone online hears where, after which its ring, compass mark, Timers row and notice-board note stay until it is gone. It has three foes at every spot, each four times as tough and hitting two and a half times as hard, six elite foes, half again the loot and no campfires. At its deepest point stands the Rift into the Shattered Hour, where the Orrery of Endings, the Unmoored Steps and the Brass Remnant are met, and the Remnant's fight, spoils, collapse and one-life rule are shared by everyone in the Hour at once. Each Abyss Dungeon keeps one of six Endings and two of nine omens, and fighters earn Hours broken toward the Hourbreaker title and The Turning Hour aura. Crew and sworn companions wait outside the Rift, and leaving the Hour for any reason puts the player on the ground at the dungeon's door. A new one always rises by a town: when players are crowded at sea it goes to the busiest region on land or one of the Bay's great cities, and one that already rose at sea shows up by a great city. Once anyone steps through an Abyss Dungeon's Rift, a portal, the Way Through, opens beside everyone in the dungeon and beside everyone who enters afterwards, and takes them straight to the Shattered Hour. It closes when the Brass Remnant falls.
Arcane Essence ||| Arcane Essence is a crafting material. It stays in the player's Stores, never the pack, and sells on the market like any Stores material. From Enchanting 50, an enchanting station spends 2 Arcane Essence to roll one line of a known Magic piece again, or 5 for a Rare's, which is the Mages Guild's Reforge paid in Essence. At an enchanting station each line of a piece has its own Reforge button in a list under the piece's name and price, and the name is readable instead of wrapping one word per line. Taking apart a piece crafted with materials from the pack gives Essence marked "made from pack goods" instead of "bought with gold", and the Reforge with Essence can use it; the Stores page no longer says Essence can go to the pack.
Blood ||| Blood is the port's own blood effect. Droplets fly from the wound under gravity and leave their mark on floor, wall or ceiling, with ceiling drops dripping back down. Spatter lands the way the blow threw it in one to three uneven clumps, pools are several blobs with fingers where the blood ran, and stain art is 128 pixels per stain, generated from noise and built in the background so the first hit does not stutter. Blood splashes no longer redo their image work on every hit. The first hit's blood on screen no longer causes a freeze.
Desktop App ||| The desktop app is the downloadable version of the game, with saves as real files, built on Electron 42. The Windows installer and the Linux AppImage update themselves, the portable exe and the Mac tell the player when a new version is out with a Download button, the app has its own icon, and it finds and installs updates again. It is not yet code-signed. Updating no longer leaves the game missing or unpacks 18,000 files every launch. Downloads are named DFO-Online-... from the rename on, and an installed copy finds its update as usual. On a Mac the new version installs as DFO Online beside the old Daggerfall Online app (to be dragged to the Bin once open), and on Windows pinned taskbar or Start menu entries may need repinning under the new name. It keeps the account and settings files in memory instead of reading them from disk every frame, as the browser does, giving smoother frames in a town. The app updates the game one file at a time instead of downloading about 130 MB and replacing the whole game folder, the full installer running only when the app itself changes or a file-by-file update fails, on Windows, macOS, Linux and the portable build. A game that stops now says so and why and offers Restart, and a game that stops responding offers Wait or Restart, instead of leaving a black window. The reason is kept in crash.log beside the saves, to include in a bug report.
Enemy Sync ||| Enemy Sync keeps other players' enemies the same for everyone online. In busy areas enemies no longer die for one player and live on for another, or vanish when their controller leaves, dies or goes through a door, and at a crowded Starfall every player's enemies reach everyone in turn. Enemies run by a lagging player move smoothly for others, and on a slow computer enemies and the town watch keep pace with the player instead of moving at half speed.
Gathering Nodes ||| Every map square now holds twice the herb patches, trees and ore veins each day in every climate, with the daily limit per profession unchanged. Each node can still be worked once a day by each character, so the next harvest is wherever the next node stands. Herbs, veins, boulders, trees and fishing schools catch the light on their own picture instead of wearing an outline: small star-shaped glints flash on the node and fade, and from a distance a soft sheen passes over it now and then, in warm daylight with a hint of the profession's colour. The node being worked goes quiet, walls, rocks and fog hide or fade the glint, the glints follow the light where the node stands (bright in daylight, dim but findable at night, underground and in a storm) and hold still with reduced motion on, and nodes behind the player no longer cost a full-screen pass each frame. For a few minutes after midnight UTC, until the client catches up, the ground's gathering nodes stay on the day before's; nothing is lost.
Grass ||| Grass is the ground cover. Its default style is Meadow: hand-drawn tall and short green tufts, flowering tufts, dry tufts and bushes on crossed cards like the low-poly trees, each growing to its own size, with flowers in lush patches and dry grass in poor ones. It takes its colour and exactly the same light as the ground under it in every climate and season, including a texture mod's ground, stands on slopes, sways in the wind with stiffer bushes, follows the blended climate border, and far grass fades into the ground's colour. Grass is off by default; Settings > Grass turns it on at Full, Half or Quarter and picks the style, Meadow, Pixel (the old tufts) or Smooth. Grass no longer draws over a portal's vortex. Grass no longer freezes the game while it grows back after leaving the Overworld, and no longer stutters while riding or running fast on a slower computer, filling in around the player even when arriving at speed. Where a snowline ends, grass is buried by snow on the same slopes the snow is drawn on. It sways more gently, blades leaning less than half as far at a full gust and gusts rolling through the field more softly.
Guilds ||| Guilds are player organisations founded on Renown 10 with restrictions on disbanding and standing reviews, functioning properly across regions, and online you can set entry permissions like an online home (Only me, My party, My guild, or Anyone). Townsfolk, shopkeepers, guild members, nobles and priests are dressed from a wardrobe of their own in Morrowind garments and colours, while a fighters' guild's people wear whatever armour came to hand. Online, a player is promoted the moment they qualify for the next rank by talking to a guild-service NPC at the hall, with no 28-day wait for a promotion, while demotion, probation and expulsion still wait 28 days and offline play is unchanged. Reputation earned before joining still counts, but a new member rises no faster than the guild records standing, about a rank and a half a day from the day they join, and the guild hall tells when the next promotion can come at the earliest. A member on probation is no longer expelled over reputation the guild still owes them, the probation being renewed instead. Online, Mages Guild quests "Guard the Guild" and "Protect an Honored Mage" need standing 10 and 20, and new apprentices are no longer handed them. Becoming a vampire no longer shuts the player out of the Dark Brotherhood or the Thieves Guild: membership and rank carry over when they turn and stay if they are cured, and a vampire who lost either gets it back, rank and all, the first time the game reads their guilds. A guild's quest-giver can always be asked for work as a stranger, even after an expulsion, each quest done raising standing with that guild by 5. A guild can hold 75 members, up from 50.
Horse ||| Horses never die, can be fed to grow the player's bond, must be kept fed, and the player can own more than one. A horse walks by default and holding Run (Shift unless rebound) gallops it, as on foot. The gallop is as fast as before and spends the horse's breath, and a winded or starving horse walks even with Run held until it can gallop again. Holding Run gallops it anywhere, in town or out, as long as it has the breath, online too, though riding into someone at a gallop tramples them, which is a crime and brings the guards. A walking horse has a slower clop, a slower head nod in first person and a trot on the 3D horse, and the saddle panel says Walking. With Horse care off in Settings > Features, or on the classic interface, horses ride as they always did. A horse with a new coat met on the road no longer freezes the game. The horses' coats on the road's wagons are painted in under half the time, so the first seconds after entering the world stutter less. With Billboard Look on, Morrowind horses stand upright like the classic sprites. The saddle panel is a small block in the bottom-right corner, shown or hidden with F2 (rebindable in Controls), and the Horse care setting chooses When needed, Always or Off; on touch screens it no longer covers the compass. Horse care and wagon upkeep each have Off, Boons (the default) and Hard tiers like Climates & Calories: on Boons nothing is taken (a hungry horse is no weaker, a worn wagon no slower, and a wagon kept Sound rolls 10% faster), Hard is the system as it was, and Off is the classic horse or wagon. The first-person horse (and the cart's horse) darkens with the world around it, at night, in a cave mouth or under a storm, following the Features > First-person lighting switch like the weapon and hands. Horses, whether other players', strangers' on the road or standing in towns, are animated in full only when in view, and a horse coming into view is drawn in its current stride.
Hunting ||| Hunting is skinning bodies. A body the player's own blow felled can be skinned where it lies until midnight UTC, with a Skinning Knife, and ten foes give a hide, including Rat, Giant Bat, Grizzly Bear, Sabretooth Tiger, Spider and Harpy. Holding E at the first point of a dotted line and drawing to the last, following closely in 0.6 to 6 seconds, gives a clean pelt, and a second hide one time in two, while straying far tears the pelt. A skinned body gives its hide to the Stores, one in four also gives an ingredient, and most give Raw Meat. Skinning works at any hour but not in a settlement, at sea, with enemies nearby or fully encumbered, and an account can take 30 hides a day, only 3 from harpy, dreugh and dragonling. The text hunting minigame no longer runs. The Enemy Expansion's animals can be skinned too: bats give Bat Leather, blood spiders Spider Silk, mountain lions Tiger Pelts and land dreugh Dreugh Shells, wolves, snow wolves and dogs the new Wolf Pelt, boars the new Boar Hide and mudcrabs the new Mudcrab Chitin. The three new hides cure to Cured Leather, and Wolf Pelts and Boar Hides make Large and Small Skins at the loom. Cured Leather from mixed hides uses Wolf Pelts and Boar Hides before Bear Hides, keeping Bear Hides for Ram Kits.
Lantern ||| The lantern sits at the waist. The carrack carries seven lanterns, so she is lit at night at sea. A lantern hung at the waist on the Morrowind body is a belt-lantern size, about 30 cm, matching the Eye of the Beholder sprite's lantern. A lantern lit with O, Use or the hotbar shows in the Lantern slot, hanging at the hip, not on the shield's panel. O lights the lantern in the Lantern slot first, a hung lantern lights with both hands full and the hotbar's lantern no longer takes the shield off.
Loading ||| Pressing a key while the world loads no longer shows a red crash message. The game no longer fails to start with "boot failed: cannot access ... before initialization", which happened when starting near a port town or with the travel map's dungeon lists in play. Loading into the world does less work: the longest freeze while the world is built is about half as long, the map of Tamriel is fitted in a fraction of the time and the roads' travellers are planned faster. A rare stall where the world stopped loading new ground for the rest of the session (offline play, about one area in 37) was fixed.
Morrowind People ||| With the Morrowind body on, the people and creatures around the player stand in Morrowind bodies too: dungeon and wilderness foes, the city watch, townsfolk, quest givers, stable and wagon yard keepers, ship crews, siege fighters, road parties and the Oblivion Gate's boss and host. Foes wear the armour and carry the weapons they rolled with a race and face of their own, townsfolk are dressed by their station, foes swing, flinch and fall with their tells on the body, and a body is drawn at its sprite's size. Creatures use Morrowind's creatures where Daggerfall's have a match and keep their classic sprite otherwise. Online everyone sees a foe as its owner does. Bodies are drawn for the nearest people only within one budget for the whole frame, and a Morrowind People setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data is Off, Near (the default, up to 24 bodies) or All (up to 48). With Morrowind models on, other characters and horses keep their size wherever the camera is, and NPCs whose helmet fails to load show their head. The horses that stand in towns, at World of Daggerfall's bandit camps and forts and in the Stables stand as the 3D horse (the nearest eight within 60 m), and people who used to stay as sprites, such as a town's figures that belong to no faction, World of Daggerfall's dock people, its captives, merchants and prisoners and the crews on Come Sail Away's boats, stand in Morrowind bodies. Past the body range the sprites still stand, as do other animals, children, vampires' people and Daedra Princes. With Morrowind attached, Breton men wear three new heads and three new hairs (nine faces between them), each of the ten Breton portraits at character creation shows the face most like it (portrait 6 wearing the eyepatch), and if the heads fail to load Morrowind's own Breton faces show. Eleven of Daggerfall's townspeople (all four Redguard men, all four Nord men, two Breton men and the hooded Redguard woman) wear their own 3D outfits in their picture's colours, with hoods over the hair, sleeves, hems and boots moving with the body, legs staying inside skirts and robes, and a woman wearing her modest shirt while Show Nudity is off. Townspeople without an outfit yet keep their varied wardrobe. The new Breton heads are for player characters only (and no longer sit backwards), NPCs wearing Morrowind's own heads, and the Breton eyepatch face sits on the right head with its own hair. In towns with market stalls the food, goods and animals at the stalls show as themselves again with the Morrowind people on. Putting a helm on or taking it off no longer leaves the old head picture in GPU memory on the large HUD, and a helm put on while the large HUD is first loading shows on its head. With Morrowind People on, a small icon bobs over the people worth finding: a yellow "!" over anyone a quest needs, an amber coin over merchants (shopkeepers, bankers and innkeepers) and a blue gem over guild trainers, spell sellers and quest givers, the witches' coven and the arena's officers. Walls hide the markers and they fade out beyond about 30 metres; classic sprites get none, and Morrowind People Marks on the Features page turns them off.
Online Sign-In ||| "World: Sign in to play online" no longer repeats for ever on a slow connection. Signing in and joining the world no longer fail for an hour or two after midnight UTC: each client waits a random 0 to 10 minutes past midnight before checking in, instead of every client at once.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole. Travelling fast no longer spends time building ground already passed, and the land ahead loads up to three times faster, so holes in the view and drops to 1x speed are much rarer, including on straight roads. While ground is still loading the view may run at about 30 fps instead of 60 until the land is built. Quest destinations show as gold diamonds, the followed quest filled in and pinned to the screen edge with its distance and time left, and clicking a mark starts a journey there. Dungeons are slightly smaller and stay the same size when zooming. While travelling it forecasts the weather ahead and when it will be reached, and standing still it shows the next few hours' weather, with a forecast on hover for any place. Travel inside a town slows to x5 and follows the streets, and the journey stops with a reason if the city gates are shut for the night. A journey no longer drops to x1 while the land ahead is loading, or because of an enemy camp that has not noticed the player. It shows the land out to the horizon, the haze thinning to where land meets sky, with the camera able to tilt up to 15 degrees, while rain, snow, fog and sandstorms still close in. Cloud shadows carry out to the far hills, distant land can be clicked to travel there, and distant hills fill the gap while nearby land loads. Coming down from the Overworld, crossing into a new region and the first hit's blood no longer freeze the game, and the grass, snow on the ground and weather create far less garbage, so long journeys hitch less.
Passage Under Sail ||| Friendly merchant and navy ships moored at a port's quays can carry the player: look at her hull and press Activate, and her captain lists every port the player knows, nearest first, with the direction it lies and the fare. After confirming, the player boards at her helm and the voyage is sailed across the Overworld, with nothing paid until she sets sail. If the voyage stops she sails on by herself, at the far port she lands the player, and stepping off her anywhere ends the passage, anything put in her hold coming back to the pack. She is never the player's own ship. Online, any player at the port can book a ship, other players can board and ride along, and once she has sailed she is gone from that berth for the rest of the day. "Under Sail" counts a voyage in any boat, counted from where the player last lay still, and running aground in the next harbour or leaving the helm as the ship comes in counts as making port. A voyage survives a reload or a crash between the two ports, Pencart's harbour can be reached, and the harbourmaster says that a port counts when it is another port's harbour a quarter league off or more.
Player Trade ||| Player trade was added in 0.1.3720. In a trade each letter of credit shows its worth on its own row ("Worth 12,500 gold") across the pack, the offer and theirs, working on touch. A realm trade of an item looted in the last couple of minutes no longer fails, a character whose trade is on hold can no longer move its crafted pieces through another character, and when the realm refuses a trade because a character is under review it says whose character it is. A purse, letter of credit or pile of gold holding more than 2,147,483,647 gold, more than Daggerfall itself can count, is flagged as tampered, and that character cannot trade, sell or send gold to other players until the problem is gone or staff clear it. The third time the realm's rules hold a character's trading it is frozen for a week and then reopens on its own, each later time for a month, strikes clearing a month after the last one, and the notice names the item and says when trading reopens. Pieces of Ruhn's Regalia (and the raiding parties' sets) enchanted before enchanting them was closed no longer freeze trading. Carrying a Weir Key no longer flags the character for review or freezes their trading, and characters whose trading was frozen for a week because of the Weir Key are unfrozen, a forged piece still carried holding its trade until it is gone. A crafted piece listed, auctioned or handed to a commission leaves the character's own pack (or home) on the realm at that moment and comes back as the very piece that left if cancelled, expired or unsold, and a crafted piece bought back from another player can be sold again even while its maker's trading is frozen. An item that cannot be traded now says so when offered, instead of sitting on the player's side unseen by the other player. A Soul Gem holding the soul of one of the Enemy Expansion's creatures no longer freezes trading, and a hold and strikes it caused were lifted.
Premade Classes ||| The eighteen premade classes train skills a player will use: each starts with a weapon skill it is allowed to use, or Destruction, among its primary or major skills. Barbarian leads with Axe, Blunt Weapon and Critical Strike, Acrobat gains Short Blade and Archery, Mage and Sorcerer both lead with Destruction (the Sorcerer adding Blunt Weapon), Healer has Blunt Weapon as a major, Archer takes Critical Strike over Hand-to-Hand, and no class spends a skill slot on a language. A Burglar can wear leather armour but not chain or plate. Characters already made keep their old skills.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. Every quest shows its objectives one at a time, with the journal and the quest tracker naming the step and a herald line telling when one is finished. Many quests ask the player to choose how they end, each ending paying and being remembered in its own way, and endings with guilds, temples and houses move the player's standing with them (online, a quest that ends unfinished still costs none). Quests that pay gear let the player choose one of three pieces made for the class, better the harder the quest. A quest giver in a Mages Guild hall no longer stands inside the guild's own quest-giver, clicking an NPC a quest is waiting on shows the quest's message and reward right away, and the questlines' givers carry the names their letters give them. A quest that sends the player to a temple or a guild hall always names it, quests already in the journal get their names back on the next load, and a palace chosen on the road has its proper name (Castle Daggerfall, Castle Wayrest, Castle Sentinel).
Revenant Missions ||| Mission on a sworn revenant sends it to any of Tamriel's nine provinces to Plunder for loot, Forage for crafting materials or Sell-sword for gold, taking one to four days of the character's time depending on the province (online those days pass only while playing, a day abroad being about two hours of play), the haul going straight into the purse, pack and Materials Bag on return. Each province has its own danger and goods (Skyrim's orichalcum and pelts, Morrowind's ebony and Dwemer metal, Black Marsh's venom), the odds depend on the revenant's rank and gear against the province's danger and are shown before sending, a good trip brings the whole haul and more loyalty, a hard trip half and a hurt revenant needing rest, and a revenant never deserts while abroad. Up to two can be abroad at once, and Recall brings one back at once empty-handed after the page asks. Online a mission keeps real time and keeps going while the player is logged off, a day abroad being 2 real hours, with the time left shown in real minutes and hours; resting, waiting or travelling does not speed it up.
Sailing ||| Players can sail together and set sail from their own ship, and sailing from the Overworld tells the player how many days of their time the voyage takes. Overworld travel by sea sails straight for a port that lies upwind instead of zig-zagging, steers well clear of islands at the new speeds and sails through fog instead of rowing. A save made in a dungeon or building no longer leaves a ship adrift under sail on a load outdoors; the load has the outdoors' wind.
Sea ||| The sea deathloop was fixed. The ocean no longer stays nearly black after sunrise and before sunset; it brightens with the sky.
Ships at Sea ||| Pirates, merchantmen and the navies of Daggerfall, Wayrest and Sentinel sail the Iliac Bay, each with a name and a captain and sailing the same boats the player can own. Pirates attack anything, merchantmen run, and a navy hunts pirates and the player once notorious enough, with most pirates only coming for a ship they outgun or one crippled and running from one that outguns them. Ships sail by the wind, keep off the rocks and fight broadside to broadside, a hostile ship nearby counts as an enemy nearby blocking fast travel and rest, and ships fight each other on their own, with the navy hearing cannon fire and sailing toward it. When a ship strikes to another the winner boards her and sets her on fire, a ship with no crew left surrenders, a galley's ram forces surrender rather than sinking, and packets sail lanes between neighbouring port towns on one shared schedule, berthing, waiting and sailing on. Friendly ships have green health bars and turn hostile if fired on, ships are named for their trade and waters, and the Ships at sea setting decides how many are out, none when it is off. Warm Ashes' Overworld raiders are real ships at sea. Sea ships fly their own colours: navies use their kingdom's paint and royal ensign (Daggerfall's blue with the dagger, Wayrest's crimson with the rose, Sentinel's gold with the sun), merchant ships belong to one of eight trading houses with the house's paint and mark (the Isles' teal past the Veil), pirates use one of six crews' dark paint and black flags with false colours painted as the merchant house until the black flag is raised, and legendary ships have their own colours. A ship that surrenders, sinks or is captured lowers its flag, and cloth flags show on ships within 450 m (up to 24 at once) with the old pennant further away. Winning a raid at sea ends it, removing the raid's crew and any late wave so travel, resting and the Overworld stop saying enemies are near, and an enemy ship that leaves the sea mid-fight takes its raid's crew with it. The pirate attack's victory gong rings again. Going below to the ship's cabin is not allowed while enemies are nearby. Ships far out at sea, other players' boats and the player's moored boats far away cost much less to draw.
Snow ||| With Snowless Swamps and Jungles switched on, swamps and jungles get no deep snow or falling snow in winter, and rain/snow no longer become choppy after hours of open play. Steep mountainsides keep their snow in winter, the straight green band up snowy ranges being gone, though faces steeper than 50 degrees still grow no trees. Turning Weather as places or Enhanced environments on or off mid-game redraws the ground properly.
Snowfall ||| In winter, outside the deserts, real snow lies on the ground, deeper in the wild than in towns, building up while snow falls and melting back in clear weather. The player, monsters, guards and townsfolk leave tracks in it, which slowly fill back in (faster while it snows) and are kept in the save, and a fallen enemy leaves a hollow where it lay. With Basic Roads, paths and tracks keep only a thin layer of snow and main roads get a bank along their edges, and the winter ground under it has new textures. It has its own switch in Features, on by default in the enhanced outdoors. Foes' ground telegraphs and the player's technique marks always draw on top of deep snow, and deep snow can be turned off at Settings > Graphics > Sky, water & weather > Snowfall. Snow settles where it falls and melts as the air warms, so in a thaw white mountains stand over green valleys. The weather's snow no longer costs about 18 ms a frame while the snow on the ground catches up with the weather: it keeps to its 3 ms and the snowline settles in over a few seconds. A memory leak in winter snow, about 1.8 MB for every map area travelled through, was fixed.
Social ||| The social arc was audited under four lenses: the hub, the link, the surfaces, and the maps and input (world79). Clicking Social, Chat, Show or Play online here no longer leaves that button selected, so the next jump does not open the friends panel again.
Soul Trap ||| The player's soul trap fills their own gem whoever lands the killing blow, and when their strike traps another player's monster they do not see "Trap active." A soul-trapped Dragonling gives -5000 enchantment points as Soul Bound, where before only the rare quest Dragonling did. A Soul Gem holding the soul of one of the Enemy Expansion's creatures (an Ogre, a Goblin, a Troll and the rest) no longer freezes the player's trading.
Starfall ||| Every real hour during the Witches' Festival is a night, and each night one of the Bay's twelve covens pulls three stars down into the wilds of its region, each near a different town. A star burns in the sky for a minute and a half before it falls, and chat, the map, the compass and the Timers window point to it. Once it lands the player holds it through four waves of the coven and then their master, who calls a circle twice and takes only a quarter of every blow while any of it stands; an unheld star is lost 15 minutes after it falls. On the 13th of Frostfall the last star is the Heart-Star, with a larger circle and larger rewards. A held star gives Renown, 30 silver, gold, 1 to 3 pieces of Starfall Glass, a magic item or better and Soul Cakes, and one star in four also gives a piece of the Starspun Weave, Mephala's nine-piece set (always from the Heart-Star). Each wave is larger and grows as the fight goes on: alone 4, 5, 6 then 7 foes, two more for every player beside, up to 24 in the largest. The coven's waves and the master's circle have twice their usual health, while the master is unchanged. The coven's breath between waves is 5 seconds, waves gather just outside the crater, and in a group a quickly cleared wave ends when cleared. The mound around the crater is solid and cannot trap a player.
Stores ||| The Stores hold what the player gathers, up to 5,000 of each material per character, and are kept in town, so the Stores page in any town moves materials. The page has filters, search and sorting, and Withdraw to pack takes up to 200 at a time, every material able to be withdrawn, with herbs coming out as Daggerfall's own northern or southern ingredient that works in the potion maker. Emptying the bag into the pack is possible from the Stores page anywhere, and a withdrawal whose answer was lost is sent again when opening the page, never minted twice. When the Stores have no room for what a craft would refine, the box says so. The Stores page opens with a row of station tiles marked Here or Away, a station the player is away from staying folded until its tile is tapped, with smelting, other workings and patterns shown as cards, and materials and items show the game's own item pictures. Ingredients looted, bought or given go into the Stores as "loose", usable for crafting at any station but not for writs, the guild's Stores or market sales, which only what was gathered can serve. Putting goods in no longer locks the buttons for a long time, since everything put in at once is saved once, and Temper at the Anvil and Temper at the Loom are back on the page. The Stores page no longer claims Arcane Essence can go to the pack, and says "3 Mudcrab Chitin", like "3 Scorpion Chitin".
Tab Key ||| Mac's four reports about the Tab key were fixed. Pressing Tab no longer moves the keyboard onto the Social button or any other on-screen button.
Townspeople ||| Townspeople in the living world run from a monster that comes close, along the street rather than through walls, and keep their distance. Those who stop to talk turn to face each other, a group waits until all have walked over and then takes turns, and the player hears them only then. People keep the same company about two minutes before moving on, with much less repetition, and indoors people at a table start talking as soon as they sit together. A stranger who said nothing as the player passed now speaks when the player stops in front of them, and a townsperson who stops for the player stays exactly where they stopped. They no longer stand inside walls or out over the harbour water or walk on the spot, and since 0.1.6640 they keep room from one another: people waiting at one spot keep a little distance, two leaving a shop together walk out in single file, and a walker steps aside, keeping right. Each square has several gathering points and every neighbourhood has a corner where its people meet, stall-keepers set up at the square or their own market, and people head out for the evening and the morning market at their own time. Guild members, priests, adventurers, sellswords and couriers walk the streets in their class, and someone keeping their place on their own appears as one of Daggerfall's own pictures of their kind, becoming themselves again the moment they walk on or join a conversation. A cat that meowed many times a second while the player looked at certain townspeople was fixed. Townsfolk inside a building spread through the whole room and sit at tables set apart, never at a table split by a wall, nobody waits at a tavern for hours for lunch, and everyone in a room sees the same people in the same places. In the street townsfolk fall out with each other, more often in the evening between drinkers, buyers haggle at stalls, beggars ask for a coin, and when someone shouts the people nearby fall quiet and turn to look. Small voices fill the street (the night watch calling the hour, stalls crying their wares, beggars calling out, drinkers singing home), every player in a town sees the same incidents, and beggars and stall keepers turn to face whoever they are talking to. Wisps ||| Wisps were reworked, first as fewer, darker wisps (DISC17) and then as original wisps. The wind wisps are more transparent and shorter, still showing which way the wind blows without standing out against the scenery.
Witches' Festival ||| The Witches' Festival is the first live event, running from 20 October to 7 November. Killing foes, gathering, crafting, fishing, winning tavern games, finishing quests and bounties, sailing, the endgame and holding a fallen star can earn Soul Cakes, which go straight into the pack, more often from the dead and the summoned and doubled on All Hallows' Eve and on the in-game 13th of Frostfall and 3rd of Hearthfire; a linked account and being online are needed to keep them. A Glenmoril witch outside the taverns trades them for The Wyrd's Raiment, a nine-piece Aetheric set, the Witchwarden title, the Witchfire aura, profile icons and six Iliac Hand festival cards. Every town is decorated in its own region's style. Soul Cakes have a daily limit of 120; the game tells the player on reaching it and when more can be found (midnight UTC, shown in local time), and the Wyrd Sister's window shows how many were found today.
Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map. Wild Zone halls no longer vanish partway through their day after someone has been inside one. Dying in the Wrothgarian Mountains while the wagon is parked outside the zone, or sent to the stable, loses nothing from it, while a wagon driven, led or parked inside the zone still loses its cargo. A worn item looted from a player in the open PvP zone reaches the looter even if they share a guild or party, staying held until collected and going back to its owner if nobody does, and members of the same guild can no longer attack each other there, as with a party. Players can fight each other in the zone's dungeons again, as intended, and their blood shows there; every attack on another player underground had been refused. A companion's pack is at stake with the player's there: a death drops what the crew hand or sworn revenant carries, companions cannot be sent away from inside the zone, and one knocked out there leaves its pack with the player. All the gold a player drops dying in the Wrothgarian Mountains shows in their remains, however much, and taking only part of it leaves the rest showing.
