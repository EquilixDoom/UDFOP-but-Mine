Arena Bouts ||| An arena bout is a fight on the sand in which nobody dies. At 1 health the fighter goes down, and at 15% or less they can sheathe to yield. The healers make everyone whole afterwards, purses run from 50 gold for a first-tier win to 10,000 for the Grand Champion, and progress, titles and record are saved with the character. The player cannot rest, travel or leave through the gates during a bout, and set bonuses, sigil weapon bonuses and Legendary powers switch off against other players. An enchantment's own bite, such as a Damned blade's or any Health Leech weapon's, no longer kills the player in a bout but leaves them at 1 health like every other blow there. Hits from one side of the opponent no longer always count as backstabs, and backstabs no longer happen against players.
Caravans ||| A merchant's caravan travels with a horse and wagon (two wagons from a great house's city) with its guards walking in front and behind, a noble's procession brings a baggage wagon, and pedlars and farmers lead a pack horse. The wagons' wheels turn, at camp the wagons are parked round one fire without standing in each other with each horse tied alongside its cart, and a wagon follows its horse round a bend. Caravans and travellers keep to the right of the road, and a caravan's mark on the travel map is a wagon. Talking to a caravan's merchant, a farmer or a pedlar allows buying and selling from a shop's stock and a limited purse, with friends getting 10% off. A merchant's caravan offers a guard contract from the day before it sets out until it arrives, paid by the day plus more for each foe beaten if the player stays within 300 m, and unpaid if they fall behind over an hour, let the merchant die or rob or kill any of them; offline the player can travel on with it to its next stop. Beating every guard of a caravan makes it surrender and its goods and purse are the player's to take, but any survivor who reaches a town reports the theft, pickpocketing or murder as a crime in the region where it happened. A road caravan's campfire is a fire again with Morrowind models on, and a roadside fight the player walks up to no longer ends by itself. If the overhead lantern or another built-in piece is taken out of a caravan, visitors no longer see it or its light.
Chat Window ||| The chat window had a run of fixes. The closing Enter belongs to the window, the chat list holds its rows, the hide button survives a frame, a doubled line was removed and the chat fits its space. The player list no longer rebuilds and re-sorts every frame while the chat is open, and the friends list sorts faster. The chat has a search bar above the player list where part of a name, or # and a tag, shows every tab's lines from or about that player, and /search <name> searches from the chat field while /search alone clears it. Chat tabs scroll sideways with the mouse wheel, the edge with more tabs fading and a clicked tab scrolling fully into view.
Companions ||| Companions are allies who follow the player: a boat's hands taken ashore and sworn revenants. Up to three slots are shared between sworn revenants and any ship's crew ashore. They attack anything hostile, never take damage from the player's own blows, follow through doors by the path the player walked, and arrive and leave through a swirling violet portal. A companion who falls is carried off to recover for 8 hours, and loyalty matters, since a neglected one deserts and a disloyal Unhinged, Craven or Brutal one may turn on the player. A pause-menu Stats page shows who is At your side or Away, with Send away, Call and Release, and each companion's pack has a weight limit by strength. Companions do not follow the player into the Burning Court, and a companion through the carrack's middle house leads down to her gun deck. A companion standing between the player and a body, loot pile, ship's helm, door or container within reach no longer takes the click, which uses that object, while with nothing usable behind them clicking still opens their pack, and the on-screen label shows which one a click will use. With Morrowind data attached, crew companions and sworn revenants keep their own race, face and clothes through every door, dungeon and reload, and a revenant is the same person every time it returns.
DFO Grimoire ||| DFO Grimoire is a fourth look under Main menu > Overhauls > UI, dressing everything Enhanced Plus does in GrimoireUI's parchment, wood, red-and-gold presses, glass vials and lettering. The vitals are three vials, upright in the corner in Daggerfall's order or lying in a row under the hotbar, and the card offers Pages, Backdrop, Vitals, and Spellbook choices. The front door stands on a detailed desk, rarity/set/sigil names are inked deeper, parchment text is readable, stray ruled lines are removed, health bars scale with resolution, and settings open as scrolls. New features include a Text font setting under Overhauls > UI with five choices (Game, Sans, Monospace, Dyslexic, My font), distinct channel colours with colour squares and toggle options, player name colours, language channels in 15 languages with `/lang` and `/lc` commands, gold-highlighted `@mentions` with chimes, unread count seals on Grimoire tabs, and various UI/inventory/scry fixes. Its Tab dial uses Daggerfall's own icons, the character's face for Skills, the HUD star for Magic, the sack for Inventory and the scroll for Map, and the old paintings return under Settings > Overhauls > DFO Grimoire > Tab dial. Quick loot rarity marks show in it as well as in Enhanced Plus.
Decorator ||| The decorator offers furnishings for homes. With the town mods on it adds 297 pieces such as beds and rugs, changing within a second when a town mod is turned on or off. It also offers what Daggerfall stands in its dungeons (thrones, cages, coffins, statues, chains, braziers) and a yard offers Daggerfall's street pieces and its own town's climate trees and plants, each with its own picture. People placed with the decorator (the Vendors list) can no longer be resized, with Smaller and Bigger gone for them. Those placed earlier at another size now stand at their normal size, moving one sets it to normal, an enlarged one gives back half the price difference and a shrunk one costs nothing to move. Decorations set against a wall sit in front of it instead of sinking into it, at any size and with the grid on, and the grid snaps along the wall.
Duels ||| Duels are the player-versus-player foundation where fights are judged by the server against actual weapons and gear, using a separate duel vitality bar that leaves real health untouched, showing both bars from the start and recording wins and losses from server results. Melee and ranged attacks in duels, sieges and the Wrothgarian Mountains share the same damage ceiling as spells, at most 180 damage in any 5 seconds counting blows and casts together.
Etheric Sea ||| Sailing west of the Bay's mouth on the Abecean asks whether to sail into the open sea, leading to Veilhaven three days later, while sailing back asks the way home with warnings if enemies/players are close. Past the Veil lies an endless sea of isles (port isles with towns/quays, ruins with dungeons, wild isles with goods, and bare rocks), growing deadlier and richer further west through Shallows, Reaches, Deep Aether, and Abyss. No crown's law reaches past the Veil (no navy, crimes aren't counted, corsairs sail under sea names), ether-amber, starglass, and voidsilk are traded, wild isles float casks and refill in seven days, six legendary ships haunt the depths with purses, and the travel map key opens a chart of sighted isles. The Veil blocks magic (Recall, teleports, portal stones, party travel, Fleet ships) and falling wakes players at the nearest port isle. The light is cold and violet, the sea west of the Bay is water again, and while classic keeps Daggerfall's sea, enhanced/online play features this crossing. After crossing the Veil a ship too heavily loaded to move says so ("You're going to need a bigger boat"), and everything carried, gold and wagon included, counts against a ship's hold.
Furniture Stores ||| Furniture Stores show in the shop colour on both town maps, and like every other shop are named once the player has been inside or someone has marked them. Townspeople can be asked where they are, from a "Furniture stores" group in the Where-is list. Linen for beds is sold only at Furniture Stores.
Grass ||| Grass is the ground cover. Its default style is Meadow: hand-drawn tall and short green tufts, flowering tufts, dry tufts and bushes on crossed cards like the low-poly trees, each growing to its own size, with flowers in lush patches and dry grass in poor ones. It takes its colour and exactly the same light as the ground under it in every climate and season, including a texture mod's ground, stands on slopes, sways in the wind with stiffer bushes, follows the blended climate border, and far grass fades into the ground's colour. Grass is off by default; Settings > Grass turns it on at Full, Half or Quarter and picks the style, Meadow, Pixel (the old tufts) or Smooth. Grass no longer draws over a portal's vortex.
Home Votes ||| The door of another player's home offers Vote up and Vote down, becoming "Take back your vote" once voted, and on touch screens, the Classic interface or with World Tooltips off the door is clicked in Info mode. Each account has one vote per home, none on its own home, its own guild's hall or a Knightly Order house, voting needs a username and password held for a week and up to 30 votes an hour can be cast. If a home's votes add up to less than zero other players see it as Daggerfall built it, with no placed decorations and the town's own paint, while a hired trader stays open and the owner and a hall's guild members still see everything; nothing is deleted and the decorations return once the total is zero or above. A home with votes shows the total on its door and the owner is told when the decorations are hidden. A house's decorations are hidden from other players only when its votes reach -5 or lower, so a house at -1 to -4 stays fully visible and can be voted up.
Hoods ||| The hood of a worn cloak or robe can be raised and lowered from its card in the pack. A raised hood shields a vampire from the sun and protects a character whose class has the Damage from Sunlight disadvantage. Bare-headed, the sun works as before, and a hood carried in the pack, not worn, doesn't count. For vampires a raised hood still protects from the sun's penalty but no longer gives the night's +20 in daylight, while indoors, underground and at night the full +20 is kept.
Joinery ||| Every city and town has a Joinery beside its Stables and Wagon Yard, room permitting: a carpenter's open-air yard with a shingled workshop, a timber rack, a log pile, sawhorses and a chopping block, and a table, chairs and a bench on show by the gate. Its workbench is a Carpentry station, opened by talking to the carpenter or using the sign or the bench, which opens the Stores page at The Workbench, with a craft or a saw costing 50 gold as at a Furniture Store's bench and carpentry still done online. It can be found by asking townspeople under "Carpenters", on either town map, or by the saw mark on the Overworld map.
Loot Rarity ||| Loot rarity is a Diablo-style ladder over Daggerfall's loot, built on Unleveled Loot's law and on by default, with the ladder shown on the items. Clothing can drop as Magic and Rare with lines of its own: standing with a social group, warmth and weatherproofing (for Climates & Calories), Personality, and street skills, with footwear leaning to movement skills. Superior and Masterwork clothing the player makes comes out Magic and Rare, as crafted weapons and armour already did. Six Legendary garments each have a power: the Alik'r Wayfarer's Robes (heat felt 20 less), the Wrothgar Pass Mantle (cold felt 20 less), the Vestments of Stendarr (Restoration spells cost 25% less), the Pilgrim's Sandals (tire 25% slower, falls hurt half as much), the Silks of Queen Barenziah (+5 standing with every group) and the Shade's Cowl (+25 Stealth while hooded). With the loot rarity setting off, clothing stays exactly as in Daggerfall. Resistance from rolled gear counts up to 45% per element, and only fills an Aetheric piece's resistance up to that same 45%. Hone raises one line within its own range, and some Rares and Legendaries carry an empty socket. One Rare or Legendary item in twelve from a body or a pile is cursed, with one extra bonus line and one drawback: a bad reputation, bad reactions, damage or wear in holy places, weaker blows against a kind of foe, or a health leech. Gilded is the new top of the rarity ladder, above Aetheric and Artifacts, with its name in gold leaf and a frame breathing light in every slot. A rarity affix of +N% casting speed rolls on jewellery and weapons and adds up across everything worn, up to 30%. Rare weapons can no longer roll Vampiric Effect (weapons that already have it keep it), life leech from a weapon's lines and gems together is capped at 10% of each hit, Hist-Sap regenerates 1% a round (2% under half health), and Open Hand deals half damage on the second bare-handed hit.
Magic Costs ||| Online, resting, exhaustion collapse and cautious journeys briefly refilled magicka only to 50% of maximum and Restore Power potions were sold in alchemists and the Mages Guild. That change was undone in 0.1.5235: resting refills magicka fully again, as do cautious journeys, and Restore Power potions returned to their old price and shops. Online, a magic school past 100 no longer makes spells nearly free, since a mastered school with gear on top used to push a cost below zero so everything cast for 5 magicka. Past 100 each point still makes spells cheaper, a school at 200 casting at half what it costs at 100, the spell maker no longer shows negative costs, and at 100 or below and offline spell costs are unchanged.
Mining ||| Mining is a gathering profession utilizing wilderness ore veins, dungeon walls, and boulder quarrying with Pick-Axes and glint strikes, featuring region-specific ores and progression tied to confirmed land and player gathering activity. Copper, silver, platinum, moonstone, dwarven scrap, mithril, adamantium, ebony and orichalcum veins each have their own colour, while lead and lodestone keep their classic look. Gold, Platinum, Mithril, Ebony and a kingdom's own ore only appear on ground that three different players, each on an account at least a week old, have gathered on, since the servers cannot check which land a player claims to stand on, and the world map card shows which ores are waiting on that for each place. Ore veins are clearly different colours, Moonstone a pale blue, Lodestone rust red and Copper orange among them, and a boulder no longer looks like Lodestone.
Morrowind People ||| With the Morrowind body on, the people and creatures around the player stand in Morrowind bodies too: dungeon and wilderness foes, the city watch, townsfolk, quest givers, stable and wagon yard keepers, ship crews, siege fighters, road parties and the Oblivion Gate's boss and host. Foes wear the armour and carry the weapons they rolled with a race and face of their own, townsfolk are dressed by their station, foes swing, flinch and fall with their tells on the body, and a body is drawn at its sprite's size. Creatures use Morrowind's creatures where Daggerfall's have a match and keep their classic sprite otherwise. Online everyone sees a foe as its owner does. Bodies are drawn for the nearest people only within one budget for the whole frame, and a Morrowind People setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data is Off, Near (the default, up to 24 bodies) or All (up to 48). With Morrowind models on, other characters and horses keep their size wherever the camera is, and NPCs whose helmet fails to load show their head. The horses that stand in towns, at World of Daggerfall's bandit camps and forts and in the Stables stand as the 3D horse (the nearest eight within 60 m), and people who used to stay as sprites, such as a town's figures that belong to no faction, World of Daggerfall's dock people, its captives, merchants and prisoners and the crews on Come Sail Away's boats, stand in Morrowind bodies. Past the body range the sprites still stand, as do other animals, children, vampires' people and Daedra Princes.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes.
Privateer's Hold ||| Online, every character starts Privateer's Hold on their own, with its enemies alive, its doors shut and its loot untouched. Local chat in the Hold tells the player that nobody can hear them there. Online, Privateer's Hold is a shared dungeon like every other, with the same enemies, doors, levers and loot for everyone inside, and other players can be seen, chatted with and partied with there. Once the player leaves it they cannot go back in: its door, a Recall to a spot marked inside it and a quest that would send them there all say "You cannot go back into Privateer's Hold." and a Recall or quest arrival lands outside instead. A new game still starts inside the Hold and a character saved inside still loads back in, and offline nothing changes.
Project Legacy ||| Project Legacy is built-in and enabled by default, where characters found houses with surnames and seats, choosing death mechanics (Enduring, Bloodline, No lineage). Features include inherited stats/reputation, bequests, realm synchronization, permadeath protections, vampire/werewolf/wereboar Enduring exemptions, a fourth Family tab page ("How it works") explaining rules/inheritance/heirlooms/marriage/symbols, tree links ("New to the family?"), and wrapping long names. Passing the mantle online can no longer overwrite a sibling with the new heir's name and class, heirs resumed from the Online page start at the family seat instead of Privateer's Hold, and a home owned on a farm or manor can be made the family seat, with family members met there beginning their story there.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see.
Quick Loot ||| Quick loot takes from the plaque without a window, obeys the carry limit, no longer soft-locks, and has a classic version. Quick loot shows each item's rarity as a coloured gem before the name and a soft band of that colour under the row (blue Magic, gold Rare, orange Legendary, pale blue Aetheric, purple Artifact, gold-leaf Gilded), in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire.
Salvage ||| A sunk ship leaves her wreckage afloat beside her casks, larger and paler than a cask. Sailing through wreckage hauls it aboard for Carpenter's Stores (2 to 6) and powder for fire barrels, letting the player repair to full and refill their fire barrels without making port. If swimming, the stores go into the pack one at a time and what is too heavy is lost, a fast travel or a door first has the crew stow wreckage from ships the player sank, and online everyone in the room sees the wreckage and whoever reaches it first hauls it in. A button on the Salvage page salvages every Magic item at once, asking first. Classic Daggerfall magic items (guild-shop and Item Maker pieces, like a "Blazing Holy Dagger of Fireballs") appear on the Salvage page marked "Classic magic" and explain why they give no shards, instead of silently missing.
Travellers ||| On coastal roads travellers no longer camp, fight or leave their fallen in the sea. New travellers set out from the towns: farmers leading a pack horse to market day, hunters, patrols of knights walking a round between a city and its region's towns, nobles travelling between courts with retainers and minstrels going to play another town's tavern, and bigger towns send out more pedlars, pilgrims and couriers. At night parties stop at a roadside inn when one is close ahead and otherwise make camp with a fire, parties meeting on the road greet each other, warn each other of trouble and share a camp, roads a patrol has walked recently see half the trouble, and travellers and caravans keep to the right of the road. Caravans and other road parties set off from outside the city walls instead of walking through them.
Werewolf ||| The werewolf (WEREWOLF1) shipped at last. A beast cannot surrender, so when the watch stops the player they choose F frighten or N fight on. Frighten roars at the watch with a chance of 50% at even level, 5% per level of difference, from 10% to 90%, and if it works the guards run and the crime is forgotten, otherwise they stand their ground and the guard's blow lands. A frightened guard runs a few seconds then leaves, no body left behind, guards already on their way are called off while guards defending the town from monsters stay, and the watch never stops a beast on sight. Online, the full moon forces the change for its night only, dusk to dawn, 15 real minutes, with full-moon nights two real hours apart then none for fourteen. The curse's +40 Strength, Agility, Endurance and Speed and +30 to its seven skills apply only in beast form, so in human form the player is their own character again and the beast itself is unchanged. Changing form fully heals once a day, with changing back or changing again with Hircine's Ring no longer healing, and the beast's claws count as silver against creatures that need a better metal, so a Lich or an Ancient Lich takes reduced damage from them.
Yard ||| A yard is the ground around the player's house. Standing on their own lot and pressing Decorate places catalogue pieces outside, priced as inside, wholly on the lot, up to 60 pieces a yard, seen by everyone walking the street. A piece can stand at most 4 m above the ground, so stacking towers is no longer possible, though pieces already higher stay put and can be moved down or removed. Outside, pieces hold nothing and give no light and doors are for doorways only, a piece on the road is refused, and a long piece turned at an angle can no longer be placed through the house's corner. Other players' yard decorations no longer draw across the whole map and tiny yard pieces stop drawing once only a few pixels tall, with a Settings > Other players > Yard decorations option of Off, 75 m, 150 m or 300 m (the default), the player's own yard always showing. Yard pieces standing above the 4 m height limit were removed with no refund, and property lines are shared with neighbours, the ground between two houses split down the middle, so decorating in front of a neighbour's door is no longer possible. A shop, temple or tavern next to a house no longer claims half its yard, while the ground between two houses is still split down the middle.
