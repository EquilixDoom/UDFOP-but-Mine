Alchemy ||| Alchemy is a crafting profession for brewing potions. The cheapest potions are available from rank 0, resistances and Restore Power from rank 10, Invisibility at rank 70 and Purification at rank 90. Every brew earns XP by the potion's rank, plus 500 the first time each potion is brewed. A Novice's brew makes two potions instead of one.
Alchemy Station ||| An alchemy station brews Daggerfall's twenty potions, at any Alchemist's for 25 gold a brew or transmutation or at a home station. Ingredients come from the player's Stores. A brew makes one potion, two from Journeyman and three as a Master, and may come out Potent with +25% magnitude for healing, stamina, strength or power potions. Daggerfall's own potion maker is unchanged and earns nothing toward Alchemy. The stations name the craft they serve, "Provisioning" at the fire and the alchemy station. Online, pressing a home's alchemy station offers Make Potions or Brew on the Stores page, while offline it opens Daggerfall's maker as before. A Novice's brew makes two potions.
Climbing ||| Climbing lets the player pull up onto ledges, wall tops, parapets and roofs by pressing Jump at them, and hang from edges. Climbing skill sets reach, speed, grip time and how long a fall can still be caught, with every point past 100 making the player faster up to about 1.4 times at 200. From the ground a shoulder-height ledge is reachable at skill 0 and one above the head at 100, a full pack lowers reach, and a roof can be climbed from its eave up to 50 degrees. With Roleplay and Realism on, the player cannot climb while holding a weapon, and online others see the player on the wall and hear them climb. Climbing is not possible while fast travelling on the Overworld, a journey cannot be started while hanging on a wall, and climbing works as before at walking pace. In first person (classic view) the hands stay on the wall when climbing or hanging, sliding across with the stone as the view turns and going down the wall when looking up, and the two fists grip about shoulder width apart. When looking away from the wall to find the next jump, the arm on the side looked reaches out and stays fixed to the screen while its hand lets go, and the other hand keeps its hold. Holding a spell while climbing no longer shows a second pair of arms: the casting hands step aside while on the wall and come back on letting go, the spell staying ready, and casting while climbing shows the casting hands alone before the hands go back onto the wall.
Cooking ||| Cooking is a profession for making dishes at a lit fire. The player stands by it and opens The Fire on the Stores page, with no fee. Dishes are Hunter's Stew and Fisherman's Supper (rank 0), Orchard Tart (rank 10) and Feast of the Hearth (rank 70), each giving a bonus such as Endurance plus 5 for 2 hours or Strength, Endurance and Willpower plus 5 for a day for the player and nearby party. They are cooked in three pans, or five for a feast, and eating a dish again refreshes it instead of stacking. XP follows rank, plus 500 the first time each dish is cooked, and cooking a raw fish from the pack still works as a plain meal. Raw Fish in the Materials Bag can be cooked over a fire, the cooked fish going into the pack. Raw meat and raw fish weigh 1 kg and cooked meat and fish 0.5 kg; Hunter's Stew and the Fisherman's Supper weigh 0.5 kg.
Curfew ||| The curfew's night watch comes out at the sky's night. In a town under Curfew, a sentence gives back the whole doubled charge, and Curfew doubles only the crimes the town's watch charges the player with, not a crime reported on the road or a defaulted loan.
DFO Grimoire ||| DFO Grimoire is a fourth look under Main menu > Overhauls > UI, dressing everything Enhanced Plus does in GrimoireUI's parchment, wood, red-and-gold presses, glass vials and lettering. The vitals are three vials, upright in the corner in Daggerfall's order or lying in a row under the hotbar, and the card offers Pages, Backdrop, Vitals, and Spellbook choices. The front door stands on a detailed desk, rarity/set/sigil names are inked deeper, parchment text is readable, stray ruled lines are removed, health bars scale with resolution, and settings open as scrolls. New features include a Text font setting under Overhauls > UI with five choices (Game, Sans, Monospace, Dyslexic, My font), distinct channel colours with colour squares and toggle options, player name colours, language channels in 15 languages with `/lang` and `/lc` commands, gold-highlighted `@mentions` with chimes, unread count seals on Grimoire tabs, and various UI/inventory/scry fixes. Its Tab dial uses Daggerfall's own icons, the character's face for Skills, the HUD star for Magic, the sack for Inventory and the scroll for Map, and the old paintings return under Settings > Overhauls > DFO Grimoire > Tab dial. Quick loot rarity marks show in it as well as in Enhanced Plus. Under the DFO Grimoire dress every mark on the card tables shows in the dress's own colours, a chosen, hovered or keyboard-selected tile is marked on every page that uses tiles, and buttons that cost something (Concede, Scuttle, Steal and the like) change when hovered or reached with the keyboard. In the main menu the two pages appear only where the screen is tall enough, otherwise one page is shown, dotted lines are no longer drawn, the pause window's tabs wrap in long languages, and on phones the side menu (including "Main menu") is readable. On the DFO Grimoire page, Gilded names are written in a deep crimson ink.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top. A headstone the party has searched counts as searched for the whole party and the game says who searched it, coffins a stranger opened in tombs are the player's to search (including standing right next to one), and party members no longer vanish for a few seconds when the player goes through a tomb door. Bodies and their loot no longer return on re-entering a dungeon while another player there has the game in the background, since the room follows whoever is actually playing, and a room left stuck by a background player's game closing without saying goodbye now moves on to whoever is playing. Dungeon lifts and moving floors no longer trap the player or push them out of the map: a lift that meets the head waits until the player steps clear, a lowering floor stops above them, and a player who falls out of a dungeon is returned to its entrance. Walking under hanging decorations, such as the roots in Edworyan Laboratory, no longer teleports the player: 27 such objects across Daggerfall's dungeons now work only when clicked or struck, as in Daggerfall.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are sold wherever weapons are, in Weapon Smiths, Armorers, General Stores and Pawn Shops, and restocked each day. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang. On the Morrowind body it is held like a rifle, the right hand on its pistol grip and the left supporting the forend, carried at rest at a low ready with the muzzle down and across toward the left foot and coming up to a level ready stance while shooting, for a few seconds after and while foes are hurting the player, in first and third person, the Hourlock held the same way. In the Morrowind first-person view the aimed gun sits below the middle of the screen instead of riding over the crosshair, with the left hand still on its forend. The Dwarven Pistolet shares a single find with it, so each turns up half as often as the Thunderlock did on its own.
Elite Dungeons ||| Elite Dungeons were added in 0.1.4216. A spawned or Elite dungeon's despawn timer (7 in-game days, about 14 real hours online) starts only when someone enters it, so walking past no longer starts it and a dungeon nobody enters stays where it is. A dungeon that has despawned comes back 24 real hours later in the same spot with fresh timers, so emptied regions repopulate, and a character's save no longer keeps despawned dungeons gone forever. In an Elite Breach, leaving one Redoubt's loot behind and taking the next no longer makes the first Redoubt's leftovers vanish until a restart.
Family Tab ||| The Family tab is part of Project Legacy, in the pause window between Holdings and System. It has the Tree (zoom, pan, play as a sibling, pass the mantle, and where each member lives), the House (model, seat, generations, the living and the fallen, and the family's homes) and the Hall of Ancestors (every house founded), and keypad / opens it. The character sheet on the Stats tab also shows the house, its model and the player's generation and, in an Enduring house, the age against the lifespan, the years Arkay has taken and when the player becomes an elder. The Family tab explains who can be courted and how regard grows and fades.
Feast ||| A feast is shared with the party members at the table, not elsewhere in the town. A feast shared by a party member refreshes the player's and does not stack, a shorter feast never cuts a longer one short, one cast by a player outside the party is ignored, and only a real feast counts, not a spell merely named Feast of the Hearth. The Feast of the Hearth weighs 2 kg, down from 8.
Friends ||| Friends is the social list. The friends, requests and party now belong to the account, the same list wherever the player signs in, and a friend list someone else had set up under the account before was thrown away. An old build tells the player to update to see friends and party. Online, how townspeople feel about the player fades only over days actually played, half a point a day apart, not while logged out, and the player is told when someone comes to count them a friend and whether they can be courted.
Gilded Weapons ||| The Dragonfire Oath is a Daedric Dai-Katana that can drop from the Warden of the Burning Gate about one fight in a hundred, giving +40% damage, +15 Strength, +30 Long Blade and +15 Endurance; its Dragonfire makes a blow that lands on a Daedra burn it again for another third of the hit and burn every other Daedra within 3 m for the same. The Sunken Sovereign is an Ebony Saber that can drop from the Old Coil about one kill in a hundred for a ship that did damage, giving +40% damage, +15 Agility, +30 Long Blade and 10% life leech; its Undertow adds +10% damage to each blow on the same foe up to +50%, fading after 6 seconds without a hit, and heals the player for 10% when it kills. Both are shown in the Collections codex with their own hints. Gilded items are marked in crimson instead of gold, so they no longer look like Rares, and show a small gold crown with red jewels in place of the two stars on the inventory tile, the item card and the loot banner; the gold item art is unchanged. The Sunken Sovereign is now a gold-leaf Dwarven Pistolet using Archery, not an Ebony Saber, keeping its damage, Agility, life leech and Undertow, which also charges on shots that land, and its muzzle flash stays a flash. A Sovereign saber already owned stays as it is and stays legal to trade, sell and carry into sieges.
Market ||| The Market is a Notice Board tab for trading in Marks or gold, featuring grouped views (Buy, Sell, History), silver display, column names, supplier counters, and detailed split lists. Listings stand 72 hours for a 1% fee with immediate sales (minus 5% tax) and courier delivery, with strict gold/Drakes currency separation and item exclusions. Seat boards, market sales, and courier deliveries no longer slow down account services in regions where a guild holds a Charter. Market stalls look the way the pack draws them, with the keeper behind the counter and goods on the counter. Crafted, Goods, Auctions and Orders have pages so everything listed can be reached, not only the first 100; Crafted pieces and Auctions can be searched by the name on the listing, in the player's own language too; Crafted can be filtered by minimum quality; and listings can be sorted by cheapest, dearest, newest, best quality or ending soonest.
Notice Board ||| The Notice Board replaces rumor boards with corkboards featuring town/server news, player notes, pinning/reporting, merit/chapter standings, background loading, regional trader directories, a "What this is" panel, guild pledge buttons, seat war mechanical explanations, terminology guides, larger text/tap headers, and Work tab chapter/writ displays. The Work tab has a filter bar to pick a kind of work, search by material, guild, seat or name, tick "Only what I can fill" and sort by ending soonest, most silver or A to Z; it shows 40 cards at a time and keeps the choices until logging out, and the region's chapter panels sit below the writs. The Work tab posts three crafting writs a day.
Online Sea ||| Everyone nearby shares one online sea with the same ships, broadsides and sinkings, which another player's game takes over if its host dies or goes indoors. Pirates attack every player's boat but players cannot hurt each other's boats, and a navy ship that sees any player fire on a merchantman turns on them, judging each by their own notoriety. Boarding is shared and everyone in the room sees the fight on deck with the crew as allies with green bars. When players share a sea it has the fewest ships any of them chose under Ships at sea, a sunk ship's floating cargo shows for every player with each cask going to whoever reaches it first, and other players stand on a ship's deck as it sails and rocks. In a storm a galleon or Carrack no longer vanishes from other players' screens, and a short connection hiccup no longer puts a player off a friend's ship they are riding. Online, everywhere past Veilhaven's peace is open PvP, with the sea's depth bands as its tiers and the mountains' rules applying: a death at sea drops the bag and half the purse, and the dead rise at the nearest freeport. Foes on the isles are four times as strong plus the band's share, there are no private instances, and no teleport works into, out of or around the open sea. Sea travel has no speed cap.
Quests ||| Quests feature fixes for shared progress, combat visibility/hits for nearby players, sky-based timing, quest-givers, town layout compatibility, abandoned/reclaimed quest systems without timer/item loss, and dungeon sizing placement fixes. Quest monsters appear only for the quest's owner and the owner's party, in dungeons, buildings and outdoors, for side quests and the main quest. Other players cannot see them, hit them or be attacked by them, party members holding the same quest still get kill credit, and a player standing next to the owner may see them fighting something they cannot see. Quest enemies, like the Fighters Guild's rats, appear in shops as well as houses, and quests whose shop is in another town, such as "Open a Chest", show that town in the quest list, on the travel map and on the compass. When a party member's progress on a shared quest reveals a place on their map it appears on the player's too (Settings > Repair active quests fixes a copy that already says "Not on your map yet"), and answering "No" by mistake in The Lost Child no longer locks the quest. A stuck quest can be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle (main-story steps included), picking fresh places and clearing its old items, links and topics. A party member's kill no longer steals the player's own copy of a main-story foe, quest markers in small and medium buildings sit inside the building, quest foes no longer stand on a hall's ceiling, the player is told when a quest's foe falls (HUD, chat and classic line) with the quest box ignoring presses for a moment, and an abandoned quest's item can be dropped and is handed back if the quest is reclaimed. Daedra summoning whose quest cannot be offered where the player is no longer takes their gold or marks the Daedra as summoned. Quests no longer pick another player's home as a quest residence, and if a quest's residence is in another player's home its door shows the quest's name, such as "The Hawkley Residence (Aldric's home)". A foe that appears inside a pillar is moved out beside it. A stuck quest can also be restarted from its first step by pressing Restart twice in the pause window's Quests tab or in the chronicle. Quest dungeons are always the full Daggerfall layout, so quest targets like the main quest's mummy can no longer end up unreachable from the entrance of a smaller online dungeon, and Quest Repair in a shared dungeon tells the player where the quest's things are instead of "Nothing was missing". Online, "Guard the Guild" needs Mages Guild standing 10 and "Protect an Honored Mage" standing 20. The Possessed Child can turn out to be a scam (the child fakes, the Daedroth never appears and the child runs off with the relic), and a journal entry now records what happened and sends the player back to the tavern where the guardian was staying. Every quest shows its objectives one at a time, with the journal and the quest tracker naming the step and a herald line telling when one is finished. Many quests ask the player to choose how they end, each ending paying and being remembered in its own way, and endings with guilds, temples and houses move the player's standing with them (online, a quest that ends unfinished still costs none). Quests that pay gear let the player choose one of three pieces made for the class, better the harder the quest. A quest giver in a Mages Guild hall no longer stands inside the guild's own quest-giver, clicking an NPC a quest is waiting on shows the quest's message and reward right away, and the questlines' givers carry the names their letters give them. A quest that sends the player to a temple or a guild hall always names it, quests already in the journal get their names back on the next load, and a palace chosen on the road has its proper name (Castle Daggerfall, Castle Wayrest, Castle Sentinel). "The Lost Artifact" finishes about two minutes after reporting back to the knight, and Settings > Repair active quests puts back a quest's townsperson who went missing from their home, such as the person The Courier's or The Delivery Boy's package goes to.
Sea Chart ||| The chart past the Veil (opened with the map key) uses the game's own enhanced font, with names drawn after every island marker with a dark outline, each on whichever side of its island is clear. A depth ruler along the top names each stretch of water in view (the Shallows, the Reaches, the Deep Aether, the Abyss, the Veil and the world's edge), each depth band is its own shade of water, a league grid replaces the old stars and the world's edge is marked. Each island type has its own shape as well as colour (a port is round with a harbour dot, a ruin a diamond, a wild isle a peak, a bare rock a cross), larger islands show their real size when zoomed in, Veilhaven is ringed in gold, and a key under the chart explains every icon. A legend's haunt is a red-washed square while she sails and hatched grey while she lies sunk, the player's ship is a white arrow in a brass ring with an edge arrow giving the distance when off-screen, and pointing at or tapping an island rings it, draws a bearing line and shows a card with its name, type and distance. The chart fits the screen at any size and its words follow the chosen language, though the sea's own names are still in English for now. The captain's chart draws Veilhaven's peace as a green ring, with a key entry, and marks each day's vaults, the Aether storm and the treasure fleet.
Sea Serpent ||| Every four hours Sethrakul the Old Coil, a great serpent, rises on a packet lane in open sea. Harbour bells ring fifteen minutes before, chat names the port and the map rings its waters, and players sail out and fight it with their ships' guns, each ship that joins making it tougher. It lashes with its tail, rams down a lane, bursts up under its mark and spits venom, from two-thirds health the Coil wraps a ship (broken by focus fire to stun it), and from one-third the Maelstrom drags ships toward a whirlpool's eye. It no longer teleports but swims every move, with a wake showing where it is going, and aims where the player's ship will be when an attack lands, surging at the player on the surface when out of reach and giving up after about 12 seconds of being outsailed. While exactly two ships fight it its attacks deal two thirds of their damage, so two ships can win, though one alone cannot in fair weather and three or more take full damage. A broadside is judged where the serpent was when the shot landed. A storm closes its waters fifteen minutes after it rises and ten minutes before it dives, every ship whose guns did a tenth of her share earns a hoard of gold and Magic-or-better pieces including a Deadlands Ember, and each serpent slain is worth six quests of Renown and pays 40 silver (20 for a ship standing by), toward the same 150 silver a day. Sethrakul's waters stay open until it dives, so the 08:00 storm no longer locks ships out, and the serpent has a sixth less health, so two ships at average gunnery win every time in about 8.5 minutes though one ship alone still cannot. Inside its waters a gale fills the sails from every direction and ships turn 1.5 times faster, its blows throw a ship half as far and tear half as much canvas, it is 1.6 times thicker so slow cannonballs fired with lag still land, and players on someone else's ship can hurt it with their own bows and attack spells. Ships have a wide invisible buffer and bounce off each other, rewards no longer depend on being near when it dies, a real part of the damage or a quarter of the fight within range earns a hoard, and party members of any ship that dealt heavy damage also get one if they came to the fight. Killing the serpent has a rare chance to grant the title Coilbreaker (1 in 20 kills) and the aura The Maelstrom (1 in 50), a three-armed whirlpool of tide and foam around the player's feet, kept for good and announced in a popup the first time. A ship that did damage may also win The Sunken Sovereign, about one kill in a hundred. Its Gilded weapon is a gold-leaf Dwarven Pistolet, not an Ebony Saber.
Sentences ||| Paying a fine or serving time now gives back what the arrest cost the player's standing, though assault, murder and treason still leave half of it as a lasting mark, and winning in court clears it all. Serving a prison sentence counts like paying a fine: once the days are served it improves standing with the region's law by 5, and reputation with its people, up to the point where the watch stops knowing the player's face. It mends the name held before the arrest, not the mark the crime leaves, counts once a day and tells the player, and road patrols leave the player alone for a day after release.
Spawned Dungeons ||| Spawned dungeons are ported and expire, 2 in-game days when cleared and 7 in general. They are announced on their own pixel in metres, appear only where the ground is dry land above the beach line and no longer on beaches or at sea, and pixels that once held a shoreline spawn are now empty land or sea for everyone. Random dungeons in the online wilderness are a quarter fewer; one that remains is the same place as before.
Titles ||| Titles are worn from the account card beside an aura or glyph, some given to particular players: SirMcMobdon, Tabby, Flylighter (Crystal Fist, with three purple slashes, alongside Disciple), Penitent (Diggleborf's, gold fading into sky blue), Aegis of Oblivion (Sureme's, violet, with a glyph of three pillars through a ring) and Primarch (made for GA00250, in light gold with a three-barred cross glyph). Players who first played on the evening of September 24 in the Americas hold the Founder title. Changing the aura, buying an insignia or unlinking Patreon no longer hides a Charter's title. A title or aura earned, including the Abyss Dungeon's Hourbreaker and The Turning Hour, gets a loot popup in Enhanced Plus the first time each is earned. The $50 Hierophant Patreon tier grants its title and glyph in game, the rose "Hierophant" and the crown beside the name, appearing on the next sign-in for an already linked Patreon. WikiMaid is SylviaBun's title, drawn amethyst into lilac into a bunny's blush, with a glyph of a lilac bunny in a maid's lace headdress beside her name (and on the account card, the chat roster and the travel view). The Elite Breach adds Spirewalker, Beacon-Lighter, Kynbane, Battlemage of the Spire, Wellhead-Walker, the Unfallen, the Lone Beacon, Spire Champion and First of the Spire. First of the Spire goes to Serveride (seen in the world as Severide), the first ever to take Redoubt 10 of the Battlespire, alone, in 1:17:40, with its aura Firstlight, and a letter from the Developers waits in the mailbox. Wearing a profile icon, or showing or hiding a glyph, no longer makes the Arena, Iliac Hand, King's Gambit or Spire champion titles vanish until a reload, and the profile card names a worn title instead of its internal key.
Travel Speed ||| The Overworld's speed dial reaches ×100 on a road and ×60 off it, on foot or mounted, fed or hungry. Max travelling speed via Travel view was briefly reduced to ×10, or ×20 on a fed horse, before the limit was removed.
Aether Storm ||| Every two hours an Aether storm blows for fifty minutes somewhere in the Deep Aether or the edge of the Abyss, the same storm in the same place for everyone and shown on the captain's chart. Inside it are thunder, rain and wind, and lightning can strike a ship, damaging her hull and sails and sometimes setting her on fire, though a strike alone never sinks her. Every 90 seconds it throws up a cask of wrack, Eltheric goods and now and then a Tidemail piece, each cask hauled once.
Auto-Run ||| Auto-run, which used to be the middle button, is on F3.
Booked Passage ||| A ship booked passage on is sailed by her master while under way, so the player cannot steer her; if the voyage stops, from an enemy nearby or running aground, the player can handle her until the master takes her up again. Her helm panel shows where she is bound, with the time dial and Leave the helm. Booked ships no longer sail onto land near docks, including the stretches of sea chart near Daggerfall that are really dry land, and finish the last stretch into port.
Collections ||| Holdings > Collections is open to every player in six parts: Titles, Auras, Icons, Glyphs, Codex and Cards. Every title, aura, profile icon and glyph is listed, held or not, grouped by where it comes from, with a tile showing how many are held of the total and a row saying whether it can be had now (Obtainable, By chance, Ranked, Seasonal, Patron reward and so on). Rows open to say how to get it, can be filtered by All, Held, Not held or Obtainable, and anything held can be worn, taken off, shown or hidden from the page. It is readable on every theme and usable with a keyboard or a gamepad, and the Codex is translated.
Crafting Writs ||| The Work tab on every Notice Board posts three crafting writs a day for an account: a potion, a dish and a Repair Kit, or a second potion if none of its characters smiths. The Mages Guild, the Fighters Guild and the Temples of Stendarr and Mara ask for them. They are filled with potions, dishes and kits the player made, not found or bought ones, by any of the account's characters, and are drawn at the best crafter's rank. Each pays silver, crafting experience, Renown and standing with the guild that asked, and the card shows what is held toward each writ and why Deliver is waiting.
Deep Vaults ||| Every day four ruin isles of the Deep Aether and three of the Abyss are vaults: elite dungeons that work like the Wrothgarian Mountains' daily halls. A vault's foes are the band's toughest, leaving locks the player out for an hour unless their own remains lie inside, and an empty vault resets after twenty minutes. Crows gather over a vault where someone has fallen, which everyone on the open sea past Veilhaven's peace hears, and the captain's chart marks each day's vaults. Their treasure piles have a quarter chance at a Tidemail piece in the Deep Aether and half in the Abyss.
Dwarven Pistolet ||| The Dwarven Pistolet is a one-handed Dwemer flintlock that fires the Thunderlock's Dwemer Pellets for 4-15 damage, using Archery, weighing 2.5 kg and worth 260 gold. With one in each hand the pistols fire in turn, each reloading on its own, the off-hand shot hitting softer and leaning on Agility. It has its own look in first person, on the paper doll and in inventory lists, a modelled 3D gun on Morrowind models, and its own stance on Morrowind bodies: low with the muzzle down at rest, the arm out on the eye line when aiming. It is found from tier 4 sources and up with 6-18 Pellets and never sold in shops, and Magic and Rare guns no longer repeat their metal in the name.
Eltheric Tidemail ||| The Eltheric Tidemail is a set of nine Adamantium pieces found only in the Deep Aether and the Abyss. Two pieces give Stormwrack (shock resistance and Speed), four give Undertow (extra damage on blows, in full on the open sea past Veilhaven's peace and half elsewhere) and six give The Captain's Due (the ship's guns hit harder and her hull takes less damage). It drops from the Deep Aether's legendary ships one time in four, the Abyss's legends every time, ruin treasure piles, the daily vaults, the treasure fleet's flagship and the Aether Storm's wrack.
Lost and Found ||| Loot left on the floor after a boss (Oblivion Gate, Abyss Dungeon, Elite Breach Redoubts) waits in the mailbox instead of being put in the pack, each item arriving from the Lost and Found to claim into the character being played, while gold goes straight to the purse. The mailbox has a Claim all button when two or more items are waiting.
Party Ping ||| Pressing the middle mouse button marks anything looked at for teammates, in a dungeon, a house, outdoors and in the travel view. The mark lasts 5 seconds and fades in and out, and a teammate can lay a dotted route to it by pressing the middle button on the pin.
Potion Drops ||| Enemies and loot piles drop Potions of Healing and Restore Power less than half as often: about 9 potions in 100 looting foes at Renown 20, down from 19. Brewing is the steady supply and a drop a windfall.
Sea Bounties ||| A freeport's harbourmaster keeps a Wanted board of every player who killed another player at sea in the last 14 days and anyone with a bounty on their head. Players post 500, 2,000 or 10,000 gold of their own on a wanted captain, bounties add up, and whoever ends the captain at sea by another player's hand or wrecks the ship with their guns is owed the whole bounty, collected at any freeport's harbourmaster. A bounty returns to its poster if the captain's character is deleted or stays dead two hours, a character with bounties, plunder or hold goods waiting cannot be deleted, and posting on oneself or one's own account's characters is not allowed. Only kills at sea count, for online realm characters.
Season Boards ||| A freeport's harbourmaster shows two seasonal boards for the whole realm: the deepest voyages past the Veil and the most legends sunk or taken. Seasons are the realm's eight-week seasons, the same as the arena's, and each board lists the top ten, the player's standing and last season's leaders. Figures reach the boards with the character's saves, and voyages made offline or in a copy brought online do not count.
Ship Battles ||| Past Veilhaven's peace, broadsides hit other players' ships and never those of the player's party or guild. The servers referee every shot (rate, damage and reach), a ship's hull setting how hard she hits, and a ship is whole again after two minutes nobody hits her. A ship battered to nothing is wrecked and lies crippled, not sunk; for three minutes another captain can come alongside to board her and take a share of her hold (her most valuable piece, then more of her best goods up to a third), plunder going to the pack or the harbourmaster's "Prizes taken at sea", and unclaimed goods can be reclaimed at a freeport after three days. Wrecking a player's ship counts as a kill at sea for the bounty board.
Treasure Fleet ||| Every three hours a treasure fleet sails for ninety minutes, a laden merchant carrack and two or three corsair escorts heading east through the Deep Aether or the Abyss, shown on the chart, with a freeport's harbourmaster saying when the next sails. Sinking or taking the flagship earns her strongroom (6,000 gold in the Deep Aether, 12,000 in the Abyss) and a Tidemail piece, once per sailing, for every captain whose guns were on her in her last twenty seconds. Online, a flagship that goes down scatters the fleet for everyone until the next sailing.
Unstuck ||| /unstuck past the Veil, where it works in Veilhaven's peace or offline, takes the player to the nearest freeport. It does not work outdoors on the open sea.
