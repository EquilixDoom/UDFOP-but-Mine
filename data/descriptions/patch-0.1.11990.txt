Account Recovery ||| Account recovery lets an operator issue a recovery code (RECOVER-OP) so a player can regain access to an account. New accounts get a set of 8 recovery codes, each working once if the password is forgotten. The Account section of the profile shows how many are left, and Make new codes gives a fresh set on typing the password and one current code, after which the old codes stop working. Codes can be copied or saved as a file (dfo-recovery-codes-<username>.txt), using a code spends only that code and using the last one gives a fresh 8, someone who learns the password alone cannot replace the codes, and the codes screen cannot be closed by accident until the player confirms they have written them down. A recovery now always holds, so a person who knows the password cannot undo it, and two recoveries at the same time no longer sign out the second one.
Bank ||| The Bank holds gold and makes loans. Online, one account is kept for the player at every bank, drawable at any other, and houses, ships, guild deposits, the market, homes and rent use that one account. A loan still belongs to the region where it was taken and the account repays it. Every online loan was forgiven in a one-time amnesty, the bank window names the region whose account it shows, and the Bank's card shows combat silver earned today. On phones a tap on the amount box opens the number keypad, and the box typed in sits above the Bank window.
Blocked Players ||| Trades and journal pages from a player the character blocked no longer arrive.
Bloodline ||| A Bloodline house with no relatives alive offers a newborn heir, where the Bloodline rules allow one.
Bounty Hunts ||| A bounty hunt is the fight a notice sends the player to. An open-ground notice marks a land spot 4 to 10 map pixels from the town with a black circle, and walking onto it summons 4 to 8 monsters whose kinds depend on the player's level, from Rats and Giant Bats up to Gargoyles, Wraiths and Daedroths. A real dungeon 4 to 10 pixels away can instead be entered, and a farm notice builds a farmstead copied from a nearby farm. A pack of 6 to 8 is hunted in two groups, and if the pack despawns the kills are kept and only survivors return. A bounty completed in a region can give influence to the seat of that region, if the player's war guild pledges to that seat and the player has been a member for 7 days or more; at most 10 bounties a week give influence.
Caravans ||| A merchant's caravan travels with a horse and wagon (two wagons from a great house's city) with its guards walking in front and behind, a noble's procession brings a baggage wagon, and pedlars and farmers lead a pack horse. The wagons' wheels turn, at camp the wagons are parked round one fire without standing in each other with each horse tied alongside its cart, and a wagon follows its horse round a bend. Caravans and travellers keep to the right of the road, and a caravan's mark on the travel map is a wagon. Talking to a caravan's merchant, a farmer or a pedlar allows buying and selling from a shop's stock and a limited purse, with friends getting 10% off. A merchant's caravan offers a guard contract from the day before it sets out until it arrives, paid by the day plus more for each foe beaten if the player stays within 300 m, and unpaid if they fall behind over an hour, let the merchant die or rob or kill any of them; offline the player can travel on with it to its next stop. Beating every guard of a caravan makes it surrender and its goods and purse are the player's to take, but any survivor who reaches a town reports the theft, pickpocketing or murder as a crime in the region where it happened. A road caravan's campfire is a fire again with Morrowind models on, and a roadside fight the player walks up to no longer ends by itself. If the overhead lantern or another built-in piece is taken out of a caravan, visitors no longer see it or its light. Leaving a caravan puts the player on open ground.
Card Tables ||| The cash-out of a card table says that the gold went into the player's account at the Bank of the Empire.
Cast When Used ||| Cast When Used items, such as a bracer of Ice Storm or a scarab of Fireball, now cast in dungeons. A spell readied from an item goes through doors with the player and no longer fires by itself, and shows a cost of nothing in a dungeon instead of its full cost. A worn item with Cast When Used on the hotbar, such as a jewel or a robe, casts its spell; if it also gives armor or another power, the first push puts it on.
Collections ||| Holdings > Collections is open to every player in six parts: Titles, Auras, Icons, Glyphs, Codex and Cards. Every title, aura, profile icon and glyph is listed, held or not, grouped by where it comes from, with a tile showing how many are held of the total and a row saying whether it can be had now (Obtainable, By chance, Ranked, Seasonal, Patron reward and so on). Rows open to say how to get it, can be filtered by All, Held, Not held or Obtainable, and anything held can be worn, taken off, shown or hidden from the page. It is readable on every theme and usable with a keyboard or a gamepad, and the Codex is translated. Each armor piece, shield and weapon held adds its appearance to the collection, listed under a new Appearances part, and Trophies, Fish and Annals parts were added.
Controller Bindings ||| Controller bindings show a button with more than one use with a slash, like Draw / sheathe, and Overworld and Quick dial can be bound in the Controller bindings window. The A button on the world map no longer causes an error in Firefox.
Crafting ||| Crafting received fixes to how crafts settle. A craft whose answer was lost settles on its own, a slow answer still pays the smith or furnisher when it lands, a station's fee the purse cannot cover is shown, and a recipe cannot be changed while the heat or plane is under way. A maker's mark is part of the piece's signed record, and a Masterwork piece, or any furniture a Master Joiner makes, carries its maker's name. Refining for a craft costs no extra station fee. When short of something, the box names the raw good needed (for example "short of Iron x2"), not the ingot, and a refused refining step is named in the message. Crafted Superior and Masterwork pieces can roll the special lines loot can, such as focus, cast speed, potency and wellspring on jewelry and the weapon and armour equivalents, while pieces crafted before stay as they were. A Crafting page sits in the menu next to Professions; at a station the Professions key opens it at that station, showing the player's rank in that craft, a bar to the next rank and the experience, and the help text for each craft is shorter. A crafter can no longer take back a crafted item after trading or selling it to another player.
Crafting Stations ||| Crafting stations use the Enhanced Plus look with diamond strikes for mini-games like the anvil's heat, loom's beat, and workbench's plane, and every station now uses materials in your Materials Bag and pack as well as your Stores. Stations show each pattern's XP and the 500 XP bonus for making it the first time, while pieces made only of cloth bought from the Weavers (Linen and Wool, including the Fishing Net) earn no first-time bonus, which the stations and craft messages say. The profession stations' short notes ("Needs 2 Arcane Essence; your Stores hold 1.", "Needs 2 Steel Ingot.", the Stores-full note) span the row under the piece instead of being squeezed into a narrow column. A station says when the crafter's limit stops a craft's XP: only two crafts rise past Journeyman (rank 50), and the station names them. The anvil, workbench, loom and Provisioning fire have three filters: All, Can make now and First-time bonus. Craft x N makes the same item up to 10 times and stops when the materials or gold run out.
Crashes ||| A crash when the game was minimised or put in the background while a world was loading was fixed. On the desktop app, crash.log also records the last ten minutes of graphics work before the game stops, and when the graphics process itself crashes it records the moment the graphics were lost in that crash's own entry and marks when they came back, so a minute with no stutter reads hitch=0. The red crash report has a Dismiss button, except the report for a lost graphics card, which needs a page reload, and the graphics failure message says the card reset or ran short of memory.
Crew Halls ||| Inside every shipyard the crewmaster has six named sailors looking for a berth each day, each with a class, a grade from Green to Veteran, a trade (First Mate, Bosun, Gunner, Carpenter, Lookout, Cook, Deckhand or a Bard) and a signing fee, and the player chooses which ship they join and at what post. A ship comes with her whole crew, so on a full deck a recruit signs on in place of a chosen hand, a hire that adds men costs what the shipwright would charge if that is more than the fee, and hands can be paid off from the same page, naming who steps up and never the last hand. Hiring and paying off need Naval Combat on and cannot happen during a fight, and the crew page shows which hands are ashore with the player. Online, one character at a time can hire each recruit, and the crew hall does not show recruits that other characters hired today.
Crowded Events ||| Crowded events no longer break the sound: the quietest and farthest sounds stop first and the player's own sounds always play.
Decorator ||| The decorator offers furnishings for homes. With the town mods on it adds 297 pieces such as beds and rugs, changing within a second when a town mod is turned on or off. It also offers what Daggerfall stands in its dungeons (thrones, cages, coffins, statues, chains, braziers) and a yard offers Daggerfall's street pieces and its own town's climate trees and plants, each with its own picture. People placed with the decorator (the Vendors list) can no longer be resized, with Smaller and Bigger gone for them. Those placed earlier at another size now stand at their normal size, moving one sets it to normal, an enlarged one gives back half the price difference and a shrunk one costs nothing to move. Decorations set against a wall sit in front of it instead of sinking into it, at any size and with the grid on, and the grid snaps along the wall. The furniture catalogue, the pieces in the room, the built-in pieces and the player's own things are laid out as picture tiles showing each piece's 3D model, name, price and badges for what it does, with a Pictures / List switch bringing the old lines back. Every tab, type, size and filter has its own icon, filters sit under named rows and fold behind one Filters button on phones, a placed piece's buttons are grouped by what they do with the costly Remove standing alone, and the placing bar is icon buttons with a Keys button. The Confirm button works again after a slow answer from the server and two clicks no longer send the changes twice, and a click on a placed piece picks it up on every interface.
Disconnects ||| A poor connection no longer causes the game to disconnect the player after a stall, also during a fight.
Docked Ships ||| Docked ships keep their berths. A ship left made fast at a quay keeps her berth out of sight so none moors inside her, summon and docking agree, and online another player's ship keeps a berth even while her captain is invisible at her helm. Moored boats no longer play their sounds at full volume after the player leaves a building or a caravan.
Docks ||| Port towns' docks now appear even with Naval Combat or Come Sail Away turned off, and always connect to the town, with a pier running to land when Deep Waters is on. Ports with docks some distance from the town, such as Daggerfall, Wayrest and Pencart, show their docks on arrival by sea.
Dungeon Interior ||| A black interior clear was fixed. A player can no longer pass through a low ceiling into the room above or out of a dungeon.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top. A headstone the party has searched counts as searched for the whole party and the game says who searched it, coffins a stranger opened in tombs are the player's to search (including standing right next to one), and party members no longer vanish for a few seconds when the player goes through a tomb door. Bodies and their loot no longer return on re-entering a dungeon while another player there has the game in the background, since the room follows whoever is actually playing, and a room left stuck by a background player's game closing without saying goodbye now moves on to whoever is playing. Online, an open window no longer stops the monsters, though the character still stands still, and offline play pauses as before; a technique does not start when its key is pushed under an open window. Spells hit other players in the dungeons of the PvP zone.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor. If the game window is in the background when an Elite Breach moves to its next level, the player goes there too.
Enchanting ||| Enchanting is a crafting profession. The item maker costs 10% less from Journeyman and 20% less as a Master, and disenchanting a piece gives XP by its tier, a quarter for pieces far below the player's rank. A Masterwork piece can be enchanted further and the item maker takes any crafted piece with its enchantment, though a piece's points cannot exceed what its recipe makes and Aetheric pieces such as Ruhn's Regalia and the raid sets are refused. An enchanted jewel that was a Wand and became an Amulet on loading from a guild vault or market listing no longer gets the character flagged by the anti-cheat check. One filled soul gem binds only one item in the item maker.
Farm Bounties ||| Farm bounties were fixed so foes cannot stand inside the farmhouse or barn where they cannot be reached, and farms no longer stand on mountainsides, since a spot rising more than 8 m across the block is refused. The Quests tab keeps the way to the farmstead where the beasts wait.
Firmament ||| The Firmament is a passive tree for everything done online, opened from the Firmament button on the Stats page. The player chooses one of seven birthsigns, then spends Stars on combat stars and Craft points on the life ring (gathering, crafting, trading, hunting and sailing), earning 2 Stars for every Renown level and 1 Craft point for every 8 profession ranks. Changes are free to plan until confirmed, taking stars back is free below Renown 10 and then costs 10 silver per star, and a full reset costs 10,000 gold. The stars never affect hits or spells against other players and switch off in duels and while mentoring. Every star adds something: small stars and roads give their bonus one for one as a percent, and an attribute bonus counts on a stat already at 100 (with the sky's full +15 a 100 Strength reads 115, offline the cap is 100). It works in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire and on phones, and its text is English for now. Every weapon family has two Firmament notables that raise its skill, and Axe and Blunt get a third; Blade Dancer, Iron Knuckles, Headsman and Bonebreaker keep every stat and gain the skill on top, and the Ritual's Battlemage's Focus is now Templar's Mace, with no star moved so shared build codes still load. Fist stars now apply to bare-handed blows. It is drawn inside the pause menu's Growth tab and can go full screen with one press, over the whole screen on a phone. Accepting stars no longer shows a red error.
Fishing ||| Fishing is a gathering profession done with a Fishing-Net, standing in water or at sea by daylight (07:00 to 17:59). Holding E winds the net and letting go throws it 3 to 12 metres, and the player hauls in by holding E to raise a band and keeping the net's weight inside. A haul gives 1 to 2 Raw Fish to the Stores, a missed tug still gives a plain catch, and two schools rise in each area every day for an extra fish. At sea there is a 1 in 50 chance of a Pearl and 1 in 100 of a Slaughterfish, about 1 haul in 200 brings a trophy fish, and the limit is 40 hauls a day per account. Each haul gives XP as if at the highest tier the rank unlocked. The net will not cast in a dungeon or settlement or below a quarter of fatigue. Fishing online no longer piles up memory while travelling. Intelligence and Agility set the net's tug window as well as the haul, and a school's extra fish counts on the first 40 schools of the day. Each port has two to four boats that catch fish each day, going out to sea in the morning and coming back in the evening.
Frame Rate Cap ||| A frame rate cap (FPS-CAP1) was added in 0.1.4108. In the desktop app, with Wait For Screen Refresh off and Frame Rate Cap Off, the game holds at 300 frames a second.
Gear Wear ||| Gear wear is how weapons and armour degrade. Wear per hit is at Daggerfall's own rate, only damage that gets through armour wears the weapon and the piece hit, good armour wears slower, and claws and bites no longer wear armour. Every weapon type now has the same base condition, the Warhammer's 1,600, with the material ladder unchanged, so daggers, tantos and short bows no longer break far sooner than a warhammer of the same metal; an iron short bow takes about 800 hits of 20 damage. Enchanted weapons gain the most: a Cast-When-Strikes enchantment costs 10 condition a strike, so an iron Worm's Tooth lasts 160 strikes, and a Cast-When-Held enchantment pays its casting cost the first time it is equipped without breaking the weapon. Weapons update on load keeping the same percentage of condition, repairs are priced by the share of condition lost so light weapons and bows cost less to keep up, and broken enchanted gear stays in the pack for a smith to repair. Legendary items no longer lose condition when equipped, where the Aegis of Dawn lost about a third the first time it was put on, while other magic items still pay their usual cost. Rare, Legendary and other rarity gear that its own enchantment wears out breaks and stays in the inventory, where it can be repaired.
Giants ||| Giants no longer appear inside rocks.
Goldsmith ||| Goldsmith is a rank 50 reward that makes the player's Silver pieces hold Gold's points and worth. Its card says that Silver jewelry the player makes has the enchantment points and the value of Gold.
Guild Hall ||| A guild hall is a guild-owned house bought by the guildmaster with treasury funds, featuring member chests, crafting, resting, customizable painting and yard furnishings, and a hall's roll as the first book on its shelf detailing its chapter's standing, Master, and officers. Windows inside buildings are now see-through, town boards fill in once three players have visited guild halls, and in rooms with many lamps, stationary people cast shadows from every lamp rather than just the nearest twelve. Online, if the player does not yet qualify for the next rank, the guild popup lists what is missing: the rank's name ("Next rank: Swordsman"), reputation against what is needed ("Reputation 14 of 20"), the guild-skill level the player is short of with their best guild skill named, a second guild skill at the lower bar and the guild's own skills by name. It does not appear when nothing is missing or at the top rank, and it shows on the enhanced UI skin and in all 13 supported languages. At rank 7 the hall says the next rank is a chapter chair (a council chair or the Master's) won by Merit at each Sunday's Turning, asking reputation 80, 14 real days in the guild and an account 7 real days old, and a service only a chair opens (the Mages Guild's Teleport, Zenithar's summoning) says so at any rank. The new Guild Chapters tutorial page opens when the rank is held at 7. The hall and the Standing page show what the realm still owes the player (already earned, arriving at most 15 a real day) and warn before a review puts the player on probation or lowers their rank, with when and why, and on probation they are told the stakes: below -10 expelled at the review, below 0 it runs 28 more days, at 0 or more it ends, and online earning the next rank ends it sooner. The rank text scrolls on its own so Talk, Service and Reforge stay on screen. Expelled or turned away for standing, the player sees at the hall their standing, the standing it takes members at, how many quests bring them back there and how fast it recovers on its own, and the hall names skills the guild also wants and says that its halls in other regions still have work when one is shut for the Season; the classic look's refusal says the same. Banners hang only in front of a wall.
HUD ||| The HUD's lost-part strip on the fatigue, health and magicka bars is now a dim strip and stays at most about a second before catching up. Notifications that have been moved now fade in where the player put them. The compass can be turned over in the HUD editor (Alt+U), whose length handle follows the pointer and which no longer pushes a resized piece off the screen. The riding panel and the ship's helm panel are pieces in the HUD editor (Alt+U) that can be moved, resized or hidden. The Enhanced HUD shows the stacks of the Blademaster's Flowing Steel and the time they stay.
Horse ||| Horses never die, can be fed to grow the player's bond, must be kept fed, and the player can own more than one. A horse walks by default and holding Run (Shift unless rebound) gallops it, as on foot. The gallop is as fast as before and spends the horse's breath, and a winded or starving horse walks even with Run held until it can gallop again. Holding Run gallops it anywhere, in town or out, as long as it has the breath, online too, though riding into someone at a gallop tramples them, which is a crime and brings the guards. A walking horse has a slower clop, a slower head nod in first person and a trot on the 3D horse, and the saddle panel says Walking. With Horse care off in Settings > Features, or on the classic interface, horses ride as they always did. A horse with a new coat met on the road no longer freezes the game. The horses' coats on the road's wagons are painted in under half the time, so the first seconds after entering the world stutter less. With Billboard Look on, Morrowind horses stand upright like the classic sprites. The saddle panel is a small block in the bottom-right corner, shown or hidden with F2 (rebindable in Controls), and the Horse care setting chooses When needed, Always or Off; on touch screens it no longer covers the compass. Horse care and wagon upkeep each have Off, Boons (the default) and Hard tiers like Climates & Calories: on Boons nothing is taken (a hungry horse is no weaker, a worn wagon no slower, and a wagon kept Sound rolls 10% faster), Hard is the system as it was, and Off is the classic horse or wagon. The first-person horse (and the cart's horse) darkens with the world around it, at night, in a cave mouth or under a storm, following the Features > First-person lighting switch like the weapon and hands. Travel on horseback with the Overworld map or Travel Options no longer tramples townsfolk.
Hunter Rank ||| Online, each completed bounty adds to the hunter rank. There are six ranks, from Unblooded to Huntmaster, and from Stalker up bounty boards show a named beast each week.

Instances ||| At a dungeon or building where one of the player's quests is running, at Privateer's Hold, or where a party member is already in an instance, the door asks whether to enter the public world or the player's own instance, which only the player and their party can enter. An instance keeps its own looted chests and slain monsters (returning after half an hour) and closes for good when the quest that opened it ends, a party member's instance shows at the door as "Name's instance", and the door only asks when there is a real choice (homes and the Wrothgarian open zone never have instances). Interior Lighting ||| Interior light flicker was fixed, and every lamp in a room gets a shadow map. Rooms with no lamps of their own, like some inns, are no longer dark, and a lamp put in such a room does not make it dark again.
Invisibility ||| Invisibility works online. Party members can interact with each other while under Chameleon, Shadow or Invisibility. Concealed players outside the party stay hidden, as before. Monsters that cannot see through Invisibility no longer swing at the player just because they walked in front of them in dungeons, while the undead, vampires and Daedra still see through it, and wilderness enemies no longer notice an invisible traveller during fast travel. Loot Window ||| A loot-side click takes immediately, and the loot buttons were fixed. The loot list on a body or pile scrolls, so the mouse wheel (or the pad) reaches every item and the list shows how many items are above and below. Searching the wagon or storage keeps the player's place in the list, and on phones the window's two columns stay the same width and no longer run off the screen. Chests, corpses and the wagon can be filtered by name, item type, rarity and material, each menu listing only what the pile holds with a count. Market ||| The Market is a Notice Board tab for trading in Marks or gold, featuring grouped views (Buy, Sell, History), silver display, column names, supplier counters, and detailed split lists. Listings stand 72 hours for a 1% fee with immediate sales (minus 5% tax) and courier delivery, with strict gold/Drakes currency separation and item exclusions. Seat boards, market sales, and courier deliveries no longer slow down account services in regions where a guild holds a Charter. Market stalls look the way the pack draws them, with the keeper behind the counter and goods on the counter. Items bought from a home trader or the market come to the pack automatically and the game says when a purchase arrives; a purchase waiting from before 0.1.11990 arrives the next time that character plays online. A lost connection during a delivery no longer sends the player to the title screen, the Bought message no longer claims a piece is in the pack before it arrives, a character with a purchase on its way can be deleted again after one play, and an item taken back from a market stall after a failed connection returns to the pack. The List form of the market and the guild Stores say which materials they cannot take and why, and a stolen item for sale on the market, at a home trader or on the Vendor page, or in a trade, shows that it is stolen.
Master Skills ||| Master Skills raises the skill hard cap from 100 to 200, with everything up to 100 working as before. Each point from 100 to 125 is about 4 times slower, 125 to 150 about 8 times slower and 150 to 200 about 16 times slower, and each point above 100 gives 25% of the benefit of a point below 100, with milestones at 125, 150, 175 and 200. A skill at 200 fights like a 140. Every skill can reach 100, but only mastered skills pass it: 2 of 3 primary, 2 of 3 major and 1 of 6 minor, chosen at 100 and permanent. It is always on online and a choice offline. Online, every mastered school past 100 strengthens its own spells and not just their price, by 1% per effective point up to +40% at 200, the same rule as Destruction's damage: Restoration makes heals heal more, Alteration makes the Shield hold more, Illusion makes invisibility, chameleon and shadow last longer, Thaumaturgy makes Spell Reflection and Spell Resistance hold more often, and Mysticism makes Silence, Dispel and Soul Trap take hold more often.
