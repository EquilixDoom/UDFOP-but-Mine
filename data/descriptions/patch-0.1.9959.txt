Beautiful Cities ||| Beautiful Cities is a mod pair, both on by default, each with a switch under Mods that applies on the next start. City walls no longer have 3-metre gaps beside their corner towers, and the crate of belongings that comes with a moved house now shows up in the new house when the city uses the Beautiful Cities layout. Bushes no longer grow out of house walls in towns rebuilt by Beautiful Cities and Beautiful Villages, including the one by Penwall Derry's east gate.
Boulders ||| Boulders can be quarried for stone. They glow and show on the compass, and the player presses E at "Quarry the stone". Each square holds a number of boulders a day: 1 to 3 in woodland, haunted woodland and subtropical squares, 2 to 4 in mountain woods, and 3 to 5 in mountains and deserts. A rock field's boulders now all appear. A boulder's glint sits on its stones, not in the air above them. Boulders in rebuilt towns and villages, such as the stream rocks in Ipsmoth, are solid again instead of drawn inside out, and their stone texture is no longer smeared.
Burning Court ||| The Burning Court is a world feature where the bridge portal is replaced by a fire gate that opens and closes for players, the way home opens upon the Warden's fall and closes as the court comes apart, the only ways out until he falls are death and the Wrath with /unstuck disabled, and stalled rooms return players to Tamriel after 20 seconds. The court lets a crowd in about five times faster when the gate opens, and a busy gate makes players wait at its door instead of being thrown out. When all 256 places in the fight are taken a player stays at the court's edge and watches, joining if a place frees (one frees when someone who took a place leaves without fighting); a watcher cannot strike, cannot be hurt and earns no share of the spoils.
Captain's Stars ||| A captain level (up to 30) is earned from sinking and capturing ships, beating off boarders, claiming prizes, finishing contracts, distance sailed, treasure charts, charting isles and selling goods at the exchange. Each level after the first gives one point to spend in the Captain's Stars, a skill tree in the Firmament's style opened from the Captain page: the Master (left) improves seamanship (speed under sail and oars, turning, cargo, hull, repairs and better exchange prices) and the Commander (right) improves combat (reload speed, gun damage, accuracy, grapeshot, chain shot and heated shot, boarding, ram damage and how long a rammed ship stays locked). Keystones give a big bonus with a cost (for example Master of the Wind: +12% speed under sail, -12% under oars), stars apply to every ship the player owns including laid-up and packed ones, removing or changing stars is free, and keystone costs apply in combat. The tree is drawn inside the pause menu's Growth tab, where it can go full screen with one press.
Chronicle ||| The chronicle (L) records the history of seats and people. It says how long a captured seat's siege lasted and what Conscription and fealty paid, each line sits under its guild's shield, and the Hall of Records ends with a Roll of Arms. A People page lists friends, enemies, the people who know the player, and the dead. It keeps its place when a quest or note is folded, a note removed or a quest shared or abandoned, and a long Chronicle no longer stalls.
Controls Screen ||| The Controls screen lists the game's key bindings; it once wrongly said attack was left click. The Professions key's row says it opens the Professions page, and the line for the L and N keys names the window they open: the Chronicle, on its Quests or Notes page, on Enhanced Plus, and the logbook on Classic.
Corpse Loot ||| Corpse loot is what a dead foe leaves to take. A party member's kill opens like any other body instead of reading "Empty", and a take from another player's kill lands exactly once however late the answer comes, two players taking the same piece getting one copy between them ("Someone took that first."). Anything put into another player's kill, or into a foe killed inside a building, comes back when the window closes, even if the other player left or lost connection. Earlier fixes stopped a corpse being granted twice and repaired loot after death online.
Crafting Stations ||| Crafting stations use the Enhanced Plus look with diamond strikes for mini-games like the anvil's heat, loom's beat, and workbench's plane, and every station now uses materials in your Materials Bag and pack as well as your Stores. Stations show each pattern's XP and the 500 XP bonus for making it the first time, while pieces made only of cloth bought from the Weavers (Linen and Wool, including the Fishing Net) earn no first-time bonus, which the stations and craft messages say. The profession stations' short notes ("Needs 2 Arcane Essence; your Stores hold 1.", "Needs 2 Steel Ingot.", the Stores-full note) span the row under the piece instead of being squeezed into a narrow column. A station says when the crafter's limit stops a craft's XP: only two crafts rise past Journeyman (rank 50), and the station names them.
Death Screen ||| The death screen was fixed and audited, including resolving the online screen's rise and ensuring quick-loading (F11) after dying in town no longer leaves the death screen up. It shows the amount of gold lost and says Rise instead of "End the journey". It names any letters of credit lost and what they were worth, and when the character's trading is frozen it says that what was dropped was lost outright.
Dual Wielding ||| Equipping a one-handed weapon (sword, dagger, mace or war axe) in each hand draws and swings both, with H choosing which hand leads and no switch delay with both hands up. A shield, two-handed weapon, bow, or torch or lantern held in a hand still means one weapon. A pair is quicker, not twice as strong: the next blow straight after a strike comes from the other hand a little sooner, the off hand does 80% damage with -5 to hit at Agility 50 (shrinking to nothing at higher Agility and rising to -10 at lower), a heavy off-hand weapon swings slower, and the off hand's blows count as 0.8 of a blow for on-strike enchantments, per-hit powers and skill training. Measured, a matched pair of light weapons deals about 9% more damage per second than one of them and a heavier pair about 4%, for up to a quarter more fatigue per second and giving up the shield. Each weapon in a pair shows its own element with weapon glow on, the other weapon lowers straight down when one attacks, and the same rules apply in duels, sieges and the open PvP zone. Under Physical Combat And Armor Overhaul (on by default) a weapon in the left hand no longer rolls a shield block, since dual wielding gives up the shield's block, while shields block as before. In the Morrowind first-person view the arms spread wider with two weapons drawn, further for a pair of longswords than a pair of daggers, easing out on draw and back on sheathe, so long blades no longer cross in the middle of the screen; third person is unchanged.
Dungeons ||| Dungeon holes were closed, and foes, quest foes included, no longer spawn stuck inside dungeon crates. Stepping out and back in during the same session leaves defeated enemies and emptied chests that way for up to an hour, as in other dungeons online. Towns no longer sit in pits behind sheer walls of earth. Online, a dungeon says what it is, Regular, Elite or Abyss Dungeon, with its size beside it as in "Elite Dungeon, Small", on the entrance sign, overworld name plates and sighting message, as a line on entering, and on the held map for the Bay's own dungeons. Remains found in a dungeon are no longer laid again after saving and loading inside it. Searching a crate or chest in a dungeon no longer wakes Slaughterfish, Dreugh or Lamia on dry land, tall enemies (mages, healers, spellswords, liches, Daedra lords) duck under dungeon doorways instead of jamming in them, and party members in a dungeon see the same loot on the same body or container, each getting their own bonus on top. A headstone the party has searched counts as searched for the whole party and the game says who searched it, coffins a stranger opened in tombs are the player's to search (including standing right next to one), and party members no longer vanish for a few seconds when the player goes through a tomb door. Bodies and their loot no longer return on re-entering a dungeon while another player there has the game in the background, since the room follows whoever is actually playing, and a room left stuck by a background player's game closing without saying goodbye now moves on to whoever is playing.
Dwarven Thunderlock ||| The Dwarven Thunderlock is a gun, a new weapon type prototyped in a lab and integrated into the game. It works in the Morrowind arm rig, with its icons, paperdoll grip, cadence, flash and shot all fixed to match the prototype. Dwemer Pellets are sold wherever weapons are, in Weapon Smiths, Armorers, General Stores and Pawn Shops, and restocked each day. The gun and its pellets show their own art in the pack, on the hotbar and in the quick slots, and a list shows the whole gun. The Morrowind 3D gun has no holes left, kicks back at the shot, and tips down and back up on reload with the breech heard after the bang. On the Morrowind body it is held like a rifle, the right hand on its pistol grip and the left supporting the forend, carried at rest at a low ready with the muzzle down and across toward the left foot and coming up to a level ready stance while shooting, for a few seconds after and while foes are hurting the player, in first and third person, the Hourlock held the same way. In the Morrowind first-person view the aimed gun sits below the middle of the screen instead of riding over the crosshair, with the left hand still on its forend.
Elite Gates ||| The Elite Breach is the tier above Dagon's Breach: a party of up to 8 goes down through the torn halls of the Battlespire under a 30-minute count, opened with a Weir Key used outdoors, online and signed in (or the free Rehearsal, the first circle of five halls, paying nothing), the party at that moment becoming the company and being called. A start window chooses Hall 1 or a reached Redoubt, a Wager of up to three decrees can be taken on, and an arch with a 400 m gold beacon rises where the key is used. There are four regions, the Broken Galleries (circles 1-5), the Soul Cairn (6-10), the Shade Perilous (11-15) and the Havoc Wellhead (16 and deeper), and each circle is five halls (a fight, a crossing, a trial, a second fight and the lord's Court) with Yards, Ramparts, crossings of drifting, blinking and crumbling platforms, Rails, Gusts, Pendulums and Vents, trials, twists, sixteen kinds of enemy and thirteen lords, a Valkynaz every fifth Court. Every enemy blow is drawn on the floor before it lands, the Count runs during fights, falling into the void casts the player back for a tenth of their health, a death is a fall after which the next Redoubt calls the player back once and a witness can ping the floor, and the Spire has no saving, resting, map, Mark, Recall, levitation or companions. A Redoubt (taken by felling a circle's lord) records itself on the account, heals the company and calls the fallen back, offers three Vestiges, votes on the next circle's decrees and pays once per arena week with gold, a Rare or Legendary piece, more Magic-or-better pieces, the lord's named Legendary 1 in 8, its Iliac Hand card 1 in 5, 20 Marks, Deadlands Embers and Weir Keys, twice at a Valkynaz's. Feats, titles, auras, boards (the Descent, the Depths and the Deepest Redoubts) and the herald mark progress, a breach survives a server restart and can be returned to after a reload, and the Spire is rebuilt as the Fractured Battlespire with its own air, light, stone and score of fourteen songs. In the Athenaeum the Orrery's stones are turned to their true hours by looking just above a stone's gold ring, with the six riddles, each stone's name and hour and a "Stand closer to the stone." message on the HUD. The Spire's own words are in English for now. Bosses weigh each attack first and pick another, or move in, if it would leave someone nowhere to stand, and a boss that cannot fairly hit someone turns on the rest of the party for a few seconds. The bridges between sections now break apart as the Broken Way, crossed on Drifts, Beats, Crumbles, Pendulums, Vents and Rails, with forks as real choices, the rooms before the trial in fight halls and platforms checked across the whole jump; breaches opened before that update keep their old halls until they end. Every song is re-orchestrated with a driving fight score, halls that build to a peak and a ticking clock in a trial's last two minutes, and the Spire has its own ambience of the void's roar, war horns and breaking stone. Blows that hit a ward or a lord still asleep say "Warded" and blows that land nothing say "No footing", and none of them heal, leech or give set-bonus returns; a Court lord can be hit the moment it wakes, and Valkynaz Ilthys's banners can be broken with arrows and spells from the court floor.
Enchanting ||| Enchanting is a crafting profession. The item maker costs 10% less from Journeyman and 20% less as a Master, and disenchanting a piece gives XP by its tier, a quarter for pieces far below the player's rank. A Masterwork piece can be enchanted further and the item maker takes any crafted piece with its enchantment, though a piece's points cannot exceed what its recipe makes and Aetheric pieces such as Ruhn's Regalia and the raid sets are refused. An enchanted jewel that was a Wand and became an Amulet on loading from a guild vault or market listing no longer gets the character flagged by the anti-cheat check.
Exchange ||| An Exchange tab at a port buys and sells eight goods of the Bay: wine, grain, wool, iron, spices, silk, silver and ivory. Each crown's ports make some goods cheaply and pay well for others, every port has a twist of its own, prices drift day to day and each port deals in only so much of a good each day. Goods load into and out of the hold of the ship at the quay, a full hold slows her, and the exchange says how much more she can carry and will not sell her so much she cannot move. Shops do not buy the exchange's goods. The exchange no longer lets the player buy a cheap good back at the price they sold it for when their trade stars are maxed. A port's exchange prices by its town's economy, cheaper in a Booming port and dearer in a Destitute one, and the Exchange tab shows the port's economy. Every deal there raises that town's share of the players' activity, like trading at a player's stall.
FPS Counter ||| An in-game FPS counter shows script time and the renderer's counts. The draw count includes what a building draws through its windows (view) and the shadow maps (shadow), and the counter shows whether VSync is off or a Frame Rate Cap is set. It remembers stutters: when a frame took 50 ms or longer in the last minute, a "hitch" line shows the worst one and how much of it was the game's own work. It no longer shows "0 fps" after the player switches away from the game and back.
Firmament ||| The Firmament is a passive tree for everything done online, opened from the Firmament button on the Stats page. The player chooses one of seven birthsigns, then spends Stars on combat stars and Craft points on the life ring (gathering, crafting, trading, hunting and sailing), earning 2 Stars for every Renown level and 1 Craft point for every 8 profession ranks. Changes are free to plan until confirmed, taking stars back is free below Renown 10 and then costs 10 silver per star, and a full reset costs 10,000 gold. The stars never affect hits or spells against other players and switch off in duels and while mentoring. Every star adds something: small stars and roads give their bonus one for one as a percent, and an attribute bonus counts on a stat already at 100 (with the sky's full +15 a 100 Strength reads 115, offline the cap is 100). It works in Enhanced Plus and DFO Grimoire and on phones, and its text is English for now. Every weapon family has two Firmament notables that raise its skill, and Axe and Blunt get a third; Blade Dancer, Iron Knuckles, Headsman and Bonebreaker keep every stat and gain the skill on top, and the Ritual's Battlemage's Focus is now Templar's Mace, with no star moved so shared build codes still load. Fist stars now apply to bare-handed blows. It is drawn inside the pause menu's Growth tab and can go full screen with one press, over the whole screen on a phone.
Gem Sockets ||| Weapons carry visible sockets, round wells under the picture on the item card and small pips along the top of the tile in the pack. A weapon's size sets how many it can have: one on a short blade (dagger, tanto, shortsword, wakizashi), two on a one-handed weapon and three on a two-handed weapon, a bow or the Thunderlock, while armour and jewellery keep their single socket. Socketed weapons drop far more often, Magic weapons can have one socket and Rare and Legendary weapons can roll extra sockets up to their size. A gem is set from the pack by clicking an empty socket or choosing Set in... on its card, free apart from the gem being spent and possible on a worn weapon. Clicking a filled socket twice shatters its gem, the Mages Guild's Reforge extracts a gem whole for gold based on its grade, a piece holding a gem is never salvaged, and a set gem adds its worth to the piece's price. A gem bought at a shop keeps its shop price when set in a socket and taken out again.
Growth Tab ||| The Growth tab sits in the pause menu next to Stats and holds everything about how the character grows: a Level page for attributes and, with a level up waiting, where to place points and press Ascend; a Specialisations guide to all thirty paths, fifteen arcane and fifteen martial, showing availability, requirements skill by skill, passive bonus, signature spell or technique and how to use it, where the path is chosen; the online Firmament; and the Captain's Stars. The Firmament and the Captain's Stars can go full screen with one press, and the page list marks what is waiting. The Stats page's Ascend and Firmament buttons, the Master Skills specialisation cards and the Captain page's Open the stars lead to it, and on the classic interface the S and W rows open the guide. Unsaved star changes prompt before the player leaves the page, a newly chosen specialisation's stat bonuses apply at once, and the wording of specialisations, the level-up screen and the Firmament is in plain terms and translated into every language.
Homes ||| Homes have a Who may enter setting, including My guild. Room furniture can be taken out, knightly order free houses are never owned by other players, graveyard crypts are not sold as homes online, and online homes/guild halls on the arena's block move to new houses on login without emptying storage. A phantom room tab in two-storey homes (often "Room 3") has been fixed. A home's chests no longer lose their contents when furniture fails to load, anything without a chest or box to go in being set down at the player's feet. When the servers are under heavy load the game waits longer between tries to load a town's homes, and loading a town's homes costs the servers half as many database requests. A home's furnishings load faster, especially when the server is busy. A home's crafting stations (forge, workbench, loom, mason's and jeweller's benches, alchemy and enchanting) can be used by the account's other characters as well as the buyer, while the storage still belongs to the character who bought it.
Hotbar ||| On a controller, LT raises the hotbar, A picks up and moves or swaps slots and Y clears a slot. With a controller, Add to hotbar picks the entry up and raises the bar so the player chooses the slot, and picking up the mouse while something is in hand drops it and lowers the bar. Spamming the hotbar no longer makes the game stutter. Number keys 1-9 pick rows in the Use Magic Item list and other windows inside dungeons as well as outdoors, and a number pressed with a window open in a dungeon no longer fires the hotbar slot behind it.
Letters of Credit ||| Online, a letter of credit cannot be put into a chest, cupboard or body, since a shared container is everyone's, so it stays in the pack and is given to another player in a trade. The player's own storage (house cupboards, ship's chest), a pile they dropped themselves and the wagon still take letters, and nothing changes offline. Outside a trade a letter never reaches the player through another player's game, and letters already lying in a shared chest or on a body when the update arrived were removed. A revenant that kills the player never takes a letter, a letter a revenant already holds returns to the wallet on the next load, and a sworn revenant that deserts gives back any letter it carried.
Level-Up Window ||| The level-up window shows curse bonuses. It sits on the Growth tab's Level page, which shows attributes and, with a level up waiting, where to place points and press Ascend; leaving before pressing Ascend keeps the points unspent and the bonus never re-rolls, and the fanfare plays once per level.
Loot Drops ||| Loot drops are what enemies leave. In the latest changes regular enemies keep 3 in 4 of their items offline, up from half, and online 5/8 to 7/8 by Renown. They drop blue, yellow and orange items more often, stepping up at Renown 10, 20, 30 and 40 and using the rolling player's Renown (the host's in a shared dungeon), and gear an enemy was wearing can drop as Magic, Rare or better, even from champions, elites and bosses. A full body keeps a potion or rest supply ahead of plain gear. Earlier, normal enemies were capped at 3 items, gold included (5 in an Elite Dungeon), champions stopped always dropping a Rare, and ordinary foes left each item only half the time with gold untouched. Luck multiplies drop chances, half at Luck 0 and half again at Luck 100, instead of adding a flat amount. Animated loot piles stop being animated behind the scenes after the player leaves a room, loads a save or empties a pile, and magic items from guild vendors, shops and loot no longer come on wands or holy items when their power only works while worn.
Martial Specialisations ||| Mastering any two weapon skills (Long Blade, Short Blade, Axe, Blunt Weapon, Archery, Hand-to-Hand) allows choosing one of fifteen martial paths, such as the Blademaster, Ranger, Juggernaut, Assassin or Brawler, a permanent choice like a mastery. Each path has a passive of its own (for example the Vanguard's foes hit 10% lighter and the Woodsman gets fatigue back on every kill) and a signature technique that uses the Weapon Technique key whenever the player wields one of the path's two weapons and that weapon has no technique of its own, a path with Archery having a bow form and a melee form that share one cooldown, working with the loot ladder off. Timed-effect techniques (Rallying Cleave, Cyclone, Phantom Strike, Earthsplitter, Savage Whirl) start their effect as they are used so every foe the sweep reaches gets it, an arcane path and a martial path can be held at once, and the choice is made on the skill screen (W on the classic sheet or the Martial Specialisation card under Master Skills), with the game also asking once the first time a path opens. The Brawler's, Bloodmage's and Berserker's descriptions now say exactly what they do, and paths are chosen from the Growth tab's Specialisations guide.
Moderation ||| Moderation gives moderators a shield, /mute and /unmute, and tabbyvish and Starempire42 are moderators with the blue shield. /siege void lets a Moderator void a seat's battle of the week won by an exploit. Before a result, the battle is called off and Sellsword fees go home, after a capture the Charter returns to the previous holder with its prior Standing, Tithe and Legacy, and after a hold the Standing and next-defence bonus are removed, with rewards already claimed staying claimed. A void can no longer land after the Turning. The Shadow Fang title carries moderator powers (/mute, /unmute, /ban, /unban, /strikes and the reports' queue), and moderators cannot ban or mute a Shadow Fang holder.
Morrowind People ||| With the Morrowind body on, the people and creatures around the player stand in Morrowind bodies too: dungeon and wilderness foes, the city watch, townsfolk, quest givers, stable and wagon yard keepers, ship crews, siege fighters, road parties and the Oblivion Gate's boss and host. Foes wear the armour and carry the weapons they rolled with a race and face of their own, townsfolk are dressed by their station, foes swing, flinch and fall with their tells on the body, and a body is drawn at its sprite's size. Creatures use Morrowind's creatures where Daggerfall's have a match and keep their classic sprite otherwise. Online everyone sees a foe as its owner does. Bodies are drawn for the nearest people only within one budget for the whole frame, and a Morrowind People setting under Settings > Combat > With Morrowind data is Off, Near (the default, up to 24 bodies) or All (up to 48). With Morrowind models on, other characters and horses keep their size wherever the camera is, and NPCs whose helmet fails to load show their head. The horses that stand in towns, at World of Daggerfall's bandit camps and forts and in the Stables stand as the 3D horse (the nearest eight within 60 m), and people who used to stay as sprites, such as a town's figures that belong to no faction, World of Daggerfall's dock people, its captives, merchants and prisoners and the crews on Come Sail Away's boats, stand in Morrowind bodies. Past the body range the sprites still stand, as do other animals, children, vampires' people and Daedra Princes. With Morrowind attached, Breton men wear three new heads and three new hairs (nine faces between them), each of the ten Breton portraits at character creation shows the face most like it (portrait 6 wearing the eyepatch), and if the heads fail to load Morrowind's own Breton faces show. Eleven of Daggerfall's townspeople (all four Redguard men, all four Nord men, two Breton men and the hooded Redguard woman) wear their own 3D outfits in their picture's colours, with hoods over the hair, sleeves, hems and boots moving with the body, legs staying inside skirts and robes, and a woman wearing her modest shirt while Show Nudity is off. Townspeople without an outfit yet keep their varied wardrobe. The new Breton heads are for player characters only (and no longer sit backwards), NPCs wearing Morrowind's own heads, and the Breton eyepatch face sits on the right head with its own hair. In towns with market stalls the food, goods and animals at the stalls show as themselves again with the Morrowind people on. Putting a helm on or taking it off no longer leaves the old head picture in GPU memory on the large HUD, and a helm put on while the large HUD is first loading shows on its head.
Online Saving ||| Online saves can be up to 16 MB, up from 4, and are compressed before going to the realm. An outage no longer costs online progress because a kept copy of the save survives closing the page, and closing the game no longer loses the last minutes. A kept save is used only if nothing newer reached the server from another device, trade or purchase, and an older game copy can read the character but cannot save one grown this big. An online character saves itself every five minutes instead of two, while trades, crafting, profession work and anything the server settles still save at once.
Overworld ||| The Overworld is a travel view over the real world, where the player clicks to travel and sees other players, weather from above, the sea, shared roaming bands, camps and dungeons. The controls are one compact block in the bottom-right corner, with a Path switch that is remembered: Roads (default) prefers road travel and Free walks across country around mountains and across water. A Show section filters Towns, Distant, Dungeons, Enemies, Travellers and Gathering groups, and since 0.1.6646 these filters sit in their own box at the bottom left, which folds away and can be moved like the rest of the HUD. A Nodes button picks how far gathering groups show, and right-clicking the map places a waypoint (see Waypoints). Double-clicking a band or camp marker asks "Attack the Zombie band?", and Yes travels straight toward them at full speed. Players close together show as one larger dot, only the six nearest show full title, Renown, tag and glyphs, towns with a carriage driver show a wheel, and friends' names are blue and guild-mates' violet, with player filters on both maps. Hovering a capturable town shows who holds it and this week's battle. Routes go around World of Daggerfall mountains, and rain, snow, sand and wind fill the air in front of the camera. The dungeons near the player (Daggerfall's own, random dungeons online and a standing Abyss Dungeon) show their own entrance model, enlarged so they can be seen from above, and the Overworld no longer refuses a walk over mountains. At high travel speeds (x100) the character, the ring under them and the route line move smoothly, the speed no longer drops to x60 for the rest of a trip after a small gap in the road, and the ring under the player is smaller, centred on their feet and rimmed dark. The route is a trail of gold dots fixed to the ground, the clicked spot is a gold X (a slightly larger one for a town), and map dots and diamonds are symbols in the same colours: a skull for roaming bands, a stone arch for dungeons (red if undiscovered, brass if found), a person for party members and other players (two for a crowd), a backpack for pilgrims and pedlars, and a pickaxe, pine, leaf, paw print and fish for Mining, Logging, Herbalism, Hunting and Fishing nodes. Ships, their map lookups and wagon harness ropes do the same work for less effort and memory garbage, so walking the coast runs faster. A found dungeon shows as a dungeon on the Overworld grid. Overworld travel no longer drops to 1x speed and stays there, and land that fails to load is loaded again instead of leaving a hole. Travelling fast no longer spends time building ground already passed, and the land ahead loads up to three times faster, so holes in the view and drops to 1x speed are much rarer, including on straight roads. While ground is still loading the view may run at about 30 fps instead of 60 until the land is built.
Pause Menu ||| The enhanced pause menu displays guild reputation and features an online mailbox represented by an envelope beside the hourglass. When developers send a message, the envelope displays a count and a line in world chat announces post arrival; messages can be opened to read or accessed from anywhere by typing /mail in chat on either menu style, and messages with unclaimed items cannot be accidentally discarded. A Growth tab sits beside Stats. Enter on a menu tab no longer presses Ascend, and the Yes/No confirmation keeps keyboard focus inside the box and answers only once.
Professions ||| The Professions page shows all thirteen professions. At ranks 50 and 100 the player chooses a specialisation, the first choice free and later changes costing 1,000 Marks (silver today) and taking a week, with the old one working until then. A toast says what each harvest brought, a banner marks ranks 25, 50, 75 and 100, the first harvest each session says where goods went (the Stores) and which key opens them, and the Down arrow opens Professions and Stores on either skin. Professions listed as practised grew from Herbalism, Mining and Logging to include Smithing, Carpentry, Fishing, Masonry, Cooking and Jewelcrafting, with the page explaining how to gather each. Mining, Logging, Herbalism (the Basket included), Fishing and Hunting no longer stop for the day, and the chip under the compass and the Professions tab show the day's count without a limit. Some crafts were merged so there are five instead of eight: a merged craft keeps the higher of its two XP totals, offers four choices at a rank each named for the craft it acts on, and a player may still take two crafts past Journeyman, now two of five. Each rank's choice is the higher craft's where it chose, else the other's. A pending perk change no longer blocks choosing perks when the player reaches a new rank. Smithing, Building, Outfitting and Provisioning's cooking say on the Professions page where their stations are, how to work them from the Stores page and what earns XP. A character raises only two crafts past Journeyman (rank 50); the Professions tab explains the limit before it is reached, a station's XP includes the Firmament stars' bonus, the service decides the limit inside each craft so two crafts finished at once can no longer freeze three at rank 50, and a craft below 50 shows its next rank's XP.
Rare and Legendary Gear ||| Rare and Legendary gear breaks and stays instead of vanishing. Sweet Mother's Kiss gives its +50% against bosses under a quarter of their health, the Oblivion Gate's Warden and host, the Abyss Dungeon's Remnant, the sea serpent and every foe in an Elite Breach, and the other "under its health" effects (Serpent's Kiss's double poison, Run Them Down, Gutting Lunge, Headsman's Chop and the Axe and Blunt Weapon mastery bonuses) work against those foes too.
Revenants ||| Revenants are special foes that kill the player or escape them and return to hunt. Elite and Champion foes of level 3 or more that kill the player become their revenant, as do special foes that flee below 20% health and anything that finishes the player with a spell or poison. They rank up each kill or escape to rank 5, each rank adding +25% health and +10% damage, and up to 5 can hunt at once. Each has a personality (Brutal, Witty, Humorous, Arrogant, Cold, Zealous, Unhinged, Honourable, Craven or Weary) and a named epithet, and learns from how it was beaten, returning with up to three adaptations, never immunity, and a hidden weakness that takes 50% more once revealed. From rank 3 its will must be broken before it kneels. A beaten revenant kneels and the player chooses Kill or Spare in the loot window: Kill is an execution that drops a unique Magic, Rare or Legendary weapon of its own plus all it carried, and Spare swears it as a companion for good, while leaving it 90 seconds makes it escape and rank up. Each has a lair, kin and a signature attack, and one that kills the player online takes an unlocked item. Under Project Legacy the revenant that killed an ancestor comes after their heir. Online, revenants wait for Renown 10: below it no foe becomes a revenant or breaks and runs to become one and none comes back, while existing revenants are held and marked Held on the Revenants page with when they will come. Revenants come back a little less often everywhere, a revenant that is due now taking 2 in 5 encounter chances instead of 1 in 2. Another player's Disintegrate no longer deletes the player's revenant outright but counts as a killing blow, and only the owner decides their revenant's fate. Not every elite or champion can become a revenant: about one in four is marked Unquiet ("Unquiet Elite Orc") and only a marked foe can rise, at most three hunt the player at once, and one comes back after two to four days. A marked foe breaks and runs at a third of its health one time in two, so most revenants are born by getting away, and a revenant ranks up every time it gets away (killing the player or tearing away from the killing blow ranks it only every second time, and from rank II its will must be broken or it tears away). When one gets away in the open it goes to ground in the nearest dungeon and leaves a trail, weakened at 60% of its health if reached within twelve hours, and left alone its wounds heal, the oldest every five days. From rank II each flees its own way (Smoke for people, Blink for casters, spirits, daedra and atronachs, Sink for skeletons, zombies, mummies, vampires and spriggans, Bolt for beasts and monsters), the blow that broke it leaves a wound (a blade bleeds it, a hammer or fist cracks it, an axe maims it, an arrow lames it, an element sears it, three kept), and it wants Vengeance, Mastery or Plunder by temperament, with omens on the day it returns. Each rank adds +40% health, +15% damage and +3 levels for a class foe (sworn revenants keep the old values), and a rank-V revenant that gets away once more becomes the Dread, which goes to ground for good, never comes to the player, has half again its health, makes two last stands and drops a Legendary trophy. The player's own Disintegrate no longer deletes a revenant: it still breaks through the last stand and will but brings the revenant to its knees like any killing blow, to Kill (its trophy weapon drops) or Spare (it joins as a companion).
Server ||| The server does much less work per player, and reconnecting and moving no longer rewrite the player's details. When nobody else is in the player's area their game stops sending its enemies and ship cabin. The account service makes fewer trips to its database, so busy evenings should load faster.
Shared Containers ||| Shared Containers covers a building's cupboards and chests and the coffins and other search spots in dungeons, each one shared list for everyone using it. Closing a window no longer restores an item someone took or erases one someone put in, two players opening a cupboard see the same contents, and Take all and Stash all land every piece. A find from a coffin or search spot is shared by the party as one list with one copy of each piece, two separate groups searching the same coffin each loot their own find, and searching a spot again after its five hours locks it for the whole party again. An item put into a cupboard the building's host never accepted comes back on leaving, and players on an older game version still see the old behaviour in the same room until they reload.
Ship Hold ||| F4 (rebindable, "Cargo" under Come Sail Away) opens the hold of the ship the player is on, at the helm, on deck or in the cabin, and away from it opens the last ship they helmed or the first ship in their fleet with a hold, wherever it is moored or laid up. The helm panel and each ship on the Fleet page have a Hold button, and the hold works as real storage in the Enhanced inventory, where cargo can be put in as well as taken out, a laid-up ship's packed weight updating when the hold closes, taking cargo out of a packed ship's hold counting its weight once, and the hold bindable to a controller button. A packed ship's parts weigh only her hull (120 kg at most) and cargo in her hold no longer adds to it, so storing things in a packed ship cannot make her too heavy to pick up.
Ships ||| Ships feature carrack and large boat variations with gunports, wheels, steering, rigs, and swivel guns. Bank ship loans are removed and bug fixes cover audio and navigation. Far-off ship identification requires proximity, Lookouts, or spyglasses rather than automatic cards, and distant ships sailing around other players across the map are no longer built/animated on your screen. Ships far out at sea, including those other players leave behind on logging off, no longer keep a full animated hull far away and decide less often while nobody can see them, with their voyages, fights and damage carrying on as before. The ships' stern windows can be seen through by day (the galleon's into her great cabin, the carrack's transom windows) and close and glow warm at night when the lanterns are lit, while the caravan's windows stay see-through day and night. The galleon, carrack and large boat wear their own painted liveries again, with the oxblood strakes, the gilt and the stern gallery, in Daggerfall's own colour palette. Towns with ships in port run smoother online, the ships costing about a quarter of their old share of each frame and leaving far less garbage. Ship text no longer calls a ship "her" or "she" across the sea, the Fleet page, the shipyard, the crew hall, the Captain's Stars, the sea chart, the tutorials and the crew's chatter, saying "the ship", "this ship" or "it", and the 13 translated languages match. There is no stutter the first time a named or painted ship comes into view and painted sea ships no longer slow rendering. Ship parts already too heavy to take out of a chest or other storage are fixed when the save loads, with nothing in their hold lost, and a character too weak to carry a ship's parts is told "The ship's parts are too heavy for you to carry."
Shops ||| Shops operate on night shifts with attribute caps for online pricing, and player-transferred items from chests, bodies, or shops undergo server validity checks. Shops pay for a crafted Superior or Masterwork piece what they pay for the plain piece its recipe makes, since the magic or rare lines are power and not resale value, and patrons follow the same rule while found magic and rare items are priced as before. The keyed shelf prices a sale as the trade window does and no longer buys conjured pieces. General Stores and Pawn Shops no longer sell or buy ship deeds and parts, which are traded at shipyards, and shops do not buy the exchange's goods. Weapon Smiths always keep 50-100 arrows on the counter, Armorers and General Stores 14-29 and Pawn Shops 7-14. Every shelf item shows its price before it is clicked and a List / Pictures switch shows both sides as item tiles, the player's side is labelled "Your pack", the footer shows "To pay" (or "You get") and "Your gold" separately, a magic-only counter is titled "Sell magic items", Steal sits apart from the other buttons, and a button that cannot be used yet explains why when pressed. A shop never pays more for an item than was paid a shop for it, so carrying goods between regions makes no gold, while found loot sells as before and improving a bought item (setting a gem, honing, reforging) is paid for on selling. On a seat's Market Day and for a holder's members shops pay less for what is sold as well as asking less, essentials cannot be bought and sold back for profit, and a friend's caravan gives its friend's price both ways. Materials bought at a shop stay in the pack instead of the Stores, and selling a very large batch of gear no longer pays below zero but pays its real price as a letter of credit.
Specialisation Reset ||| An arcane or martial specialisation can be given up for 200,000 gold each: in the Growth tab's Specialisations guide the player opens the chosen path and presses Reset, and a confirmation shows the price, what is lost and which paths can be picked afterwards. Mastered skills stay mastered, so any path the masteries unlock, including the same one, can be chosen, and a bought signature spell stays in the spellbook as an ordinary spell. Online the price is paid from coins, then letters of credit, then the bank account, and offline from coins or letters of credit. If no other path of that kind could ever open, the Reset button explains why instead of charging, and the specialisation choice boxes state what a later reset costs.
Spellbook ||| The spellbook gesture was recorded, and the Icon button lets the player pick any icon for a spell, or none so it shows its initials. The spellbook can no longer be dropped, put in a chest, pile or companion's pack, shift-dropped or sold at a shop, though it still goes into the player's own wagon, with the message "Your spellbook never leaves you." A spell the player made shows its real settings in the Spellbook, and only the parts its effects use. Picking a spell no longer jumps the list back to the top, the list scrolls on its own so the picked spell stays in view beside it (below it on a phone), and deleting a spell no longer opens the next one scrolled down to where the deleted one was.
Town Economy ||| Every city, town and village has its own economy online, rated from Destitute to Booming and shown on hover on the Held Map or Overworld with whether its market is rising or falling, how its prices compare with the region's usual ones and who holds the area. A Booming town's shops ask up to a quarter less, and a Destitute town's ask up to a quarter more and pay less for what is sold. What players do in a town raises it (walking its streets, buying at players' traders, driving a raid from its gates), while gold spent never moves it and each player's part is capped per town per day. Townsfolk talk about how trade is going, a Booming town holds a second market day each week and a Destitute one a market only every other week, and a port town's sea lanes lift its economy, the busier the harbour the better the town does.
Town Map ||| The town map marks Notice Boards, player homes, quest buildings, and the Arena of Daggerfall, while keeping guild halls named during active contracts. A town's card shows two chapter patrons by name and a count of the rest, the guild holding most chapters first, only when the card has room so it fits a phone screen.
Tutorials ||| New characters get a short welcome tour covering moving and climbing, using things, the HUD, and the menu and saving, replacing the old tutorial, and any key a tutorial mentions is the key currently bound. The first time the player reaches something new a short note explains it, with about 56 systems having one from the inventory and maps to shops, banks, guilds, card tables, horses and wagons, boats, the arena, the Abyss Dungeon, family, revenants, gathering, chat, parties, duels and the open zone. Each note shows once, closes on a click or key press (not from a key already held), and waits until the pause menu and loading screens are gone. A Tutorials tab in the pause menu and a Tutorials button on the main menu hold all 68 notes in 13 sections, which can be searched with unread ones marked, and popups can be turned off, all shown again or everything marked read. The Sailing page sends the player to the shipyards and there is a Lighthouses page. A Crafting page lists which shops hold which station.
Wagon ||| Wagons stay attached without stretching, are drawn on the Overworld, and feature multiple vehicle types with storage, bench seating, harnesses, turning wheels, third-person camera support, and performance culling. Worn-out wagons never become unusable, broken-down save wagons are marked "Failing" so they can be driven again, and smiths/Repairs Objects enchantments cannot repair wagons or horses. A parked team's harness stops recomputing its ropes while nothing moves. Wagon paints are Daggerfall's colours, fabrics, woods, rugs, tiles and night sky, with every paint still applying, and carts, open wagons and caravans roll on the painted wheels they had before the Daggerfall-texture pass (seven wooden spokes, a dark hub and an iron tyre with its rivets). The wagons and the caravan have their own paint back, with the caravan's livery, shutters, curtains and sunburst, in Daggerfall's colours. Companions riding in the back climb down over the side when the wagon stops instead of dropping through the bed and getting stuck, and a parked wagon no longer closes around a companion standing where it was laid.
Weapon Techniques ||| Magic, Rare and Legendary weapons can drop with a technique, a special attack with its own key, by default the mouse's back side button and rebindable under Controls > Combat, with a Tech button for touch corners and a d-pad placement on controllers, and unarmed fighters find theirs on Gauntlets. There are twelve: Volley and Piercing Shot for bows (the Dwarven Thunderlock can carry Piercing Shot), Leap Strike and Whirlwind for long blades, Shadowstep and Lunge for short blades, Cleave and Headsman's Chop for axes, Ground Slam and Skull Crack for blunt weapons, and Flying Kick and Haymaker for bare hands. Holding the key aims, with a blue ground marker that turns red out of reach, letting go fires and a quick tap fires where the player looks. Every technique costs fatigue and has a cooldown shown on the Enhanced HUD, damage multiplies a normal hit by the item's +N% roll so a miss is still a miss, and techniques are disabled in duels and player-vs-player bouts, never hit another player and need the loot ladder. Each has its own feel, with view movement, shake, sparks and sounds, and a Technique camera motion setting under Settings > Accessibility > Motion scales the movement from Full to Off. Flying Kick can be used while jumping, falling or stepping off a ledge, while every other leap and dash still needs solid ground, and a kick near the ground no longer cancels the damage from a long fall. Leap and dash techniques, Flying Kick included, work from the ground in Slowfalling gear such as The Raven's Wings and land where aimed.
Weir Keys ||| A Weir Key opens an Elite Breach and is spent on opening. Closing a Dagon's Breach can drop one: the first close always gives one, after that the chance is 1 in 6 (1 in 4 with the rite broken), and after five misses in a row the next close is certain; clearing an Abyss Dungeon's Hour drops one 1 in 3, a Valkynaz's Redoubt 1 in 4 (at most once a week), and elite foes in dungeons can drop one too (1 in 60 in an Abyss Dungeon, 1 in 150 in an Elite Dungeon, 1 in 400 in a Regular Dungeon). Everyone who had already closed a Dagon's Breach got one key as a launch gift, and the player must have closed a Dagon's Breach before they can open a keyed one. A key that fails to open comes back on its own, and carrying one no longer freezes trading. The Weir Key's window keeps its list: tapping the top line (the key count) or the quest warning line no longer closes it, answering No to "Go without ...?" brings the list of starts back, and using a key from the pack closes the pack so the window can open.
World of Daggerfall ||| World of Daggerfall features ported assets with shared online camps, fixed foliage and texture scales, rounded default mountains, and properly aligned rock-field boulders. Its rocks, camps, shrines and ruins no longer stand on the low open sea or the lowest shore of the coastal regions, because the mod's check now works.
Wrothgarian Zone ||| The heart of the Wrothgarian Mountains is an open PvP zone, marked on the world map with a red border and drifting fog, with Wrothgaria itself outside it. Other players in the zone, its dungeons included, can attack except the party, guild and duel opponent; attacking a player inside a city calls the guards, and the zone's guards are ten times as strong. Foes are four times as strong and travel in packs four times the size, and the zone has four tiers from the Foothills to the Heart with loot rising +25%, +50%, +75% and +100%. On death the player keeps potions, torches, campfire kit, food, ammunition and anything that never changes hands, bags and the cart's load drop for 10 minutes, a letter of credit drops at full value, the killer may take one piece of worn gear, and the player waits two minutes to respawn in the nearest town outside the zone. Fast travel in or out is not allowed, and inside it a fast journey costs 2,500 to 15,000 gold once every 10 minutes with travel speed capped at x20 in the wilds and x40 on roads. Each tier has two Greater Giants and the zone has its own daily-changing dungeon halls, banks, house purchases, world events and revenants are restricted (one revenant at a time), and clicking the zone on the world map or pressing V opens its own map. Wild Zone halls no longer vanish partway through their day after someone has been inside one. Dying in the Wrothgarian Mountains while the wagon is parked outside the zone, or sent to the stable, loses nothing from it, while a wagon driven, led or parked inside the zone still loses its cargo. A worn item looted from a player in the open PvP zone reaches the looter even if they share a guild or party, staying held until collected and going back to its owner if nobody does, and members of the same guild can no longer attack each other there, as with a party. Players can fight each other in the zone's dungeons again, as intended, and their blood shows there; every attack on another player underground had been refused.
